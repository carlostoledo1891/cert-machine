\begin{abstract}
The depth of a planetary transit measures the stellar light blocked, and how
much light sits where the planet passes is fixed by a limb-darkening law that is
not measured for the star in question. Published radius ratios therefore carry an
atmosphere model inside them. We ask instead what the photometry forces on its
own. Writing the stellar surface brightness as an arbitrary nonnegative measure
$\mu$ on the unit disc, normalised so that the out-of-transit flux is one, every
photometric datum becomes a two-sided \emph{linear} constraint on $\mu$, and the
set of radius ratios consistent with the light curve is decided exactly: a value
is admitted by exhibiting an atomic measure and refuted by a Farkas certificate
verified over the continuum in interval arithmetic. On Kepler short-cadence
photometry of TrES-2\,b and Kepler-7\,b the resulting intervals contain every
published value for each planet and are strongly one-sided. We give the reason
in closed form: for $k<b$ the circle of radius $r<b-k$ is never occulted, so any
amount of light may hide there and only the normalisation notices, which makes
the set of achievable depth vectors star-shaped about the origin. Light can hide
and cannot un-hide, so the photometry bounds the planet tightly from below and
loosely from above --- and the upper bound quoted in the literature is bought
almost entirely by the assumption that the star does not brighten outward.
\end{abstract}

\section{The constraint set}

Let the star have unit radius and circularly symmetric specific intensity
$I(r)\ge 0$ on $r\in[0,1]$. Write the flux emitted between $r$ and $r+dr$ as
$d\mu(r)=2\pi r I(r)\,dr$, so that $\mu$ is a nonnegative measure and the
statement that the light curve equals one outside transit is exactly
\begin{equation}
\int_0^1 d\mu = 1 .
\label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}
A planet of radius $k=R_p/R_\star$ whose centre lies at sky-plane distance $z$
from the stellar centre covers a fraction
\begin{equation}
w(r;z,k)\;=\;\frac{1}{\pi}\arccos\!\Big(\mathrm{clamp}\Big[\frac{z^2+r^2-k^2}{2zr},-1,1\Big]\Big)
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
of the circle of radius $r$, so the missing flux at that epoch is
$\int_0^1 w(r;z,k)\,d\mu(r)$.

\begin{remark}
The clamp in \eqref{eq:kernel} is the entire case analysis. The argument is
$\le -1$ exactly when $z+r\le k$, which is when the circle lies wholly behind the
planet and the covered fraction is $1$; it is $\ge 1$ exactly when $r+k\le z$ or
$r\ge z+k$, which is when the two do not meet. So \eqref{eq:kernel} \emph{is} the
function on all of its domain, not an approximation valid on one regime, and an
interval enclosure of it requires no branching --- which matters, because
$w$ has a square-root kink in $r$ at $r=|z-k|$ and $r=z+k$ where $\partial_r w$
is unbounded, so a Lipschitz or second-order margin cannot close the continuum
here and interval subdivision must.
\end{remark}

For a circular orbit of period $P$, scaled semi-major axis $a/R_\star$ and impact
parameter $b$, $z(t)=\sqrt{(a/R_\star)^2\sin^2\theta+b^2\cos^2\theta}$ with
$\theta=2\pi t/P$, so $z(0)=b$. Grouping the folded photometry into $m$ bins with
measured depths $d_j$ and a stated allowance $\varepsilon_j$, define
\begin{equation}
S(k)\;=\;\Big\{\mu\ge 0 \ \text{on}\ [0,1]\ :\ \int d\mu=1,\quad
l_j\le \int W_j\,d\mu \le u_j,\ j=1,\dots,m\Big\},
\label{eq:S}
\end{equation}
$W_j(r)=w(r;z(t_j),k)$ and $[l_j,u_j]=[d_j-\varepsilon_j,\,d_j+\varepsilon_j]$.
No limb-darkening law, no smoothness, no monotonicity, no pixel basis and no
prior enter \eqref{eq:S}.

\section{Feasibility is a hull question}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hull}
$S(k)\neq\emptyset$ if and only if
$\operatorname{conv}\{W(r):r\in[0,1]\}\cap\prod_j[l_j,u_j]\neq\emptyset$, where
$W(r)=(W_1(r),\dots,W_m(r))$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:norm} $\mu$ is a probability measure, so $\int W\,d\mu$ is a
barycentre of $\{W(r)\}$ and lies in its convex hull; conversely every point of
the hull is $\int W\,d\mu$ for a finitely supported $\mu\ge0$ of unit mass, by
Carath\'eodory.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:hull} makes both verdicts constructive. When the hull meets
the box, the meeting point is a finite convex combination, i.e.\ an atomic
measure that can be exhibited and re-checked bin by bin. When it misses, a
separating direction certifies it:

\begin{proposition}[refutation certificate]\label{prop:farkas}
Suppose the kernel is only known to lie between $W^{\mathrm{lo}}_j$ and
$W^{\mathrm{hi}}_j$ pointwise (as it is when the orbit is a box rather than a
point, and when a bin spans a range of epochs). If there exist
$\alpha,\beta\in\R_{\ge0}^m$ with
\begin{equation}
\min_{r\in[0,1]}\ \Big[\sum_j \alpha_j W^{\mathrm{lo}}_j(r)-\sum_j \beta_j W^{\mathrm{hi}}_j(r)\Big]
\;>\;\sum_j \alpha_j u_j-\sum_j \beta_j l_j ,
\label{eq:cert}
\end{equation}
then $S(k)=\emptyset$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mu\in S(k)$ and write $h$ for the bracketed function and $c=-\min_r h$.
Since $\mu\ge0$ and $\int d\mu=1$, $\int (h+c)\,d\mu\ge0$, i.e.\
$\int h\,d\mu\ge -c=\min_r h$. Because $\mu\ge0$ we also have
$\int W^{\mathrm{lo}}_j d\mu\le\int W_j d\mu\le u_j$ and
$\int W^{\mathrm{hi}}_j d\mu\ge \int W_j d\mu\ge l_j$, so
$\int h\,d\mu\le\sum\alpha_j u_j-\sum\beta_j l_j$, contradicting \eqref{eq:cert}.
\end{proof}

The minimum in \eqref{eq:cert} is over a continuum. It is bounded below by
subdividing $[0,1]$ and enclosing $h$ on each cell in outward-rounded interval
arithmetic; the subdivision is adaptive and is refined in $z$ as well as in $r$,
because the closed form \eqref{eq:kernel} mentions $z$ five times and over a
$z$-interval of width $10^{-2}$ the naive enclosure of the area kernel of
Section~\ref{sec:ladder} returned $[0.16,1.00]$ where the true value was $0.997$.
Whatever multipliers are proposed, \eqref{eq:cert} is checked against the
definition; the optimiser is never trusted.

\section{The ladder is a change of kernel}\label{sec:ladder}

An assumption about the star enters as a different kernel on the same machinery.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mono}
$I$ is nonnegative and non-increasing on $[0,1]$ if and only if
$I(r)=\int_0^1 \mathbf 1\{r\le s\}\,d\nu(s)$ for a nonnegative measure $\nu$.
Writing $d\nu'(s)=s^2\,d\nu(s)$, the constraint set becomes \eqref{eq:S} with
$\mu$ replaced by $\nu'$ and $W_j(r)$ replaced by
$\widetilde W_j(s)=A(s;z_j,k)/\pi s^2$, where $A(s;z,k)$ is the area of
intersection of the disc of radius $s$ with the planet disc.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The representation is the Lebesgue--Stieltjes decomposition of the non-increasing
function $I$. Then
$\int_0^1 w\,d\mu=\int_0^1\!\big[\int_0^s w(r;z,k)2\pi r\,dr\big]d\nu(s)
=\int_0^1 A(s;z,k)\,d\nu(s)$ by Fubini, and $\int d\mu=\int \pi s^2 d\nu$, so
normalising by $\pi$ gives the stated form.
\end{proof}

So ``the star does not brighten outward'' costs one substitution and no new code.
Its price is read off in the units of the answer.

\section{Why the answer is one-sided}

\begin{proposition}[invisible core]\label{prop:core}
Let $k<b$ and $r<b-k$. Then $w(r;z(t),k)=0$ for every $t$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$z(t)^2=(a/R_\star)^2\sin^2\theta+b^2\cos^2\theta\ge b^2$ because
$a/R_\star\ge b$, so $z(t)\ge b$. If $r+k\le b\le z(t)$ the circle of radius $r$
and the planet disc are disjoint and the covered fraction is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:star}
Fix $k<b$ and let $\mathcal D(k)=\{(\int W_j d\mu)_j : \mu\ge0,\ \int d\mu=1\}$
be the set of achievable depth vectors. Then $\mathcal D(k)$ is star-shaped about
the origin: if $d\in\mathcal D(k)$ then $\lambda d\in\mathcal D(k)$ for every
$\lambda\in(0,1]$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix $r_0<b-k$ and put $\mu_\lambda=\lambda\mu+(1-\lambda)\delta_{r_0}$. It is
nonnegative of unit mass, and by Proposition~\ref{prop:core} the added atom
contributes nothing to any $\int W_j d\mu_\lambda$, which is therefore
$\lambda\int W_j d\mu$.
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:star} says the amplitude of a transit carries no information
that the geometry does not already fix: a larger planet blocking a smaller share
of a dimmer visible annulus reproduces the same depths exactly. What it cannot
reproduce is the same \emph{duration} --- a larger planet reaches the limb
earlier, and the visible annulus cannot be silent then. Light can hide and cannot
un-hide, and that asymmetry is what the computed intervals show.

\section{Photometry, and what was checked before anything was believed}

Kepler short-cadence PDCSAP photometry is masked on the mission's own quality
flag, cut into transits, and divided by a straight line fitted to each transit's
out-of-transit baseline; the root-mean-square disagreement between the two
baseline halves is carried into every error bar as a common-mode allowance.
Three checks precede any enclosure.

\emph{Are the stated errors honest?} The out-of-transit scatter of the binned
curve is $1.02$ times the propagated PDCSAP errors. They are.

\emph{Is the fold centred?} The archive ephemeris leaves Kepler-7\,b asymmetric
under time reversal by $135$\,ppm against a $56$\,ppm floor; its mid-transit is
$248$\,s out. A mis-centred fold is a systematic that looks exactly like a
limb-darkening residual, so the fold is re-centred by minimising
$\sum_t [F(+t)-F(-t)]^2$ over a shift and a period correction --- using the
mirror symmetry of a transit and nothing else, with no law, no radius and no
impact parameter.

\emph{Does the pipeline reproduce the field?} A conventional quadratic
limb-darkening fit of the same extracted curve gives $k=0.1253$ for TrES-2\,b,
inside the published cluster $0.1239$--$0.1278$, at $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}\approx0.9$.
It does. The per-bin error budget used for the enclosure is then \emph{measured}
rather than chosen: it is the smallest allowance that does not refuse that fit.

The intervals contain every published value for each planet, and are markedly
one-sided in the direction Corollary~\ref{cor:star} predicts. The lower edge sits
close beneath the conventional fit; the upper edge is far above it under
nonnegativity alone and is pulled in substantially by the monotone rung of
Proposition~\ref{prop:mono}. The quantity a published error bar reports is
therefore not the width the photometry supports: it is the width the atmosphere
model supports, and the two differ by a factor that this construction measures
rather than assumes.

\section{Limitations}

The error budget is a per-bin box at $n\sigma$, which permits
$\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}$ up to $n^2$ and is looser than the likelihood set behind a
published bar; the like-for-like budget is the $\ell^2$ ball
$\sum_j((v_j-d_j)/\sigma_j)^2\le \chi^2 m$, which is convex and needs no new
machinery but does need a point-valued kernel, whereas a bin spanning a range of
epochs makes every kernel interval-valued. The orbit is carried as a box handled
by Proposition~\ref{prop:farkas}, which lets each bin's geometry vary
independently and is therefore a relaxation --- wider than the truth, never
narrower. Eccentricity is not carried. The star is taken circularly symmetric;
spots and gravity darkening would replace the measure on $[0,1]$ by a measure on
the disc, which changes the kernel and not the argument.

Finally, one metered test exists and is not yet run. SDO/HMI watched Venus cross
the Sun in June 2012: a real transit of a real star for which the apparent radius
ratio is known from the ephemeris to about $0.1\%$ and the intensity profile is
measured by the same instrument. The disc-integrated HMI keyword does contain the
transit, under a $2900$\,ppm instrumental drift over the nine-hour window that is
three times the $1000$\,ppm signal; forming the blocked-to-total ratio within
each image cancels a global gain exactly and is the way in.
