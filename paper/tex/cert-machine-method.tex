\begin{abstract}
Search programs, surrogate models and language models propose constants, counterexamples, fitted parameters and operating points faster than they can be refereed, each checked by its producer. We present cert-machine, a system that decides such claims independently of the claimant. \MtOutsideRerunsWordCap{} re-derivations by \MtOutsidePartiesWord{} outside parties, \MtOutsideOwnWord{} with their own code, reached its verdicts. A claim is read as a conjunction of inequalities over a declared input box, under a universal or an epistemic reading, and receives one of three verdicts---certified, refuted or refused---each published with the object that justifies it: a certificate, a falsifying input, or a refusal's reason and, for a declared parameter, the threshold that would change it. Verdicts are sound under the inclusion property, secured by exact rational and outward-rounded interval arithmetic with a stated tail bound for every function; classical certifiers reduce analytic questions to such decisions, and executable gates guard the code. On a pre-registered benchmark of \MtSaMutants{} claims of known truth, built by mutating published objects with exact witnesses from programs sharing no code with the deciders, the system produced \MtSaFalseCert{} false certifications and \MtSaFalseRef{} false refutations, and exposed two of its own defects, fixed on the record. Its interval kernel enclosed the exact value in all \MtArbCases{} comparisons with an independent library and in every enclosure case of the IEEE~1788 suite it implements; a $\delta$-complete decider agreed on every engineering claim and contradicted none. Applied to \RegRows{} machine-generated claims, it exposed gaps between what a producer's checker verified and what its output states.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Machine-proposed numbers are decided by a checker that shares no code with the claimant.
\item Three verdicts, each published with the object that justifies it; a refusal names its reason.
\item Soundness proved under the inclusion property; every function of the arithmetic table carries a stated tail bound.
\item A pre-registered benchmark of \MtSaMutants{} claims of known truth: no false certification, no false refutation; two defects found and fixed.
\item Exact certificates re-check without the engine; interval ones re-decide from pinned inputs.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Context}
Machines now propose mathematics and engineering numbers at a rate no referee can follow. Conjectures on fundamental constants are generated from numerical coincidences~\citep{Raayoni21}; constructions in combinatorics and analysis are found by program search guided by language models~\citep{RomeraParedes24,Novikov25,GGSWT25} and by learned search~\citep{Wagner21,Davies21,Fawzi22}; autonomous research systems publish record configurations with their own verification files~\citep{Yang26}; long proofs arrive with machine formalisations~\citep{OpenAINS,OpenAIMath,Trinh24}; and in engineering, surrogate models, optimisers and language models propose operating points and design values~\citep{P2}. In every case the number reaches its reader together with a checker written by its producer.

\paragraph{Problem}
A checker written by the producer of a claim is subject to the same modelling and implementation errors as the claim. The register of this project~\citep{Register} records, among other defects, a floating-point product printed as an exact constant, witnesses that hold only within the verifier's own tolerance, a checker that compares in double precision what it encloses at a hundred digits, a pairwise condition proved by structure and never checked pair by pair, and a clause present in a paper's theorem but absent from the formal statement that was proved~\citep{Bastounis26}. What is missing is a decider that reads the claim from its published statement and bytes, never runs the claimant's program in its trust path, and returns an answer that a third party can re-check without trusting the decider either.

\paragraph{Existing tools, and the gap}
The ingredients of such a decider are decades old: interval arithmetic with outward rounding~\citep{Moore79,Neumaier90,IEEE1788std}, the Krawczyk operator~\citep{Krawczyk69,Rump10}, verification methods and ball arithmetic~\citep{Rump99,Johansson17}, interval global optimisation~\citep{HansenWalster04,Kearfott96}, filtered exact predicates~\citep{Fortune96,Shewchuk97,Yap97}, $\delta$-complete decision procedures~\citep{GaoAvigadClarke12,dReal13}, and certificates checked inside a proof assistant~\citep{SolovyevHales13,MartinDorel16,Mahboubi19}. Each answers a question posed by its own user about its own model (\cref{sec:related}). None of them, to our knowledge, is organised around deciding \emph{other parties'} published numbers: reading a printed digit string as the box it denotes, distinguishing a claim about every input in a box from a claim about an unknown true input inside it, and turning a failure to decide into a published result that states what would decide it. In the engineering instances of this paper the second, epistemic reading is the one asked, and neither verification and validation nor uncertainty quantification~\citep{OberkampfRoy10} answers it: the former qualifies a model, the latter gives a dispersion, whereas the engineer asks whether a given rule holds for every input that the declared evidence allows.

\paragraph{Objective}
This paper asks three questions. (Q1)~Can a single three-valued decision grammar, proved sound under one hypothesis, cover claims arriving from mathematics, from engineering and from metocean statistics? (Q2)~What must the arithmetic and the software process guarantee for that hypothesis to hold in an implementation? (Q3)~On claims whose truth is known, how often does the system certify, refute or refuse, and at what cost?

\paragraph{Contributions}
The contribution is a system, not a numerical technique:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a three-valued decision grammar with witnesses, refusal as a result and flip thresholds, with a universal and an epistemic reading, and its soundness theorem (\cref{sec:grammar});
\item an arithmetic layer that secures the theorem's single hypothesis on IEEE-754 doubles and exact rationals, with a lemma for each elementary and special function it uses (\cref{sec:intervals}, \ref{app:arith});
\item a catalogue of standard certifiers, each mapped to the grammar with its witness and its refusal reasons (\cref{sec:certifiers});
\item a verification protocol whose rules are executable gates rather than guidelines, with the gates that are not yet enforced listed as such (\cref{sec:protocol}); and
\item a pre-registered evaluation of the system against claims of known truth, built by mutating its own public register with exact witnesses from programs sharing no code with the deciders, which found two defects of the system by its rule and measures what soundness promises (\cref{sec:metrics}, \cref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}
Interval arithmetic, the Krawczyk operator, filtered predicates, sums of squares and Sturm sequences are cited, not claimed.

\paragraph{Outline}
\Cref{sec:method} presents the method in four layers: foundations, mathematical formulation, metrics and decision rules, and software architecture with its protocol. \Cref{sec:results} reports the benchmark with known truth, the kernel's comparison with an independent library, the public register with its three largest exercises, and the coverage across domains; \cref{sec:discussion} counts the independence the system can claim, places it beside existing tools, and states the threats to validity and the limits; \cref{sec:conclusions} concludes. The supplementary material holds the proofs (S1, S2), the certifiers in detail (S3), the decision loop (S4) and the reproduction table (S5).

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}
%% =====================================================================

The four layers run from the mathematics on which a verdict rests to the code that computes it. Four implementations recur below as running instances. (a)~The \emph{flow-line gate} of the engineering demonstrator~\citep{P2} decides, under the epistemic reading, whether the wellhead pressure of a water-injection line stays below a rule over a declared envelope of roughness, viscosity, diameter, density, elevation and length, with Colebrook's friction law solved inside the enclosure; the pump pressure is its operating choice. (b)~The \emph{rock-physics engine}~\citep{P3} decides, over declared boxes of rock and fluid properties, whether a time-lapse seismic change is guaranteed to be detectable. (c)~The \emph{likelihood certifier} of the metocean study~\citep{P1} certifies each maximum-likelihood fit of an extreme-value family to a buoy's hourly record as the unique maximiser in its box, and ranks the families. (d)~A \emph{universal branch-and-bound} decides a statement for every input in a box by bisection on the widest coordinate; its instance in the register bounds, for every monic polynomial of a given degree with all roots in $[-1,1]$, the measure of the set where the polynomial is below one in absolute value (one register row~\citep{Register}).

\subsection{Foundations}\label{sec:foundations}
%% ---------------------------------------------------------------------

The system rests on three results. The first is the \emph{fundamental theorem of interval arithmetic}: the interval evaluation of an expression encloses the range of the function it denotes~\citep{Moore79,Moore09,Neumaier90}, which turns finite floating-point computation into proof. The arithmetic is standardised as IEEE~1788-2015~\citep{IEEE1788std,Revol17}, test suites check conformance~\citep{Benet24}, and directed rounding can be emulated when the rounding mode cannot be set~\citep{Rump16fixed}, the situation of the implementation described here (\cref{prop:outward}); the interval layer is not claimed conformant, and its operations were run on the standard's test suite (\cref{sec:arb}). The same arithmetic underlies verification methods~\citep{Rump10,Rump99,Johansson17}, interval branch-and-bound~\citep{HansenWalster04,Kearfott96,Neumaier04,Rump18global} and computer-assisted proofs from dynamics to the Kepler conjecture and partial differential equations~\citep{Tucker02,Immler18,Hales17,GomezSerrano19,NakaoPlumWatanabe19}. Exact geometric computation~\citep{Yap97} gives the same guarantee by another route, filtering a floating-point evaluation through a proved error bound and falling back to exact arithmetic when the filter fails~\citep{Fortune96,Shewchuk97}.

The second concerns what cannot be decided. The first-order theory of real closed fields is decidable~\citep{Tarski51}, but with the elementary functions it is undecidable in general whether an expression is nonnegative everywhere~\citep{Richardson68}, so a sound procedure over $\exp$, $\log$ or $\sin$ must be allowed to return no answer. $\delta$-complete procedures weaken the question by a perturbation $\delta$ chosen by the user~\citep{GaoAvigadClarke12,dReal13}; interval constraint propagation returns boxes of unknown status~\citep{Loup13}. Three-valued logic gives the undetermined answer a semantics~\citep{Kleene38}: a conjunction is false if any conjunct is false, undetermined if none is false and some is undetermined, and true otherwise. The verdict lattice of \cref{sec:grammar} adopts this operationally, with refusal as the undetermined value: a refusal records that the procedure did not decide, not that the claim lacks a truth value.

The third is the separation between finding an answer and checking it. \emph{Program checking}~\citep{BlumKannan95} asks for a checker, simpler than the program it checks, whose answer on each run is correct with high probability whatever the program does. A \emph{certifying algorithm} returns, with each output, a witness from which a simple and independent checker can confirm the output without trusting the algorithm~\citep{McConnell11}, as proof-carrying code~\citep{Necula97}, SAT solvers with replayable proofs~\citep{Wetzler14,CakeLPR21}, rational sums of squares~\citep{Parrilo03,PeyrlParrilo08} and Taylor-interval certificates replayed in a proof assistant~\citep{SolovyevHales13,Magron15,MartinDorel16} do. From statistical decision theory comes the last ingredient: a classifier that may abstain is evaluated by its risk on the cases it decides and by the fraction it decides~\citep{Chow70,ElYaniv10,Geifman17,Franc21}. Test batteries sound by construction, whose reported defects are never spurious, have recently been built for language-model outputs~\citep{Li26}; cert-machine asks the same of every verdict, not only of refutations.

\subsection{Mathematical formulation}\label{sec:math}
%% ---------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection{Interval arithmetic and the inclusion property}\label{sec:intervals}

Let $\IR$ denote the set of closed bounded real intervals $X=[\underline{x},\overline{x}]$, with width $w(X)=\overline{x}-\underline{x}$ and midpoint $m(X)=(\underline{x}+\overline{x})/2$. A box is a vector $X=(X_1,\dots,X_n)\in\IR^n$, with $w(X)=\max_i w(X_i)$. For $\circ\in\{+,-,\times,\div\}$ the interval operations are the set images $X\circ Y=\{x\circ y : x\in X,\ y\in Y\}$ ($0\notin Y$ if $\circ=\div$), computed from the endpoints~\citep{Moore09,Neumaier90}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoints}
\begin{aligned}
X+Y&=[\underline{x}+\underline{y},\ \overline{x}+\overline{y}], &
X-Y&=[\underline{x}-\overline{y},\ \overline{x}-\underline{y}],\\
X\times Y&=[\min S,\ \max S],\ S=\{\underline{x}\underline{y},\underline{x}\overline{y},\overline{x}\underline{y},\overline{x}\overline{y}\}, &
X\div Y&=X\times[1/\overline{y},\ 1/\underline{y}].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Interval extension]\label{def:ext}
Let $g:D\subseteq\R^n\to\R$. A map $G$ from boxes in $D$ to $\IR$ is an \emph{interval extension} of $g$ if $g(X)=\{g(x):x\in X\}\subseteq G(X)$ for every box $X\subseteq D$ (the \emph{inclusion property}), and \emph{inclusion isotone} if $X\subseteq Y$ implies $G(X)\subseteq G(Y)$. This is Neumaier's inclusion function~\citep{Neumaier90}, weaker than Moore's extension, which also asks $G([x,x])=g(x)$; nothing below needs more.
\end{definition}

The \emph{natural extension} of an expression replaces each real operation by its interval operation and each elementary function by an enclosure of its range.

\begin{theorem}[Fundamental theorem of interval arithmetic~\citep{Moore09,Neumaier90}]\label{thm:ftia}
If every elementary function occurring in an expression for $g$ has an inclusion-isotone interval extension, then the natural extension $G$ of $g$ is an inclusion-isotone interval extension of $g$.
\end{theorem}

\Cref{thm:ftia} is the only property that the soundness of a verdict requires (\cref{prop:sound}). Its price is overestimation. Writing $q(A,B)=\max(|\underline{a}-\underline{b}|,|\overline{a}-\overline{b}|)$ for the Hausdorff distance between intervals, an extension has \emph{convergence order}~\citep{Scholz12} $\omega$ on $B$ if there are $\kappa>0$ and $\eta\ge0$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:order}
q\bigl(g(X),G(X)\bigr)\le\kappa\,w(X)^{\omega}+\eta\qquad\text{for every box }X\subseteq B.
\end{equation}
In exact arithmetic $\eta=0$; in floating point, outward rounding and the tail bounds of the function enclosures add a floor $\eta>0$ that does not shrink with $w(X)$. The natural extension has order $\omega=1$ when every elementary function has a Lipschitz extension, and the mean-value form $G_{\mathrm{MV}}(X)=G([c,c])+\nabla G(X)^{\top}(X-c)$, $c\in X$, has order $\omega=2$ when $\nabla G$ is a Lipschitz extension of the gradient~\citep{Neumaier90}. cert-machine uses the natural and mean-value forms, endpoint evaluation of monotone functions, the Krawczyk operator and second-order Taylor remainders in quadrature; it uses no Taylor models~\citep{BerzMakino98} or affine forms, because the claims it meets are low-dimensional and either exact or decided with a margin that bisection reaches. The order governs how much subdivision a decision costs (\cref{prop:termination}), never whether the decision is sound.

\paragraph{Outward rounding on doubles}\label{sec:arith}
IEEE-754 arithmetic in round-to-nearest returns, for each of $+,-,\times,\div$, the double nearest the exact result~\citep{IEEE754,Goldberg91,Muller18}, so evaluating \cref{eq:endpoints} in round-to-nearest does not enclose the exact endpoints. ECMAScript specifies each of these operations on binary64 doubles with round-to-nearest and no fused multiply--add contraction or extended precision, so \cref{prop:outward} presumes nothing beyond the standard; JavaScript cannot set the rounding mode, and directed rounding is emulated by moving each computed endpoint one unit in the last place outward~\citep{Rump09,Rump16fixed}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:outward}
Let $\circ\in\{+,-,\times,\div\}$, let $A,B$ be intervals of doubles---pairs of non-NaN doubles with lower end at most upper end, an infinite endpoint allowed as a bound---with $0\notin B$ when $\circ=\div$, and let $y_-,y_+$ be the round-to-nearest values of the smallest and largest endpoint combinations $a\circ b$. If no endpoint combination is indeterminate ($0\cdot\infty$, $\infty-\infty$, $\infty/\infty$), then $A\circ B\subseteq[\mathrm{down}(y_-),\mathrm{up}(y_+)]$, where down and up step one double toward $\mp\infty$ and fix $\mp\infty$; an exact result beyond the largest double rounds to $\pm\infty$, which the step fixes, so overflow is enclosed.
\end{proposition}
The proof is in \ref{app:arith}. The domain of \cref{prop:outward} is enforced, not assumed: a malformed operand or an indeterminate result is refused by exception and never decided; division by an interval containing zero is refused, not extended; a non-integer or overflowing integer exponent is refused. The trust base is the correctly rounded $+$, $-$, $\times$, $\div$ and square root that IEEE-754 mandates, the engine's arbitrary-precision integers, and nothing in the platform's mathematical library, which ECMAScript leaves ``implementation-approximated''---with the one exception of \cref{sec:limits}: six modules, among them the radii-polynomial certifier, still call an older helper that pads the platform's cosine and sine by a margin that is derived, not proved. Where the quantity is rational or a tie must be decided, exact arithmetic is used instead: reduced fractions of arbitrary-precision integers, to which doubles convert losslessly, with exact elimination that reports a singular matrix rather than a small pivot.

\paragraph{Elementary and special functions}
\Cref{thm:ftia} requires an enclosure for every elementary function, and no library function is trusted as one. Each function $f$ is evaluated as a truncated series $S_N$ in interval arithmetic plus an explicit bound $R_N$ on the omitted tail, so that $f(x)\in S_N(x)+[-R_N,R_N]$, the bound itself evaluated in interval arithmetic and its upper end added by an outward-rounded addition, so that nothing is absorbed into the widening of \cref{prop:outward}.

\begin{assumption}[Function enclosures]\label{ass:functions}
For every function $f$ of \cref{tab:functions} and every box $X$ in its declared domain, either the call is refused or the returned interval $F(X)$ satisfies $f(X)\subseteq F(X)$, including near zeros of $f$.
\end{assumption}

\Cref{tab:functions} (\ref{app:arith}) lists each function with its reduction, series, tail bound and domain; the lemma that establishes \cref{ass:functions} for it is stated and proved in \supp{1}. The kernel and its functions have been compared with Arb~\citep{Johansson17} on \MtArbCases{} seeded cases over \MtArbOps{} operations, with no enclosure that failed to contain the exact value (\cref{sec:arb}); that is evidence on sampled inputs, and the lemmas are the proof.

\paragraph{Long sums and derivatives}
A running interval sum rounds outward at each of $n$ additions, so its width grows linearly in $n$; at the \RBuoyN{} hourly values of one buoy it is about a thousand times what the terms carry. Each endpoint stream $p_1,\dots,p_n$ is therefore summed by the compensated algorithm Sum2 of Ogita, Rump and Oishi~\citep[Proposition~4.5]{Ogita05}, whose result $\tilde s$ satisfies, also under underflow,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sum2}
\Bigl|\tilde s-\sum_{i=1}^n p_i\Bigr|\le u\,\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^n p_i\Bigr| +\gamma_{n-1}^2\sum_{i=1}^n|p_i|,\qquad \gamma_k=\frac{ku}{1-ku},\quad u=2^{-53},
\end{equation}
provided $nu<1$ and no overflow occurs; both hypotheses are checked by name and a stream that fails either is refused. The implementation evaluates the bound from computable quantities as $u|\tilde s|+2\gamma_n^2\sum|p_i|$, each step rounded upward, the doubled term covering the difference between $u|\tilde s|$ and $u|\sum p_i|$ (\supp{1}). Gradients and Hessians come from second-order forward differentiation over the same interval arithmetic, or from univariate Taylor jets, so that the derivative a proof uses is an extension of the function the proof is about.

\subsubsection{Claims, readings and verdicts}\label{sec:grammar}

\paragraph{Boxes and claims}
A box with rational endpoints is the object a claim is decided on; a double is a rational, so a box of doubles is such a box. A \emph{declared envelope} $B$ is a box each of whose sides carries the source of its bounds (a survey, a data sheet, a chart). A printed number is the box its digits allow: a decimal $\hat d$ printed with $k$ fractional digits denotes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:printed}
\bigl[\hat d-\tfrac12 10^{-k},\ \hat d+\tfrac12 10^{-k}\bigr],
\end{equation}
so a printed $0.0634$ is $[0.06335,0.06345]$, both endpoints exact rationals. \Cref{eq:printed} assumes that a printed \emph{value} was rounded to nearest; the claim schema fixes that convention and refuses any other box for it. A printed \emph{bound} ($\le$, $<$, $\ge$, $>$) is taken as the exact rational its digits denote, and a value that was truncated or given to significant figures must be declared as an explicit rational box. Declared envelope ends are read as the nearest double, widened outward by one ulp unless the conversion is exact. One claim of \cref{sec:register} shows the whole path: a registry bound printed as $C_{1d}\le\MtAcDPrinted$ is read as the exact rational of its digits; the certificate it rests on evaluates, in exact arithmetic, to $\MtAcDExact\ldots$, which exceeds the printed bound, so the claim as printed is \REFT{} with that value as its falsifier, and the bound $\MtAcDRepaired$, the certificate's own value rounded up, is \CERT{} beside it.

\begin{definition}[Claim]\label{def:claim}
A \emph{claim} is a pair $(B,\varphi)$ of a domain $B$---a box, or a finite set of rational points, covered by its thin boxes---and a predicate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:claim}
\varphi(x)\equiv\bigwedge_{j=1}^m g_j(x)\le c_j
\end{equation}
whose functions have interval extensions $G_j$; an equality, or an exact predicate---the orientation of three points, or a Boolean combination of exact integer tests such as the angular test of a kissing configuration---is the special case decided in exact arithmetic. It is read either \emph{universally}, as $\forall x\in B:\varphi(x)$, or \emph{epistemically}, as $\varphi(x^\dagger)$ for an unknown true input $x^\dagger\in B$.
\end{definition}
The form is deliberately narrow: a conjunction of inequalities over one box, with disjunction allowed only among exact tests and no alternation of quantifiers. A statement outside it---an existential claim, an inequality quantified over a parameter that is itself unknown, a claim about an object that was not published---is not forced into the schema; it is refused with the reason \emph{the instrument declined}, or marked \emph{needs data} with the bytes that would decide it named (\cref{tab:words}). Under the universal reading a single proved counterexample refutes the claim; under the epistemic reading it shows only that the declared evidence does not settle the question.

\begin{definition}[Verdicts]\label{def:verdicts}
Let $\mathcal{X}$ be a finite cover of $B$ by boxes. The claim is \emph{certified} if $\sup G_j(X)\le c_j$ for every $j$ and every $X\in\mathcal{X}$; \emph{refuted}, under the universal reading, if some box $X^*\subseteq B$ has $\inf G_j(X^*)>c_j$ for some $j$, and under the epistemic reading if every $X\in\mathcal{X}$ has $\inf G_j(X)>c_j$ for some $j$; and \emph{refused} otherwise. An epistemic refusal is \emph{split} when two points $x^-,x^+\in B$ have been proved to violate and to satisfy $\varphi$, and \emph{unresolved} otherwise.
\end{definition}
Certification is the same test under both readings; the readings differ in what refutes---one proved violation, or a violation proved on all of $B$---and therefore in what a refusal leaves open.

\begin{proposition}[Soundness]\label{prop:sound}
If every $G_j$ is an interval extension of $g_j$, then \CERT{} implies $\varphi(x)$ for every $x\in B$; \REFT{} implies $\neg\varphi(x)$ for some $x\in B$ under the universal reading and for every $x\in B$ under the epistemic reading; and a split \REFS{} implies that neither $\forall x\in B\,\varphi(x)$ nor $\forall x\in B\,\neg\varphi(x)$ holds, so that $\varphi(x^\dagger)$ is not determined by the declared envelope.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $x\in B$ and $X\in\mathcal{X}$ contain it. By the inclusion property $g_j(x)\in g_j(X)\subseteq G_j(X)$, hence $g_j(x)\le\sup G_j(X)\le c_j$ for every $j$. The refutations follow by the same argument with the inequality reversed, on $X^*$ or on every member of the cover. For a split refusal, $x^+$ and $x^-$ are points of $B$ at which $\varphi$ is proved true and false; either could be $x^\dagger$.
\end{proof}
In the engineering gates the box decided is the double hull $B^+\supseteq B$ obtained by widening each declared end outward; certification and epistemic refutation transfer from $B^+$ to $B$, and the witness of a split refusal is a corner of $B$ itself, the declared decimal enclosed by its two neighbouring doubles and decided as that thin box. A witness that a floating-point search proposes (the rock-physics engine) is a double of $B^+$, within one ulp of $B$, and is reported as such.

Soundness says nothing about how often the procedure decides. The following result, in the spirit of $\delta$-completeness~\citep{GaoAvigadClarke12}, states when bisection is guaranteed to certify and what it costs; the proof is in \supp{2}.

\begin{proposition}[Termination for robust claims]\label{prop:termination}
Let each $G_j$ have convergence order $\omega$ with constants $\kappa$ and $\eta$ on $B$ (\cref{eq:order}), and let the claim hold with margin $\varepsilon>\eta$: $g_j(x)\le c_j-\varepsilon$ for every $j$ and every $x\in B$. Then every box $X\subseteq B$ with $w(X)\le((\varepsilon-\eta)/\kappa)^{1/\omega}$ is certified, and bisection of the widest coordinate certifies the claim at depth at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:depth}
d^*=n\Bigl\lceil\log_2\Bigl(w(B)\,\bigl(\kappa/(\varepsilon-\eta)\bigr)^{1/\omega}\Bigr)\Bigr\rceil^{+},
\end{equation}
with at most $2^{d^*}$ boxes in the final cover, where $\lceil t\rceil^{+}=\max(0,\lceil t\rceil)$.
\end{proposition}

\Cref{prop:termination} explains the refusals the evaluation must expect. A claim whose margin does not exceed the floor ($\varepsilon\le\eta$) is not guaranteed to be certified by bisection alone---in the arithmetic of \cref{prop:outward}, a bound attained at a point by an expression that ends in an operation never is (\cref{prop:complete})---and needs one of the certifiers of \cref{sec:certifiers} or a higher precision that lowers $\eta$; and the box count grows as $(w(B)(\kappa/(\varepsilon-\eta))^{1/\omega})^{n}$, so a depth budget turns high-dimensional or nearly tight claims into refusals by budget rather than into wrong verdicts. Read in the contrapositive, a refusal by budget at depth $d_{\max}$ states that the claim is not robust by more than $\delta=\eta+\kappa\,(2^{-d_{\max}/n}w(B))^{\omega}$, the counterpart of a $\delta$-satisfiable answer. The bound is proved for widest-coordinate bisection; the engineering gates split by sensitivity instead (\cref{tab:decisions}), for which no depth bound is claimed.

\Cref{prop:termination} bounds the cost of the decisions that bisection reaches. Which decisions those are is the quasi-decidability of inequality constraints over the reals~\citep{Ratschan06}: a claim that holds or fails with a margin is decided by refinement, and a claim that is tight need not be. The next proposition states that result in the terms of \cref{def:verdicts}. It is known, and is cited, not claimed; it uses only the inclusion property and a width bound of the kind the natural extension satisfies~\citep{Moore09,Neumaier90}.

\begin{proposition}[Completeness up to tightness]\label{prop:complete}
Let a claim $g(x)\le c$, with $g$ continuous, be read universally over a box $B\subseteq\R^n$, and let $G$ be an interval extension of $g$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:width}
w\bigl(G(X)\bigr)\le L\,w(X)+\eta\qquad\text{for every box }X\subseteq B,
\end{equation}
where $L>0$ and $\eta\ge0$. Starting from $\{B\}$, the procedure bisects, at each depth and on its widest coordinate, every box that is neither certified ($\sup G(X)\le c$) nor refuted ($\inf G(X)>c$), and stops when every box is certified or one is refuted.
\begin{enumerate}\renewcommand{\theenumi}{(\roman{enumi})}\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\theenumi}
\item\label{it:robust} If the margin $\varepsilon=c-\max_B g$ exceeds $\eta$, every box $X\subseteq B$ with $w(X)\le(\varepsilon-\eta)/L$ is certified, and the claim is certified at depth at most $n\lceil\log_2(L\,w(B)/(\varepsilon-\eta))\rceil^{+}$.
\item\label{it:false} If $\varepsilon_0=g(x_0)-c$ exceeds $\eta$ at some $x_0\in B$, the claim is refuted at depth at most $n\lceil\log_2(2L\,w(B)/(\varepsilon_0-\eta))\rceil^{+}$.
\item\label{it:tight} If $\max_B g=c$, attained at $x^*$, no box is refuted, and a box $X\ni x^*$ is certified only if $\sup G(X)=c$. If $\sup G(X)>c$ for every box $X\subseteq B$ that contains $x^*$, none of them is certified at any depth, and at every finite depth budget the verdict is \REFS.
\item\label{it:arith} Let $G$ be the natural extension of an expression in $+,-,\times,\div$ whose divisors are enclosed away from zero over $B$. In exact rational arithmetic it satisfies \cref{eq:width} with $\eta=0$. In the outward-rounded doubles of \cref{prop:outward}, with no overflow, it satisfies \cref{eq:width} with $\eta\le C\,u$, $u=2^{-53}$, for a constant $C$ that depends on the expression and on the magnitudes of the enclosures of its subexpressions over $B$; and if the expression ends in one of the four operations, $\sup G(X)>\max_X g$ for every box $X\subseteq B$, so that the hypothesis of \ref{it:tight} holds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $g(X)\subseteq G(X)$, $\inf G(X)\le\min_X g$ and $\sup G(X)\ge\max_X g$. (i)~For $X\subseteq B$, $\sup G(X)=\inf G(X)+w(G(X))\le\max_B g+L\,w(X)+\eta=c-\varepsilon+L\,w(X)+\eta$, which is at most $c$ when $L\,w(X)\le\varepsilon-\eta$. Along any branch, $n$ consecutive bisections at least halve the widest side: while it exceeds half its value at the start, each bisection halves a coordinate not yet halved since the start, and there are $n$ of them. So every box at depth $nk$ has width at most $2^{-k}w(B)$, at most $(\varepsilon-\eta)/L$ for the $k$ of the bound; and no box is refuted, by \cref{prop:sound}, since the claim holds. (ii)~For $X\ni x_0$, $\inf G(X)=\sup G(X)-w(G(X))\ge g(x_0)-L\,w(X)-\eta$, which exceeds $c$ when $L\,w(X)<\varepsilon_0-\eta$. A box containing $x_0$ is never certified, since $\sup G(X)\ge g(x_0)>c$, so a box containing $x_0$ is bisected at every depth until one is refuted, and at depth $nk$, for the $k$ of the bound, its width is at most $(\varepsilon_0-\eta)/(2L)$. (iii)~A refuted box would contain a point with $g>c$, which $B$ does not. A box $X\ni x^*$ has $\sup G(X)\ge g(x^*)=c$, so it is certified only if equality holds; under the second hypothesis no box containing $x^*$ is certified, every cover of $B$ has such a box, and the procedure neither certifies nor refutes. (iv)~The endpoint formulas \cref{eq:endpoints} give $w(X\pm Y)=w(X)+w(Y)$, $w(X\times Y)\le|Y|\,w(X)+|X|\,w(Y)$ and, if $|y|\ge\beta>0$ on $Y$, $w(X\div Y)\le w(X)/\beta+|X|\,w(Y)/\beta^2$, with $|X|=\max_{x\in X}|x|$; by inclusion isotonicity (\cref{thm:ftia}) the magnitudes and $\beta$ over a box $X\subseteq B$ are bounded by those over $B$, and induction on the expression gives \cref{eq:width} with $\eta=0$. Under \cref{prop:outward} each operation, and each conversion of a constant, moves each end $y$ of its exact result outward by at most three spacings of the doubles at $y$, that is by at most $3(2u|y|+2^{-1074})$, where $2^{-1074}=2^{-1021}u$; the rounded operations are inclusion isotone, rounding being monotone, and the same induction gives $\eta\le Cu$. For the last claim, let $y_+$ be the exact upper end of the last operation applied to the computed enclosures of its operands, so that $y_+\ge\max_X g$, and let $\tilde y$ be its round-to-nearest value. If $\tilde y\ge y_+$, then $\mathrm{up}(\tilde y)>y_+$. If $\tilde y<y_+$, no double lies strictly between them, since $\tilde y$ is the double nearest $y_+$, so $\mathrm{up}(\tilde y)\ge y_+$; and equality would make $y_+$ a double, whose round-to-nearest value is $y_+$ itself and not $\tilde y$. In both cases $\sup G(X)=\mathrm{up}(\tilde y)>\max_X g$.
\end{proof}

Part~\ref{it:robust} is \cref{prop:termination} at $\omega=1$, since $g(X)\subseteq G(X)$ gives $q(g(X),G(X))\le w(G(X))$, and part~\ref{it:false} is its counterpart for refutation; with~\ref{it:tight} they are Ratschan's quasi-decidability~\citep{Ratschan06}: a robust claim is decided, a tight one possibly never. With exact rationals the floor is zero and every claim with a margin is decided; with doubles a margin below $Cu$ need not be, and a function of \cref{tab:functions} adds its tail bound to $\eta$. Part~\ref{it:tight} is why a refusal on a claim with no margin is the expected outcome and not a defect, and why a tight claim is decided when its domain is a finite set of rational points and the arithmetic is exact, every box then being thin and the extension of a rational expression its value: the $\MtKwContactsSci$ kissing pairs at exactly sixty degrees lie on the bar, and each is decided by an exact integer test (\cref{sec:register} states when its floating-point paths are exact). The \MtEvStraddles{} cosine-minimum refusals of \cref{sec:eval} are the case the records leave open: a finer cover separates each one whose minimum lies off the bar by more than the floor, by~\ref{it:robust} and~\ref{it:false}, and none whose minimum lies on it, by~\ref{it:tight}; the records do not say which holds, and the refusals stand.

\paragraph{Composition}
Order the verdicts as $\REFT\prec\REFS\prec\CERT$. A record with several checks $\varphi=\bigwedge_i\varphi_i$ receives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compose}
v(\varphi)=\min_{\prec}\{v(\varphi_i)\},
\end{equation}
that is, \REFT{} if any check is refuted, otherwise \REFS{} if any is refused, otherwise \CERT---the strong conjunction of Kleene's three-valued logic~\citep{Kleene38}.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:compose}
Under \cref{prop:sound}, if all checks $\varphi_i$ are decided on the same box $B$ and under the same reading, the composition rule \cref{eq:compose} is sound for $\varphi$ under that reading (proof in \supp{2}).
\end{corollary}
A split refusal does not survive conjunction: if $\varphi_1$ holds only on one half of $B$ and $\varphi_2$ only on the other, each is split while $\varphi_1\wedge\varphi_2$ is false on all of $B$. The implementation composes from the checks' words alone, so a composed refusal is never labelled split; split refusals are issued by the two engineering gates at the level of one check, a single inequality. Items of one record decided on different objects are not composed but reported as \verdict{mixed} with the counts of each verdict (\cref{tab:words}). A refusal is recorded permanently and is reopened only by a new exact argument---a finer cover, an extension of higher order, another certifier, or the bytes the claimant had not yet published; no probabilistic grading is attached to any verdict.

\paragraph{Witnesses}
Every verdict carries the object that justifies it, in the sense of a certifying algorithm~\citep{McConnell11}. A certification carries its \emph{certificate}: the inputs pinned by digest, the hull of its enclosures with the counts of its cover, and a statement of what would make it fail (the \emph{falsifier}); the constructor refuses a certificate without a stated falsifier and a refusal without a reason. A refutation carries the falsifying object, printed; a split refusal its two witnesses, decided as thin boxes by the same enclosures. An exact certificate re-checks without the system's code; an interval certificate records the hull and the counts of its cover, not the cover, and is re-checked by re-deciding the claim from the pinned inputs. The public register composes the three verdicts into words that say what they are made of (\cref{tab:words}); none is a fourth verdict, and the kinds of refusal (instrument declined, claimant published nothing, budget exhausted, box did not close, audit reach stated) are never summed into one total.

\subsubsection{Flip thresholds}\label{sec:thresholds}
A split refusal leaves open a question that has a size. Let the claim depend on a declared scalar $p$---an operating choice, or one end of an interval that a measurement could narrow---through boxes $B(p)$ and predicates $\varphi_p$, and write $\Tp(p)$ for ``$\varphi_p(x)$ holds for every $x\in B(p)$'' and $\Fp(p)$ for ``$\varphi_p(x)$ fails for every $x\in B(p)$''.

\begin{assumption}[Monotone truth]\label{ass:mono}
$\Tp(p)$ implies $\Tp(p')$ for every $p'\le p$, and $\Fp(p)$ implies $\Fp(p')$ for every $p'\ge p$. A sufficient condition is that the boxes do not depend on $p$ and every $g_j(x;p)$ is nondecreasing in $p$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:thresholds}
Let \cref{ass:mono} and the hypothesis of \cref{prop:sound} hold, let $B(p)$ be nonempty, and define $p_T=\sup\{p:\Tp(p)\}$ and $p_F=\inf\{p:\Fp(p)\}$, so that $p_T\le p_F$. If the procedure, at a value $m$ of the parameter, certifies the claim, then $\Tp(p)$ holds for every $p\le m$ and $p_T\ge m$; if it refutes it epistemically, then $\Fp(p)$ holds for every $p\ge m$ and $p_F\le m$; if it returns a split refusal, then $p_T\le m\le p_F$.
\end{proposition}
The proof, the closed form where $p$ enters additively, the second case---a measurement that narrows one side of the box, where nested boxes replace the monotone-truth assumption---and the way each running instance verifies the hypotheses are in \supp{2}. The threshold turns a refusal into a measurement or an operating change with a known effect on the verdict: at this pump pressure the rule is proved, and with the roughness shown to be at least this value it is proved too~\citep{P2}.

\subsubsection{Metrics}\label{sec:metricsdef}

\paragraph{Verdict--truth confusion matrix}
Each benchmark claim $i=1,\dots,N$ carries a ground-truth label $\tau_i\in\{\text{true},\text{false},\text{malformed}\}$---it holds on all of $B$, it fails at a proved witness, or it is not a well-formed instance of its claim schema---established by an oracle that shares no code with cert-machine and is itself exact (\cref{sec:evaluation}). It receives a verdict $v_i\in\{\CERT,\REFT,\REFS\}$, a refusal being split or unresolved. The confusion matrix $N_{v\tau}=\#\{i: v_i=v,\ \tau_i=\tau\}$ is the primary result. \Cref{prop:sound} predicts structural zeros in the cells that would be errors:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeros}
N_{\CERT,\text{false}}=0,\quad N_{\REFT,\text{true}}=0,\quad N_{\CERT,\text{malf.}}=N_{\REFT,\text{malf.}}=0,
\end{equation}
the last pair because a malformed object must be refused by its shape check and never decided. Any nonzero entry in \cref{eq:zeros} is a defect in code or in a hypothesis, and is reported individually. Under the epistemic reading a fourth label, \emph{undetermined} (the predicate holds at one proved point of $B$ and fails at another), has the split refusal as its correct answer; the benchmark of \cref{sec:results} contains universal and exact claims only, so that label does not arise there.

\paragraph{Upper bound on the error rate}
Observing zero errors on $N_\text{F}$ false claims does not show that the error probability is zero. The one-sided Clopper--Pearson bound~\citep{ClopperPearson34} at level $1-\beta$ after $x$ errors is the beta quantile $\theta_U=\mathrm{Beta}^{-1}(1-\beta;\ x+1,\ N_\text{F}-x)$, which at $x=0$ is $1-\beta^{1/N_\text{F}}\approx 3/N_\text{F}$ for $\beta=0.05$~\citep{Hanley83}; the same bound is reported for false refutations on true claims. The bound presumes independent draws from a population of claims, whereas the benchmark is assembled from chosen records and injected faults, and a defect in one code path affects every claim of its class; it is read as a description of this benchmark, not as an error rate that transfers. \Cref{prop:sound} is what the system claims mathematically; the bound measures the gap between the code and the theorem on the claims tested.

\paragraph{Coverage and selective risk}
A sound three-valued decider is a selective classifier with a reject option~\citep{Chow70,ElYaniv10,Geifman17}, in which a split refusal is an informative answer and only an unresolved refusal is an abstention. Writing $D$ for the number of informative answers, its \emph{coverage} is $C=D/N$ and its \emph{selective risk} $R$ is the number of errors of \cref{eq:zeros} divided by $D$. Because the mix of labels is set by the benchmark's design, $C$ is also reported per label---the certification rate on true claims, the refutation rate on false claims and the refusal rate on malformed ones---with the unresolved refusals broken down by reason and never summed across reasons. Soundness predicts $R=0$; the measured $R$ tests the implementation against that prediction, and the performance of a sound implementation is then carried by $C$. A floating-point checker is placed on the same risk--coverage plane~\citep{ElYaniv10,Franc21}: it has $C=1$ and $R\ge0$; cert-machine is predicted to have $C\le1$ and $R=0$.

\paragraph{Cost and agreement}
For a split refusal the thresholds of \cref{sec:thresholds} say how far an operating change or a measurement must go. The harness records wall-clock time, CPU time and peak memory for each run, under two profiles (a portable one with a wall-clock cap, an extended one without); \cref{sec:selfaudit} reports the campaign's totals. Verdicts are deterministic, so agreement with a second implementation is exact agreement, field by field or by digest, and a single disagreement is a defect; against an established library the primary result is the table of containment per operation.

\subsubsection{Evaluation design}\label{sec:evaluation}
The evaluation answers Q3 with a benchmark of known truth and Q1 and Q2 with observational records.

\paragraph{Benchmark with known ground truth}
The benchmark is the system's own public register, mutated. Each register row that carries a published finite object---a tensor decomposition, a point configuration, a set of circles or points, a printed constant, a polynomial map, an entropy law, a truth table---is an \emph{original}; from it the benchmark builds \emph{mutants} of three kinds, each with a label known by construction: \emph{false} (a coefficient raised by one, a digit moved, a point displaced, a bar raised one unit past the certificate's own printed end), \emph{true} (an invariance of the claim: a translation, a rotation, a relabelling of coordinates), and \emph{malformed} (a missing exponent, a weight off a probability law, a digit beyond the alphabet). Every label is established by an \emph{oracle} written for the benchmark in the Python standard library---exact fractions, directed decimal rounding, integer convolutions---that shares no code with the deciders it judges; a false mutant counts only when the oracle exhibits the violating element (a \emph{witnessed} false claim), and a mutant the oracle cannot witness is discarded and counted as such. The design, the classes, the kill switch (the campaign stops if fewer than half the rows yield a witnessed false mutant) and the outcome words were pre-registered before the first mutant ran; the note is pinned by digest in every record, and its \MtSaAmendments{} dated amendments moved no definition. This is the planted-solution design of benchmarks for other verifiers~\citep{Troxell26} applied to the system's own record. Every claim is also decided by a baseline (B0): a floating-point checker that evaluates the predicate in double precision at a tolerance $\tau\in\{10^{-6},10^{-9},10^{-12}\}$, of the kind shipped with the releases audited here~\citep{Yang26,OptConst}.

\paragraph{The arithmetic against an independent library}
The interval kernel's operations and functions are compared, case by case on seeded inputs, with the exact value or range computed in Arb~\citep{Johansson17}: the rule is containment, and two planted wrong kernels must be caught. This answers Q2 on sampled inputs; the lemmas of \supp{1} answer it on paper.

\paragraph{Coverage across domains, and the register}
Q1 is answered by reporting, for each domain, how many claims could be stated in the form of \cref{def:claim} and with which verdicts they were decided, and by listing what could not be so stated. The register of machine-generated claims~\citep{Register} is reported as an observational study: how many claims of each source certify, refute or refuse, and what kinds of defect the refutations expose. It measures what independence finds in practice, not a false-certification rate, since the ground truth of its claims is not known independently.

\subsubsection{System overview}\label{sec:overview}

The mathematics of \cref{sec:math} is implemented in four layers (\cref{fig:system}): an arithmetic layer (exact rationals, outward-rounded intervals, function enclosures, compensated sums and derivatives); a certifier layer (the certifiers of \cref{tab:certifiers} and the decision loop of \supp{4}); a record layer (certificates, witnesses, digests and the register's builder); and a verification layer (the batteries, red controls and second implementations of \cref{sec:protocol}). A \emph{proposer}---a search, a model, a language model or a printed table---lies outside the trust path: its output is read as a claim over a box and decided by a \emph{certifier}. A floating-point \emph{screen} may discard candidates before certification but can never admit one. The verdict, its witness and the inputs pinned by SHA-256 form a \emph{record}.

\subsubsection{Verification protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
\Cref{prop:sound} makes the verdicts as sound as the code that computes the extensions. The protocol below is how that code is guarded. Each rule is an executable gate---a test battery that exits non-zero, a page builder that will not write its page, a numbers generator that will not write a paper's macros---and \cref{tab:protocol} states which gates are enforced at every level and which are not yet.
\begin{description}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item[R1. Screens prune, never admit.] A floating-point screen may discard candidates; only the certifier can record a hit, and the campaign runner plants known answers that must pass every stage before a run may start.
\item[R2. Adversarial controls must fire.] A \emph{red control} is a deliberately wrong variant of a test that must be rejected (a ``gate'' evaluating the model only at the centre of a box, which the corner rule must catch); a battery whose red controls do not all fire fails. Red controls are mutation testing~\citep{DeMillo78,JiaHarman11,Petrovic18}, by which soundness defects have been found in SMT solvers~\citep{Winterer20,Mansur20}. Of the \MtBatteries{} batteries the test target runs (\MtBatteriesNode{} in JavaScript, \MtBatteriesPy{} in Python), \MtEvRedBatteries{} state their red controls, \MtEvRedTotal{} in all.
\item[R3. Pin the bytes.] Every claim is decided against a byte sequence named by path and SHA-256 and re-hashed at decision time; certifying modules are pinned in a ledger; a page refuses to build when a pinned input changed, when the record was written by another version of its verifier, or when its battery fails.
\item[R4. Independence from the claimant.] The claimant's program is never in the trust path; the decision is re-derived from the published statement and bytes, by a system that shares no code with the claimant, and every exact certificate re-checks with no engine present. The exceptions known at the time of writing are listed in \cref{tab:protocol}.
\item[R5. Second implementations.] Where a verdict is the product, a program written from the specification in another language and arithmetic, sharing no code, re-derives it (the list is in \supp{5}). Sharing no code does not make failures independent~\citep{KnightLeveson86,Nogueira26,Ron26}; agreement is evidence against defects the two do not share, not proof of their absence (\cref{sec:threats}).
\item[R6. Records re-run.] Published records re-run in the reader's browser by the bytes that wrote them and are compared with the record by digest or field by field; a rerun kit of \MtKitCommands{} commands re-derives the register's records (\cref{sec:selfaudit}).
\item[R7. Counting rules.] Every number in a paper is a macro written from a record by a generator that refuses when the record no longer supports the sentence quoting it; queued rows are not decided rows; refusal kinds are never merged; a printed enclosure is rounded outward, never cut.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Two defects found by the benchmark's rule}
The campaign's first run, at commit \MtSaFixCommit{} on \MtSaFixDate, showed \MtSaFixOneMutants{} \MtSaFixOneOutcome{}s and \MtSaFixTwoMutants{} malformed objects decided, and both were defects of the system. The first: the ledger of point-set configurations composed its word from whether the published bytes witnessed a value, while the agreement of the printed digits with that value was a field beside the word, gated only by the page build; a printed value moved one unit in its last digit past the bytes therefore stayed \CERT. The fix composes the word in one module---a witnessed object whose printed value the exact value does not reproduce is \textsc{misprinted}, the register's \REFT{} with the kind \emph{not from the published data}---and the ledger was re-derived under it with no row changing word. The second: the tensor and kissing deciders answered a malformed object (a tensor with the wrong number of slices, a configuration that is not $n$ vectors in $\R^d$) with the word \REFT, the identity having failed; the fix makes every shape check answer \REFS{} with its reason. Both fixes are pinned in the campaign's record with the commit each was found at, the campaign was re-run whole after them, and \cref{tab:selfaudit} is that re-run. The pre-registration's rule---a defect found by the campaign is fixed on the record and the campaign re-run, never the definition moved---was followed; each of the \MtSaAmendments{} amendments is dated and lists what it does not change.

\paragraph{The conventional check, on the same claims}
The baseline B0 evaluates each predicate in double precision at a tolerance. At $\tau=10^{-6}$ it accepts \MtSaFloatFalseSix{} of the witnessed false claims (\MtSaFloatFalseNSix{} of \MtSaFalseWitnessed), at $10^{-9}$ \MtSaFloatFalseNine{} (\MtSaFloatFalseNNine) and at $10^{-12}$ \MtSaFloatFalseTwelve{} (\MtSaFloatFalseNTwelve). The tolerance cuts both ways: at the same three settings it confirms only \MtSaFloatOrigSix, \MtSaFloatOrigNine{} and \MtSaFloatOrigTwelve{} of the \MtSaMutObjects{} true originals, and \MtSaFloatTrueSix, \MtSaFloatTrueNine{} and \MtSaFloatTrueTwelve{} of the \MtSaTrue{} true mutants, because four of the nine polynomial maps are decided by no tolerance at all: their determinants are exact integers that double precision does not hold. On the risk--coverage plane of \cref{sec:metricsdef} the checker decides every claim it can evaluate in double precision (the four polynomial maps it cannot are its abstentions) and accepts between $\MtSaFloatFalseTwelve$ and $\MtSaFloatFalseSix$ of the witnessed false claims at the three tolerances; the decider has $R=0$ on those claims and $C=\MtSaCoveragePct$.

\paragraph{Concordance, re-runs and cost}
Where a register row is decided by two or more programs---the deciders under test, the benchmark's oracles, the rerun kit's standard-library verifiers, the second implementations of rule R5---the concordance record compares their answers field by field: \MtSaConcord{} comparisons on \MtSaConcordRows{} rows---\MtSaConcordField{} on the enclosure or the violating count beside the word, \MtSaConcordWord{} on a re-derivation's exit and \MtSaConcordObject{} on the exact object alone---with no disagreement. The rerun kit, run whole on \MtRunDate, moved none of the \MtRunRows{} rows; the campaign re-derived the register's records in \MtSaRuns{} runs under two profiles and found every record identical to its pin outside declared time stamps (\MtSaRunRecordsExt{} records and \MtSaRunRowsExt{} rows without a wall-clock cap; \MtSaRunRecordsPort{} records and \MtSaRunRowsPort{} rows under a \MtSaRunCap-second cap, the other \MtSaRunRefused{} refused for resources). The \MtSaHostedChallenges{} kernel replays of the mathematics release ran on hosted runners and are carried as recorded, never merged into the laptop's table: \MtSaHostedCertified{} \CERT{} under two kernels, \MtSaHostedRefused{} \REFS{} for resources, wall-clock from \MtSaHostedSecondsMin{} to \MtSaHostedSecondsMax{} seconds (median \MtSaHostedSecondsMedian) and peak memory up to \MtSaHostedRssGiB~GiB. The mutation campaign took \MtSaMinutes{} minutes on the laptop (\MtSaRunMachine); the re-derivation of the records took \MtSaRunWallMinutesExt{} minutes of wall-clock under the extended profile and \MtSaRunWallMinutesPort{} under the portable one, with a peak resident memory of \MtSaRunPeakGiB~GiB. Per record (\supp{5}, Table S2), the median re-derivation takes \MtRtMedianWall{} s, \MtRtUnderMinute{} of the \MtRtRecords{} records finish every command within a minute, and the longest, the \MtRtMaxLabel, takes \MtRtMaxWall{} s. Nothing was rented.

\paragraph{What the benchmark does not cover}
Every claim in it is universal or exact, so the epistemic reading, the split refusal and the undetermined label are not measured; the engineering gate's planted faults (\GFaults{} faulty proposals, \GFaultsRefuted{} refuted and \GFaultsRefused{} refused~\citep{P2}) are the only ground truth of that kind and are targeted, not sampled. The mutation classes were chosen by the first author, so the benchmark bounds the defects it can find by the classes it plants. B0 is the only baseline run on the whole benchmark; a $\delta$-complete decider was run on the two claim classes it can express (\cref{sec:dreal}).

\subsection{The kernel against an independent library}\label{sec:arb}

The interval kernel was run on \MtArbCases{} seeded cases across \MtArbOps{} operations---up to \MtArbPerOp{} each, drawn from full-precision doubles, subnormals, arguments near overflow and underflow, and boxes that straddle a function's critical points---and every enclosure it returned was compared with the exact result, or for a box the exact range, computed in Arb~\citep{Johansson17} at \MtArbPrec{} bits and more (\cref{tab:arb}). Every one of the \MtArbContained{} enclosures it returned contained the exact value, and it refused the other \MtArbInDomainRefused; none of the \MtArbMalformed{} malformed inputs (a NaN, an inverted interval, division through zero, a non-integer or overflowing power, a logarithm at or below zero) was decided; and the comparison caught two planted wrong kernels, a round-to-nearest addition in \MtArbRedAdd{} of \MtArbRedAddOf{} cases and the platform's exponential at the ends in \MtArbRedExp{} of \MtArbRedExpOf. The audit covers the special functions the likelihood certifier uses---the gamma function by Spouge's formula, the Bessel function, the inverses of the normal and incomplete-gamma distributions, each judged against a bracket bisected in Arb on certain signs---and the compensated sum against the exact rational sum of its terms. The widths tell where the enclosures are loose: the gamma function's tenfold Spouge margin and the Bessel series near the zeros of $J_\nu$ are contained by many ulps, which costs tightness, never soundness.

\paragraph{The standard's test suite}
The same kernel was run on ITF1788 (\url{https://github.com/oheim/ITF1788}, commit \MtItfCommit), the IEEE~1788-2015 test framework discussed by Benet, Ferranti and Revol~\citep{Benet24}: \MtItfFiles{} test libraries holding \MtItfTests{} hand-written cases from libieeep1788, FI\_LIB, MPFI, C-XSC and the standard's examples, each giving the tightest interval an operation must return, read here by a parser that executes nothing of the suite. The \MtItfRun{} cases of operations the kernel implements with the standard's meaning and without decorations or the empty set were run: no result failed to contain its expected interval; \MtItfTight{} were the tightest interval, \MtItfContained{} valid and wider (the one-ulp outward step), and \MtItfRefused{} inputs were refused by design (a divisor containing zero, $0\cdot\infty$, an infinite endpoint, the exponential's reduction range). The suite found one defect, in a predicate rather than an enclosure: the membership test answers true for $\pm\infty$ against an interval with that infinite end (\MtItfMemberViolationsWord{} cases; \cref{sec:limits}). The two planted faults were caught (round-to-nearest addition on \MtItfRedAdd{} of \MtItfRedAddOf{} addition cases, the platform's exponential at the ends on \MtItfRedExp{} of \MtItfRedExpOf). The other \MtItfNotApplicable{} cases---decorations, the empty set, reverse and set operations, and operations the kernel lacks---were counted and not run; the measurement is of soundness on the kernel's own operations, not a conformance claim (\texttt{certs/kernel-itf1788.json}).

\paragraph{Refusals by cause}\label{sec:eval}
The refusals are enumerated by cause. Of the \MtEvChallenges{} Comparator challenges of the mathematics release, \MtEvResources{} are refused for a stack, memory or time limit that more resources might clear. Of the \MtEvCosineDecisions{} cosine-minimum decisions of the generation loop, \MtEvStraddles{} are refused because the enclosure straddles the bar, which a finer cover or a higher-order form might separate. The kernel's \MtArbInDomainRefused{} refusals near underflow and overflow are a design choice: the enclosure $[0, 2^{-1074}]$ would be valid at underflow, and the kernel does not return it. These remain refused, with their cause recorded, pending a stronger method.

\subsection{Comparison with a \texorpdfstring{$\delta$}{delta}-complete decider}\label{sec:dreal}

The claims that dReal~\citep{GaoAvigadClarke12,dReal13} can express were put to it on a hosted runner (version \MtDrVersion, the wheel pinned by digest, default configuration; run \MtDrRun): the flow-line gate's \MtDrGateClaims{} claims and \MtDrReceiptsWord{} receipts, and the benchmark's entropy family (\MtDrEntropy{} claims). A claim $\varphi$ over its box becomes two satisfiability queries: \CERT{} corresponds to an unsatisfiable $\exists x\in B\colon\neg\varphi(x)$, an epistemic \REFT{} to an unsatisfiable $\exists x\in B\colon\varphi(x)$, and a $\delta$-satisfiable answer on both sides to a refusal; Colebrook's friction factor enters as an extra variable on an interval both systems prove contains the root for every declared input, and every constant as an exact enclosure. At $\delta=10^{-3}$ and $10^{-9}$ alike, the two systems agree on every gate claim and every receipt: the certified rule, the refutations and the printed enclosures are unsatisfiable, and the split refusal is $\delta$-satisfiable on both sides, the gate proving the midpoints of dReal's two witness boxes on their sides. On the entropy family dReal agrees on \MtDrEntropyAgreeWord{} claims and returns $\delta$-satisfiable on both sides for the other \MtDrEntropyWeakerWord, whose distance to the bar lies below the spacing of the doubles: a closed-form inequality is one double-precision interval evaluation for dReal, and exact for cert-machine. dReal has no shape check: of the \MtDrEntropyDoorWord{} malformed laws, which cert-machine refuses before deciding, it certified one as written. No contradiction was found in either direction (\texttt{certs/dreal-compare.json}).

\subsection{The register, and its three largest exercises}\label{sec:register}

The public register holds \RegRows{} claims from \RegClaimants{} claimants, decided from \RegSources{} records between \RegFirstDate{} and \RegLastDate{} and frozen on \RegDate; decisions made after the freeze are not register rows. Of its rows \RegDecided{} are decided and \VQueued{} is queued, not counted: \VCertified{} \CERT, \VPartial{} \verdict{partial}, \VRefuted{} \REFT, \VMixed{} \verdict{mixed}, \VRepaired{} \verdict{repaired} and \VNeedsData{} \verdict{needs data}. \RegDefects{} rows carry a kind of defect other than none; the most frequent is \emph{\KTopDefect} (\KTopDefectRows{} rows), a formal statement or a check that covers less than the claim beside it. Of the \AiRows{} decided rows whose claimant is a machine or a machine-assisted pipeline, \AiRowsDefects{} carry a defect; of the other \OtherRows, \OtherRowsDefects. The register measures what independence finds, not an error rate: no row's truth is known independently of the decision. Three exercises, begun inside the register and continued after its freeze, are told below from their own records, each with a public report in the archive~\citep{CMArchive}.

\paragraph{A configuration of points}
An autonomous discovery system published new lower bounds for the kissing number in \MtKwRows{} dimensions (\MtKwDims), each a configuration of integer or algebraic vectors, their sizes proved by moment identities and the angular condition by structure, with the release's own files recording that the pairwise inner products were not checked~\citep{Yang26}. Two configurations were published as exact objects and decided at once; the other seventeen were \verdict{needs data} with the missing files named, and the claimant published them within days~\citep{QiushiRelease}. Every pair was then decided exactly: $\MtKwPairsSci$ pairs, $\MtKwContactsSci$ at exactly sixty degrees and none closer, in three arithmetics---single-precision BLAS on the laptop, exact algebraic integers for the two closed-form constructions, and half-precision tensor cores on one rented GPU for the seven largest (\MtKwGpuHours{} GPU-hours, \$\MtKwGpuCost{} billed)---the floating-point paths exact under a Cauchy--Schwarz bound asserted on the data before any path runs and, for the GPU, under the stated assumption on tensor-core accumulation (\cref{sec:threats}). All \MtKwRows{} bounds hold; the claimant linked the check from its release. The record (\texttt{certs/kissing-ledger.json}) and its report give the materials, the calibration, the second implementation and the limit of the GPU argument.

\paragraph{A curated record}
An optimization-constants registry marks with an asterisk a bound whose verification is minimal and states what removes the mark~\citep{OptConst,OptConstRule}. Three asterisked bounds rest on finite certificates---two entropy laws for the sums--differences constants and a truth table for the Fourier entropy--influence constant---and were decided twice from the registry's bytes by programs that share no code with each other or with the claimants, both written by AI agents under the first author's direction; all three are \CERT{} with their ratios enclosed. The registry merged the replay and removed the marks, and its pages cite the replay; over three rounds, the last on \MtRgThirdDate, \MtRgMarksTotalWord{} marks have been removed on replays made here, and on \MtRgCorrectedDate{} the registry corrected one of its rows on the check. The registry's open queue offered \MtRqRows{} further bounds: \MtRqComputedCertified{} are \CERT{} by a computation made here, one on an earlier two-kernel record, one was checked by reading alone and is counted as a reading, and \MtRqRefused{} is \REFS---a package that certifies its own assembly and not the analysis it assembles, with the three inputs that would decide it named. One bound was printed on the wrong side of its own certificate, $C_{1d}\le\MtAcDPrinted$ against the certificate's exact $\MtAcDExact\ldots$; the record holds the printed bound \REFT{} and the repaired one \CERT. The sums--differences certificate came with a checker of its own, which encloses the ratio at a hundred digits and compares in double precision: given \MtEvCheckerRuns{} claims here, it printed OK on all of them, \MtEvCheckerFalse{} of which are false. \subsection{What the results show}

\paragraph{Measured soundness}
\Cref{prop:sound} predicts zero false verdicts, and on \MtSaMutants{} claims of known truth the implementation produced no false certification and no false refutation, after two defects that the benchmark's own rule exposed were fixed on the record. Neither defect was in the arithmetic: one was a ledger that said \CERT{} about the bytes while the printed digits beside them disagreed, the other a verdict word given to an object that was not a claim at all. Both are gaps between what was checked and what the word said, and both were found by planting claims against the system's own register, which is what the protocol asks of claimants. Writing the lemmas of \supp{1} against the code found eight more places where the implementation was weaker than the stated lemma, and an outside rerun and the sweep it started found four programs that printed an upper end cut toward zero rather than rounded outward (\cref{sec:limits}); each lay below the enclosures' widths, none moved a verdict, and each was unproved until written down.

\paragraph{Defects found in the register}
No bound of the three exercises was found false beyond its last printed digit and no theorem was false. One registry bound is printed on the wrong side of its own certificate; a pairwise condition was proved by structure and never checked pair by pair; a certificate's checker compares in double precision what it encloses at a hundred digits; a package certifies its own assembly and not the analysis it assembles; a clause of a theorem is absent from the formal statement. Each was closed, or named, by a finite computation or a recorded reading. A refusal carries its threshold or its missing input: seventeen kissing rows were \verdict{needs data} until the points were released, each naming the files that would decide it; the gate's split refusal carries the pump pressure at which it would become a proof; the package is \REFS{} with its three missing inputs named. The cost of exactness is small: the objects are integers, rationals and bounds, and the cost of the one expensive decision---$\MtKwPairsSci$ pairs---scales with the number of pairs, not with the exactness of the arithmetic.

\paragraph{Outcomes following the decisions}
Each exercise ends outside the system. The kissing claimant published its points in the form the check needed and linked the decision from its release. The registry's maintainer, who had written down beforehand what removes the mark of an unverified bound, removed \MtRgMarksTotalWord{} marks on the replays, corrected a row on the check, cites the replays, and on \MtRgThirdDate{} made a second party a requirement for every removal; the first confirmations under that rule were given here, for another contributor's bounds. The release's next commit, made the day after the audit pinned the repository, proves the clause the reading found missing. We draw no causal conclusion from these cases. They show what the grammar makes possible: a \CERT{} with its certificate can be cited, a \REFS{} that names its files can be answered, and a scope sentence can be closed.

\subsection{Independence, by level of evidence}\label{sec:independence}

Independence is a property of the program that decides, not of whoever typed the code. A verdict is independent of its claimant when the deciding program---the kernel, the exact or interval arithmetic, and the certifier that calls them---shares no code with the claimant and is not the claimant's system (rule R4). Tooling may be written with any assistant, including a model of the claimant's own, provided the deciders are not the claimant's; such code is pinned and re-derived like any other, and its place on the levels below is set by what re-derives its results, not by what wrote it.

We distinguish five levels of evidence of independence (\cref{tab:evidence}) and place every result of this paper on them. Every computed result reaches the first level; most reach the second; the third is reached for parts of claims only---the kernels' claim, the arithmetic on sampled inputs, and the rows outside parties re-derived with their own code---and none reaches the fourth or the fifth. Outside parties have re-derived \MtOutsideReruns{} records of the repository (\MtOutsideParties{} parties, \MtOutsideFirstDate{} to \MtOutsideLastDate{}, each with the same verdicts; \texttt{corpus/external-reruns.json}): \MtOutsideRerunsOnRows{} of them on rows of this paper's exercises, by \MtOutsidePartiesOnRows{} parties, of which \MtOutsideOnRowsOwn{} with their own code (twelve register rows, the registry's $C_{31}$ and $C_{84b}$ rows: level 3 for the parts re-derived) and \MtOutsideOnRowsTools{} with the tools that write the records (the three ledgers and the $C_{42}$ record: level 1 by the table's definition); and \MtOutsideRerunsElsewhere{} outside it, the $\lambda(4)$ audit~\citep{Lambda4Audit} and the Ramsey graphs. One of the tool re-runs read the $C_{42}$ decider and found three defects in what it printed, none moving a verdict, fixed and recorded in the benchmark's amendments. The registry's merges are not on the table at all: its maintainer applied his rule to the replays and did not re-derive them, so they are outcomes, not checks.

\subsection{Relation to existing tools}\label{sec:related}

\Cref{tab:related} places the system beside the tools that share its ingredients, by the properties that \cref{sec:grammar} and \cref{sec:protocol} define. Interval libraries~\citep{Rump99,Johansson17,Benet24} and interval global optimisation~\citep{HansenWalster04,Rump18global} compute the enclosures; $\delta$-complete solvers~\citep{dReal13,Kong18} decide formulas over the reals up to a user-chosen perturbation and can export checkable traces~\citep{Gao14proof}; proof assistants check a certificate against a formal statement inside a trusted kernel, with independent kernels that replay the check~\citep{SolovyevHales13,MartinDorel16,Mahboubi19,Carneiro24}, but whether the statement is the theorem intended is not checked~\citep{Bastounis26,Kallel26}; certifying algorithms and proof-checking solvers return a witness a small checker verifies~\citep{McConnell11,Wetzler14,CakeLPR21}; reproducibility initiatives re-run or re-implement published computations~\citep{Rougier17,ReproBuilds22}; and certificate architectures for language-model pipelines treat abstention as an outcome~\citep{Koomullil26,Li26}. Each enforces one or two of the properties; none, to our knowledge, is organised around other parties' published numbers, and none reads a printed digit string as the box it denotes or distinguishes the universal from the epistemic reading. The combination, enforced by code that refuses, is what is claimed; the properties the system itself meets only in part are marked as such, with the reason in \cref{tab:protocol} and \cref{sec:limits}.

Two lines of work are the method's lineage rather than its contribution. Its certifiers are those of validated numerics~\citep{Rump10,Tucker11}, in which a computation returns an enclosure that its own arithmetic proves, or no answer. Its re-checks descend from program checking~\citep{BlumKannan95}: the benchmark's oracles, the rerun kit's verifiers and the re-check of an exact certificate with no engine present use a language's standard library and none of the system's arithmetic layer, so each is simpler than the decider whose answer it checks, and none shares its code. A program checker in that sense judges one output of a program and may call the program on other inputs, so it decides another party's claim only in part; its answer holds whatever the program does, and it publishes neither a witness nor a refusal (\cref{tab:related}).

\subsection{Threats to validity}\label{sec:threats}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item \emph{Selection.} Every claim was chosen by the first author: the register records every row as self-initiated, and the exercises were picked because they had finite objects to decide. The results describe these claims, not machine-generated mathematics at large.
\item \emph{The benchmark's classes.} The mutation classes were designed by the first author, so the benchmark can find only the defects its classes reach; the Clopper--Pearson bounds describe this benchmark and do not transfer. Every claim in it is universal or exact, so the epistemic grammar is measured only by the gate's targeted faults.
\item \emph{The author in the outcomes.} The outcomes of \cref{sec:register}---the points published, the marks removed---followed requests and pull requests the first author made. They are reported as what happened, and no causal claim rests on them.
\item \emph{Correlated implementations.} The deciders, their second implementations and the benchmark's oracles were written by AI agents under the first author's direction, from shared briefs. N-version programs written that way can fail together~\citep{KnightLeveson86,Nogueira26,Ron26}, and level 2 of \cref{tab:evidence} is counted apart for that reason.
\item \emph{The arithmetic's evidence.} The comparison with Arb covers sampled inputs, not every input; the lemmas cover the functions, not the code that implements them; the standard's test suite was run only on the kernel's own operations, without decorations or the empty set, so it measures soundness there and not conformance.
\item \emph{The readings.} The statement readings and the Navier--Stokes reading were made by AI agents and checked only by second readings made the same way; their recall was measured once, on short statements under an alert brief. No human specialist has confirmed them.
\item \emph{The GPU argument.} The exactness of the seven largest kissing decisions rests on the documented single-precision accumulation of tensor cores~\citep{Fasi21,Blanchard20,HighamMary22}, characterised for earlier devices than the one rented, on the bound asserted before the run, and on a calibration that reproduced the laptop's exact contact counts on two configurations; the calibration is a check, not a proof.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scope and limits}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Not claimed} Interval arithmetic, the Krawczyk operator, floating-point filters, sums of squares and Sturm sequences are cited, not claimed. The validity of a declared model is not claimed: a \textsc{proved} engineering number (the demonstrator's word for \CERT) holds for the declared model over the declared envelope, not for the sea or the reservoir. A kissing bound ``decided here'' is a statement about one published configuration, not about the record in that dimension. A certified maximum is the maximum in its box; a global statement over a family would need a branch-and-bound over the family's whole parameter space, which the system does not run. Nothing is certified in the regulatory sense; the word is used in the sense of \cref{def:verdicts} only. Nothing is a probability.

Three results of the register rest in part on readings rather than computations---the clause found absent from the Navier--Stokes formal conclusions, the statements of the release found short of their claims, and the inventory of the record package---and are reported as what the readings found, with their recall as measured (\cref{sec:threats}).

\paragraph{Soundness rests on code} \Cref{prop:sound} is a theorem about interval extensions; whether a program computes one is a fact about the program. The defences---second implementations, red controls, pinned bytes, calibrations, the planted-claim benchmark, the comparison with Arb, outside reruns---reduce that risk and do not remove it, and what they found is on the record. A read of the code on 2026-10-09 found that the generation loop's closed-form hunt had counted \MtErrFloatMillions{} million float comparisons as refutations; every refutation is now a proved disjointness, with the correction published beside the old numbers. The benchmark's two findings are in \cref{sec:selfaudit}. Writing the lemmas of \supp{1} found, and the build of this version closed, with every record that pins the changed modules re-derived: a square root of $\pi$ taken from a thin box of the double nearest $\pi$, which does not contain $\pi$; an error-function tail short of the alternating-series bound by its own factor $2/\sqrt\pi$; a gamma-function margin computed in the platform's floating point; four transcendental tails evaluated in round-to-nearest; the lower end of $\ln\Gamma$ across its minimum as a hard-coded double; the flip thresholds rounded to nearest before the decimal rounding; the gate's witnesses one ulp outside the declared box; and the kissing runner recording an exactness bound it did not assert. An outside rerun of the $C_{42}$ decider and the sweep it started (\MtSaAmendPrintFirst{} to \MtSaAmendLast) found four programs---three deciders and one of the benchmark's oracles---whose \emph{printed} upper end was cut after $k$ decimals rather than rounded up, so the digits shown lay below the enclosure they reported; every decision was made on the full enclosure and none moved, and every printed end is now rounded outward. Each of these lay below the widths in play; each was a place where the implementation was weaker than the stated lemma. What remains open:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item the older helper that encloses the platform's cosine by a derived pad, contained in every case of \cref{tab:arb} but wide, is still called from six files, among them the radii-polynomial certifier, whose records are re-run when it is retired;
\item the interval kernel's membership test answers true for $\pm\infty$ against an interval with that infinite end (\cref{sec:arb}); every call in the repository passes a finite point (a constant or a computed double), and the one-line fix moves the pins of four records, which are re-derived when it is made;
\item the partial path of one polynomial-map decider reads a term with a missing exponent silently and answers \verdict{partial} where its full path refuses (the one malformed object decided in \cref{tab:selfaudit});
\item one quadrature routine, rigorous on dyadic cells of $[0,1]$, is called on another interval by one instrument; one battery labels some positive assertions as red controls; in the engineering demonstrator a \textsc{proved} consistency check means that the claim meets an outer enclosure;
\item two of the system's own instruments reuse its code across a producer--checker line (\cref{tab:protocol}), and the same-author second implementations are not independent re-derivations (\cref{tab:evidence});
\item only the exact-class certificates are in a form a program other than this system's can check; an interval certificate records the hull and the counts of its cover, not the cover.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusions and recommendations}\label{sec:conclusions}
%% =====================================================================

cert-machine decides the numbers and claims that machines propose with three verdicts, each carrying the object that justifies it. The interval and exact verdicts are sound under the inclusion property, which the arithmetic is built to secure and whose function enclosures carry a stated lemma each; the certifiers reduce the questions that arrive---a zero, a maximum, a statement for every input, a sign, a nonnegative polynomial, a minimum, a formal proof---to finitely many exact or outward-rounded decisions; the protocol is enforced by executable gates; and the record is public and re-runs. Measured against \MtSaMutants{} claims of known truth, the system produced no false certification and no false refutation and refused \MtSaRefusalsOnFalse{} for resources; measured against an independent library on \MtArbCases{} cases, every enclosure held; applied to \RegRows{} machine-generated claims, it found defects of reporting rather than of mathematics, after which the claimants published their points, the registry removed its marks and corrected a row, and the next upstream commit of the release proved the missing clause---outcomes reported without a causal claim. The three questions are answered as far as the record allows: one grammar covers the claims of \cref{tab:coverage} in the three domains (Q1); the hypothesis holds on the inputs tested and is proved on paper for the functions of \cref{tab:functions} (Q2); and on claims whose truth is known the system certifies what is true, refutes what is false and refuses a stated fraction by resources, the benchmark taking \MtSaMinutes{} minutes on a laptop and the exercises the costs of \cref{sec:register} (Q3).

\paragraph{Recommendations} Three practices would make such claims decidable from their publication. (i)~Producers publish the object, not only its description---the points of a configuration in one integer form, the witness a theorem rests on, the analytic inputs a certificate freezes, the data a fit was made from---with a digest beside each file, state the scope of every check in the release itself, and say where the theorem is stated when a formal statement is narrower than it. (ii)~Maintainers of the tables and records of a field distinguish a bound decided from outside from one only claimed, and write down what removes the distinction. (iii)~Builders of checkers print every enclosure outward and plant claims of known truth against their own record, with an oracle that shares no code with the decider.

\section{The arithmetic in detail}\label{app:arith}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:outward}]
For these operations the extremes of $a\circ b$ over $A\times B$ are attained at endpoint combinations (monotonicity in each argument on each sign region, with $0\notin B$ for division). The exact extreme lies within half the spacing of the doubles on its side of the rounded value, and the adjacent double on that side is a whole spacing away---also when the rounded value is a power of two, where the spacing below is half the spacing above, and in the subnormal range---so it lies on the correct side of $\mathrm{down}(y_-)$ and $\mathrm{up}(y_+)$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{tab:functions} lists every elementary and special function the certifiers call, with the lemma of \supp{1} that establishes \cref{ass:functions} for it. Throughout, a series is evaluated by the natural extension on the enclosed argument, the tail bound is evaluated in interval arithmetic and its upper end $\overline{R}$ taken, and $[-\overline R,\overline R]$ (or $[0,\overline R]$) is added by an outward-rounded addition, so the bound is never absorbed into the widening of \cref{prop:outward}. The enclosures of $\pi$ and $\ln 2$ are exact rationals (Machin's formula; $\operatorname{atanh}(1/3)$) converted outward, and every constant below ($\sqrt\pi$, $\sqrt{2\pi}$, $\pi/2$) is derived from them.
