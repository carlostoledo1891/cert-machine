\begin{abstract}
We prove, by validated numerics, that a stationary discounted congestion
mean-field game on the torus admits equilibria whose population density has
strictly more local maxima than the cost potential has wells. With
$V(x)=A_1\cos 2\pi x+A_2\cos 4\pi x$ and $A_1>4A_2$---exactly one well---the
certified equilibrium density at our main instance has exactly \TOnePeaks{}
strict local maxima (Theorem~\ref{thm:two}); a third-harmonic instance yields
exactly \TSixPeaks{} (Theorem~\ref{thm:three}). The mechanism is elementary and
we state it as such: the equilibrium re-weights harmonics \emph{the potential
already contains} through the band-pass linear-response gain
$c(\kappa)=\kappa/[(1+\sigma\kappa)^2+\gamma\kappa]$, so the density counts
wells with gain-weighted amplitudes while the potential counts them flat. The
second-versus-first harmonic crossover $\sigma^*=1/(8\pi^2)$ is independent of
the coupling $\gamma$---decided here as an exact-rational polynomial identity
(the $\gamma$-coefficient of the crossover polynomial is identically zero), not
by float agreement---and the limiting splitting window is the exact rational
interval $(\WindowTwoLo,\,\WindowTwoHi)$ (third harmonic:
$(\WindowThreeLo,\,\WindowThreeHi)$, the Chebyshev $U_{k-1}$ law). Around the
two theorems sit \NumBracketRowsWord{} further certified instances forming a
bracket table: negatives below the amplitude threshold and above $\sigma^*$,
replications, and a threshold pin---the splitting threshold at
$\sigma=\TOneSigma$, $\gamma=\TOneGamma$ lies in $[\PinLo,\PinHi]$ by certified
counts, with the exact-rational linear-response prediction
$r_c=\RcDigits\ldots$ inside. All enclosures are radii-polynomial certificates
at radii between $\RadiusMin$ and $\RadiusMax$, with local uniqueness in the
full sequence-space ball (even and odd blocks) and both positivity walls
certified. Every number in this document is interpolated from those
certificates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Under Lasry--Lions monotonicity \cite{LasryLions}, mean-field-game theory
guarantees a unique equilibrium, and the intuition that travels with the theory
is stronger than the theorem: the population's density is expected to mirror
the cost---wells of the potential become peaks of the crowd, one for one. The
known rigorous results reinforce that reading (in the Cesaroni--Cirant regime
\cite{CesaroniCirant}, density peaks sit where the potential's wells sit), and
the natural working assumption is a \emph{ceiling}: the equilibrium cannot
carry more local maxima than the cost has wells.

This paper proves the ceiling false, by validated numerics, for a congestion
system with a monotone coupling. At explicit parameters of a stationary
discounted congestion mean-field game on the torus we certify exact equilibria
whose density carries exactly two---and, at a third-harmonic instance, exactly
three---strict local maxima over a potential with exactly one well. The
mechanism is not exotic and we state it at its true altitude: the linearized
response to the cost is a band-pass filter in harmonic frequency, so the
equilibrium counts wells with \emph{gain-weighted} amplitudes while the
potential counts them flat, and between those two counts a single well splits
the crowd. What the linear analysis cannot give---and the certificates do---is
the nonlinear statement: these are exact equilibria of the full congestion
system, with local uniqueness in the full sequence-space ball and both
positivity walls certified. The uniqueness is the certificate's own, local to
the enclosure ball; no uniqueness theorem for the congestion system is invoked.

\section{The model and the exact instances}\label{sec:model}

The stationary discounted congestion mean-field game on the unit torus
$\Torus=\R/\Z$, with congestion exponent $a=1/2$, is the system
\begin{align}
u-\sigma u''+\tfrac12\,\frac{(u')^2}{\sqrt m} &= V(x)+\gamma m, \label{eq:hjb}\\
m-\sigma m''-\bigl(\sqrt m\,u'\bigr)' &= 1, \qquad m>0, \label{eq:fp}
\end{align}
with the congestion Hamiltonian $H(p,m)=\tfrac12|p|^2m^{-1/2}$, viscosity
$\sigma>0$ and the coupling $\gamma m$ with $\gamma>0$, which is increasing in
$m$: the crowd pays for density. The discount enters both equations as the
zero-order terms at unit rate, and the Fokker--Planck equation \eqref{eq:fp}
carries the uniform unit source of a population renewed at that rate. The
potential is the trigonometric polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:V}
V(x)=A_1\cos 2\pi x+A_2\cos 4\pi x+A_3\cos 6\pi x .
\end{equation}
The system is augmented with the branch variable $w=m^{-1/2}$ and the
reciprocal constraint $m\,w^2=1$, so that the congestion terms become
polynomial in the unknowns, and solved in even cosine Fourier space---$u$, $m$
and $w$ are even---with the derivative field $p=u'$ carried as an odd (sine)
sequence. The unknown is the tuple $(a_0,p,m,w)$: the mean of $u$ and the
coefficient sequences of $p$, $m$ and $w$.

\section{Linear response: the band-pass gain and the exact crossover}\label{sec:linear}

At first order in the potential amplitudes the congestion term drops out and
each harmonic responds independently. Linearize
\eqref{eq:hjb}--\eqref{eq:fp} about the flat state $(u,m)=(\gamma,1)$ of
$V\equiv0$: writing $u=\gamma+\hat u$, $m=1+\hat m$ and keeping first-order
terms,
\[
\hat u-\sigma\hat u''=V+\gamma\hat m,\qquad \hat m-\sigma\hat m''=\hat u'' .
\]
For the $k$-th cosine mode, with $\kappa_k=(2\pi k)^2$, this reads
$(1+\sigma\kappa_k)\hat u_k=A_k+\gamma\hat m_k$ and
$(1+\sigma\kappa_k)\hat m_k=-\kappa_k\hat u_k$, whence
$\hat m_k=-c(\kappa_k)A_k$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gain}
c(\kappa)=\frac{\kappa}{(1+\sigma\kappa)^2+\gamma\kappa}.
\end{equation}
To first order the density is $m\approx1-\sum_k c(\kappa_k)A_k\cos 2\pi kx$:
the crowd's harmonics are the potential's, each multiplied by the gain
$c(\kappa_k)$ and reflected. Three facts about \eqref{eq:gain} are decided in
exact rationals (\texttt{certs/terra-sigmastar.json}; standard-library
fractions, no floating point in any verdict).

\begin{proposition}[band-pass]\label{prop:bandpass}
Let $D(\kappa)=(1+\sigma\kappa)^2+\gamma\kappa$ be the denominator of
\eqref{eq:gain}. As polynomials in $\Q[\sigma,\kappa,\gamma]$,
\[
D-\kappa\,\partial_\kappa D=(1+\sigma\kappa)(1-\sigma\kappa)=\BandpassLhs .
\]
The $\gamma$-term cancels identically, so
$\operatorname{sign}c'(\kappa)=\operatorname{sign}(1-\sigma\kappa)$ for every
$\gamma$: the gain peaks at $\kappa=1/\sigma$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[$\gamma$-free crossover]\label{prop:crossover}
With $s=\sigma\kappa_1$ and $g=\gamma\kappa_1$, the $k$-th-versus-first
crossover polynomial
\[
N_k(s,g)=k^2\bigl[(1+s)^2+g\bigr]-\bigl[(1+k^2s)^2+k^2g\bigr]
\]
factors as $(k^2-1)(1-k^2s^2)$: its $g$-coefficient is the zero polynomial and
its positive root is $s=1/k$ exactly, with $N_k>0$ below the root and $N_k<0$
above it (decided for $k=2,\dots,\KMax$). Hence $c(\kappa_k)=c(\kappa_1)$
exactly at $\sigma=1/(4\pi^2k)$; at $k=2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigmastar}
\sigma^*=\frac{1}{8\pi^2}=\SigmaStarDigits\ldots
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The decimal digits in \eqref{eq:sigmastar} are read from an exact rational
bracket of $\sigma^*$ of width below $\SigmaStarWidth$, obtained from a
rational bracket of $\pi$ (Machin's formula with alternating-series bounds)
and outward division; the certificate carries the rational endpoints, and the
digits shown are those the two endpoints share. The $\gamma$-independence is
an exact cancellation, not numerical smallness. The crossover polynomials as

\begin{proposition}[the splitting windows]\label{prop:windows}
Since $\sin 2\pi kx=\sin 2\pi x\;U_{k-1}(\cos 2\pi x)$, with $U_{k-1}$ the
Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind, the two-harmonic potential
$V=A_1\cos2\pi x+A_k\cos2\pi kx$ with $r=A_k/A_1>0$ has
\[
V'(x)=-2\pi A_1\sin 2\pi x\,\bigl[1+k\,r\,U_{k-1}(\cos 2\pi x)\bigr],
\]
so $V$ acquires interior critical points beyond $x=0,\tfrac12$ if and only if
$k\,r\,|\min U_{k-1}|>1$. The flat thresholds are exact rationals read from the
ranges of $U_1=2c$ and $U_2=4c^2-1$ on $(-1,1)$: $r=\FlatThresholdTwo$ at $k=2$
and $r=\FlatThresholdThree$ at $k=3$. To first order the equilibrium density
obeys the same criterion with the gain-weighted ratio
$r_{\mathrm{eff}}=r\,c(\kappa_k)/c(\kappa_1)$, and
$c(\kappa_k)/c(\kappa_1)\to k^2$ as $(\sigma,\gamma)\to0$ (the limits
$\GainLimitTwo$ and $\GainLimitThree$ are exact evaluations), so the limiting
splitting windows---where the density splits while the potential does not---are
exactly
\[
\bigl(\WindowTwoLo,\ \WindowTwoHi\bigr)\ \text{at } k=2, \qquad
\bigl(\WindowThreeLo,\ \WindowThreeHi\bigr)\ \text{at } k=3 .
\]
\end{proposition}

One sentence, stated honestly: between the gain-weighted count and the flat
count, the crowd splits at the bottom of a single well. The mechanism---a
non-flat linear gain changing the critical-point count of a two-harmonic
signal---is elementary, and Proposition~\ref{prop:windows} is its complete
statement; stating it as elementary isolates the actual contribution, which is
the mean-field-game setting, the exact $\gamma$-free constant, and the
nonlinear certificates below. Linear response predicts; only the certificates
prove.

\section{Theorem 1: two peaks over one well}\label{sec:two}

Write $\lnu$ for the weighted sequence space with norm
$\|f\|_\nu=|f_0|+2\sum_{k\ge1}|f_k|\nu^k$ on even (cosine) sequences, and the
same weights on odd (sine) sequences; the augmented unknown $(a_0,p,m,w)$ lives
in a product of such spaces, and ``the ball'' below is the ball of radius $r$
about the stored candidate in that augmented space.

\begin{theorem}[two peaks over one well]\label{thm:two}
At the instance T1 of Section~\ref{sec:model} ($A_1>4A_2$: $V$ has exactly one
well) there is an exact equilibrium $(u,m,w)$ of the full congestion system
\eqref{eq:hjb}--\eqref{eq:fp} within $\lnu$ distance $r=\TOneR$ of the stored
candidate, locally unique in the full sequence-space ball---even (cosine) and
odd (sine) blocks alike---with $m\ge\TOneMinM$ and $w\ge\TOneMinW$ over the
whole ball. Every density in that ball has exactly \TOnePeaks{} strict local
maxima and \TOneMinima{} strict local minima on the torus, while $V$ has
exactly \TOneWells{} well. (Records: \texttt{certs/terra-recert-t1.json},
\texttt{certs/terra-peakcount-t1.json}.)
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{The enclosure.}

approximate inverse $A$ of the Jacobian $DF(\bar x)$ at the candidate $\bar x$
and bounds, in outward-rounded interval arithmetic, the defect
$Y_0\ge\|A\,F(\bar x)\|$, the contraction constant $Z_1\ge\|I-A\,DF(\bar x)\|$
(taken over the even and the odd block) and a second-order constant $Z_2$; a
radius $r$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radii}
p(r)=\tfrac12 Z_2\,r^2-(1-Z_1)\,r+Y_0<0
\end{equation}
yields a unique zero of $F$ in the ball of radius $r$, and the same zero is the
unique one in the ball of any radius in $(r_{\min},r_{\max})$, the roots of
$p$. The T1 numbers are
\[
Y_0=\TOneYZero,\qquad
Z_1=\TOneZOne\ \text{(even and odd blocks alike)},\qquad

\]
with admissible radii $(r_{\min},r_{\max})\supseteq(\TOneRMin,\ \TOneRMax)$;
the certificate takes $r$ one factor-$\RStep$ step above $r_{\min}$ and

approximation; and the odd block is certified because that is where a

with no potential,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cap}

\textstyle\int m=1,\ \int u=0,\ m>0,

\section{Method and verification posture}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{The enclosure instrument.}
The enclosures are radii-polynomial certificates on the augmented
$(a_0,p,m,w)$ system with the reciprocal constraint, with $\lnu$
Banach-algebra tail bounds and both blocks of the linearization certified. The
verifier is the frozen standard-library Python verifier that ships, byte for
byte, inside the companion page of the program's base instance,
\texttt{reports/mfg-congest.html}; the file re-extracted from that page is
pinned by its sha256, whose first digits are \texttt{\FrozenVerifierSha}. A
living module extends it by only the second- and third-harmonic data terms of
$V$ and the construction of the approximate inverses by Gauss--Jordan
elimination. The
extension is battery-pinned to the frozen file: on the frozen file's own
embedded instance the extended validation must reproduce the frozen one bit
for bit. An approximate inverse is only ever a \emph{candidate} for a
contraction---every bound is recomputed from it rigorously, so a bad inverse
fails $Z_1<1$ and cannot fake a proof.

\paragraph{Two implementations.}
The candidates were first enclosed by a JavaScript kernel at the origin bench;
the certificates here are the re-certification by the Python lineage, with
independently computed approximate inverses on each side. On T1 the two agree
on the certified radius exactly (ratio $1$ to the last digit) and on $Z_1$ and
on the floor of $w$ exactly; the density floors differ by
$\TOneCrossMinMDiff$ (origin $\TOneCrossMinMOrigin$, here $\TOneMinM$),
because the positivity routine here subtracts a modulus-of-continuity slack
the origin did not---ours is the lower, and it is the one stated. Float
witnesses accompany the certificate and are labeled as such: on a pointwise
grid the candidate's HJB residual is below $\TOneWitHjb$, the Fokker--Planck
residual below $\TOneWitFp$, and $|\sqrt m\,w-1|$ below $\TOneWitRecip$. No
proof step consumes them.

Every certificate carries \NumFalsifiers{} controls that must fire for the
record to be accepted; all \NumFalsifiers{} fire on all \NumInstances{}
instances. Two of them attack the extension's own new lines.

\paragraph{The float map, and the falsified prior.}
The companion atlas page draws a float phase map of $\PhaseMapSolves$
equilibrium solves at $N=\PhaseMapN$ over $(\sigma,A_2/A_1)$, pinned by

and no proof step consumes it. The falsified prior worth naming: the belief
that equilibrium peaks cannot exceed potential wells was this program's own
working assumption, formed on an unreproduced \PriorSamples-sample float
campaign at the origin bench---not folk belief, and not literature. The
certificates above refute it.

\section{Related work and fences}\label{sec:related}

\begin{itemize}
\item Karuturi \cite{Karuturi}---the mandatory fence: spontaneous instability
of the uniform state under an interaction kernel with \emph{no} potential,
with a cognate $8\pi^2\nu^2$ constant for a different quantity. Ours is the
driven response to a structured cost under a fixed potential, with a monotone
coupling.
\item Cesaroni--Cirant \cite{CesaroniCirant}---the foil: there, density peaks
mirror the potential's wells; here they certifiably exceed them.
\item Osborne--Smears \cite{OsborneSmears}---different in kind: a posteriori
finite-element analysis, not equilibrium enclosures.
\item Cecchin--Dai Pra--Fischer--Pelino \cite{CecchinEtAl}---non-uniqueness via
a different mechanism, on a finite state space.
\item van den Berg--Lessard \cite{vdBL} (radii polynomials) and Rump
\cite{Rump} (the Krawczyk operator and interval verification)---the
computer-assisted-proof lineage this work instantiates.
\end{itemize}

\section{Open problems}\label{sec:open}

A PDE-level census (the count of Section~\ref{sec:companion} lifted from the
truncation to the system); $k$-peak uniformity in $k$; sharper digits of the
splitting threshold than the pin $[\PinLo,\PinHi]$; non-even branches as
$\gamma\to0$; and the time-dependent forward--backward computer-assisted
proof.

\section{Status and priority}\label{sec:status}

Released from the repository; not peer-reviewed; not independently rerun. Two
priority claims, each stated with its fence and open to refutation:
\begin{enumerate}
\item To our knowledge, these are the \textbf{first certified equilibria of a
mean-field game whose exact peak count strictly exceeds the potential's well
count}. The nearest published phenomena are spontaneous instability
\cite{Karuturi} and peaks that mirror the potential \cite{CesaroniCirant};
neither carries validated-numerics certificates, and we found no
validated-numerics equilibrium enclosures for mean-field games anywhere in the
literature.
\item The base instance of this program---the $\sigma=1/2$ single-harmonic
enclosure of the companion page \texttt{reports/mfg-congest.html}, released
together with this paper and its atlas---is, to our knowledge, the
\textbf{first validated-numerics enclosure of a mean-field-game equilibrium at
all}: an exact solution within an explicit radius, locally unique in the full
sequence-space ball, with strictly positive density.
\end{enumerate}
The companion page's embedded verifier serves as the calibration gate of every
certificate here; the two are one program, released together, and the
priority claims above are made by that joint release.
