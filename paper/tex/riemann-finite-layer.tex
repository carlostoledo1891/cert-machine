\begin{abstract}
In 2026 a proof that more than two thirds of the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function are simple and on the critical line was found by a language model, verified by Alp\"oge and Furman, and formalised in Lean. The formalisation has one numerical input: 256 integer enclosures of a form factor at scale $2^{\RfKPow}$, kernel-checked downstream of a hypothesis, EnclOK, that the kernel does not check and that rests on a law file the release describes as available from the authors. We re-check the certificate from its integers with two programs that share no code with Lean, state exactly what that establishes and what it does not, and ask for the file. On the way, the Montgomery--Taylor constant and the four $\xi'$ constants of the paper are decided by exact rational arithmetic alone, with no interval kernel. The window of the paper's Remark~7.1 is then optimised exactly: the constants depend on the window through a Rayleigh quotient, the paper's quartic sits $\RfQuarticGapTex$ below its own family's optimum, and a degree-six window with the certificate's series truncated at fifteen terms instead of nine raises the formalisation's eight-digit constants from \RfLeanP{} and \RfLeanD{} to \RfNewP{} and \RfNewD, with an exact certificate that no even polynomial window of degree up to twenty does better at $\lambda=1$; the same machinery with the $\zeta$ kernel recovers the Montgomery--Taylor constant to \RfMtAgreeDigits{} digits, which calibrates it. The paper's abstract prints one constant truncated where a rounding would print another digit, true as a lower bound; and a survey of everything the story's sources print---the manuscripts, the Lean files, the research post, a video's captions---finds that \RfShaped{} of \RfSentences{} sentences are finite claims at all. No theorem is touched. Every number here is read from a public record at build time and re-derived by a third implementation before it is printed.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item The formalisation's one numerical certificate holds over its printed integers by a route that shares nothing with Lean; its hypothesis EnclOK rests on an unpublished file, which we ask for.
\item The Montgomery--Taylor and $\xi'$ constants are decided by exact rational arithmetic, no interval kernel; the $\xi'$ rationals re-derive from their definitions.
\item The $\xi'$ window optimised exactly: the formalisation's constants rise from \RfLeanP{} and \RfLeanD{} to \RfNewP{} and \RfNewD{} by a degree-six window and fifteen series terms, with a certificate that no even polynomial window of degree up to twenty does better.
\item With the $\zeta$ kernel the same machinery returns the Montgomery--Taylor constant to \RfMtAgreeDigits{} digits, and exactly $0.6725$ at degree two: the calibration against a closed form.
\item A truncated print is not a rounded one: the abstract's $0.8362$ is true as a lower bound and false as a rounding.
\item Of \RfSentences{} sentences the story's sources print, \RfShaped{} reduce to decidable claims; every theorem is refused, with the rule.
\end{highlights}

\section{The certificate, and what it rests on}\label{sec:cert}

Alp\"oge and Furman \citep{AlpogeFurman26} prove that at least two thirds of the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$, counted with multiplicity, are simple and on the critical line, and that at least five sixths are distinct; with the Montgomery--Taylor window \citep{Montgomery73,CCLM17} the constants become $2-c_{\mathrm{MT}}^{-1}=0.67250\ldots$ and $\tfrac12(3-c_{\mathrm{MT}}^{-1})=0.83625\ldots$. The argument was discovered and written by Claude, an AI developed by Anthropic; the authors verified it and take responsibility for it, as the paper's own dedication says. Anthropic formalised the result in Lean \citep{Zeta23Lean} and described the work in a research post \citep{AnthropicPost26}; a video explained the checking to a wide audience \citep{Video26}. The formalisation's audit prints the axioms of every main statement; they are Lean's three standard ones.

One part of the formalisation is numerical. The bandwidth-one ceiling, \texttt{Zeta23/PairCeiling/}, is the stability inequality behind the paper's remark on the optimality of its method: a bound on the simple-point fraction of any law of 256-periodic marked configurations whose form factor is near the CUE values on the band. Its input is the generated file \texttt{Zeta23/PairCeiling/LawN256.lean} (\RfLawLeanBytes{} bytes, sha256 \texttt{\RfLawLeanSha}), whose header states that it was computed by 70-digit interval arithmetic at scale $K=2^{\RfKPow}$ and holds enclosures $\mathrm{lo}_j\le K\cdot S(j)\le\mathrm{hi}_j$, $j=1,\ldots,256$, of the grid form factor of a law recorded in a certificate file, \texttt{\RfLawFile} (sha256 \texttt{\RfLawFileSha}), ``available from the authors''. The enclosures are \RfKDigits-digit integers, every one exactly \RfEnclWidth{} unit wide. The row certificate they satisfy is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rows}
|256\,S(j)-j|\le\tau:=\RfTauTex\quad(0<j<256),\qquad |D(1)|\le\RfEdgeBar,
\end{equation}
with the closed-band row $j=256$ free; the law's simple-point fraction is the exact rational $p_0=\RfPZeroFrac$, that is $\RfPZeroExact\ldots$, which the paper prints as $p_0\le0.6818287$.

The Lean side is as the audit prints it. \texttt{LawN256\_check}, the kernel check of \eqref{eq:rows} over the enclosures, depends on the axiom \texttt{propext} alone. \texttt{ceiling\_law256} and the other ceiling theorems depend on the three standard axioms and, as the audit says of all of them except the two kernel checks, carry the displayed hypothesis \texttt{EnclOK}: that the true form factor of the law lies in the enclosures. That hypothesis is about the law itself, the weights and positions in \texttt{\RfLawFile}, and the release does not publish that file. The audit is exemplary in saying so. But it means that the one place where the formalisation's numerical certificate meets the real numbers is checked by interval arithmetic outside Lean, from bytes no one outside can read.

\section{What was checked without Lean}\label{sec:checked}

We decoded the integer literals of \texttt{LawN256.lean} into a JSON file beside it, naming the Lean file's digest, and decided three claims over those integers with two programs, one in Python and one in JavaScript, that share no code with each other or with Lean and evaluate in exact rational arithmetic (Table~\ref{tab:run}; the programs are described in Section~\ref{sec:method}):
\begin{itemize}
\item the band rows, $|256\,S(j)-j|\le\tau$ over the \RfBandRows{} rows, with both ends of each enclosure tested: the worst row sits at $\RfWorstRowTex$ from its target against the tolerance $\RfTauTex$;
\item the edge bound, $|D(1)|$ with $D(1)=\sum_{j\le256}S(j)/256-\tfrac12$ evaluated at both ends of the enclosures, which is exact because $D(1)$ is monotone in each $S(j)$: $|D(1)|$ at the ends is $\RfEdgeExact\ldots$, under the printed bound by $\RfEdgeMarginTex$;
\item $p_0$, the file's rational against the printed bar.
\end{itemize}
All three hold, in milliseconds. Beside them, three pieces of the paper's own arithmetic on the ceiling were decided from the file's $p_0$ and $\tau$: $p_0-\tfrac23=\RfPZeroGap\ldots$ is within the paper's $0.016$; the decimal constant the Lean prints beside the ceiling, $1/(6\cdot256^2)+\tau/512=\RfCTwoFiveSixTex$, is under the paper's rounded $2.55\cdot10^{-6}$; and the paper's bound for any window with $|r'(1)|+\int|r''|<8.2$, $p_0+2.55\cdot10^{-6}\cdot8.2=\RfBEightTwo\ldots$, is under its printed $0.6819$.

\paragraph{A property of the file} Every band enclosure contains $j\cdot K/256$ exactly: \RfCueUpper{} rows at the upper end, \RfCueLower{} at the lower, counted from the decoded integers and decided as $|2\,j\,K/256-\mathrm{lo}_j-\mathrm{hi}_j|\le1$ over the \RfBandRows{} rows. The law's form factor is thus consistent with $S(j)=j/256$ on every band frequency to $2^{-\RfKPow}$: the enclosures do not merely lie within $\RfTauTex$ of the CUE values, they sit on them, and $D(1)=S(256)/256-1/512$ to the same precision, with $256\,S(256)=\RfRowClosed\ldots$. This is most likely so by construction, an optimal law of a linear programme whose constraints are the CUE band values being expected to meet them exactly, and it is reported as a property of the file, not as a finding about the method.

\paragraph{What this establishes, and what it does not} The three checks are decided from the same integers the Lean kernel accepted; the independence is in the checking code, not in the data. They establish that the row certificate \eqref{eq:rows} holds over the printed enclosures by a route that does not pass through Lean, its elaborator or its \texttt{decide} tactic, a no-shared-code concordance with \texttt{LawN256\_check}. They do not establish that the enclosures enclose anything. That is EnclOK, and it waits on the law file: there is no reading of any published byte that decides it, because the bytes it is about are not there.

\paragraph{The request} The file had been asked for on the formalisation's repository twice before, in August 2026 (its issues 8 and 14), without an answer; on 2026-10-22 we joined that request as a comment on issue 8, with the re-check above and the ask for the interval-arithmetic script as well. With the file, the enclosures can be re-derived by an independent interval arithmetic from the law's weights and positions, and the certificate becomes checkable end to end by exactly the pipeline described here with one more claim. Until then it is a conditional certificate, for the kernel and for us alike.

\section{Two families of constants, decided by rational arithmetic}\label{sec:constants}

\paragraph{The Montgomery--Taylor constant} The formalisation defines $c_1^*=\sqrt2\tan\vartheta/(1+\vartheta\tan\vartheta)$ at $\vartheta=1/\sqrt2$, the reciprocal of the paper's $c_{\mathrm{MT}}^{-1}=\tfrac12+\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\cot\tfrac1{\sqrt2}$. An exact checker over rationals cannot evaluate a tangent, and an interval kernel would be the usual answer. None is needed. With $\vartheta^2=\tfrac12$ the Maclaurin series give $\sin\vartheta=\vartheta s$ and $\cos\vartheta=c$ for
\begin{equation}\label{eq:series}
s=1+\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{(-1)^k}{2^k(2k+1)!},\qquad c=1+\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{(-1)^k}{2^k(2k)!},
\end{equation}
alternating series of rationals whose terms fall by a factor of at least twelve at every step; hence $\tan\vartheta=\vartheta s/c$ and, since $\sqrt2\,\vartheta=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cmt}
c_1^*=\frac{2s}{2c+s},\qquad 2-\frac1{c_1^*}=\frac32-\frac cs,\qquad \frac12\Bigl(3-\frac1{c_1^*}\Bigr)=\frac54-\frac c{2s}.
\end{equation}
For an even truncation order $K$ the partial sums bracket the limits, $s_{K+1}\le s\le s_K$ and $c_{K+1}\le c\le c_K$, and $2s/(2c+s)$ increases in $s$ and decreases in $c$ on positive values, so $2s_{K+1}/(2c_K+s_{K+1})\le c_1^*\le2s_K/(2c_{K+1}+s_K)$. At $K=\RfSeriesK$ this is the enclosure $[\RfCmtLo,\ \RfCmtHi]$, of width $\RfCmtWidthTex$, in sums, products and powers over integer ranges. The three prints of the sources, $c_1^*=0.7532960\ldots$, $2-1/c_1^*=0.67250\ldots$ and $(3-1/c_1^*)/2=0.83625\ldots$, hold as truncation boxes, and so do the two bounds one unit of the eighth digit either side, $c_1^*\le0.75329607$ and $c_1^*\ge0.75329606$. This is elementary; the point is only that it removes the kernel, and that the paper's own print of $2-c_{\mathrm{MT}}^{-1}$, kept on the closed-form route on purpose, stands admitted and undecided in Table~\ref{tab:run} to show what the checker cannot do without it.

\paragraph{The $\xi'$ constants} The paper's Remark~7.1 states that at least $0.85838$ of the zeros of $\xi'$ are simple and on the critical line and at least $0.92919$ are distinct (flat window), $0.86864$ and $0.93432$ with the quartic window $v(s)=1-\tfrac7{100}(2s)^2-\tfrac{51}{200}(2s)^4$; the formalisation proves eight-digit versions, $0.85838371$, $0.92919185$, $0.86864017$, $0.93432008$, from a certificate every number of which is rational: the diagonal density $D_1(s)=s-4s^2+\sum_{k\ge0}a_ks^{2k+3}$ with $a_k=2\cdot4^{k+1}k!/(2k+2)!$, truncated at nine terms ($k=0,\ldots,8$) with the certified tail $\varepsilon_9=a_9\cdot231/211=\RfXiEpsTex$ (the coefficient ratio is at most $20/231$ from $k=9$ on); the window integral $J(v)=2\int_0^1D_1^{(9)}(r)\,(v{*}v)(r)\,dr$ with $v{*}v$ the window's autocorrelation; $\kappa_9(v)=(\int v^2+J(v))/(\int v)^2$; and the proportions $2-\kappa_9-\varepsilon_9$ and $\tfrac32-(\kappa_9+\varepsilon_9)/2$. We re-derived every number from those definitions in exact fractions, the quartic window's degree-nine autocorrelation polynomial included, twice and independently (once in the translator's session, once in the generator of this paper's numbers, which stops the build if any differs), and each equals the Lean's computer-algebra-generated closed form:
\begin{gather*}
J_{\mathrm{flat}}=\RfXiJFlat,\qquad \varepsilon_9=\RfXiEps,\\
\kappa_9^{\mathrm{flat}}=\RfXiKappaFlat,\\
J_{\mathrm{quartic}}=\RfXiJQuartic,\\
\kappa_9^{\mathrm{quartic}}=\RfXiKappaQuartic.
\end{gather*}
The four eight-digit constants hold with margins $\RfXiPFlatMarginTex$, $\RfXiDFlatMarginTex$, $\RfXiPQuarticMarginTex$ and $\RfXiDQuarticMarginTex$; the paper's five-digit prints hold; $P_{\mathrm{flat}}=\RfXiPFlatFrac=\RfXiPFlat\ldots$ exactly, and a control one unit of the eighth digit above it, $P_{\mathrm{flat}}\ge0.85838372$, is refuted as it must be. (The $\xi'$ certificate's Lean files---\texttt{Statement}, \texttt{Defs}, \texttt{Certificate} and its \texttt{D1}, \texttt{Poly} and \texttt{AtOne}---are pinned by digest with the other sources; the identity claims quote the derivation note as the line that prints them.)

\paragraph{Two corrections the machine made} Both re-derivations began wrong, and both times the exact decision was the correction. The grammar's first reading gave a print cut off with an ellipsis, $0.7532960\ldots$, the half-unit rounding box; the checker then refuted the true print and certified a false $0.7532961\ldots$, and it was right, since $c_1^*=\RfCmtDigits\ldots$ lies in $[0.7532960,\,0.7532961)$ and not within half a unit of $0.7532961$. An ellipsis is a truncation, and the grammar now reads it so. And a first pass took ``\texttt{D1trunc 9}'' as ten terms and reproduced none of the printed rationals; nine terms reproduce all of them, and the generator checks that a ten-term reading still matches none. We report both because they are the kind of error an exact decision catches and a careful reader does not.

\section{The $\xi'$ window, optimised exactly, and the tail recovered}\label{sec:window}

\paragraph{The constants are a Rayleigh quotient of the window} For an even polynomial window $v(s)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}c_is^{2i}$ on $[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$, every quantity in $\kappa_9(v)=(\int v^2+J(v))/(\int v)^2$ is a rational form in the coefficients: $\int v=b^{\mathsf T}c$ with $b_i=\int s^{2i}=1/(4^i(2i+1))$, and $\int v^2+J(v)=c^{\mathsf T}Mc$ with $M_{ij}=\int s^{2i+2j}+\tfrac12(H_{ij}+H_{ji})$, $H_{ij}=2\int_0^1D_1^{(K)}(r)\,(s^{2i}{\star}s^{2j})(r)\,dr$, where the pair autocorrelation $(s^{2i}{\star}s^{2j})(r)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2-r}t^{2i}(t+r)^{2j}\,dt$ is a polynomial in $r$. So $\kappa_9=c^{\mathsf T}Mc/(b^{\mathsf T}c)^2$, minimised over each degree exactly at $c\propto M^{-1}b$ with minimum $\kappa^*=1/(b^{\mathsf T}M^{-1}b)$: rational linear algebra, no numerics. The formalisation's own comment on its quartic window says it ``saturates the numerically optimal window to $10^{-6}$''; this section makes that exact, and then takes the two cheap steps the exact picture shows.

\paragraph{The optima by degree and truncation order} Table~\ref{tab:tail} gives the minimum of $2-\kappa^*-\varepsilon_K$ over even polynomial windows of each degree, for the certificate's nine-term truncation of $D_1$ and for three longer ones. At nine terms the paper's quartic, $\RfQuarticP\ldots$, is $\RfQuarticGapTex$ below the optimum of its own degree, and the optimum saturates from degree six on: twelve digits agree up to degree twenty, $\RfCeilingGapTex$ above the paper's window. The larger lever is in the rows: the nine-term truncation costs $\varepsilon_9=\RfEpsNineTex$, more than a third of the whole window gain, and the formalisation's truncation lemma is stated for every $K\ge9$ with the tail $\varepsilon_K=a_K\cdot\frac{(K+2)(2K+3)}{(K+2)(2K+3)-2(K+1)}$, the geometric comparison from the coefficient ratio at $k=K$, which decreases. At fifteen terms $\varepsilon_{15}=\RfEpsFifteenTex$ ($=\RfEpsFifteenFrac$) and the constants move by the whole of $\varepsilon_9$; at twenty terms nothing moves in twelve digits.

\paragraph{The certified window} The degree-six optimum rounded to thousandths,
\[

satisfies the formalisation's window profile by inspection of its coefficients: every coefficient after the first is negative, so $v_6\le1$ on $\R$ and $v_6$ decreases in $|s|$, and $v_6(\tfrac12)=10737/16000>0$; both facts are decided from the certificate's bytes. With the nine-term certificate it gives $P\ge0.86864115$ and $D\ge0.93432057$ (\RfWSixKNineP{} and \RfWSixKNineD{} exactly), one unit of the sixth digit above the Lean's \RfLeanP{} and \RfLeanD; with fifteen terms, $P\ge\RfNewP$ and $D\ge\RfNewD$ (\RfWSixKFifteenP{} and \RfWSixKFifteenD), a gain of $\RfGainTex$ over the formalisation's constants, by the same window shape and three more terms of a series it already holds. The controls one unit of the eighth digit beyond are refuted, as they must be. The paper's five printed digits, $0.86864$ and $0.93432$, do not move.

\paragraph{The optimality, decided} That no window of a degree does better is the statement that $N:=M-\kappa^*bb^{\mathsf T}$ is positive semidefinite, with exactly one zero eigenvalue at $c^*$. The certificate prints the pair autocorrelations as coefficient rows, from which the checker recomputes $M$ exactly, together with $L$, $D$ and $c^*$ for $N=LDL^{\mathsf T}$; the claims are that the factorisation's residual is zero, that every pivot is at least zero, that the least pivot is not positive (the one zero), that $\kappa_9(c^*)-\kappa^*$ is zero and not negative (attainment), and that $P^*$ lies in an eleven-digit bracket. All hold, and the two controls that must fail do, at degrees six and twenty, at nine and at fifteen terms. So ``no even polynomial window of degree at most twenty gives the method more than \RfOTwentyKFifteenP{} at $\lambda=1$'' is a decision of the checker, not a sentence. The generator of this paper's numbers re-derives every one of these certificates from the definitions alone, in a third implementation over arbitrary-precision rationals: the autocorrelation rows, $M$ and $b$, the solve, $\kappa^*$ and the pivots, and it stops the build if any differs from the record.

\paragraph{The parameter} The paper's Theorem~8.2 holds at every fixed $\lambda\in(0,1)$ with $\kappa_1(\lambda,v)=1/c_\lambda(v;D_1)$, $c_\lambda(v;D)=\lambda(\int v)^2/(\int v^2+\lambda\mathcal J)$ and $\mathcal J=2\int_0^1D(\lambda r)(v{\star}v)(r)\,dr$, the limit form at $\lambda=1$ stated separately; the functional at $\lambda$ is a Rayleigh quotient too, with $M=G/\lambda+\operatorname{sym}H(\lambda)$. For every window and degree tried the constant falls as $\lambda$ leaves $1$ (Table~\ref{tab:lam}); on record, $v_6$ at $\lambda=19/20$ gives $P\le0.83686$ and at $9/10$ $P\le0.79415$, with the control $P\ge0.84$ refuted. The limit form is the right place, and there is nothing to gain in that direction. This is closed by evaluation at the sampled values, not by a monotonicity proof.

\paragraph{The calibration} With the $\zeta$ kernel $D(s)=s$ in place of $D_1$ the same functional is the Montgomery--Taylor problem, whose optimum over all windows is the paper's closed form $2-c_{\mathrm{MT}}^{-1}=\RfMtSixteen\ldots$ (the series bracket of Section~\ref{sec:constants}), attained at the window $\cos(\sqrt2\,s)$: the Euler--Lagrange equation of the quotient, $v''+2v=0$. Polynomial windows must stay at or below it and approach it. They do (Table~\ref{tab:zeta}): degree two gives exactly $2-\RfZetaDegTwoKappa=\RfZetaDegTwo=0.6725$, the abstract's four digits to the digit; degree four gives $0.6725007036667\ldots$; from degree six on, $\RfZetaDegSix\ldots$, inside the series bracket of width $2\cdot10^{-19}$, the closed form to \RfMtAgreeDigits{} digits, and the distinct proportion $\RfMtDistinctSixteen\ldots$ likewise. The optimality certificates for $\zeta$ at degrees two, six and twenty are decided by the same route, controls refuted. \paragraph{What this is, and what it is not} It is a certified improvement of the formalisation's $\xi'$ constants by $\RfGainTex$, within the paper's own method and hypotheses, with an exact certificate of optimality over polynomial windows of degree up to twenty and the tail taken three terms further on the formalisation's own route; and it is a calibration of the machinery that produced it. It is not a new digit in the paper's print, not an optimum over all windows, and not a Lean proof: a Lean instance needs the truncation lemma at $k\ge15$ (the coefficient ratio is at most $\RfRatioFifteen$ there, beside the $\RfRatioNine$ of $k\ge9$) and the four lemma instances the file \texttt{AtOne.lean} proves for the quartic---its two integrals, the closed form of $v{\star}v$, positivity---with $v_6$'s rationals. Everything a formaliser needs is printed in the certificates.

\section{A print, and a rule}\label{sec:print}

The paper's abstract prints: ``\RfAbstractSentence'' Its theorem prints $0.67250\ldots$ and $0.83625\ldots$. The exact second constant is $\RfDistinctLo\ldots$, which lies above the box the four printed digits allow as a rounding, $[0.83615,\,0.83625]$: $0.8362$ is a truncation, and a rounding would print $0.8363$. As a lower bound on a proportion the truncated print is still true, a weaker true statement, and the paper says nothing else about it. The rule it illustrates is general: a print cut off with an ellipsis and a print rounded to four digits are different claims, and a reader with exact arithmetic will tell them apart. In our grammar the two are read differently, and Table~\ref{tab:math} in the appendix shows the claim ``$(3-1/c_1^*)/2$, published as $0.8362$'' refuted under the rounding reading and its companion $0.6725$ holding.

\section{How the claims were read, and how much of the story is finite}\label{sec:method}

The claims above were not typed by hand. They were extracted from the sources by a translator with a closed grammar, \texttt{claimbox} \citep{Claimbox}, and decided by an exact checker, cert-machine, whose grammar of three verdicts with witnesses is described in the method paper this is a companion to \citep[the method paper]{CMArchive}. The translator is a standard-library pattern matcher, with no model and no parser framework. A sentence is read only if it matches one of a short list of shapes: a trailing finite range; $|\mathrm{EXPR}|\ \mathrm{REL}\ \mathrm{NUMBER}$; $\mathrm{EXPR}=\mathrm{NUMBER}\ldots$, a value printed to its digits and cut off; $\mathrm{NAME}\ \mathrm{REL}\ \mathrm{NUMBER}$; a constant ``published as'' a number; an exact identity $\mathrm{NAME}=\mathrm{RATIONAL}$; a few others for other corpora. The number is kept as the source spells it and read as the exact rational it means. Everything else is refused with a rule: an asymptotic, an unbounded quantifier, an approximation sign, two relations in one sentence, no printed quantity, prose. The object the translator emits carries the line of the source that prints the claim with the file's digest, the pin of the bytes and a sentence saying how they were decoded, the predicate, the hypotheses under which the predicate is the claim, and the shape a witness against it would take; the checker resolves the pin, reads the data as stated, evaluates the predicate over exact rationals and prints the word with a witness. Two checkers, in Python and in JavaScript, share no code and agree on every object here. Neither runs the claimant's code, reads a proof, or evaluates a transcendental function: a closed form over $\exp$, $\log$, $\sin$ or $\cos$ is admitted and left undecided.

Put to the whole story, the grammar is a measuring instrument. The \RfSentences{} sentences the sources print were extracted byte for byte, tagged by author, and read: \RfShaped{} match a shape and \RfRefused{} are refused (Table~\ref{tab:refusals}), \RfRefProse{} as prose with no claim shape, \RfRefNoQuantity{} with no printed quantity, \RfRefAmbiguous{} as ambiguous, \RfRefAsymptotic{} as asymptotic, \RfRefApproximate{} as an approximation. Every theorem is among the refused: a statement about the limit inferior of a ratio of counting functions is not a finite claim, and the grammar says so rather than inventing one. The \RfShaped{} read are the certificate's inequalities and the digits prints of Sections~\ref{sec:checked} and~\ref{sec:constants}, and nothing else. The $11/12$ companion manuscript of the \texttt{openai/math} release \citep{OpenAIMath} (\RfManuscriptBytes{} bytes, sha256 \texttt{\RfManuscriptSha}; \RfManuscriptLines{} lines by \texttt{pdftotext} \RfPdftotext) states \RfManuscriptStatements{} numbered theorems, lemmas, propositions and corollaries and prints no decimal constant at all; it is a proof for human readers, and nothing in it is a claim this pipeline can touch. The same is true of eighty phrasings of \RfPhrasingsClaims{} of the story's claims written by a fresh AI agent in the voices of a paper, an abstract, a README, a Lean docstring, a blog, a tweet, a referee report and a chat assistant: \RfPhrasingsShaped{} read, \RfPhrasingsRefused{} refused, because a claim is read at the head of its sentence or not at all, and nothing in the grammar was extended for a phrasing no pinned source prints.

That the finite layer of a celebrated result is this thin is, we think, the most useful number in this paper. It is not a criticism of the result; a proof is not a list of inequalities. It is a measurement of what independent machine checking can reach in a story of this kind without reading proofs, and the answer, here, is one certificate and a handful of printed constants. The battery that holds the instrument to account, planted claims with their expected words, the author's own claims as a mathematician with expectations recorded before each run, and \RfPlants{} planted defects at scale on the certificate with \RfControls{} controls undisturbed, is in \ref{app:battery}.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusions}

The formalisation's numerical certificate holds over its printed integers by a route that shares nothing with Lean; its hypothesis EnclOK rests on a file that only its authors can read, and we have asked for it. The constants that looked as if they needed an interval kernel needed only an alternating series and the certificate's own definitions, and once they were rational the window and the tail could be optimised exactly: the formalisation's $\xi'$ constants rise by $\RfGainTex$, with an optimality certificate and a calibration that returns the Montgomery--Taylor constant to \RfMtAgreeDigits{} digits. One print in the abstract is a truncation. Of everything the story's sources print, \RfShaped{} sentences in \RfSentences{} are finite claims. None of this bears on the theorems. What it offers their authors is small and concrete: publish the law file, and the one conditional step in an otherwise kernel-checked certificate becomes a step anyone can check; and take the window $v_6$ and fifteen terms, which raise two formalised constants by the same proofs with new rationals.

\paragraph{Recall at scale} A defect planted in the bytes, the sentence or the page must come out as a refusal with its rule or as a changed decision, and an edit that is not a defect must leave the decision as it was. Every enclosure was moved by one unit either way and widened by one and two units; rows were dropped and exchanged; $K$ and $N$ were edited; the free row was moved; $p_0$ was edited in the file; the bar was moved in the sentence and in a copy of the page with the sentence following; another claimant's file was given as the bytes; the last digit of $c_1^*$ was changed in a copy of its file (Table~\ref{tab:plants}). The expectation of every row edit was computed from the edited integers by hand: an enclosure sits on one side of $j\cdot K/256$, which side varies by row, and $\tau$ is $1.63$ units of $256/K$, so a move away from $j$ crosses the tolerance and a move towards it does not. The first draft of the recall assumed every one-unit shift flips the decision; the first sample showed half do not, which is what the controls are for. \RfPlants{} plants, \RfCaught{} caught; \RfControls{} controls, \RfControls{} undisturbed; \RfRecallUndecided{} undecided as recorded, the digits plant on the paper's closed-form claim (\RfUndecidedPlant).
