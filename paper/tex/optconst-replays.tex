\begin{abstract}
A curated registry of the best known bounds on \MtRgConstants{} constants of analysis and combinatorics marks with an asterisk every bound whose only verification is the producer's own, and states what removes the mark: refereeing, formalisation, or an independent replay. We replayed the certificates behind the asterisked bounds that rest on finite objects, from the bytes the registry cites and with no code of any producer's run: entropy laws for the sums--differences constants $C_{3b}$ and $C_{3c}$, a truth table on \MtRgFeiN{} variables for the Fourier entropy--influence constant $C_{71}$, step functions for the autocorrelation constants $C_{1a}$--$C_{1d}$, two constructions for the Zhang--Zagier height $C_{82}$, a step measure for the Schur--Siegel--Smyth trace constant $C_{86}$, Lueker's automaton for the Chv\'atal--Sankoff constant $C_{31}$, the explicit chain behind a sum--product exponent $C_{84b}$, and a ``certified record package'' for the de Bruijn--Newman constant. Each certificate is read as a finite exact object and decided by two programs that share no code with each other or with the claimants (the $C_{84b}$ chain by one, and again by an outside party with its own code), in exact rationals where the object is rational and in outward-rounded intervals where an entropy or a logarithm enters; the deciders' arithmetic is compared with Arb, and claims of known truth planted on the certificates are decided as constructed. Every finite certificate holds. One bound, $C_{1d}$, is printed below its own certificate's exact value: refuted as printed, certified one unit up. The $C_{84b}$ row quoted the constant its note suggests rather than the one it proves, and is repaired to the latter. The package is refused, because the analytic inputs its bound rests on are not in it. One certificate's own checker printed OK on false claims, since it compares in double precision what it encloses at a hundred digits. Over three rounds the registry removed \MtRgMarksTotalWord{} asterisks on the replays, corrected the $C_{84b}$ row and cites the replays on its pages; it then made a second party a requirement for every removal, and the first confirmations under that rule were given here, for another contributor's graphon bounds $C_{89b}$ and $C_{89c}$, decided by two implementations sharing no code. In \OcOutRuns{} outside reruns of these decisions, \OcOutOwn{} with the parties' own code, the verdicts were the same. We report the registry's actions as what happened and draw no causal conclusion. A replay decides that a certificate establishes a printed bound, and nothing about the constant beyond it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Curated records of the best known bounds are kept by hand, and machine-assisted contributions now reach them faster than referees read them. The optimization-constants registry \citep{OptConst} keeps the best known bounds on \MtRgConstants{} constants of analysis and combinatorics, each with its source, and many of its recent rows come from program search and AI-assisted contributors \citep{GGSWT25,RomeraParedes24,Novikov25}: two of the rows decided here carry the registry's own note that they were prepared with an AI system, and a third producer signs its archive ``AI Autoresearch'' \citep{NumaroC71}. Such a bound arrives as a self-archived artifact---a certificate file, a script that checks it---checked so far only by the program its producer wrote. The registry's answer is a mark: \OcStarNote{} \citep{OptConst}. Its contributing guide states how the mark comes off: it ``is removed when that changes---when the paper is refereed and published, or when the argument is formalized or independently replayed''. On \MtRgRuleDate{} the maintainer, writing that rule into the guide, added of one such bound: ``If the 13-point certificate does get independently replayed, or formalized, the asterisk comes off'', and that he would take a pull request that removes it on those grounds \citep{OptConstRule}.

This paper is the replay the rule asked for, made with cert-machine \citep[the method paper]{CMArchive}, a system that decides machine-generated claims independently of the claimant and records one of three verdicts---\vC, \vR\ or \vF---with the object that justifies it. A claim is read from its published statement and bytes; the claimant's program is never in the trust path; a verdict is a computation with a witness, and a reading---a file inventoried, a five-sentence argument checked line by line---is recorded as a reading and never counted as a verdict. The claims decided are the registry's asterisked bounds that rest on finite objects, as they stood on three dates: three lower bounds before the replays, the \MtRqRows{} bounds of its open pull-request queue on 2026-10-15, and the rows its merges of \MtRgMergeDate{} added; then two rows decided before its third round, $C_{31}$ and $C_{84b}$, and another contributor's two graphon bounds, decided under the rule that round introduced (Section~\ref{sec:materials}).

\paragraph{Contributions} Every finite certificate behind those bounds decided twice, by programs sharing no code with each other or with any producer (the $C_{84b}$ chain once here and once by an outside party), with the verdict, enclosure and scope of each recorded; three findings beside the verdicts, a bound printed on the wrong side of its own certificate, a row quoting a constant its source suggests and does not prove, and a producer's checker that accepts false claims; a refusal with its reason; what the registry did afterwards, from its pinned bytes; and the reruns of these decisions by outside parties. The constants are not the contribution: a replay decides that a certificate establishes a printed bound and nothing more. Sections~\ref{sec:materials}--\ref{sec:after} give the materials, the method, the verdicts and the registry's response; Section~\ref{sec:outside} the outside reruns; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} what the results show and their limits; \ref{app:repro} the command that re-derives each result.

\section{Materials}\label{sec:materials}

\paragraph{The bounds} On \MtRgBeforeCommit{} (\MtRgRuleDate) three asterisked lower bounds rested on finite certificates: two constants of the sums--differences problem \citep{KatzTao99}, $C_{3b} \ge \MtRgCbBound$ and $C_{3c} \ge \MtRgCcBound$, each certified by an entropy law on \MtRgCbPoints{} and \MtRgCcPoints{} points of $\Z^2$ \citep{MosaicC3b,LinC3c}; and the Fourier entropy--influence constant \citep{FriedgutKalai96}, $C_{71} > \MtRgFeiBound$, certified by a Boolean function on \MtRgFeiN{} variables \citep{NumaroC71}. On 2026-10-15 the open pull requests (\MtRqPrs) offered \MtRqRows{} new bounds: two step functions, five one-line constructions, a nine-dimensional counterexample, a two-law entropy certificate \citep{JaswalC3c}, and a ``certified record package'' for the de Bruijn--Newman constant, $\Lambda \le 0.1595$ \citep{MosaicLambda}, against the recorded $0 \le \Lambda \le 0.2$ \citep{RodgersTao20,PlattTrudgian21} and an asterisked $0.1875$ from the same claimant. The merges of \MtRgMergeDate{} added asterisked rows that rest on finite objects: four step functions for $C_{1a}$ and $C_{1b}$, two for $C_{1c}$ and $C_{1d}$, two constructions for $C_{82}$ and a step measure for $C_{86}$. Two more asterisked rows were decided before the third round: the binary Chv\'atal--Sankoff constant $C_{31}$ \citep{ChvatalSankoff75}, bounded above at $\OcLuPrintedNine$ and $\OcLuPrintedTen$ by \OcLuClaimant{} with Lueker's method \citep{Lueker09} (pull request \OcLuPr), and the exponent $C_{84b}$ of the sum--product problem \citep{ErdosSzemeredi83} over the reals, whose row quoted $\OcSpPrinted$ from a note of \OcSpNoteAuthor{} dated \OcSpNoteDate. On \OcGnPosted, under the rule the third round introduced, two asterisked graphon bounds of another contributor, \OcGnClaimant, were open as pull requests \OcGnPrB{} and \OcGnPrC: the Ramsey multiplicity constants $C_{89b}$ and $C_{89c}$ of $K_5$ and $K_6$ \citep{Thomason89,PPSS22}.

\paragraph{The bytes} Every object decided is a byte sequence named by path and SHA-256 digest in the record that decided it, fetched from the source the registry cites and refused if its digest does not match: deposit and gist files for the entropy laws and the two-law certificate, the artifact that binds the truth table by digest, the pull requests' bytes at the registry commit \OcQueueRegistryCommit{}, the package at its pinned commit, and the files the new pages cite. The registry's own bytes---README, contributing guide and constant pages---are pinned at \MtRgBeforeCommit{} before the replays and at \MtRgRegistryCommit{} and \MtRgSecondRegistryCommit{} after each round of merges on \MtRgMergeDate{}; what the registry prints beside each bound is read from those bytes. Each decision writes a record naming its inputs by digest, the verdict, the witness, the scope, the time and the second implementation's answer; every number in this paper is a macro written from one of the records named in the Data availability statement by a generator that refuses to write when the record no longer supports the sentence quoting it. Everything ran on an \MtDeskCpus-core laptop with \MtDeskMemory~GB of memory, the JavaScript deciders in Node v24.14.1 and the Python ones in the standard library alone; nothing was rented.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The certificates and their reading}\label{sec:reading}

Every certificate here is a universal claim over one finite object---a law, a truth table, a step function, a polynomial, a step measure---and the claim decided is that the object establishes the printed bound, given the published theorem that turns such an object into a bound; the theorem is read and named, never re-proved, and each record's scope sentence says so.

\paragraph{An entropy law} The sums--differences problem of \citet{KatzTao99} asks for the best exponents in bounds on $|A - B|$ by $|A|$, $|B|$ and $|A + B|$; its constants have entropy forms, as Ruzsa's entropy analogues of sumset inequalities make precise \citep{LauNair23}, and the registry states $C_{3b}$ and $C_{3c}$ in that form. A certificate is a finite law $\mu$ on $\Z^2$ with rational weights, claiming for $(X, Y) \sim \mu$
\[
C \;\ge\; \frac{H(X - Y)}{\max\{H(X),\, H(Y),\, H(X + Y)\}},
\]
with $H(X + 2Y)$ added to the maximum for $C_{3c}$. The pushforward of $\mu$ under each linear form is computed exactly in rationals; each $-p \ln p$ is enclosed in an interval, the logarithm by its series with an outward-rounded tail; the ratio is a quotient of intervals, and $C \ge c$ is \vC\ when the enclosure's lower end is at or above $c$, \vR\ when its upper end is below $c$, \vF\ when it straddles $c$. Earlier lower bounds were found by hand and by search \citep{Lemm15}; these were found by AI-assisted search \citep{GGSWT25}, which is why they come as artifacts. The two-law certificate draws $10^{20}$ independent base-55 digits from laws on \MtRqTwoLawPoints{} points; its entropies add digit by digit only if the map from digits to the linear form is injective, which holds when every form is at most $\MtRqTwoLawMax$ in absolute value on both supports---a condition of the claim, checked and not assumed.

\paragraph{A truth table} The Fourier entropy--influence conjecture of \citet{FriedgutKalai96} asks for a constant $C$ with $H(\hat f^{\,2}) \le C \cdot I(f)$ for every Boolean function $f$; the best general results are of the form $H \le O(I \log I)$ \citep{KKLMS20}, and lower bounds on $C$ come from explicit functions \citep{ODonnellTan13,Hod17}. The $C_{71}$ bound is read from a truth table of $2^{\MtRgFeiN}$ entries: the integer Walsh--Hadamard transform gives the spectrum exactly, the influence $I$ is a rational, the entropy $H$ of the spectral weights is an enclosure, and the bound $H/(I - 1)$ follows from an amplification rule that is cited, not re-proved \citep{ODonnellTan13,Hod17}. The decision is \MtRgFeiChecks{} named checks, from the digest the artifact binds and balance to $I = \MtRgFeiI$ exactly, the bound, the claimant's printed floor inside the enclosure and monotonicity.

\paragraph{A step function} The autocorrelation constants $C_{1a}$--$C_{1d}$ are suprema and infima of autoconvolutions and correlations of functions on an interval \citep{MatolcsiVinuesa10,CloningerSteinerberger17,BarnardSteinerberger20}, and a bound is exhibited by one function. A step function with $n$ heights written as integers over one denominator has an autoconvolution that is one exact integer polynomial, so the bound it exhibits is one exact rational; the printed bound is \vC\ when it lies on the safe side of that rational and \vR\ as printed when it does not, in which case the rational the certificate does establish is recorded beside the refutation, as a repair.

\paragraph{A Zhang--Zagier construction} The essential minimum of the Zhang--Zagier height \citep{Zhang92,Zagier93} is bounded above by limit-point constructions; Doche's \citep{Doche00} gives the bound as a ratio of a Mahler measure and an integral of $\max(0, \log\lvert A\rvert - \log\lvert Q\rvert)$ on the unit circle. Every root of every composed factor is certified by Gershgorin's theorem on exactly computed Weierstrass corrections, the integral is bounded on dyadic panels, the theorem's hypotheses are checked exactly, and the enclosure of the construction's value is compared with the printed bound; Doche's theorem is read.

\paragraph{A step measure} The Schur--Siegel--Smyth trace problem asks for the smallest limit of $\operatorname{tr}(\alpha)/\deg(\alpha)$ over totally positive algebraic integers \citep{Smyth84}; Smith's criterion \citep{Smith24} turns an explicit measure and finitely many integer polynomials into an upper bound, the route recent bounds take \citep{OrloskiSardari23}. The $C_{86}$ certificate is a step measure on \OcTraceCells{} cells, \OcTraceNonzero{} nonzero, and \OcTracePolys{} integer polynomials with weights. Decided are the six finite conditions the claimant states---the hypotheses of the criterion and the arithmetic of the bound: densities nonnegative; each polynomial primitive, irreducible over $\Z$ and totally real; the weighted degree below the mass; each $\int \log\lvert Q\rvert\,d\mu$ positive, by enclosures; the potential minus the weighted logarithms nonnegative on every cell of positive density, by adaptive bisection with second-order Taylor forms; and the first moment over the mass at or below the printed bound. The chain from the conditions to the bound is the published one, read.

\paragraph{An automaton and a witness vector} The binary Chv\'atal--Sankoff constant $\gamma_2$ is the limit of the expected length of a longest common subsequence of two random binary strings, divided by their length \citep{ChvatalSankoff75}. Lueker's method \citep{Lueker09} bounds it above through an automaton $M_h$ that reads the strings in windows of length $h$ and its weighted transfer matrix $A_z$: any positive vector $v$ with $A_z v \le \lambda v$ componentwise gives $\gamma_2 \le 2\log(2z)/\log\lambda$. The replay rebuilt $M_h$ from Lueker's paper alone in two implementations that share no code with each other or with the claimant, required them to reproduce Lueker's own state counts for $h = \OcLuCalFrom$ to $\OcLuCalTo$, computed a witness vector of its own at $h = 9$ and $h = 10$, evaluated $\lambda$ under directed rounding and the bound outward, and compared the result with the printed rows; the theorem is read.

\paragraph{An explicit chain} The $C_{84b}$ row bounds the exponent from above by $2 - c$, where $c$ is the explicit constant of a published construction as a note the registry cites optimises it; the note states $c \ge \OcSpNoteC$ as its theorem and $\OcSpClaimedC$ as the value the same calculation suggests. The replay decides every inequality of the chain in interval arithmetic at the note's choice of parameters, and bounds $c$ from above over every choice of them; the lemmas the chain cites are read, not re-proved.

\paragraph{A step graphon} The Ramsey multiplicity constant of $K_t$ is the least density of monochromatic copies of $K_t$ in a two-colouring of a large complete graph; Erd\H{o}s conjectured that the random colouring attains it, which \citet{Thomason89} disproved, and computer search has since found better colourings \citep{PPSS22}. An upper bound is exhibited by one two-coloured step graphon $W$, and the claim is that $t(K_t, W) + t(K_t, 1 - W)$ is at most the printed bound. For $C_{89b}$ ($t = 5$) the graphon has \OcGnBlocksB{} equal blocks with red values that are integers over $2^{\OcGnDenB}$; for $C_{89c}$ ($t = 6$) it lives on $\Z_2^{\OcGnCubeC}$, its red value depending on the weight of $x \oplus y$. The density is one exact rational, computed twice: in Python with exact fractions, and in JavaScript by modular counting and Chinese remaindering, with the reduction in arbitrary-precision integers. The claim is \vC\ when the two agree, equal the fraction the claimant's page states, and lie at or below the printed bound. That a step graphon's density bounds the constant from above is the standard argument, read.

\paragraph{A one-line construction, a counterexample, a package} Two of the queue's one-line bounds are decided from the moments of an explicit law, exactly; two more needed derivations the pull request did not contain, which their author supplied when asked, and were decided from those with no code of his run. A five-sentence argument was checked by reading alone and is counted as a reading, not a verdict. The nine-dimensional counterexample is a Lean development this project had already built and checked under two kernels; the request's statement was read against that record. The package was inventoried without running anything.

\subsection{Two implementations, and checks on the deciders}\label{sec:deciders}

\paragraph{Two implementations that share no code} Each certificate was decided by two programs written from the specification in different languages and arithmetics: the entropy certificates in JavaScript over arbitrary-precision integers with dyadic intervals at 128 bits, and again by the Python standard library alone, its \texttt{decimal} arithmetic at 120 digits rounded outward; the truth table in Python \texttt{decimal} intervals at \MtFeiPyDigits{} digits and again in JavaScript over BigInt with the entropy in closed form; the step functions, autocorrelation rows, Zhang--Zagier rows and step measure each in JavaScript over BigInt rationals with the interval kernel's series logarithm and again in Python with \texttt{fractions} and its own logarithm; the $C_{31}$ automaton in Python, the reference, and in C, held to it state by state (every state for small $h$, seeded samples for the large ones); the $C_{84b}$ chain by one Python program only, its second derivation being the outside rerun of Section~\ref{sec:outside}; the graphon densities as described above. Each record states the agreement: the same word, and the same exact fraction or overlapping enclosures. These are N-version checks written by one author with AI agents from one specification, the correlated-failure case \citet{KnightLeveson86} measured, and level~2 of the method paper's five levels of evidence: they catch a slip in one arithmetic, not a shared misreading.

\paragraph{The deciders' arithmetic against Arb} The real functions of the entropy deciders---outward conversions, logarithms, entropies and ratios, directed-rounding contexts---were run on \MtEaReal{} cases from one seed, among them the registry's own certificates, and every enclosure was compared with the exact value computed in Arb \citep{Johansson17} at \MtEaPrec{} bits and more, or exactly in rationals when the value is rational. Of the \MtEaArith{} arithmetic cases, \MtEaContained{} returned an enclosure and every one contained the value; the other \MtEaRefused{} were refused, each outside the amplification rule's domain; all \MtEaMalformed{} malformed certificates were refused and all \MtEaReds{} planted wrong functions caught. The first run, on \MtEaPinDate, found \MtEaPrintedFindings{} kinds of printed enclosure cut toward zero and \MtEaMalformedFindings{} malformed inputs a Python decider let through; no published verdict was affected, since every verdict compared the full-precision ends, each finding was fixed the same day, and the record keeps all \MtEaFindings{} with their witnesses. The audit took \MtEaSeconds{} s and runs in reduced form in every build.

\paragraph{Planted claims and red controls} A checker that refuses everything never accepts a false claim, so claims of known truth are planted beside the real ones. On the three entropy certificates: the bound claimed just above the ratio, false; the law translated by an integer vector, true; a weight moved off a probability law, and a base too small for the digit map to be injective, malformed---\OcEvFalse{} false, \OcEvTrue{} true and \OcEvMalformed{} malformed claims, every one decided as constructed. On the truth table: \OcFeiPlantedFalse{} false, \OcFeiPlantedTrue{} true and \OcFeiPlantedMalformed{} malformed, all decided as expected by both implementations after a first run whose Python word differed from the reading on \OcFeiFindingsAtPin{} of them and refuted \OcFeiFindingRefutedTrue{} true claim, kept in the record and fixed on \OcFeiFixedDate. In the method paper's pre-registered self-audit of \OcSaDate, the \OcSaRows{} entropy rows of the public register were mutated in \OcSaMutants{} ways---\OcSaFalse{} false, \OcSaTrue{} true, \OcSaMalformed{} malformed, each witnessed by a standard-library script sharing no code with the deciders---and every one came out as required. Every battery also carries deliberately wrong variants that must be rejected (\MtSfReds{} on the step functions, \OcAcReds{} on the autocorrelation rows, \MtCzReds{} on the Zhang--Zagier rows, \OcTraceReds{} on the step measure), and fails if any does not fire. The planted sets are targeted, not sampled, and give no rate.

\subsection{The rows decided after the merges}

The \OcLaterCount{} rows the merges of \MtRgMergeDate{} added were decided the same way (Table~\ref{tab:later}). \section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the results show} No bound was found false. Where there is a gap it lies between what was claimed and what was checked: a checker that compares in double precision what it encloses at a hundred digits, so that a bound one unit above the truth in its seventeenth significant digit prints OK; a bound printed below its own certificate's exact value, because a print rounds to nearest and an upper bound rounded down is no longer a bound; a row quoting the constant its source suggests in place of the one it proves; a package that certifies its own assembly and not the analysis it assembles. Each was closed, or named, by a finite exact computation, made by programs the claimants did not write. The refusal is neither a claim that the bound is wrong nor that it is right: it records what an outside party can decide from the submission and names what would convert it.

\paragraph{Relation to the literature} The certificates are instances of certifying algorithms \citep{McConnell11}: an output with a witness a small independent checker can confirm. The checkers the producers ship are the usual form; the replay is the independent check the form presumes and rarely gets. For the constants nothing is claimed beyond the certificates: the sums--differences and entropy literature \citep{KatzTao99,Lemm15,LauNair23}, the entropy--influence results \citep{FriedgutKalai96,ODonnellTan13,Hod17,KKLMS20}, the autocorrelation inequalities \citep{MatolcsiVinuesa10,CloningerSteinerberger17,BarnardSteinerberger20}, the Zhang--Zagier height \citep{Zhang92,Zagier93,Doche00}, the trace problem \citep{Smyth84,Smith24,OrloskiSardari23}, the Chv\'atal--Sankoff constant \citep{ChvatalSankoff75,Lueker09}, the sum--product problem \citep{ErdosSzemeredi83} and Ramsey multiplicity \citep{Thomason89,PPSS22} are cited, not advanced. Curated records with an explicit rule---the registry's asterisk, the status of a problem in the Erd\H{o}s problems database \citep{ErdosProblems}---are the setting in which a replay can change a record, and the one in which an unreplayed artifact otherwise stands indefinitely.

\paragraph{Threats to validity} \emph{Selection.} The rows were chosen because they rest on finite objects; rows resting on proofs, or on packages whose evidence is elsewhere, were not decided, and the results describe these rows, not the registry. \emph{The author in the outcomes.} The merges, the removed marks and the correction followed pull requests the author made; they are reported as what happened and no causal claim rests on them. \emph{Correlated implementations.} The two deciders of each row were written by AI agents under the author's direction from shared briefs; N-version programs written that way can fail together \citep{KnightLeveson86}, and the comparison with Arb covers the entropy deciders only, so for the step-function, Zhang--Zagier, trace, automaton and graphon deciders the second implementation is the only check beyond the first. Outside code reaches level~3 for the sums--differences certificates, the $C_{31}$ rows and the $C_{84b}$ chain (Section~\ref{sec:outside}), and for no other row. \emph{Reciprocity.} The confirmations of the graphon bounds and the outside replay of $C_{31}$ are given by each party for the other's rows; each is a computation with its own record, and the relation is stated wherever either is cited. \emph{Read theorems.} Every chain from a finite object to a bound---the entropy inequality, the amplification rule, Doche's theorem, Smith's criterion, Lueker's theorem, the lemmas of the sum--product chain, the graphon argument---is the published one, read and not re-derived; a slip in a published theorem would not be caught here.

\paragraph{What is not claimed} Nothing about any constant beyond what its certificate establishes: an exhibited bound holds and says nothing about the true value or about optimality, and the registry's other rows are not decided here. ``Independent'' means independent of the claimant, not that the two implementations are independent of each other. Nothing is certified in the regulatory sense.

\section{Conclusions and recommendations}\label{sec:conclusions}

Every finite certificate behind the registry's asterisked bounds holds, each decided twice from its bytes with no code of its producer's run; one bound was printed on the wrong side of its own certificate and is now printed on the right side; one row quoted a suggested constant and now quotes the proved one; one producer's checker accepts false claims and was not needed; one package was refused with the inputs that would decide it named. The registry removed \MtRgMarksTotalWord{} marks on the replays, corrected a row, cites the replays beside the certificates, and now requires a second party for every removal; the first such confirmations, for another contributor's graphon bounds, were given here. Outside parties have re-derived the decisions in \OcOutRuns{} reruns with the same verdicts. The cost was seconds to hours on a laptop. To those who maintain curated records: distinguish a bound decided from outside from one only claimed, write down what removes the distinction, and keep the mark until it is met, as this registry's rule does. To producers: publish the object with a digest beside each file, quote the constant a source proves rather than the one it suggests, print bounds rounded toward the safe side, and make the checker compare at the precision it encloses. A replay that shares no code with the producer can be made only once the bytes are published.
