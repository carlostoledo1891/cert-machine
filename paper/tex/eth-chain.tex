\begin{abstract}
In October 2026 OpenAI and Anthropic each published a Lean~4 proof that the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) is false. Each statement is about a machine and an input encoding of its laboratory's making: Anthropic's \lean{ETHIsFalse} is the negation of $s_3>0$ with $s_3$ taken over Mathlib's multi-stack Turing machines (\lean{FinTM2}) reading a custom encoding of duplicate-free formulas over the variables $x_0,\dots,x_{n-1}$; OpenAI's \lean{exact\_count\_and\_sat} is a statement about its own algorithm on the multitape machines of its ParityLifts library. The step from either to a standard model is left to prose. We prove in Lean that \lean{ETHIsFalse} implies ETH is false as stated in the cslib-community library \lean{complexitylib}: for every $\delta>0$ a deterministic multitape Turing machine decides 3-SAT on the plain binary encoding of every formula within $c\cdot 2^{\delta n}\cdot(L+1)^{e}+c$ steps. We then port both proofs into the same Lean, Anthropic's (\EcPortFiles{} files, \EcPortEdited{} edited for the newer toolchain) and OpenAI's together with Daniel Hawkley's two theorems that carry it to \lean{ETHIsFalse} (\EcOaPortFiles{} files, \EcOaPortEdited{} edited), so that on each side the proof, the statement and the bridge are checked in one environment and no step crosses a version boundary. Every step was checked from pinned bytes by Lean's kernel, by nanoda (\EcChainNanoda{} and \EcOaNanoda{} declarations under the two chains), by \lean{leanchecker --fresh}, and by Lean's judge \lean{lake comparator}: with six checkers on Anthropic's chain; on OpenAI's chain, whose export is \EcOaExportGb{}~GB, the judge's four bundled external checkers stop at their own resource limits as shipped, and the verdict reads the judge re-run with named checkers (Lean's paranoid kernel, our pinned nanoda, lean4lean with its fuel raised, Lean's default kernel), which also accepts OpenAI's own challenge file against the ported proof. \EcPlanted{} planted wrong variants were refused. Whether the formal statement is the textbook ETH remains a reading of a definition. The proofs were written by AI agents directed by the author, who also wrote the checker; this is not an independent review.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is claimed, and what is not}\label{sec:claim}

Two AI laboratories published Lean proofs in October 2026 that the Exponential Time Hypothesis of \citet{IP01} is false \citep{AnthropicETH,OpenAIETH}. Both proofs are accepted by Lean's kernel, which we confirmed independently for both, against their own statements, with a second kernel \citep{CMEth}. The question that readers raised is not whether the proofs check but what they state: each statement is about a machine and an input encoding of its laboratory's making, and the step from there to a standard machine is argued in prose.

This note closes that step for both statements. \emph{Claimed:} a Lean theorem, checked as described in Section~\ref{sec:check}, that Anthropic's \lean{ETHIsFalse} implies the negation of ETH as defined over cslib's deterministic multitape Turing machine with a binary encoding of arbitrary formulas; a second Lean theorem in which Anthropic's own proof of \lean{ETHIsFalse}, ported, supplies the hypothesis in the same environment; and a third in which OpenAI's own proof, ported together with the two theorems of \citet{Hawkley26} that carry it to \lean{ETHIsFalse}, supplies the hypothesis in the same environment. \emph{Not claimed:} that ETH is false; the correctness or novelty of either laboratory's algorithm outside its Lean development; that a formal definition is the textbook one, which no machine decides (Section~\ref{sec:limits}). v0.1 of this note covered Anthropic's chain only; the title footnote lists what v0.2 adds.

\section{The statements}\label{sec:defs}

\paragraph{Anthropic's} \lean{MainResults.lean} at commit \EcAnthropicCommit{} (Lean \EcAnthropicLean, sha256 \texttt{\EcMainResultsSha\dots}) defines \lean{InTime 3 $\delta$}: some \lean{FinTM2} machine with finite alphabets and a constant $c$ decide, for every $n$ and every 3-CNF $\varphi$ on $x_0,\dots,x_{n-1}$ without repeated clauses, the satisfiability of $\varphi$ within $\lfloor c\,2^{\delta n}\rfloor$ steps, reading \lean{encClauses 3 $\varphi$}. Then $s_3=\inf\{\delta: \text{\lean{InTime 3 $\delta$}}\}$, \lean{ETH} is $0<s_3$, and \lean{ETHIsFalse} is its negation.

\paragraph{OpenAI's} The theorem OpenAI's release checks is \lean{exact\_count\_and\_sat} of \lean{OAI.Algorithms.\allowbreak SubexponentialSAT.\allowbreak SqrtLogSpace} (family 378 of \lean{openai/math}, commit \EcOpenAICommit, Lean \EcOpenAILean): a statement about its own algorithm, on the multitape machines of its ParityLifts library and that library's encoding \lean{encodeFormula}. The step to Anthropic's statement is Hawkley's \citep{Hawkley26}, in two theorems written inside OpenAI's Lean: \lean{not\_positiveRateETH : $\neg$\,PositiveRateETH}, derived from OpenAI's \lean{original\_sat\_main}, where \lean{PositiveRateETH} is the ETH predicate of the ParityLifts library; and \lean{not\_positiveRateETH\_implies\_ethIsFalse : $\neg$\,PositiveRateETH $\to$ ETHIsFalse}, which runs ParityLifts' machines on \lean{FinTM2} within a linear number of steps and converts \lean{encClauses} into \lean{encodeFormula} within a polynomial of one fixed degree. We had replayed both with two kernels inside OpenAI's Lean \citep{CMEth}; until v0.1 the copy of \lean{ETHIsFalse} there and the one the bridge starts from were only read to be the same proposition, across Lean \EcOpenAILean{} and v4.36.0-rc2.

\paragraph{complexitylib's} \lean{ETH} is $\exists\,\delta>0,\ \neg\,\text{\lean{DecidableInExpTime (KSAT 3) encCNF numVars $\delta$}}$, where \lean{DecidableInExpTime} asks for one deterministic multitape Turing machine (cslib's \lean{Turing.MultiTapeTM}) and an exponent $e$ such that the machine decides membership in \lean{KSAT 3} within $O(2^{\delta\,n}\,(L+1)^{e})$ steps on the binary encoding \lean{encCNF} of length $L$, for every formula: variable labels are arbitrary natural numbers, and literals and clauses may repeat. This is the form of the hypothesis on a standard machine model with a reasonable binary encoding; its distance from the textbook definition is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\section{The bridge, and the two chains}\label{sec:bridge}

\begin{quote}
\lean{EthBridge.Anthropic.not\_eth\_of\_ethIsFalse : MainResults.ETHIsFalse $\to$ $\neg$\,ComplexityAbstracts.SubexponentialSATAbstract.ETH}
\end{quote}
Given $\delta>0$, take $m=\lceil 2/\delta\rceil+1$ and a \lean{FinTM2} decider at a rate below $1/m$. The proof has four parts.
(i)~\emph{One step of any program in degree one.} complexitylib already simulates a \lean{TM2} program on the multitape machine in polynomial time, but the degree of that polynomial depends on the program, and here the program is chosen after $\delta$, so the bound would be circular. We prove that a single step of any \lean{TM2} program is computable in degree one, uniformly in the program (\lean{DegreeStep.stepTotalD}).
(ii)~\emph{The run on a unary clock.} The $t$-step run is a degree-two fold over the pair (input, $t$ in unary) (\lean{DegreeRun.runBitD}), tied to Mathlib's \lean{TM2OutputsInTime} (\lean{runBit\_eq}).
(iii)~\emph{The reduction.} A polynomial-time map from \lean{encCNF} to \lean{encClauses 3} relabels the variables densely, removes repeated clauses, and passes $n$ along, so that the decider can write $2^{n/m+c_0}$ in unary and run that many steps (\lean{Words.prep\_polyTime}, \lean{Prep.prep\_spec}: satisfiability and duplicate-freeness preserved).
(iv)~\emph{The rate.} The composed decider runs within $2^{2n/m}\cdot\mathrm{poly}(L)$ steps, and $2/m\le\delta$ (\lean{Assemble.decidableInExpTime}).

\paragraph{Anthropic's chain} The bridge elaborates Anthropic's \lean{MainResults.lean}, byte for byte, against complexitylib's Lean (v4.36.0-rc2) rather than Anthropic's (\EcAnthropicLean). To remove that version boundary from the chain, Anthropic's proof (the import closure of its \lean{Solution.lean}, \EcPortFiles{} files) was ported to the same toolchain: \EcPortSame{} files are byte-identical; \EcPortEdited{} were edited for library renames, missing imports and tactic repairs, each edit listed with both digests in the port's ledger. No edit touches \lean{MainResults.lean}, and every statement changed only by a forced rename is listed. Then
\begin{quote}
\lean{EthBridge.AnthropicChain.not\_eth : $\neg$\,ETH :=}\\
\lean{\ \ not\_eth\_of\_ethIsFalse MainResults.ethIsFalse}
\end{quote}
is checked in one Lean. The ported proof is a modified copy; since Lean checks whatever proof is supplied against the unchanged statement, the edits affect no decided fact, but the proof checked is not Anthropic's bytes.

\paragraph{OpenAI's chain} The same was done on OpenAI's side. The import closure of Hawkley's two bridge files, \EcOaPortFiles{} files (\EcOaOpenAIFiles{} of OpenAI's, byte-equal to \lean{openai/math} at \EcOpenAICommit; \EcOaHawkleyFiles{} of Hawkley's, at \lean{thinkthoughts/math} \EcHawkleyCommit), was ported to the same toolchain: \EcOaPortSame{} files are byte-identical to their pins and \EcOaPortEdited{} were edited for the newer Lean and Mathlib, each edit listed with both digests in the port's ledger. Hawkley's import of \lean{MainResults} is redirected to the vendored copy, so that his second theorem proves the same \lean{ETHIsFalse} the bridge consumes; his two statements, \lean{MainResults.lean} and OpenAI's own challenge file are unchanged. Then
\begin{quote}
\lean{EthBridge.OpenAIChain.not\_eth : $\neg$\,ETH :=}\\
\lean{\ \ not\_eth\_of\_ethIsFalse (not\_positiveRateETH\_implies\_ethIsFalse not\_positiveRateETH)}
\end{quote}
is checked in one Lean. The same remark applies: the proof checked is a modified copy of OpenAI's and Hawkley's bytes, against statements that did not change.

\section{How it was checked}\label{sec:check}

Each trusted statement was written by hand and pinned by digest before any proof was decided (bridge \texttt{\EcCtwoPinSha\dots}, Anthropic's chain \texttt{\EcChainPinSha\dots}, OpenAI's chain \texttt{\EcOaPinSha\dots}). On rented Ubuntu machines, from the published commits (claimbox \EcClaimboxCtwo, \EcClaimboxChain{} and \EcOaClaimbox, on complexitylib \EcComplexitylib), each run (a)~checked the statement block byte for byte against the pin, \lean{MainResults.lean} against Anthropic's published digest and, for a ported proof, every file against its pin or against the port's ledger (byte-equal, or modified exactly as declared, nothing else); (b)~built from source; (c)~printed the axioms from our own file: \lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice} and \lean{Quot.sound} only, for the theorem and its helpers; (d)~exported the theorem with \lean{lean4export} and replayed it in nanoda, a kernel written independently of Lean's, which accepted \EcCtwoNanoda{} declarations under the bridge, \EcChainNanoda{} under Anthropic's chain and \EcOaNanoda{} under OpenAI's; (e)~ran \lean{leanchecker --fresh}; (f)~ran Lean's judge \lean{lake comparator} against a challenge written on the machine from the pin. Three planted variants per theorem had to be refused, and were: the statement closed by \lean{sorry} (shown by the axioms file and refused by nanoda), the statement through a new axiom (likewise), and the statement silently changed (refused by the statement check and rejected by the judge for a mismatch). The judge's sandbox could not start on the rented containers; the judge ran without it, and the records say so.

\paragraph{The judge on the two chains} On the bridge and on Anthropic's chain the judge ran as shipped, \lean{--paranoid}, and Lean's kernel, \lean{leanchecker-paranoid}, \lean{lean4lean}, nanoda, \lean{con-leche} and \lean{con-ron} all accepted (the last two over \EcCtwoJudgeDecls{} and \EcChainJudgeDecls{} declarations; the chain's export is \EcExportGb{}~GB). On OpenAI's chain the export is \EcOaExportGb{}~GB and the judge as shipped did not finish: Lean's paranoid and default kernels accept, and each of the four bundled external checkers stops at a limit of its own, in its own words. The bundled nanoda \EcOaBundledNanoda{} overflows a worker thread's stack, and that build has no stack setting; \lean{lean4lean} stops with ``(kernel) deterministic timeout'', its fuel counter reaching zero; \lean{con-leche} and \lean{con-ron} stop with ``internal error: fuel exhausted: whnfCore'', a compiled constant, which their charter calls a decline, never a verdict. None is the rejection of a declaration. Each stop was reproduced by hand on the comparator's own export, and the verdict reads the judge re-run with named external kernels: \lean{leanchecker-paranoid}, our pinned nanoda (\lean{ammkrn/nanoda\_lib} \EcPinnedNanoda, 16 threads, a 1~GiB thread stack; it accepts the export in \EcOaNanodaPinnedSec{}~s), \lean{lean4lean} with every fuel field raised 100$\times$ (it accepts in \EcOaLeanFourLeanFuelSec{}~s), and Lean's default kernel. That run accepts the chain (\EcOaJudgeMin{}~min), rejects the planted statement on the statement, and accepts OpenAI's own challenge file \lean{ComparatorChallenges/SubexponentialSAT.lean}, unchanged (sha256 \texttt{\EcOaChallengeSha\dots}; its module name in the judge's configuration is the one declared change), against the ported proof of \lean{exact\_count\_and\_sat} in the new Lean (\EcOaPthreeMin{}~min). The record keeps the shipped run beside the named one, and the amendment is dated in the pre-registration. \lean{con-leche} and \lean{con-ron} are not among the checkers that decided OpenAI's chain. After the record, on their maintainer's request, \lean{con-leche} (master \EcOaMastersLeche) and \lean{con-ron} (master \EcOaMastersRon), built from source and run by hand on the same export, accept all \EcOaMastersDecls{} declarations, and all \EcOaMastersChallengeDecls{} of the export of OpenAI's own challenge; \lean{con-leche} needs \EcOaLecheSixteenGib~GiB at 16 workers and \EcOaLecheEightGib~GiB at 8. Their fuel stops are fixed upstream, not yet in a Lean release, and these runs are outside the decision. Every stop of the four bundled checkers was therefore a resource limit or an old build, and none the rejection of a declaration.

\paragraph{Beside the chains} Two further results use the same checks. The library's own proof that its \lean{ETH} is false (commit \EcDoneCommit; nanoda over \EcDoneNanoda{} declarations) is a re-proof on the standard machine, not a bridge from either laboratory's statement. And \lean{EthBridge.WordRAM.not\_eth\_ram} refutes the word-RAM form of the hypothesis, with $O(\log L)$-bit words, from complexitylib's native model counter rather than from either laboratory's statement (nanoda over \EcConeNanoda{} declarations, six checkers): complexitylib's simulator bounds simulated addresses by the running time, which rules out logarithmic words for super-polynomial runs; our variant bounds them by the work space, and the model counter runs in polynomial space. In the records the word-RAM theorem is C1 and the bridge of Section~\ref{sec:bridge} is C2. C1 and the reusable part of C2 are offered upstream: complexitylib pull requests \#2509 and \#2510 (5 November 2026, on the maintainer's invitation in \#739), approved by the library's reviewer agent, which is not human approval; merge pending.

\section{What stays open}\label{sec:limits}

(1)~\emph{The textbook match is a reading.} That complexitylib's \lean{ETH} is the hypothesis of \citet{IP01} is a reading of a definition: the machine, the binary encoding, the parameter $n$ and the polynomial factor in $L$ are standard choices, and any further distance is argued, not proved. (2)~\emph{OpenAI's chain was decided by the judge with a shorter list of checkers.} As shipped, \lean{lake comparator --paranoid} did not finish on OpenAI's chain: its four bundled external checkers stopped at their own resource limits on the \EcOaExportGb{}~GB export (Section~\ref{sec:check}), and the verdict reads the judge re-run with named kernels, four where Anthropic's chain had six. Two of the six, \lean{con-leche} and \lean{con-ron}, finish this export only in builds from their master branches, which accept it (Section~\ref{sec:check}); the full list returns with a Lean release that bundles them. (3)~\emph{The ported proofs are modified copies} (Section~\ref{sec:bridge}). (4)~\emph{The judge ran without its sandbox}, which protects the judge from a hostile build; the builds were the author's. (5)~\emph{One author on both sides.} The statements were written by the author; the proofs, the ports and the corollaries were written by AI agents (Claude, Anthropic) under the author's direction; the checks were run by the author's tooling. Roles are separated, but the same person is on both sides, and no second party has replayed the records; doing so is the next step, and the Data availability statement gives what it needs: for OpenAI's chain, a \EcOaExportMb~MB download and one checker on a 64~GB machine.
