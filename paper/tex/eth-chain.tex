\begin{abstract}
In October 2026 Anthropic published a Lean~4 proof that the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) is false. Its statement, \lean{ETHIsFalse}, is the negation of $s_3>0$ with $s_3$ taken over Mathlib's multi-stack Turing machines (\lean{FinTM2}) reading a custom encoding of duplicate-free formulas over the variables $x_0,\dots,x_{n-1}$; the step from that machine and encoding to a standard model is left to prose. We prove in Lean that \lean{ETHIsFalse} implies ETH is false as stated in the cslib-community library \lean{complexitylib}: for every $\delta>0$ a deterministic multitape Turing machine decides 3-SAT on the plain binary encoding of every formula within $c\cdot 2^{\delta n}\cdot(L+1)^{e}+c$ steps. We then port Anthropic's proof (\EcPortFiles{} files, \EcPortSame{} unchanged, \EcPortEdited{} edited for the newer toolchain) into the same Lean, so that the proof, the statement and the bridge are checked in one environment and no step crosses a version boundary. Every step was checked from pinned bytes by Lean's kernel, by nanoda over \EcChainNanoda{} declarations, by \lean{leanchecker --fresh}, and by Lean's judge \lean{lake comparator --paranoid} with six checkers; \EcPlanted{} planted wrong variants were refused. Whether the formal statement is the textbook ETH remains a reading of a definition. The proofs were written by AI agents directed by the author, who also wrote the checker; this is not an independent review.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is claimed, and what is not}\label{sec:claim}

Two AI laboratories published Lean proofs in October 2026 that the Exponential Time Hypothesis of \citet{IP01} is false \citep{AnthropicETH,OpenAIETH}. Both proofs are accepted by Lean's kernel, which we confirmed independently for both, against their own statements, with a second kernel \citep{CMEth}. The question that readers raised is not whether the proofs check but what they state: each statement is about a machine and an input encoding of its laboratory's making, and the step from there to a standard machine is argued in prose.

This note closes that step for Anthropic's statement. \emph{Claimed:} a Lean theorem, checked as described in Section~\ref{sec:check}, that Anthropic's \lean{ETHIsFalse} implies the negation of ETH as defined over cslib's deterministic multitape Turing machine with a binary encoding of arbitrary formulas; and a second Lean theorem in which Anthropic's own proof of \lean{ETHIsFalse}, ported, supplies the hypothesis in the same environment. \emph{Not claimed:} that ETH is false; the correctness or novelty of either laboratory's algorithm outside its Lean development; that a formal definition is the textbook one, which no machine decides (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\section{The two statements}\label{sec:defs}

\paragraph{Anthropic's} \lean{MainResults.lean} at commit \EcAnthropicCommit{} (Lean \EcAnthropicLean, sha256 \texttt{\EcMainResultsSha\dots}) defines \lean{InTime 3 $\delta$}: some \lean{FinTM2} machine with finite alphabets and a constant $c$ decide, for every $n$ and every 3-CNF $\varphi$ on $x_0,\dots,x_{n-1}$ without repeated clauses, the satisfiability of $\varphi$ within $\lfloor c\,2^{\delta n}\rfloor$ steps, reading \lean{encClauses 3 $\varphi$}. Then $s_3=\inf\{\delta: \text{\lean{InTime 3 $\delta$}}\}$, \lean{ETH} is $0<s_3$, and \lean{ETHIsFalse} is its negation.

\paragraph{complexitylib's} \lean{ETH} is $\exists\,\delta>0,\ \neg\,\text{\lean{DecidableInExpTime (KSAT 3) encCNF numVars $\delta$}}$, where \lean{DecidableInExpTime} asks for one deterministic multitape Turing machine (cslib's \lean{Turing.MultiTapeTM}) and an exponent $e$ such that the machine decides membership in \lean{KSAT 3} within $O(2^{\delta\,n}\,(L+1)^{e})$ steps on the binary encoding \lean{encCNF} of length $L$, for every formula: variable labels are arbitrary natural numbers, and literals and clauses may repeat. This is the form of the hypothesis on a standard machine model with a reasonable binary encoding; its distance from the textbook definition is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\section{The bridge}\label{sec:bridge}

\begin{quote}
\lean{EthBridge.Anthropic.not\_eth\_of\_ethIsFalse : MainResults.ETHIsFalse $\to$ $\neg$\,ComplexityAbstracts.SubexponentialSATAbstract.ETH}
\end{quote}
Given $\delta>0$, take $m=\lceil 2/\delta\rceil+1$ and a \lean{FinTM2} decider at a rate below $1/m$. The proof has four parts.
(i)~\emph{One step of any program in degree one.} complexitylib already simulates a \lean{TM2} program on the multitape machine in polynomial time, but the degree of that polynomial depends on the program, and here the program is chosen after $\delta$, so the bound would be circular. We prove that a single step of any \lean{TM2} program is computable in degree one, uniformly in the program (\lean{DegreeStep.stepTotalD}).
(ii)~\emph{The run on a unary clock.} The $t$-step run is a degree-two fold over the pair (input, $t$ in unary) (\lean{DegreeRun.runBitD}), tied to Mathlib's \lean{TM2OutputsInTime} (\lean{runBit\_eq}).
(iii)~\emph{The reduction.} A polynomial-time map from \lean{encCNF} to \lean{encClauses 3} relabels the variables densely, removes repeated clauses, and passes $n$ along, so that the decider can write $2^{n/m+c_0}$ in unary and run that many steps (\lean{Words.prep\_polyTime}, \lean{Prep.prep\_spec}: satisfiability and duplicate-freeness preserved).
(iv)~\emph{The rate.} The composed decider runs within $2^{2n/m}\cdot\mathrm{poly}(L)$ steps, and $2/m\le\delta$ (\lean{Assemble.decidableInExpTime}).

\paragraph{One environment} The bridge elaborates Anthropic's \lean{MainResults.lean}, byte for byte, against complexitylib's Lean (v4.36.0-rc2) rather than Anthropic's (\EcAnthropicLean). To remove that version boundary from the chain, Anthropic's proof (the import closure of its \lean{Solution.lean}, \EcPortFiles{} files) was ported to the same toolchain: \EcPortSame{} files are byte-identical; \EcPortEdited{} were edited for library renames, missing imports and tactic repairs, each edit listed with both digests in the port's ledger. No edit touches \lean{MainResults.lean}, and every statement changed only by a forced rename is listed. Then
\begin{quote}
\lean{EthBridge.AnthropicChain.not\_eth : $\neg$\,ETH :=}\\
\lean{\ \ not\_eth\_of\_ethIsFalse MainResults.ethIsFalse}
\end{quote}
is checked in one Lean. The ported proof is a modified copy; since Lean checks whatever proof is supplied against the unchanged statement, the edits affect no decided fact, but the proof checked is not Anthropic's bytes.

\section{How it was checked}\label{sec:check}

Each trusted statement was written by hand and pinned by digest before any proof was decided (bridge \texttt{\EcCtwoPinSha\dots}, chain \texttt{\EcChainPinSha\dots}). On a rented Ubuntu machine, from the published commits (claimbox \EcClaimboxCtwo{} and \EcClaimboxChain{}, on complexitylib \EcComplexitylib), each run (a)~checked the statement block byte for byte against the pin and \lean{MainResults.lean} against Anthropic's published digest; (b)~built from source; (c)~printed the axioms from our own file: \lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice} and \lean{Quot.sound} only, for the theorem and its helpers; (d)~exported the theorem with \lean{lean4export} and replayed it in nanoda, a kernel written independently of Lean's, which accepted \EcCtwoNanoda{} declarations under the bridge and \EcChainNanoda{} under the chain; (e)~ran \lean{leanchecker --fresh}; (f)~ran Lean's judge \lean{lake comparator --paranoid} against a challenge written on the machine from the pin, where Lean's kernel, \lean{leanchecker-paranoid}, \lean{lean4lean}, nanoda, \lean{con-leche} and \lean{con-ron} all accepted (the last two over \EcCtwoJudgeDecls{} and \EcChainJudgeDecls{} declarations). Three planted variants per theorem had to be refused, and were: the statement closed by \lean{sorry} (shown by the axioms file and refused by nanoda), the statement through a new axiom (likewise), and the statement silently changed (refused by the statement check and rejected by the judge for a mismatch). The judge's sandbox could not start on the rented containers; the judge ran without it, and the records say so.

\paragraph{Beside the chain} Two further results use the same checks. The library's own proof that its \lean{ETH} is false (commit \EcDoneCommit; nanoda over \EcDoneNanoda{} declarations) is a re-proof on the standard machine, not a bridge from either laboratory's statement. And ETH is false on the word RAM with $O(\log L)$-bit words (\lean{EthBridge.WordRAM.not\_eth\_ram}, nanoda over \EcConeNanoda{} declarations, six checkers): complexitylib's simulator bounds simulated addresses by the running time, which rules out logarithmic words for super-polynomial runs; our variant bounds them by the work space, and complexitylib's model counter runs in polynomial space. On OpenAI's side, a third party's Lean bridge \citep{Hawkley26} proves that OpenAI's theorem implies \lean{ETHIsFalse} inside OpenAI's Lean (v4.34.1); we replayed it with two kernels \citep{CMEth}.

\section{What stays open}\label{sec:limits}

(1)~\emph{The textbook match is a reading.} That complexitylib's \lean{ETH} is the hypothesis of \citet{IP01} is a reading of a definition: the machine, the binary encoding, the parameter $n$ and the polynomial factor in $L$ are standard choices, and any further distance is argued, not proved. (2)~\emph{OpenAI's side crosses one version boundary.} OpenAI's statement reaches \lean{ETHIsFalse} in OpenAI's Lean; that the copy there and the one here are the same proposition is read, not decided; a port of OpenAI's proof, as done here for Anthropic's, would close it. (3)~\emph{The ported proof is a modified copy} (Section~\ref{sec:bridge}). (4)~\emph{The judge ran without its sandbox}, which protects the judge from a hostile build; the build was the author's. (5)~\emph{One author.} The statements were written by the author; the proofs and the port were written by AI agents (Claude, Anthropic) under the author's direction; the checks were run by the author's tooling. Roles are separated, but no second party has replayed the records; doing so is the next step, and the Data availability statement gives what it needs.
