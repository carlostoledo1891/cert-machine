\begin{abstract}
In September 2026 an autonomous multi-agent research system published new lower bounds for the kissing number in \MtKwRows{} dimensions (\MtKwDims), each a configuration of integer or closed-form points, each proved by moment identities and structure, and each released with a verification file that records that the pairwise inner products were not checked. Every pair was decided here. A kissing configuration is a finite set of points with one inequality per pair, and with integer coordinates the inequality is exact: for rows of squared norms $A$ and $B$ with inner product $p$, the angle is at least sixty degrees if and only if $p\le0$ or $4p^2\le AB$. The products were formed in floating-point hardware, exact under a Cauchy--Schwarz bound asserted on the data before any path ran and, for the GPU path, under the stated assumption on tensor-core accumulation, characterised for earlier devices than the one rented; in dimensions 25 and 27, whose coordinates are integer combinations of $1$, $\sqrt2$, $\sqrt3$ and $\sqrt6$, the pairs a floating-point screen could not separate were decided in arbitrary-precision integers over the field. All \MtKwRows{} configurations hold: $\MtKwPairsSci$ pairs decided, $\MtKwContactsSci$ of them at exactly sixty degrees and none closer; every count matches the published one and every configuration whose span is recorded spans exactly $d$ dimensions. The seven largest, about $\MtKwGpuPairsEachSci$ pairs each, ran on one rented \MtKwGpuName{} for \MtKwGpuHours{} GPU-hours after a calibration that reproduced the laptop's exact contact counts in two smaller dimensions; a seeded sample of every configuration was re-decided in plain integers by a program that shares no code with the bulk path. The claimant published its points within days of the request, linked the check beside its results table and, on \QkCreditDate, credited the verification in both of its READMEs. One outside party has re-run the kissing ledger with the tools that write it; no outside party has re-decided these configurations with its own code. A configuration that holds proves a lower bound and nothing more; the field's table lists higher values in dimensions \MtKwCohnHigher, from constructions whose points have not been published.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The kissing number $K(d)$ is the largest number of non-overlapping unit spheres that can touch a central unit sphere in $\R^d$. It is known exactly in dimensions 1 to 4, 8 and 24 \citep{Leech67,OdlyzkoSloane79,Musin08,ConwaySloane99}; elsewhere it is bracketed by constructions below and by linear and semidefinite programming above \citep{BachocVallentin08,MittelmannVallentin10,LeijenhorstDeLaat24}, and the lower bounds have moved more in the last two years than in the preceding four decades. Cohn and Li raised dimensions 17 through 21 by altering signs in Leech-lattice vectors \citep{CohnLi24}, and a binary code raised dimension 19 again \citep{Ho26}; Ganzhinov's highly symmetric line systems set records in dimensions 10, 11 and 14 \citep{Ganzhinov22}; a language-model-guided program search found a 593-point configuration in dimension 11 \citep{Novikov25,GGSWT25}, and that dimension then moved twice more, each time by a machine, to 604 \citep[the dimension-eleven paper]{CMArchive}. In June 2026 a numerical 841-point arrangement was reported in dimension 12 \citep{TakhanovAssylbekovYun26}, and in September three parties published bounds between dimensions 18 and 55 within weeks of each other \citep{Kravatskiy26,TakhanovYun26,Yang26}. In high dimensions the asymptotic lower bound was improved by a factor linear in $d$ \citep{JenssenJoosPerkins18,FKLP21}.

The largest of the September releases is the one this paper decides: the Qiushi Engine, an autonomous multi-agent research system, published \MtKwRows{} new lower bounds in dimensions \MtKwDims{} \citep{Yang26}. Each bound is a configuration built from lattices, codes, sections and projections; its size is proved by moment identities and the angular condition by structure; and the release's own verification files record, for every configuration they cover, that the pairwise inner products were not checked. Whether every pair of a finite set of points satisfies one inequality is a finite computation that a party with no stake in the result can make from the published bytes.

Every pair of every configuration was decided exactly, by programs that share no code with the claimant, from files pinned by the digests the claimant's manifest and the hosting platform report. Two configurations were published with the paper as exact objects and were decided at once; the other seventeen had no published points and were recorded as \vN{} with the missing files named, a refusal that the claimant answered within days by publishing the points, in exactly the form the check needed. Every one of the \MtKwRows{} bounds holds. The contribution of this paper is not a bound---no bound is improved---but the decision: what was read, how each pair was decided exactly in floating-point hardware, what the second implementation and the calibration do and do not establish, what it cost, what the claimant did next, and what has come back from outside. It is a working companion to the cert-machine method paper \citep[the method paper]{CMArchive}, whose grammar of three verdicts with witnesses it applies to one kind of claim; the public register holds its rows \citep{Register}. Section~\ref{sec:materials} gives the materials, Section~\ref{sec:method} the method, Section~\ref{sec:results} the results, Section~\ref{sec:discussion} the discussion and the limits, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} concludes; \ref{app:repro} lists the command that re-derives each result.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The reading}\label{sec:reading}
In the grammar of the method paper a claim is a pair of a domain and a predicate, read universally or epistemically; a kissing configuration is the universal case at its simplest. The domain is the finite set of pairs of distinct rows; the predicate is $\langle x,y\rangle\le\tfrac12|x|\,|y|$. With integer coordinates the predicate is exact. For rows of squared norms $A$ and $B$ with inner product $p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:test}
\angle(x,y)\ge60^\circ\iff p\le0\ \text{ or }\ 4p^2\le AB,
\end{equation}
and a contact, at exactly sixty degrees, is $4p^2=AB$ with $p>0$. The same test rejects a repeated or parallel row, since then $p^2=AB$ with $p>0$, so distinctness of the directions is part of the pair test; a zero row is rejected separately, by its norm. The rows are first grouped by squared norm, the counts per norm compared with the claimant's, and the verdict is \vW{} if every pair satisfies \eqref{eq:test} and every count matches, \vword{unwitnessed} otherwise, with what failed; the row count, the norms and the span are facts recorded beside the verdict.

\subsection{The arithmetic of the pair test}\label{sec:arith}
Every inner product is an integer, or an integer combination of the field's basis, and three paths form them. Each path is exact under a condition stated once, checked against the data before the path runs and, since 2026-10-21, asserted in the code, so that a configuration outside the bound cannot be decided by that path at all; for the GPU path the condition is the bound together with an assumption on the hardware, stated with it below.

\emph{Single-precision BLAS on the laptop.} A product of integer vectors is an integer, and floating-point hardware computes it exactly when no partial sum can leave the range the format holds exactly---$2^{24}$ for single precision. By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality every partial sum of products is at most $|x|\,|y|$, at most the largest squared norm in the configuration, which is \MtKwDeskMaxNorm{} at most (dimension \MtKwDeskMaxNormDim) on the laptop. The tiles are therefore exact integers, and the test \eqref{eq:test} is applied to them as integers: $p\le t(A,B)=\lfloor\sqrt{AB}/2\rfloor$, with the threshold computed by exact integer square root. The script asserts the bound below $2^{24}$ before the float32 path runs.

\emph{Dimension 38.} Its \MtKwAxisRows{} rotated-axis rows have squared norm $\MtKwAxisNormSci$, a \MtKwAxisDigits-digit integer, beside \MtKwAxisOther{} for the other rows, so three parts are decided three ways: the small rows against each other in float32 BLAS under the bound \MtKwAxisOther{} (\QkDThirtyEightSmallPairs{} pairs); the small rows against the axes in 64-bit integers, every partial sum bounded by $\sqrt{48N}<2^{63}$ (\QkDThirtyEightMixedPairs{} pairs, no contact); and the axes against each other in Python's arbitrary-precision integers (\QkDThirtyEightAxisPairs{} pairs, \QkDThirtyEightAxisContacts{} contacts).

\emph{A fused GPU kernel.} The configurations of dimensions 49 to 55 are signed 8-bit rows of \QkGpuColumnsMin{} to \QkGpuColumnsMax{} integer columns with squared norms \QkGpuNorms{} and coordinates at most \QkGpuMaxCoord{} in size. A Triton kernel casts the rows to half precision in registers, forms the products on the tensor cores with single-precision accumulation, and reduces each $128\times128$ block to its largest inner product and its counts; the host applies \eqref{eq:test} per pair of norm blocks. The path is exact under the Cauchy--Schwarz bound asserted on the data and under one assumption on tensor-core accumulation: that every product and every partial sum, each an integer of magnitude at most \MtKwGpuMaxNorm, is held exactly at every stage. Half precision holds every integer below $2^{11}$ exactly and single precision every integer below $2^{24}$ \citep{IEEE754}. The block fused multiply-add of tensor cores is documented to form the products exactly and to accumulate them in single precision \citep{Blanchard20,HighamMary22}, and the alignment and truncation of partial products that \citet{Fasi21} measured on Volta, Turing and Ampere devices, and that a formal model of those three generations reproduces \citep{Valpey25}, discard bits only below the unit of the largest aligned operand, which for integers of this size is never a bit of an integer. That characterisation is of earlier devices than the one rented. For the rented device the assumption rests on the documented accumulation and on the calibration of Section~\ref{sec:calibration}, which checks it and does not prove it. The script asserts the bound below $2^{11}$ before any GPU path runs. What the argument does not cover is stated in Section~\ref{sec:threats}.

\emph{Closed forms in dimensions 25 and 27.} The claimant's two constructions state their points in closed form, as combinations such as $\sqrt3/2+\sqrt2/4$; after scaling, every coordinate is an integer combination of $1$, $\sqrt2$, $\sqrt3$ and $\sqrt6$, and so is every inner product. The engine screens the pairs in single-precision tiles under the same Cauchy--Schwarz bound and decides every pair the screen cannot separate by a proved margin---\MtKwObjFallbacks{} pairs in dimension 25---in arbitrary-precision integers over $\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$, where the sign of $4p^2-AB$ is decided exactly and a tie raises rather than returns a sign (a red control forces one). The \QkDTwentyFiveObjectRows{} rational caps and repair points of dimension 25 are decided by the exact path throughout.

\subsection{Span and counts}\label{sec:span}
That the rows span exactly $d$ dimensions is decided by the rank over $\Q$ of the exact Gram matrix $X^{\mathsf T}X$, summed in 64-bit integer chunks, which equals the rank of $X$. For the sections of dimensions 43 and 45---\QkSectionColumns-column rows of squared norm \QkDFortyThreeNorm{} and \QkDFortyFiveNorm---the rank of a sample is computed by exact rational elimination and every row is shown orthogonal to the \QkDFortyThreeNull{} and \QkDFortyFiveNull{} integer vectors spanning the sample's null space; in dimensions 25 and 27 the span is not recorded. For every configuration the row count and, where the claimant states them, the counts per squared norm are compared with the claimant's.

\subsection{The sections in Gram form}\label{sec:gram}
Dimensions 43 and 45 were decided twice. Once in Cartesian form, as above. Once in Gram form, from the claimant's published lattice data, read as data and never run: the Gram matrix $G$ of the parent lattice, its integer basis $B$ with $\QkDFortyThreeBasisIdentity$ for dimension 43, and the section conditions the construction defines. Every Cartesian row is written as an integer combination of the basis (a rounded solve, then the identity checked exactly for every row), the combination is shown to satisfy the section conditions, and every pair is decided in Gram form; the counts---pairs, contacts, the nearest non-contact as a cosine---must equal the Cartesian record's. They do. This is a second reading of the same bytes in the claimant's own terms, and it checks that the points are the set the construction defines, which the Cartesian decision alone does not.

\subsection{The second implementation}\label{sec:second}
Every bulk path is checked by a program that shares no code with it. A seeded sample of \QkSampleRowsMin{} to \QkSampleRowsMax{} rows of each configuration, together with every row of the blocks of at most two hundred rows, has all its pairs decided in plain Python integers with the test \eqref{eq:test} written out---no numpy, no BLAS, no GPU---and its contacts and violations must equal the bulk path's counts on the same rows. The sample is written into the record and decided a third time by a JavaScript reading over arbitrary-precision integers, the rows as strings, on the laptop. For the two closed-form constructions the JavaScript sample is decided by a separate basis reading; in dimension 25 that reading excludes the families \QkDTwentyFiveJsExcluded, so its \QkDTwentyFiveJsRows{} rows and \QkDTwentyFiveJsPairs{} pairs leave out the rows that make the bound new, and the Python engine's own sample of \QkDTwentyFiveSpotPairs{} pairs is the only second reading that covers them. These are N-version checks, level~2 of the method paper's five levels of evidence: written by the same author, with AI agents, from one specification, they catch a slip in one arithmetic and cannot catch a misreading the two share \citep{KnightLeveson86}, and they decide samples, not the configurations.

\subsection{Calibration}\label{sec:calibration}
No new path was used for a decision before it had reproduced a decision already made by an established one. The wave runner re-derives $K(8)=240$ from the roots of $E_8$ and $K(24)=196{,}560$ from the whole Leech shell, every pair, at the start of every run (\QkCalibrations{} calibrations), and its \QkRedControls{} red controls---a sign flipped, a point rotated by thirty degrees, a row listed twice, a coordinate moved off the sphere, a forced tie---must each be caught before a row is written. The GPU kernel was required to return the laptop's exact contact counts, \MtKwCalContacts, on the configurations of dimensions 32 and 39 (\QkCalSeconds{} seconds) before it was allowed to decide the seven large ones; and the pre-registration of the paid run stated beforehand what would be measured, what counted as a result, and at what cost the run stopped.

\subsection{Reruns by other parties}\label{sec:outside}
One outside party, \QkRerunWho, re-derived the kissing ledger (\texttt{certs/kissing-ledger.json}) together with two other ledgers of the repository, \QkRerunLedgers{} in all, with the tools that write them, at commit \texttt{\QkRerunCommit} on \QkRerunDate{} (\QkRerunEnv), and reported every verdict and count identical on the repository's public thread (\url{\QkRerunUrl}). The tool that writes the kissing ledger does not re-decide the pairs of this paper's configurations. It re-reads their record against the wave's pinned manifest, requires every calibration to hold and every red control to have fired, accounts for all $n(n-1)/2$ pairs of every row and for its count against the claim, requires the recorded second readings and the Gram-form re-decisions to agree with the bulk path, and re-decides live its own small calibrations (the $D_4$ and $E_8$ root systems and the icosahedron) and one smaller configuration of another claimant. In the method paper's count this is level~1: the same code, re-run from pinned inputs on another machine, which catches a record that does not follow from its inputs and cannot catch a defect in the code. The same party re-decided \QkRerunOwnRows{} dimension-\QkRerunOwnDim{} configurations of the kissing ledger with its own exact code on \QkRerunOwnDate{} (level~3 for those rows), none of them this paper's. No outside party has re-decided the Qiushi configurations with its own code.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the results show} No bound was found false. The gap was between what was claimed and what was checked: a pairwise condition proved by structure and never checked pair by pair, closed by a finite computation of a few hours on a laptop and a few GPU-hours. The claimant's checker stopped where the claim exceeded it, and said so; the decision here was made by programs the claimant did not write, from the published bytes. The grammar records three things a private confirmation would not: a \vW{} with its record can be cited, a \vN{} that names its files can be answered, and the scope---each configuration, not the record in its dimension---travels with the verdict.

\paragraph{Relation to the literature} Independent re-decision is routine for the exact cases of the kissing problem, whose proofs are checked by reading \citep{Musin08} or by formalisation, and for upper bounds, where the semidefinite programs of \citet{BachocVallentin08} are solved to high accuracy with the dual solution serving as a certificate \citep{MittelmannVallentin10,LeijenhorstDeLaat24}. For lower bounds the certificate is the configuration itself, and the field's convention has been to trust the construction's proof of the angular condition. The machine-generated releases of 2025 and 2026 \citep{Novikov25,Yang26} changed the size of the objects and the way they are proved---by search and by structure, with the pairwise check left to the reader---and the exercise here is the reader's check made explicit, at a cost measured in hours and a few dollars. The exactness of integer products in floating-point hardware under a Cauchy--Schwarz bound is elementary; what the tensor-core literature supplies is the characterisation of what the hardware does with aligned partial products \citep{Fasi21,Blanchard20,FasiMultiword23,Valpey25,KhattakMikaitis25}, which the argument of Section~\ref{sec:arith} needs only in its weakest form.

\paragraph{Threats to validity}\label{sec:threats}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Selection.} The release was chosen because it had finite objects to decide and had said that it had not decided them. The result describes these \MtKwRows{} configurations, not machine-generated mathematics at large.
\item \emph{The author in the outcomes.} The points were published, the check linked and the credit given after a request the author made. They are reported as what happened, and no causal claim rests on them.
\item \emph{The second implementation.} The plain-integer and JavaScript readings are N-version checks by the same author from one specification, on samples; in dimension 25 the JavaScript sample leaves out the rows that make the bound new. They are counted apart from a re-derivation by another party. The one outside rerun of the kissing ledger used the tools that write it (level~1, Section~\ref{sec:outside}); no other party has re-decided these configurations with its own code.
\item \emph{The GPU.} The exactness of the seven GPU rows rests on the Cauchy--Schwarz bound asserted on the data and on the stated assumption on tensor-core accumulation, characterised for earlier devices than the one rented. \citet{Fasi21} characterised Volta, Turing and Ampere tensor cores, and the formal model of \citet{Valpey25} covers the same three generations; the pod's \MtKwGpuName{} belongs to a later one, which an architecture-independent test scheme has since been applied to \citep{KhattakMikaitis25}, but not by us. For this device the assumption rests on the documented single-precision accumulation of the block fused multiply-add \citep{Blanchard20,HighamMary22}, and the assertion that every partial sum is an integer below $2^{11}$ is checked before the run; the calibration reproduced the laptop's exact contact counts in dimensions 32 and 39 on this device. The calibration is a check, not a proof: a device that held integers below $2^{11}$ inexactly would fail it, and one that failed only on inputs the calibration did not exercise would not. Re-deciding dimensions 49 to 55 with integer matrix multiplication, or characterising the device, would close the gap; neither has been done.
\item \emph{The span of 25 and 27.} It is not recorded; the row counts and the pair test are.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What is not claimed} A configuration that holds proves a lower bound. It says nothing about optimality, about any upper bound, about the record in its dimension, or about the other steps of the discovery process that produced it; the moment identities and the structural proofs of the paper were not read for this exercise and are not decided by it. ``Independent'' means independent of the claimant: no program of the claimant's was run, and the lattice data of Section~\ref{sec:gram} were read as data. Nothing is a probability.

\section{Conclusions and recommendations}\label{sec:conclusions}

Every one of the \MtKwRows{} kissing-number bounds of \citet{Yang26} holds, pair by pair: $\MtKwPairsSci$ pairs decided exactly from the published bytes, $\MtKwContactsSci$ contacts and no violation, for \MtKwDeskSeconds{} seconds of a laptop and \$\MtKwGpuCost{} of one GPU. The decision required the claimant's points and its statement of what it had not checked. Its cost is that of the pairs: exactness in floating-point hardware costs one inequality per path, asserted before the run, and, on the GPU, an assumption on the hardware that the calibration checks and does not prove.

To producers of machine-generated constructions: publish the object, not only its description---the points in one integer form, with a digest beside each file---since the finite check can be made only once the bytes exist; and state the scope of every check in the release itself, as the release's own ``pairwise inner products checked: false'' did. To those who maintain the tables of a field: distinguish a bound whose configuration has been decided from outside from one that has only been constructed, and say what moves a bound from the second class to the first.
