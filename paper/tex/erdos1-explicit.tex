\begin{abstract}
Erd\H{o}s asked (1931) whether a set $A\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}$ whose $2^{|A|}$ subset sums are
distinct must satisfy $N\gg 2^{|A|}$. The best construction for twenty-eight years was
$N\le 0.22002\cdot 2^{|A|}$ (Bohman, 1998). On 28 August 2026 a pre-release GPT-6 Astra,
run autonomously by Epoch AI, produced a Lean-verified disproof: for every
$\varepsilon>0$ there are such sets with $N\le\varepsilon 2^{|A|}$. The argument is
ineffective at exactly one step, the approximation of a rational lattice by a primitive
one, and no explicit set below Bohman's constant was known. We make that step effective
by replacing the Smith normal form in the formal proof with a Hermite basis and a chain
perturbation, and we compute the one quantity the proof never computed, the buffer
$K$ that controls the perturbation. The result is a family of explicit dissociated sets
with certificates that an independent program checks in exact arithmetic: the smallest
has $\SmallN$ elements with $N/2^{|A|}=\SmallRatio$, and the best has $\Nn$ elements
with $N/2^{|A|}=\Ratio$, that is \Gain\% below Bohman's constant. Two further results
ride along. The base gadget $T=I+\tfrac12P$ of the construction is not special: $T=I+\alpha P$
works for every $0<\alpha<1$ (the cube property has a three-line proof for all tilts, and the
strip property is decided exactly per $(\alpha,b)$ by a finite structured search), and the
best tilt beats $\tfrac12$ at every dimension budget. And the base weights give the first
explicit lower bounds for the constant in Siegel's lemma above Bohman's,
$C_{\SiegelBestD}\ge\SiegelBest$ in the normalisation of Bloom's exposition.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Write $f(n)=\inf\max A/2^{n-1}$ over dissociated sets $A\subset\Z_{>0}$ with $|A|=n$
(Bloom's normalisation) and $\rho(A)=\max A/2^{|A|}=f/2$ for a particular set. Powers of two
give $\rho=1/2$; Conway and Guy give $\rho\le 1/4$ for large $n$; Bohman \cite{Bo98}
gives $\rho\le 0.22002$ for all large $n$. From below, $N\ge\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$
\cite{DFX21}. Erd\H{o}s's question, whether $\rho$ is bounded below by a positive constant,
was answered in the negative by a machine \cite{Epoch26,Erdos1repo}: a Lean 4 proof, checked
against the Formal Conjectures statement with the three standard axioms,
that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there are arbitrarily large $n$ with $f(n)<\varepsilon$.

Bloom's exposition \cite{Bloom26} reads the proof as a statement about lattices and
isolates its single non-effective step. The present note is the effective version of that
step, and the objects it produces. Everything quantitative in it is interpolated from a
ledger of certificates that an independent verifier re-derived; no number is typed.

\section{The lattice, with a tilt}

Fix odd $b\ge3$ and a rational $0<\alpha<1$, and let $V_m=\{x\in\R^m:\sum x_i=0\}$. Let
$T_\alpha:\R^b\to\R^b$, $(T_\alpha z)_i=z_i+\alpha z_{i+1}$ (indices mod $b$), and
\[
\Lambda_1=T_\alpha(\Z^b\cap V_b),\qquad v_1=T_\alpha(e_0)=(1,0,\dots,0,\alpha),\qquad h_1=\textstyle\sum v_1=1+\alpha .
\]
Bloom's gadget is $\alpha=\tfrac12$. For $s\ge1$ define, in $\R^{b^{s+1}}$ written as $b$ blocks of size $d=b^s$,
\[
\Lambda_{s+1}=\{(\lambda_0+\beta_0v_s,\dots,\lambda_{b-1}+\beta_{b-1}v_s):\lambda_j\in\Lambda_s,\ \beta\in\Lambda_1\},
\qquad v_{s+1}=(v_s,0,\dots,0,\alpha v_s).
\]
Call a pair $(\Lambda,v)$, $\Lambda\subset V_d$ of full rank and $v\notin V_d$, \emph{admissible} if
$\Lambda\cap(-1,1)^d=\{0\}$ and $tv\in\Lambda+(-1,1)^d$ implies $|t|<1$.

\begin{lemma}[cube, all tilts]\label{lem:cubealpha}
For every $0<\alpha<1$ and odd $b$, $T_\alpha(\Z^b)\cap(-1,1)^b=\{0\}$; in particular $\Lambda_1\cap(-1,1)^b=\{0\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $z\in\Z^b\setminus\{0\}$ with $|z_i+\alpha z_{i+1}|<1$ for all $i$ (indices mod $b$), and let $m=\max_i|z_i|\ge1$ be attained at $i$. Then $\alpha|z_{i+1}|>|z_i|-1=m-1$, so $|z_{i+1}|>(m-1)/\alpha\ge m-1$, hence $|z_{i+1}|=m$, with the opposite sign (if $z_i=m$ then $\alpha z_{i+1}<1-m\le0$). Around the odd cycle this gives $z_i=-z_i$, a contradiction. The same inequality at the maximum gives $(1-\alpha)m<1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[strip, structure]\label{lem:stripalpha}
Let $x_0\in\R$ and $x_1,\dots,x_{b-1}\in\Z$, not all zero, satisfy $|x_i+\alpha x_{i+1}|<1$ for all $i$ (cyclic, $x_b=x_0$), and let $m=\max_{1\le i\le b-1}|x_i|$. Then $m<1/(1-\alpha)$ and $|x_{b-1}|=m$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If the largest index attaining $m$ were $i^*\le b-2$, the argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:cubealpha} would give $|x_{i^*+1}|=m$ with $i^*+1\le b-1$. If $m\ge1/(1-\alpha)$, then either some $i\le b-2$ attains $m$, contradicting $(1-\alpha)m<1$, or only $x_{b-1}$ does, and then $|x_0|>(m-1)/\alpha$ and $|x_1|>(|x_0|-1)/\alpha>(m-1-\alpha)/\alpha^2$, which with $|x_1|\le m$ forces $m(1-\alpha^2)<1+\alpha$.
\end{proof}

The strip property of $(\Lambda_1,v_1)$, $|\sum_ix_i|<1$ for every such configuration, is then decided exactly for each $(\alpha,b)$ by a finite search that Lemma~\ref{lem:stripalpha} makes small: for each $m<1/(1-\alpha)$ and each sign of $x_{b-1}$, enumerate the integer chains $x_{b-2},\dots,x_1$ backwards under $|x_j|\le m$ and $|x_j+\alpha x_{j+1}|<1$, intersect the two constraints on $x_0$, and test whether the open interval for $t=\sum_ix_i$ leaves $(-1,1)$. The search agrees with a brute-force decision of both properties for $b\le9$ (and is re-run against it for $b\le7$ at every build of the repository), and it admits every $(\alpha,b)$ used below. For $\alpha=\tfrac12$ this is Bloom's case analysis, formalised in Lean as \texttt{composeMatrix\_admissible} \cite{Erdos1repo}.

\begin{lemma}[Bloom \cite{Bloom26}]\label{lem:cube}
If $(\Lambda_1,v_1)$ is admissible then $(\Lambda_s,v_s)$ is admissible for every $s\ge1$. In particular $\Lambda_s\cap(-1,1)^{b^s}=\{0\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $y\in\Lambda_{s+1}\cap(-1,1)^{bd}$. Block $j$ of $y$ lies in
$(\Lambda_s+\beta_jv_s)\cap(-1,1)^d$, so $|\beta_j|<1$ by admissibility of $(\Lambda_s,v_s)$;
hence $\beta\in\Lambda_1\cap(-1,1)^b=\{0\}$, hence every block lies in
$\Lambda_s\cap(-1,1)^d=\{0\}$. The strip property for $(\Lambda_{s+1},v_{s+1})$ follows in the
same way with $\gamma=\beta+tv_1$ in place of $\beta$, since $v_{s+1}$ is $v_1\otimes v_s$.
\end{proof}

Let $r=d-1$ and let $B_s\in\Q^{r\times r}$ hold the first $r$ coordinates of a basis of
$\Lambda_s$ as columns; then $\Lambda_s=\lift(B_s)\Z^r$ with
$\lift(B)=\binom{B}{-\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}B}$, and, with $h=1+\alpha$ and $c=|\det T_\alpha|=1+\alpha^b$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta}
\Delta_s(\alpha,b):=|\det B_s|=\frac{(1+\alpha^b)^{(b^s-1)/(b-1)}}{(1+\alpha)^s},
\end{equation}
which is verified exactly against a determinant for small $(b,s)$ at every build and for every instance below. Bloom shows
$f(n)\le\Delta_s(1+o(1))$ along a sequence of $n$; the $o(1)$ is where effectiveness is lost.

\section{The effective transfer}

Let $D=q^s$ for $\alpha=p/q$, a common denominator of $B_s$, and $A=DB_s\in\Z^{r\times r}$.

\paragraph{Hermite basis.} Let $H\in\Z^{r\times r}$ be upper triangular with positive diagonal
and $H\Z^r=A'\Z^r$, where $A'$ is $A$ with its rows reversed. (PARI's \texttt{mathnfmod} applied to $A'$
with $|\det A|=D^r\Delta_s$ as modulus takes 19 seconds at $r=728$ and 328 seconds at $r=2196$; FLINT's generic
routine takes 53 minutes at $r=728$, and the two agree.) Reversing coordinates $0,\dots,r-1$ and
changing basis do not affect Lemma~\ref{lem:cube}, so with $F=H/D$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CA}
\norm{\lift(F)z}_\infty\ge1\qquad\text{for every }z\in\Z^r\setminus\{0\}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Chain perturbation.} Let $\operatorname{top}(X)$ append a zero row to $X$ and let
$\mathrm{sh}\in\Z^{(r+1)\times r}$ have ones at positions $(i+1,i)$. For an integer $t\ge1$ set
\[
C=\operatorname{top}(tH)-\mathrm{sh},\qquad
S=\begin{pmatrix}I_r&0\\-\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M=SC=t\,\lift(H)-E,\quad E:=S\,\mathrm{sh}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[saturation]\label{lem:sat}
$M\Z^r$ is a saturated sublattice of $\Z^{r+1}$. Consequently, if $a\in\Z^{r+1}$ is primitive
with $a^{\mathsf T}M=0$, then $\{c\in\Z^{r+1}:a\cdot c=0\}=M\Z^r$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Delete row $0$ of $C$. Entry $(i',j)$ of the remaining $r\times r$ matrix is
$tH_{i'+1,j}-[j=i']$; since $H$ is upper triangular this vanishes for $j<i'$ and equals $-1$
for $j=i'$. The minor is unimodular, so the gcd of the maximal minors of $C$ is $1$ and
$C\Z^r$ is saturated. $S\in GL_{r+1}(\Z)$ maps saturated lattices to saturated lattices.
\end{proof}

The primitive normal is explicit: $w_0=1$, $w_{j+1}=t\sum_{i\le j}w_iH_{ij}$ for
$j=0,\dots,r-1$ gives $w^{\mathsf T}C=0$, and $a=S^{-\mathsf T}w$, i.e.\ $a_i=w_i+w_r$ for
$i<r$ and $a_r=w_r$. Since $a_0-a_r=w_0=1$ the vector is primitive. This is \texttt{transferWeights} of the Lean proof with the Smith
normal form replaced by the Hermite form; the leading term is $a_r=t^r\det H+\dots$.

\paragraph{The buffer.} $E$ is fixed and small: its rows are $0$, $e_0^{\mathsf T},\dots,e_{r-2}^{\mathsf T}$
and $(-1,\dots,-1,+1)$. Put
\[
K:=D\cdot\norm{EH^{-1}}_{\infty\to\infty},
\]
so that for every real $z$,
$\norm{Ez}_\infty\le\norm{EH^{-1}}\,\norm{Hz}_\infty=K\norm{Fz}_\infty\le K\norm{\lift(F)z}_\infty$.
This is the one quantity the ineffective proof leaves existential (\texttt{exists\_perturbation\_buffer} in the Lean); it is an exact rational, one per lattice.

\begin{lemma}[relation gap]\label{lem:gap}
Let $Q=2^k$ and $t=\lceil(Q+K)/D\rceil$. If $c\in\Z^{r+1}$, $\norm c_\infty<Q$ and $a\cdot c=0$, then $c=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:sat}, $c=Mz$ with $z\in\Z^r$. If $z\ne0$ then by \eqref{eq:CA} and the buffer
inequality
$\norm c_\infty\ge Dt\norm{\lift(F)z}_\infty-K\norm{\lift(F)z}_\infty\ge Dt-K\ge Q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[binary blocks; Lean \texttt{exists\_binary\_block\_set}]\label{lem:bin}
If $a\in\Z_{>0}^{r+1}$ satisfies the conclusion of Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} for $Q=2^k$, then
$\mathcal A=\{a_i2^j:0\le i\le r,\ 0\le j<k\}$ has $(r+1)k$ distinct elements and distinct subset sums.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Two subsets with equal sums give $\sum_ia_ic_i=0$ with
$c_i=\sum_{j\in S_i}2^j-\sum_{j\in S_i'}2^j\in(-2^k,2^k)$, hence $c=0$, hence $S_i=S_i'$ by
uniqueness of binary expansions. Distinctness of elements is the case of two coefficients $\pm2^j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For $b=\Bval$, $s=\Sval$, $\alpha=\AlphaVal$ ($d=\Dim$, $r=\Rval$, $D=\Dval$, $\Delta_s=\DeltaVal$) and
$k=\Kk$ (so $t=\Tval$, $K=\Kval$), the set $\mathcal A$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:bin} is a
dissociated set of $n=\Nn$ positive integers, $\mathcal A\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}$ with
$N=2^{k-1}\max_ia_i$ of $\Ndigits$ decimal digits, and
\[
\frac{N}{2^{n}}=\Ratio<0.22002,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad f(\NnRaw)\le\RatioF<0.44004 .
\]
For $b=\SmallB$, $s=\SmallS$, $\alpha=\SmallAlpha$ ($d=\SmallD$, $K=\SmallKval$) and $k=\SmallK$ the same construction gives a dissociated set of
$n=\SmallN$ integers with $N/2^n=\SmallRatio$, the smallest set below Bohman's constant in this family.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemmas~\ref{lem:cubealpha}--\ref{lem:bin} with the exact values recorded in the certificates; the strip property of
$(\Lambda_1,v_1)$ for these $(\alpha,b)$ is decided by the structured search. The ratio is $\max_ia_i/2^{kr+1}$, computed exactly.
\end{proof}

Two programs are provided. \texttt{build.py} constructs; \texttt{verify.py} rebuilds
$\Lambda_s$ from $(b,s,\alpha)$ and checks, independently of the construction's intermediate objects:
that $H\Z^r=A'\Z^r$ (exact rational solves, integrality both ways); that the minor of
$C=S^{-1}M$ has determinant $\pm1$ (a FLINT determinant, not the triangular argument);
that the FLINT nullspace of $M^{\mathsf T}$ is one-dimensional with primitive generator $a$;
that the recorded $K$ is at least the recomputed operator norm; that $Dt-K\ge2^k$;
positivity, distinctness, $n$, $N$ and the ratio. The inputs not recomputed at full
size are Lemma~\ref{lem:cubealpha} and the strip decision, which are elementary, and the iteration Lemma~\ref{lem:cube}, formalised in Lean for
$\alpha=\tfrac12$; all are checked exhaustively for $d\le9$, and at $d=729$ a falsification probe tested
18,612 lattice vectors (every basis vector, sums and differences of neighbouring pairs, and random
small integer combinations) and found none inside the open cube. Everything is exact: no floating
point enters any certificate. Every certificate was verified twice, once on the bench that built it and
once, from the certificate file alone, on the machine that built this document; only the second run admits
an instance into the tables above.

\section{The tilt as a lever}

Larger tilts pay in covolume and gain in height, so the best tilt depends on the dimension budget, and it beats $\alpha=\tfrac12$ at every budget: under $d\le100$ the tilt $0.58$ at $b=9$, $s=2$ gives $\Delta=0.4313$ in dimension $81$, where Bloom's gadget cannot beat Bohman below dimension $729$; under $d\le500$, $\alpha=\tfrac34$, $b=21$, $s=2$ gives $0.3440$ in dimension $441$; under $d\le1000$, $\alpha=\tfrac45$, $b=31$, $s=2$ gives $0.3186$ against $0.3539$; under $d\le10^4$, $\alpha=0.65$, $b=21$, $s=3$ gives $0.2351$ against $0.2964$; under $d\le10^6$, $\alpha=0.65$, $b=31$, $s=4$ gives $0.1417$ against $0.1975$ (a float scan; the certificates are exact). Two-parameter cyclic gadgets $I+aP+bP^2$ fail the cube condition from $b=7$ on, through period-three patterns, so within cyclic gadgets the one-parameter tilt is the lever.

\section{A corollary: explicit lower bounds in Siegel's lemma}

In the normalisation of Bloom's exposition (after Aliev \cite{Al08}), let $C_d$ be the least constant such that every nonzero $a\in\Z^d$ admits a nonzero $x\in\Z^d$ with $a\cdot x=0$ and $\norm{x}_\infty^{d-1}\le C_d\norm{a}_\infty$. Bombieri and Vaaler give $C_d\ll\sqrt d$; the recorded lower bound is Schinzel's $C_d\ge1$, and Bloom remarks that the Astra construction should yield explicit lower bounds. It does, with no further work: the base weights $a\in\Z^d$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} are $2^k$-relation-free, so every admissible $x$ has $\norm{x}_\infty\ge2^k$, whence $2^{k(d-1)}\le C_d\max a$, that is
\[
C_d\;\ge\;\frac{2^{kr}}{\max_i a_i}\;=\;\frac1f ,
\]
with $f$ the Bloom-normalised ratio of the instance, an exact rational, printed truncated in Table~\ref{tab:siegel}. A Bohman-type set gives $C_d\ge1/0.44004=2.2725$ for all large $d$; these are the first explicit values above that, and they grow with the instance as the construction predicts.

\section{Limits and what would come next}

Bohman's bound is for all large $n$; Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is for specific $n$. An effective
asymptotic statement $f(n)\le n^{-c/\log\log n}$, the form Bloom expects, would follow from a
structural bound on $K$ as a function of $(b,s)$, because $k$ must only exceed
$\log_2(rK)$ by a constant. The buffer is measured here and not proved: the Hermite basis is
computed, not analytic, and an analytic triangular basis of $\Lambda_s$ with an explicit inverse
would settle it. Bigger tilted instances ($\alpha=0.65$, $b=21$, $s=3$ at $d=9261$) need a Hermite
path beyond PARI at that size. Nothing here concerns the lower bound
$N\ge\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ or the true order of $f$.
