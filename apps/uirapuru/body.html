A story · what cert-machine does, told with birds

Someone says there is a blue Uirapuru in the forest. Nobody has seen it.

The Uirapuru is a small Amazon bird famous for its song; in the old story the forest falls silent to listen. Imagine a machine that can call every Uirapuru in a patch of forest and look at each one, and that cannot call a toucan at all. That is what cert-machine does for mathematics. The birds here are invented and seeded, the notebook too; the machine is real, and the few real numbers on this page were read from its records when the page was built.

Act I · the three words a forest can give

A claim about birds is a sentence the machine can act on.

Strip a claim down to what a machine can check and you get every bird in this patch is at least this blue. The machine calls every bird in the patch, looks at each one, and answers with one of three words.

certified

Every bird in the patch answered the call, and every one is at least that blue. Shipped with the list of calls and the least blue bird, the one that would have broken the claim.

refuted

One bird answered and it is not blue enough. Shipped with that bird's tree, so anyone can walk there and look.

refused

The claim cannot be acted on: the patch has no map, "blue" has no number, the bird is a toucan. Shipped with the reason and, where there is one, the number that would change the word.

The third word is the honest one. A checker that must answer yes or no will guess when it cannot see. This one says why it cannot, and what would let it: name the patch, print the number, bring the recording. That turns the machine into an incentive. Say the number, and the word changes.

In the real machine the patch is a box of inputs, "blue" is an inequality with a printed bar, and calling the birds is exact arithmetic: every input, no sampling, no rounding. A glance through binoculars, a floating-point comparison, is never a verdict. It may only prune what is not worth calling, never admit.

Act II · which tree?
The claimant wrote 0.3 m. Which tree is that?

  
Every number a claimant prints is exact, as printed. The number their computer meant is usually a binary double, and the two can differ by less than the width of a feather. In a forest with a tree every ten centimetres, that is the difference between on the tree and just before it.

  

    

      
Instrument 2 · read the map twice

      
One printed position, two readings.

      
Trees stand at 0.0, 0.1, 0.2 … 1.0 metres. The claimant prints the bird's position with one decimal. Read it as the decimal it is, then as the double it denotes, and decide whether the bird is on the tree.

      

      

      

      
The decimal 0.3 is exactly three tenths. The double nearest 0.3 is a hair less, so under that reading the bird is a hair before the tree. Only 0.5 is the same number both ways. The gap in the drawing is not to scale: it is drawn at one fixed width for every position, a picture, while the real gap runs from {{F2_GAP_LO}} m to {{F2_GAP_HI}} m and the readout prints it. Which side the double falls on is decided.

    

    

      
That is not a curiosity. On one real record, {{SIX_N}} points claimed in dimension eleven, the printed digits put {{SIX_VIOL_DIGITS}} pairs of points too close together and the doubles those digits denote put {{SIX_VIOL_DOUBLES}}. Both readings refuted the record; the counts differ because the reading is part of the verdict.

      
So every claim the machine decides carries a sentence saying how the bytes were read, and when a platform's own checker reads them another way, that is written down too. A verdict without its reading is a bird without a tree.

    

  






  
Act III · the painted birds
We tried to fool it.

  
How do you test a judge of birds? Plant birds whose truth you know, and count the ones it gets wrong. Paint one, move one, put a toy in a tree, cut a tree down.

  

    

      
The real machine was tried this way {{SA_MUTANTS}} times on {{SA_OBJECTS}} published objects, with the plan registered before the paper was written and a kill switch declared in advance. It said certified to a false claim {{SA_FALSE_CERT}} times and refuted to a true one {{SA_FALSE_REF}} times. It found two defects of its own on the way, both fixed on the record.

      
The toy on the right is the same idea in a patch of {{F3_N_C}} trees. A painted bird must flip the word. A swap must not. A toy in a tree and a missing tree must refuse, because nothing is decided until the patch is the one the claim is about. In the toy the judge and the witness share one arithmetic; in the real audit they do not, which is the point of the audit.

Act IV · {{KW_PAIRS_WORDS}} calls
Calling every bird is cheap, if you count right.

  
In the real machine, one wave of {{KW_ROWS}} record claims meant {{KW_PAIRS_SCI_PLAIN}} pairs of points to call. The {{KW_GPU_ROWS_WORD}} largest took a rented graphics card {{KW_GPU_HOURS}} hours and ${{KW_GPU_COST}}, the other {{KW_DESK_ROWS_WORD}} ran on a laptop, and every call was exact.

  

    

      
The trick is old. A bird's blueness is a whole number, and whole numbers add exactly as long as the sum stays on the page of the ledger you are writing in. A half-precision ledger holds whole numbers below 2,048; a single-precision one below 16,777,216. Check, before the run, that no flock in the patch can run off the page, write that check into the code, and a card built for guessing becomes an engine for counting. The slowest ledger, arbitrary precision, is kept for the flocks that need it.

      
A seeded sample of every patch was counted again by a program that shares no code with the fast one, and the two agreed on every count. The real ruler, with the real numbers, is on the easy read.

Act V · the toucans
What the machine cannot hear.

  
The machine can call Uirapurus. It cannot see, hear or call a toucan. It cannot call every bird in the Amazon, nor any bird forever. And it cannot judge a song from a recording it was not given. When it cannot, it says so, and says what would let it.

  

    

      
In 2026 a language model wrote a proof about all the zeros of a famous function, a statement about infinitely many birds at once. Two mathematicians checked it; a proof assistant checked it. Most of it is toucan territory for this machine: {{RfSentences}} sentences, of which {{RfShaped}} are claims about a finite patch. Those it read, decided each one the published bytes could settle, and said which it could not. One rests on a recording the paper calls "available from the authors". The word there is refused, and the ask is the file.

      
A refusal is not a shrug. It names the rule that stopped the call, and where a number would change the word, it prints the number. The machine has never found a bird. It has looked at every bird someone claimed, and said which claims hold, which do not, and which it cannot judge, with the tree, the list of calls or the reason attached.
