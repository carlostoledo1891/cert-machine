Certified bounds on the infimum. Per rule 3 I am keeping this short and putting the derivations in a linked write-up rather than in the comment.

**Lower bound: $\inf \ge 1.828$**, which improves on the $1.814605$ recorded in this thread and leaves about $0.0064$ to the conjectured value. It uses no tail estimate, assumes no minimizer and argues by no contradiction. The route is jspier's finite-atom selector on the standard reduction, the same base as Hua Xu's Lean package: since $\int U_\nu \,d\mu = \int U_\mu \,d\nu$, a positive finite $\nu = \delta_{a_0} + \sum_i w_i \delta_{p_i}$ with $U_\nu > 0$ on $\operatorname{supp}\mu$ and $U_\mu(a_0) \le 0$ forces some $p_i$ into $E = \{U_\mu > 0\}$. Taking $a_0$ to be the infimum of the component of $E$ containing $(-\sqrt2,0)$ and sweeping a comb of unit-speed atoms whose images are pairwise disjoint and disjoint from $(a_0,0)$ gives $|E| \ge \mathrm{cap}$ for every normalized $\mu$, with no tail block needed. Certified over the full range $a_0 \in [-1.828,-\sqrt2]$: 86 $a_0$-boxes, 1350 $b$-boxes, worst certified margin $4.12 \times 10^{-7}$, 265 s single-core. A separately written checker, sharing no code with the certifier, re-derives the tiling and weight structure from the certificate file and searches for counterexamples in doubles.

**Upper bound: $\inf \le 1.8344304971959906$**, from the explicit measure $\mu = A\delta_{-1} + f(y)\,dy$ on $[0.804462, 1]$ with $A = 0.8245218$, giving $x_L \in [-1.8081074413361424,\ -1.808107441336006]$ and $x_R \in [0.02632305585964334,\ 0.026323055859847976]$. Two classical identities are quoted here and not certified — the arcsine potential identity and balayage of a point mass onto an interval (Saff–Totik); both are bridged numerically against direct quadrature, but a fully certified route would want certified log-quadrature. The lower bound has no such dependency.

**Three things that may be more useful to the thread than the bounds.** First, natso26's three posted $\lambda^{(\varepsilon)}$ duals are now verified rather than sampled: all weights positive and $U_\lambda \ge 0$ on all of $[-1,1]$ by adaptive tangent envelopes on each gap between support points, with certified off-atom minima $1.24 \times 10^{-5}$, $2.65 \times 10^{-6}$, $3.96 \times 10^{-6}$. Second, the explicit $\varepsilon$-family is certified positive for every $\varepsilon \in (0, 0.1]$ rather than at individual $\varepsilon$: 624,275 interval chunks on $[10^{-12}, 0.1]$ plus a lemma for $(0, 10^{-12}]$ via $t = \zeta_1 - 1$, which cancels the $\varepsilon \to 0$ singular pole algebraically. I take that to be the §4 model problem, but since Problem 4.1 had a typo corrected upthread by J_Koizumi_144 I am stating the inequality I certified rather than claiming the problem closed — if the intended forbidden region differs, say so and I will re-run.

Third, a caution for anyone extending duals to small $\varepsilon$. As $\varepsilon \to 0$ the margin at $x = -1$ tends to a constant fixed by the level defect of the primal constants; with rounded constants that defect can have either sign, and on the wrong side the family provably fails below roughly $4.3 \times 10^{-8}$. The certifier returned a decisively negative window before I understood why. Everything posted here sits at $\varepsilon \ge 5 \times 10^{-4}$, far above it.

The lane family is close to exhausted — its floating-point feasibility boundary is $\approx 1.8285$ and the certified margin at $1.828$ is already $4.12 \times 10^{-7}$ — but the method is not: at $a_0 = x_L$ the $b$-range reaches $x_R$, so its ceiling is $|x_L| + x_R$, the upper bound above. The remaining gap is the price of demanding positivity on all of $[0,1]$ rather than on $\operatorname{supp}\mu^*$, which looks like a support-localization question rather than more computation. I would be glad to be shown a better lane family. The certificate is finitely many boxes with one frozen rational weight vector and one positivity check each, so it should be a reasonable Lean target, and I am happy to help anyone who wants to attempt it.

I have not audited the analytic core of any of the three claimed proofs and take no position on them; the bracket above assumes none of them. Separately I re-verified Appendix A of the Darvas–Peng–Tao manuscript — existence and local uniqueness of the extremal triple by an interval Krawczyk operator, a different route to their Lemma A.1, and the thirty printed decimals of $D$ — which says nothing about the rest of that argument.
