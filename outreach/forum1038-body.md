Certified bounds on the infimum: a lower bound of $1.828$ that needs no tail, and an upper bound of $1.8344304971959906$.

Summary before the detail. The lower bound moves the best finite-atom bound recorded in this thread from $1.814605$ to $1.828$, closing about 68% of the remaining gap to the conjectured value $D = 1.83443047576\ldots$ (the problem page itself still records $1.519$). It needs no tail block, no assumed minimizer and no argument by contradiction. The upper bound is sharper than the $1.835$ on the problem page and is an interval certificate rather than numerics; it also lands $2.14 \times 10^{-8}$ above $D$ from a construction independent of the three claimed proofs, which corroborates that constant from a different route. Separately: the explicit $\varepsilon$-family is certified for the entire range $\varepsilon \in (0, 0.1]$ rather than at individual $\varepsilon$; natso26's three posted duals are now rigorously verified; and a failure mechanism for small-$\varepsilon$ duals is described that I have not seen stated before.

Conventions as in Tao's notes: $U_\mu(x) = \int \log(1/|x-t|)\,d\mu(t)$, and the quantity of interest is $|\{x : U_\mu(x) > 0\}|$ for $\mu$ a probability measure on $[-1,1]$. Every number below comes from an outward-rounded interval-arithmetic certificate over IEEE-754 doubles: each arithmetic operation is replaced by an interval enclosing the true value, so a certified inequality holds for the real quantity and not only for the computed one. Nothing rests on sampling. Each claim detaches into a JSON certificate plus a checker that re-runs it.

**1. $\inf \ge 1.828$, with no tail estimate and no assumed minimizer.**

The route is jspier's finite-atom approach on the standard reduction ($\mu$ normalized with $\operatorname{supp}\mu \subseteq \{-1\} \cup [0,1]$ and $(-\sqrt2, 0) \subseteq E := \{U_\mu > 0\}$), the same base as Hua Xu's Lean package. Beyond that the only analytic input is $\int U_\nu \,d\mu = \int U_\mu \,d\nu$, which gives the selector: if $\nu = \delta_{a_0} + \sum_i w_i \delta_{p_i}$ with $w_i \ge 0$ satisfies $U_\nu > 0$ on $\operatorname{supp}\mu$, and $U_\mu(a_0) \le 0$, then some $p_i$ lies in $E$.

Let $a_0$ be the infimum of the component of $E$ containing $(-\sqrt2, 0)$; then $U_\mu(a_0) \le 0$ and $a_0 \in [-2, -\sqrt2]$. If $a_0 \le -\mathrm{cap}$ the component already has length at least $\mathrm{cap}$. Otherwise put $R = \mathrm{cap} - |a_0|$. For every $b \in [0,R]$ the certificate exhibits
\[ \nu_b = \delta_{a_0} + w_0 \delta_b + \sum_j w_j \delta_{s_j - b}, \qquad U_{\nu_b} > 0 \ \text{ on } \{-1\} \cup [0,1] \supseteq \operatorname{supp}\mu. \]
The maps $b \mapsto b$ and $b \mapsto s_j - b$ are unit-speed and injective with pairwise disjoint images, all disjoint from $(a_0, 0)$, so the selector gives $|E \cap [0,\infty)| \ge R$ and hence $|E| \ge |a_0| + R = \mathrm{cap}$. Both cases give the bound for every normalized $\mu$. There is no tail block, no minimizer and no argument by contradiction: the tail step that the $1.8$-type arguments here need is not improved, it is removed.

Certified at $\mathrm{cap} = 1.828$ over the full range $a_0 \in [-1.828, -\sqrt2]$: 86 $a_0$-boxes, 1350 $b$-boxes, worst certified margin $4.12 \times 10^{-7}$, 265 seconds single-core. A finer box schedule certifies the same bound at higher cost (332 / 40000 / 5421 s, worst margin $7.48 \times 10^{-7}$), so the result does not depend on the tiling. Caps $1.816$, $1.82$ and $1.825$ are also on disk. A second implementation, written separately and sharing no code with the certifier, re-derives the tiling, geometry and weight structure from the certificate file alone and searches for counterexamples in doubles; the smallest value it found is $1.41 \times 10^{-5}$.

The step that made this affordable: in the shifted coordinate $y = x + b$ the teeth do not move, and the remaining part $-\log(y - b - a_0) - w_0 \log(y - 2b)$ has positive derivative in $b$ for $x \ge b$. Each two-parameter box therefore collapses to two thin one-dimensional convex problems, with no loss from bounding a box by its worst corner. Without that step the near poles cost roughly $10h$ per box half-width and the box count is an order of magnitude worse.

Please correct me if I have the state of the thread wrong. As I read it the best finite-atom bound recorded here is $1.814605$, and Hua Xu's Lean/Mathlib package stands at $1.807100$. Mine is an interval certificate, not a Lean formalization, so it is a weaker kind of object than Hua Xu's even though the number is larger. The forcing certificate is built to be a formalization target — finitely many boxes, one frozen rational weight vector per box, one positivity check per box — and I would be glad to help anyone who wants to push it through Lean.

**2. $\inf \le 1.8344304971959906$.**

For $\mu = A \delta_{-1} + f(y)\,dy$ on $[a,1]$ with $a = 0.804462$ and $A = 0.8245218$ exact, and $f(y) = \bigl[1 - A\sqrt{2(1+a)}/(1+y)\bigr] / \bigl(\pi\sqrt{(y-a)(1-y)}\bigr) \ge 0$, the set $\{U_\mu < 0\}$ is $(x_L, x_R)$ minus the point $-1$, with
\[ x_L \in [-1.8081074413361424,\ -1.808107441336006], \qquad x_R \in [0.02632305585964334,\ 0.026323055859847976]. \]
The on-support level is certified nonnegative ($c \ge 9.3 \times 10^{-8}$). The set structure follows from three one-line facts: $U' < 0$ left of $-1$, $U'' < 0$ on $(-1,a)$, and $U' > 0$ right of $1$.

Two remarks. This is sharper than the $1.835$ recorded on the problem page, and it is a certificate rather than numerics, so it can be checked without trusting the computation that produced it. And it sits $2.14 \times 10^{-8}$ above $D = 1.83443047576266171109\ldots$, the constant all three claimed proofs report, reached by a construction that assumes none of them — corroboration of $D$, though not of any proof of it.

Weak point, stated rather than buried: two classical identities are quoted here and not certified — the arcsine potential identity and the balayage of a point mass onto an interval (Saff–Totik). Both are bridged numerically to between $10^{-9}$ and $10^{-16}$ against direct quadrature, but a fully certified route would want certified log-quadrature. The lower bound in item 1 has no such dependency.

**3. natso26's three posted duals are now verified rigorously.**

For $\varepsilon = 0.002$, $0.001$ and $0.0005$ (278, 183 and 184 atoms), all weights are positive and $U_\lambda \ge 0$ on all of $[-1,1]$, with certified off-atom minima $1.24 \times 10^{-5}$, $2.65 \times 10^{-6}$ and $3.96 \times 10^{-6}$; at the atoms $U = +\infty$. Method: $U$ is convex on each open gap between consecutive support points and tends to $+\infty$ at the atoms, so a tangent line at an interior point bounds $U$ from below on the whole gap. Adaptive tangent envelopes, no quadrature and no grid. This removes the sampling-only caveat on those constructions.

**4. The $\varepsilon$-family, over the whole range rather than at sample points.**

For the explicit $\lambda^{(\varepsilon)}$ — the §4 ansatz made concrete, masses from the residue closed forms, support $\{p_0 + \varepsilon\} \cup \{q_0\} \cup [a, 1-\varepsilon] \cup \{1\}$ with $p_0 = -1.8081072518940688$, $q_0 = 0.02632310211711336$, $a = 0.804462$ — the certificate gives $U_\lambda \ge 0$ on all of $[-1,1]$ for **every** $\varepsilon \in (0, 0.1]$: 624,275 interval chunks covering $[10^{-12}, 0.1]$, each with its own rational parameter, plus a separate lemma for $(0, 10^{-12}]$ using the substitution $t = \zeta_1 - 1$, under which the pole at $1$ that is singular as $\varepsilon \to 0$ cancels algebraically rather than numerically. Consequently the two-interval scenario $\{U_\mu > 0\} \subseteq [p_0 + \varepsilon, q_0] \cup [a, 1-\varepsilon]$ is excluded for the whole range.

The difference from what is already here is the quantifier: the constructions posted in this thread are at individual $\varepsilon$ ($5 \times 10^{-4}$, $10^{-3}$, $2 \times 10^{-3}$); this is a statement for every $\varepsilon$ in the interval, including the limit direction where the family is most delicate — see item 5.

I believe that is the content of Problem 4.1 in Tao's notes, but that problem had a typo which was corrected upthread by J_Koizumi_144, so I am stating the inequality I actually certified rather than asserting the problem is closed. If the intended forbidden region differs from the one above, say so and I will re-run against the corrected statement.

**5. A failure mechanism for small-$\varepsilon$ duals, which I have not seen described.**

As $\varepsilon \to 0$, $U_{\lambda^{(\varepsilon)}}(-1)$ tends to a constant $\delta = -(\text{primal level defect}) \cdot (\text{density mass}) / A$. With rounded minimizer constants the level defect is nonzero and its sign decides everything. One natural choice of constants gives $\delta = -9.2 \times 10^{-9}$, and the family then provably fails for every $\varepsilon$ below roughly $|\delta| / 0.21 \approx 4.3 \times 10^{-8}$. Choosing the primal constants with the level defect on the other side gives $\delta = +5.3 \times 10^{-8}$ and uniform positivity. I did not predict this: the certifier returned a decisively negative window and the mechanism was worked out afterwards to explain it. Everything posted in this thread sits at $\varepsilon \ge 5 \times 10^{-4}$, far above the wall, so nobody would have met it — but anyone extending to small $\varepsilon$ from midpoint decimals will, and the failure is real rather than an artefact of the arithmetic.

**Where this stops.** The lane family above is close to exhausted: its floating-point feasibility boundary is $\approx 1.8285$, and the certified margin at $1.828$ is already $4.12 \times 10^{-7}$, so finer boxes will not buy the next thousandth. The method is not exhausted: at $a_0 = x_L$ the $b$-range reaches $x_R$, so its ceiling is $|x_L| + x_R = 1.83443\ldots$, exactly the upper bound in item 2. The remaining $\approx 0.0064$ is the price of demanding $U_\nu > 0$ on all of $[0,1]$ rather than on $\operatorname{supp}\mu^* = \{-1\} \cup [0.8045, 1]$. Closing it looks like a support-localization statement for near-minimizers rather than more computation, and I would be glad to be shown a better lane family.
