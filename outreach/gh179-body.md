Following up on the supremum side above with the other end of the same problem. Both ends of #1038 now have machine-checked results here.

**A certified lower bound, $\inf \ge 1.828$, with no tail estimate and no assumed minimizer.** This moves the best finite-atom bound recorded in the [forum thread](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/1038) from $1.814605$, closing about 68% of the remaining gap to the conjectured value $D = 1.83443047576\ldots$ (the problem page itself still records $1.519$). The route is the finite-atom selector — jspier's approach on the standard reduction, the same base as Hua Xu's Lean package: if $\nu = \delta_{a_0} + \sum_i w_i \delta_{p_i}$ with $w_i \ge 0$ has $U_\nu > 0$ on $\operatorname{supp}\mu$ and $U_\mu(a_0) \le 0$, then $\int U_\nu \,d\mu = \int U_\mu \,d\nu$ forces some $p_i$ into $E = \{U_\mu > 0\}$. Sweeping a comb of unit-speed atoms with pairwise disjoint images, all disjoint from $(a_0, 0)$, gives $|E| \ge \mathrm{cap}$ for **every** normalized $\mu$ — so there is no tail block, no minimizer and no argument by contradiction; the tail step those arguments need is removed rather than improved.

Certified over the full range $a_0 \in [-1.828, -\sqrt2]$: 86 $a_0$-boxes, 1350 $b$-boxes, worst certified margin $4.12 \times 10^{-7}$, 265 s single-core. A finer box schedule certifies the same bound at higher cost (332 / 40000 / 5421 s), so the result does not depend on the tiling. A second implementation, written separately and sharing no code with the certifier, re-derives the tiling, geometry and weight structure from the certificate file alone and hunts counterexamples in floating point; the smallest value it found is $1.41 \times 10^{-5}$.

**A certified upper bound, $\inf \le 1.8344304971959906$,** from the explicit measure $\mu = A\delta_{-1} + f(y)\,dy$ on $[a, 1]$ with $a = 0.804462$ and $A = 0.8245218$ exact, giving $x_L \in [-1.8081074413361424, -1.808107441336006]$ and $x_R \in [0.02632305585964334, 0.026323055859847976]$. This is sharper than the $1.835$ recorded on the problem page and is an interval certificate rather than numerics, so it can be checked without trusting the computation that produced it. It also sits $2.14 \times 10^{-8}$ above $D$, reached by a construction that assumes none of the three claimed proofs — corroboration of that constant from a different route, though not of any proof of it.

Two by-products that may matter more here than the bound itself. First, the three $\lambda^{(\varepsilon)}$ dual measures posted in the forum thread are now certified rather than sampled: all weights positive and $U_\lambda \ge 0$ on all of $[-1,1]$, by adaptive tangent envelopes on each gap between support points (no quadrature, no grid), with certified off-atom minima $1.24 \times 10^{-5}$, $2.65 \times 10^{-6}$ and $3.96 \times 10^{-6}$. Second, the explicit $\varepsilon$-family is certified positive with $U_\lambda \ge 0$ on $[-1,1]$ for **every** $\varepsilon \in (0, 0.1]$ — 624,275 interval chunks covering $[10^{-12}, 0.1]$, plus a separate lemma for $(0, 10^{-12}]$ using the substitution $t = \zeta_1 - 1$, under which the pole at $1$ that is singular as $\varepsilon \to 0$ cancels algebraically rather than numerically. The existing constructions in the thread are at individual $\varepsilon$ values; this is the whole interval, which excludes the two-interval scenario across the entire range. I believe that is the §4 model problem (Problem 4.1) of Tao's notes, but since that statement had a typo corrected in the forum thread, I am stating the inequality I actually certified rather than claiming the problem closed.

There is also a failure mechanism for small-$\varepsilon$ duals that I have not seen described. As $\varepsilon \to 0$, $U_{\lambda^{(\varepsilon)}}(-1)$ tends to a constant $\delta$ fixed by the level defect of the primal constants; with rounded constants $\delta$ can have either sign, and if it is negative the family **provably fails** for every $\varepsilon$ below roughly $|\delta| / 0.21$, which for one natural choice of constants is $4.3 \times 10^{-8}$. I did not predict this — the certifier returned a decisively negative window and the mechanism was worked out afterwards to explain it. Everything posted in the thread sits at $\varepsilon \ge 5 \times 10^{-4}$, far above the wall, but anyone extending to small $\varepsilon$ from midpoint decimals will meet it.

The forcing certificate is finitely many boxes with one frozen rational weight vector each and one positivity check per box, so it is a plausible Lean formalization target. Mine is an interval certificate rather than a formalization, so it is a weaker kind of object than Hua Xu's package even though the number is larger, and I would be glad to help anyone who wants to push it through Lean.

Nothing here assumes or adjudicates any of the three claimed proofs of this problem listed on the forum, all of which report the same $D$; the forum states that appearing there is no guarantee of correctness and that nobody associated with the site has examined any part of them. Separately I re-verified the computational appendix of the Darvas–Peng–Tao manuscript — Appendix A only: existence and local uniqueness of the extremal triple by an interval Krawczyk operator, a different route to their Lemma A.1, together with all thirty printed decimals of $D$. To my knowledge that is the only independent verification of any part of any of the three claims so far, and I would be glad to be told otherwise. It says nothing about the rest of that argument.
