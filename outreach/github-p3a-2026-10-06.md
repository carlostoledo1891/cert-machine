### What is the conjecture

**The hot spots conjecture** (J. Rauch, 1974). Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ be a bounded convex domain, and let $u$ be an eigenfunction for the first nonzero eigenvalue of the Neumann Laplacian on $\Omega$. Then $u$ attains its maximum and its minimum on $\partial\Omega$. The strong form asks that they are attained only on $\partial\Omega$.

Rauch posed it for general "nice" domains (Lecture Notes in Math. 446 (1975) 355–369). It is false in that generality, which is why the open case is the convex planar one. Survey: R. Bañuelos, K. Burdzy, *On the "hot spots" conjecture of J. Rauch*, J. Funct. Anal. 164 (1999) 1–33.

Candidate variants:
- **False** for some planar domains with holes: K. Burdzy, W. Werner, Ann. of Math. 149 (1999) 309–317 (arXiv:math/9803030); with one hole, K. Burdzy, Duke Math. J. 129 (2005) 481–502.
- **False** for some convex domains in high dimension: J. de Dios Pont, *Convex sets can have interior hot spots*, arXiv:2412.06344. Quantitative bounds: de Dios Pont, Hsu, Taylor, arXiv:2508.16321.
- **True** for:
  - domains with two axes of symmetry: Jerison–Nadirashvili, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 13 (2000);
  - lip domains: Atar–Burdzy, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 17 (2004);
  - all triangles: Judge–Mondal, Ann. of Math. 191 (2020), erratum 195 (2022).
- **Recent partial results:**
  - isosceles trapezoids and kites: Deng, Gui, Jiang, Yang, Yao, Zou, arXiv:2604.19003;
  - some non-convex polygons: Hatcher, arXiv:2405.19508.

Polymath7 (2012) worked on the acute-triangle case, including a validated-numerics route: https://michaelnielsen.org/polymath/index.php?title=The_hot_spots_conjecture

### Prerequisites needed

The Neumann spectrum of $-\Delta$ on a bounded Lipschitz (here convex) planar domain. To my knowledge Mathlib does not have it yet. Two routes seem workable:
- the variational one: the first nonzero eigenvalue as the minimum of the Rayleigh quotient over mean-zero $H^1(\Omega)$ functions, and $u$ as a minimiser;
- an abstract statement parameterised by an eigenpair predicate, as the repository does for other analysis problems whose setting is not yet in Mathlib.

Context, my own work (disclosed): for every $c \in [0.845, 0.85]$, the trapezoid with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(c, 9/10)$, $(1/4, 9/10)$ lies outside every class above. Its first nonzero Neumann eigenvalue is simple, and its eigenfunction attains its extrema only on the boundary. This is a computer-assisted proof in interval arithmetic, not a formal one. It takes two lemmas of Liu (arXiv:1808.08148) as inputs and is not peer-reviewed: https://carlostoledo.co/reports/ember.html. The convex quadrilateral case stays open.
