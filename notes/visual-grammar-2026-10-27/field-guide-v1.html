WARRANT v2 · one grammar for cert-machine, claimbox, Machinidae and the apps

Every mark carries two labels.

The stroke says how a number was made. Where the mark sits says what its question got. The drawing obeys the same rules as the arithmetic: the weakest input sets the stroke, and everything rounds outward, digits and pixels alike. Eight live applications below, each computed in your browser.

How was it made? The stroke.

What did the question get? The shape.

The one rule between them. Only a DECIDED mark can be CERTIFIED or REFUTED. A float, a fit or a forecast is a candidate. It can prune, it can propose, it can never admit.

cert-machine · one number · hole: compute

The number line

Is √2 below t? Is φ? Is √3? Each number is an exact enclosure, so every bar is solid. The verdict comes from where the bar sits against t. A bar that crosses t is still exact; only the question is open, and the refusal prices what would close it.

The rule. Round outward, once for digits and once for pixels. The printed lower end is rounded down and the upper end up. The drawn bar covers every figure unit the enclosure touches, so a bar narrower than a unit is drawn one unit wide, and the figure says so. The price of a refusal is a forecast, so it is dashed, set in italic, and graded when the bits are spent.

cert-machine · a claim over a region · holes: instrument, compute

The paving

The claim is P(x, y) ≥ c on the whole square. A cover proves it, one point refutes it, and what is left is a framed gap with a name. At c = 0 the claim is true but touches the bar at two points, and no number of boxes closes a gap whose margin is zero. A second instrument closes it in one box.

P(x, y) = x⁴ + 2x²y² + y⁴ − (3/4)x² − (3/4)y² − (1/2)xy + 1/4 = (x² + y² − 1/2)² + (1/4)(x − y)²

The rule. Merging deciders is a join on knowledge: a cover from either one covers, a gap stays a gap only if both leave it, and a point inside a cover is a CONFLICT. All arithmetic here is on integers below 253; if any value left that range the instrument would refuse rather than round.

claimbox · one byte string, three readings · hole: reading

The sentence

The same bytes can state different propositions. claimbox does not pick one silently. It decides every reading exactly and sets them side by side. When the readings disagree and the claimant did not say which one they meant, the claim is refused with a READING hole. When they agree, the choice does not matter and the claim is decided.

The claim a + b = c

The rule. The quoted bytes are DECIDED: they are what was said. A reading inferred by pattern is CHOSEN, so it is underlined with dots until the claimant states it. Each reading is decided exactly: doubles and singles are rationals, and their sums are checked as rationals.

Machinidae · selection is a choice · illustrative lineage

The champion

A lineage of programs, each scored by two deciders that share no code. Edges are dotted because selection chose the parents. Unjudged proposals are dashed and carry the score the model claimed, set in italic. The champion is decided only when its lower bound clears every other upper bound. An exact tie is decided as a tie, and picking one of the tied programs is a choice, drawn dotted.

The rule. Selection demotes to CHOSEN. An argmax is decided only when the optimum is unique; a tie-break, a default or a seed is a choice and is drawn as one. Two deciders whose enclosures do not overlap cannot both be right, so the program's life is voided and the era freezes until a person explains it.

the register · dependencies

The chain

Proof flows forward and refutation flows backward. If a premise is refuted, what depended on it loses its proof and is refused, naming the premise; it is not refuted. If the conclusion is refuted, at least one premise is false, and only the other premises can say which. Click c1, c2 or c3 to change what its own certifier decided.

The rule. Certificates compose forward: certified premises and a certified step give a certified conclusion. Refutations compose backward: a refuted conclusion and a certified step mean some premise is false. A refuted conclusion over certified premises is a CONFLICT, which only happens when a step or a decider is wrong.

an app · a claim with a missing input · hole: data · illustrative numbers

The missing input

A carrier claims a shipment emitted at most 1,000 kg CO₂e. Distance and payload are on the waybill. The emission factor comes from a published table as a range. The load factor was not disclosed. The verdict is NEEDS DATA, and the page publishes the exact number that would flip it. Disclosure is the only way through.

The product face says what to do. The arithmetic sits one click down, in the details, with every number's standing.

The rule. A refusal names who can fill it. A DATA hole can be filled only by the claimant, so the page prints the threshold where the claim turns green. Between the two thresholds the disclosure is not enough: the published factor is too wide, and the next thing to ask for is a fuel record.

an app · the forecast proposes, the certifier decides · illustrative numbers

The weather window

A crane lift needs every 3-hourly wave-height reading at or below 2.5 m until 18:00. The forecast enters as boxes, never points, and its numbers are set in italic: predicted and decided never share a typeface. The certifier decides over the boxes and names the forecast it relied on. As the hours pass, the buoy grades the forecaster, and a forecaster that misses its boxes stops being admitted.

The rule. Prediction is a proposer, never an authority. A verdict that leans on a forecast says so, and it is withdrawn the moment that forecast is graded out.

the grammar itself · research item R-1

Nested ink

The strokes are ordered by how much ink they carry, and each pattern sits inside the next. Overprint two marks and you see the stronger. Mask one with the other and you see the weaker. Blur or photocopy the page and darker still means stronger. The two patterns in use today do not nest: some dots land in a dash's gap.

The rule. With one common period of 6, CHOSEN (1 on, offset, round caps) sits inside COMPUTED (4 on, 2 off), which sits inside solid. Inclusion of ink sets is an exact interval check. Whether readers decode the order is the half that needs people: a perception study, not yet run.
