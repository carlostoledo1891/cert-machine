## The five deliverables, in yield order

### 1 · blind-spot as an Inspect task, with human baselines — 1–2 weeks
- `environments/blind_spot/inspect/` (new): an `inspect_ai` Task whose
  scorer IS the existing verifier (simulate the netlist under the mutation;
  SAT miter for EQUIVALENT) — the same bytes the verifiers env runs; the
  battery must prove the two scorers agree on every pooled mutant (a red
  control: a witness with one coordinate changed must stop killing under
  BOTH).
- Three rungs (`located` / `profile` / `blind`) as three Inspect variants;
  the difficulty ladder documented from the SAT pool (348 killable, 51
  equivalent, identity = mutation 1).
- **Human baselines:** 5–10 timed attempts per rung by people with digital-
  design background (the operator recruits; times and outcomes go in a
  pinned record `environments/blind_spot/baselines.json`, sha-pinned,
  with consent lines). Without baselines there is no time horizon.
- Deliverable check: `inspect eval` runs end to end on one frontier model;
  README states the task, the scorer, the baselines, and what "solved"
  means. Publish the Inspect variant to the same hub listing (a SEND — the
  operator's).

### 2 · A time-horizon curve on exactly-verified tasks — 1 week after 1
- Run frontier models (Sonnet 5, Opus 5, one open model) at increasing
  token/time budgets across the three environments; record success vs.
  human baseline time; fit the METR Time Horizons methodology (logistic in

  the hseva way (instruments/hseva/fit.js pattern: score equations,
  Krawczyk box, the horizon as an enclosure over the box), the coverage of
  the model's own confidence graded by the forecast gym.
- `certs/horizon-ledger.json`, `reports/time-horizon.html` (gated, two
  charts: success vs. human time per model; the horizon per model as an
  interval). The one-sentence claim: the first time-horizon number on
  tasks with no judge in the loop.

### 3 · A task-QA audit of a public eval — 3–4 days
