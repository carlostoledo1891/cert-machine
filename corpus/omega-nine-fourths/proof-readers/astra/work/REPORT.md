## 1. The theorem and its model

**SOURCED — Theorem 1.1, p. 1, lines 18–29, Corollary 1.2 and Lemma 2.4.** For every field $F$, the paper claims that the infimum $\omega_F$ of the real $s$ for which two $n$-by-$n$ matrices can be multiplied in $O_{F,s}(n^s)$ scalar operations is $9/4$. The inputs are the $2n^2$ algebraically independent entries of $X,Y$, constants belong to $F$, and the gates are binary addition, subtraction, multiplication and division. A divisor must be nonzero in $F(X,Y)$. Output $(i,j)$ must equal $\sum_k X_{ik}Y_{kj}$ in that rational function field, including when $F$ is finite. At each size the circuit and its field constants may be chosen separately. The upper bound gives circuits of size $O_{F,\varepsilon}(n^{9/4+\varepsilon})$ for each $\varepsilon>0$. The finite-size lower claim counts only products with both arguments nonconstant and quotients with a nonconstant divisor, and bounds their number below by $\lceil n^{9/4}/2\rceil$ for every $n\ge2$.

The mixed equation is $b_{00}f_{11}=a_{11}-b_{11}f_{00}-b_{10}f_{01}-b_{01}f_{10}$. It introduces at most two new products, even for a chain of quotients. The simultaneous evaluation point may lie outside $F$. The resulting rank inequality is over $\bar F$, precisely where the fixed-size lower bound is subsequently used. **COMPUTED.** Independently enumerating $b,f$ in $\mathbb F_p[u,v]/(u^2,v^2)$ with $b_{00}\ne0$, multiplying $a=bf$ by coefficient convolution, and recovering $f_{11}$ from the quotient equation succeeded for all 128, 4,374 and 312,500 ordered pairs for $p=2,3,5$ respectively.

- **SOURCED — Corollary 1.2, §2.4, pp. 9–10, consequences.tex lines 6–24. REASONED — ceiling and geometric border argument checked.** At any fixed side $n$, $R_K(T_n)=r$ implies $\omega_K\le\log_n r$ by recursion and padding. An all-fields exponent lower bound therefore gives $R_{\bar F}(T_n)\ge n^{9/4}$ even at one size. Lemma 2.4 gives $2q\ge n^{9/4}$, and integrality gives the ceiling. For border rank work in the fixed space $V$ over $\bar F$. The preimage under $x\mapsto x^{\otimes j}$ of the span of the powers of rank-$\le r$ tensors is closed, hence contains their closure. The span has dimension at most $\binom{j+\dim V-1}{\dim V-1}$ by the degree-$j$ coordinate monomials, in any characteristic. Thus $n^{9j/4}\le R_{\bar F}(T_{n^j})\le\binom{j+\dim V-1}{\dim V-1}r^j$. Hold $n$ fixed and take $j$-th roots. This proves geometric border rank $\ge n^{9/4}$.

- **SOURCED — Corollary 1.2, p. 10, lines 4–13. REASONED — asymptotic rank over the original field checked.** The fixed-size argument gives $R_F(T_n^{\otimes j})\ge n^{9j/4}$. For the opposite direction the circuit upper bound and Lemma 2.4 give $r_j=O_{F,\varepsilon}(n^{j(9/4+\varepsilon)})$ over some finite extension $E_j/F$. For fixed $j$, take the $t$-th power over that same field. Expand each of the three resulting vectors in an $F$-basis of $E_j$, then project their product coefficients to the coordinate of $1$. This costs at most $[E_j:F]^3$ per simple tensor and gives $R_F(T_n^{\otimes jt})\le[E_j:F]^3r_j^t$. Submultiplicativity gives the limit of the roots. Take $t\to\infty$ first, then $j\to\infty$ and $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. No uniform bound on the extension degrees is needed.

**REASONED — completeness.** The manuscript gives complete deductions of the gate bound, the division case and both rank statements from the requisite exponent inequalities. The gate and border lower bounds need only the lower exponent inequality, including over $\bar F$. Equality of asymptotic rank additionally needs the upper inequality. My unresolved findings concern the proof of the exponent premise. I found no additional mathematical gap in the passages to these consequences.

## 5. Conflicts, barriers and the asymptotic spectrum

- **SOURCED — p. 10, lines 14–22, [53] and [54].** The paper names a direct numerical conflict with Zhi-Wei Sun, *Tensor degenerations for matrix multiplication*, arXiv:2610.13971v1. At $n=256$ it attributes a geometric border-rank upper bound $2^{17}$ to that version. Corollary 1.2 gives $2^{18}$. Volker Strassen, *Algebra and complexity*, First European Congress of Mathematics II, Progress in Mathematics 120 (1994), Conjecture 5.3, predicts asymptotic rank $n^2$ here. **REASONED.** Tightness follows because the weights $i-Bj,\ Bj-B^2k,\ B^2k-i$ with integer $B>n$ are separately injective and sum to zero. Each one-party flattening has rank $n^2$. Since $n^{9/4}>n^2$ for $n\ge2$, the claimed equality would disprove this conjectural prediction. I don't know the status of Sun's argument beyond the conflict attributed to v1, or of a proved unrestricted upper bound strictly below $9/4$.

- **SOURCED — p. 5, lines 35–38, [11], [35], [36].** The earlier ordinary-rank bounds quoted are $\frac52 n^2-3n$ over every field, from M. Bläser, *Lower bounds for the bilinear complexity of associative algebras*, Computational Complexity 9 (2000), 73–112, and $3n^2-o(n^2)$ over $\mathbb C$, from J. M. Landsberg, *New lower bounds for the rank of matrix multiplication*, SIAM J. Comput. 43 (2014), 144–149. The complex border-rank bound quoted is $2n^2-\lceil\log_2 n\rceil-1$, from J. M. Landsberg and M. Michałek, *A $2n^2-\log_2(n)-1$ lower bound for the border rank of matrix multiplication*, IMRN 2018, 4722–4733. **REASONED.** A constant times $(n^j)^2$ has $j$-th root tending to $n^2$. The quarter-power gain would survive this test, a qualitative strengthening entirely compatible with the earlier lower bounds.

- **REASONED — reference from memory.** K. Efremenko, A. Garg, R. Oliveira and A. Wigderson, *Barriers for Rank Methods in Arithmetic Complexity*, prove an $O(m)$ ceiling for lower bounds on ordinary three-tensor rank obtained by a linear tensor-to-matrix rank method for format $m\times m\times m$ (characteristic-zero setting suffices here). Such a certificate divides the matrix rank by the maximum matrix rank on a simple tensor. At $m=n^2$ this ceiling is quadratic. The present paper argues by a reduction to network complexity and uses many spectral points. The linear rank-method ceiling does not imply an upper bound on the actual tensor rank. I did not check the original theorem number offline.

- **SOURCED — p. 5, lines 19–32 and 38–44, [5], [6], [14], [41].** A. Ambainis, Y. Filmus and F. - **SOURCED — §11.3, Corollary 11.1, pp. 43–44, and [52], V. Strassen, *The asymptotic spectrum of tensors*, J. reine angew. Math. 384 (1988), 102–152.** The spectral maximum formula identifies asymptotic rank with the maximum normalized spectral evaluation. Here $T_n$ is a product of three $n$-wires. At scale one the tripod bound and the arithmetic–harmonic mean inequality give $1\ge D_*(a)\ge(8/9)\sum_i a_i$. Equality forces three arms of length $3/8$, hence three wire rates $3/4$, yielding the value $(abc)^{3/4}$ on rectangular products. The spectrum theorem supplies a maximum, but identifying a value this large is precisely the substantive new assertion. Invoking Corollary 11.1 as an independent witness for the lower bound would be circular because its proof invokes Corollary 1.2.
