# FOUNDATION: compact moving-separator and closed-band CE assembly lemma

This is the package's assembly-facing theorem. Its tail and positive-stall
inputs are independently proved in THEOREM.md, not cited from another seat's
in-flight work. It completes the logical reduction, NOT the numerical inputs.

## 1. Hermite collision orientation, in our normalization

Let F(t,z) be jointly holomorphic near a real point (tau,b), real-type for
real t, and satisfy partial_t F = -partial_z^2 F. Suppose F(tau,.) has a
zero of exact multiplicity m>=2 at b, with leading coefficient c!=0.
For every fixed bounded w-domain and positive h approaching zero,

    F(tau+h,b+sqrt(h)*w) = c*h^(m/2)*(P_m(w)+O(sqrt(h))),
    F(tau-h,b+sqrt(h)*w) = c*h^(m/2)*(Q_m(w)+O(sqrt(h))),
    P_m(w) = m!*sum_(0<=j<=floor(m/2))
                  (-1)^j*w^(m-2*j)/(j!*(m-2*j)!),
    Q_m(w) = i^(-m)*P_m(i*w).

The O bounds are locally uniform in w. This follows directly from the
convergent joint Taylor series: partial_t^j partial_z^k F =
(-1)^j partial_z^(2j+k) F, so terms with 2j+k<m vanish and those with
2j+k=m give the displayed polynomials. Set h=r^2; on a compact w-domain
all remaining powers of r have degree at least m+1, so the convergent
series divided by r^m has a uniform O(r) remainder. This also explains
why both signs of time must be considered before drawing a conclusion.

The polynomials satisfy P_0=1, P_1=w and

    P_(m+1)=w*P_m-2*m*P_(m-1),     P'_m=m*P_(m-1).       (H1)

Their roots are all real and simple. Here is a proof by the recurrence,
not a finite list of checked degrees. Inductively assume the monic P_m
and P_(m-1) have real simple interlacing roots. At each root r_j of P_m,
P_(m+1)(r_j)=-2m P_(m-1)(r_j), so consecutive signs alternate and give a
root between each consecutive pair. At the rightmost root the sign is
negative, while the leading monic term is positive at positive infinity;
there is one more root to the right. The corresponding leftmost sign
and the monic parity give one more to the left. These m+1 disjoint
intervals contain all roots by degree, each with multiplicity one, and
interlace the old ones. The base m=1 has P2=w^2-2. Thus the induction
holds for all integers, not just the symbolic anchors in verify.py.

P_m(-w)=(-1)^m P_m(w); simplicity implies a zero at 0 occurs exactly
when m is odd. Therefore Q_m has simple imaginary roots, with at least
one nonreal conjugate pair whenever m>=2.

Choose disjoint small disks about the roots of P_m. Rouche transfers
one zero counted with multiplicity to each disk in the scaled forward
cluster for ALL sufficiently small h>0. Since each disk is invariant
under conjugation and F is real-type, the zero is real and simple.
A fixed small disk around b has exactly m zeros for small h by Rouche
at h=0, so none of the original cluster is omitted by scaling. Backward,
disks about a nonzero imaginary root pair of Q_m stay away from the
axis and yield a nonreal conjugate pair for ALL sufficiently small h>0.
This proves both collision orientations without assuming generic
multiplicity two. The integral definition of H_t ensures the required
joint holomorphy and PDE by differentiation under the integral.

Credit: the local normal form is classical (also in Polymath's published
zero-dynamics proposition); Kornaus's PR #180 suggested its use for a
moving-separator collision-exclusion record. It is re-proved here. No
unrefereed statement is treated as a leg.

## 2. Moving-separator theorem; continuity suffices

Let A>0, Y>0, and s:[0,A]->(0,infinity) be CONTINUOUS (not necessarily
monotone or differentiable). Assume F is jointly holomorphic on a
neighbourhood of the compact set

    {(t,z): 0<=t<=A, |Re z|<=s(t), |Im z|<=Y},

is real-type and solves F_t=-F_zz. Suppose F is not identically zero
on any time slice, F(t,z)!=0 on the ENTIRE moving rectangular boundary
for every t in [0,A], and every zero inside at t=0 is real, with arbitrary
finite multiplicity. Then:

**For every t in [0,A] all interior zeros are real; for every t in (0,A]
they are also simple.** No assumption about exterior zeros is made.

Proof details that a first-collision slogan can hide:

1. At any fixed time u, finitely many zeros lie in the compact rectangle.
   The boundary is nonvanishing. Put mutually disjoint small disks around
   the interior zeros. The complement and a thin collar of the moving
   boundary are compact and zero-free at u. Continuity of s and uniform
   continuity of F allow one common time neighbourhood on which there
   are no zeros outside the disks but inside the moving rectangle, and
   each disk retains its multiplicity by Rouche. Thus zeros cannot enter
   through an unsampled change in s(t); the full time continuum matters.
2. If at u all zeros are real, every simple zero stays real by conjugation
   and its local count one. Every multiple zero resolves into real simple
   zeros at all times just above u by section 1. The finite common time
   neighbourhood from step 1 prevents any other zeros appearing. In
   particular reality holds on some initial interval [0,delta], even
   if F(0,.) has multiple roots.
3. Let b be the supremum of times r such that reality holds throughout
   [0,r]. Reality also holds at b: a nonreal interior zero at b would
   persist in a small disk disjoint from the real axis for times just
   below b, contradicting the definition. Boundary zero-freeness rules
   out a zero escaping that argument. Step 2 extends reality rightwards
   at b if b<A. Thus b=A.
4. If a multiple zero occurs at any u in (0,A], choose a sufficiently
   small time decrement h with u-h>=0. The backwards orientation yields
   a nonreal pair near it. By continuity of s the nearby cluster remains
   in the interior. This contradicts reality just proved. This argument
   includes the closed endpoint u=A; positive-time simplicity follows.

The disk/collar construction can also be phrased by an argument-principle
count on the moving contour. The proof above needs no derivative of s and
never tracks billions of labeled zeros numerically.

## 3. Application to H_t within PT2021

For H_t choose Y=1. The two horizontal edges are zero-free: at t>0 the
de Bruijn strip has width sqrt(max(1-2t,0))<1; at t=0 the xi boundary
corresponds to Re s=0 or 1 and is zero-free by the standard zeta boundary
theorem (the completed function removes the apparent real endpoints).
Alternatively include these edges explicitly in the input certificate.

Thus it suffices to give a continuous positive s(t) on [0,A] satisfying

    H_t(+/-s(t)+iy)!=0 for all t in [0,A], |y|<=1,
    s(0)/2<=T_PT=3000175332800.

PT2021 plus H0(z)=xi(1/2+iz/2)/8 places every initial interior zero on
the real axis. Multiplicities at t=0 need not have been certified as
simple. Section 2 then proves all zeros with |Re z|<s(t) real and simple
for t in (0,A]. The boundary itself has no zeros. No slope bound, affine
form, or lower bound on gaps at t=0 is required. A future record must
state the exact RH margin T_PT-s(0)/2. This package proves the conditional
implication and does not supply s or its nonvanishing certificate.

## 4. Closed-band collision-exclusion composition

Let rational 0<l<A<=1/2. Suppose a proved unconditional anchor gives
Lambda<=A and an s satisfying section 3 has been certified for [0,A].
Suppose a finite positive wall function r(t) is specified for t in [l,A],
and, for every such t, both of the following hold:

* every real x with s(t)<=|x|<=r(t) has (H_t(x),H'_t(x))!=(0,0);
* every zero z with |Re z|>=r(t) is real and simple.

If r(t)<s(t), the first interval is empty and the interior/tail overlap
covers the intervening real axis. No monotonicity of r is needed for
this logical composition; its numerical certificate must of course
cover the full stated domain. All real abscissae are covered by
|x|<s(t), the zero-free boundary, the exterior, and the closed tail.
Therefore H_t has no multiple real zero for all t in [l,A].

If Lambda>=l, then 0<Lambda<=A and Theorem S in THEOREM.md supplies
a multiple real zero at time Lambda. This contradicts the band
certificate, including either endpoint. Hence **Lambda<l**.
If Lambda<=0, the same conclusion holds trivially since l>0; no use
of Rodgers--Tao is necessary for this upper-bound implication.

RH-height dependency: the only finite-height input is the separator's
initialization s(0)/2<=T_PT and any RH dependency already discharged
by the anchor. For the chosen banked anchor A=3/16 [V1-H-213;
second line V1-H-220], its height is 6000000185827/2 and its exact margin
350479773/2. Under PT2021 and the explicitly stated certificates this
implication is unconditional in RH. It is still CONDITIONAL ON THOSE
CERTIFICATES, which this lemma does not claim to provide.

## 5. Coverage contract and nonclaims

The t axis is [0,A] for the separator, [l,A] for the exclusion and
practical tail, and a positive LEFT neighbourhood for the stall tail.
The x/N axis is all real x, with every RS cell and transition in the
finite exterior explicitly charged by that future certificate; the
remote stall-tail proof uses no RS transition leg. The y axis is the
entire separator segment [-1,1], the real axis for the exterior pair,
and ALL complex zeros in the tail. No y0^2/2 penalty is present.

The logic remains valid at s(t)=r(t) since the endpoint belongs to both
closed pieces. A gap between supplied certificate domains cannot be
excused by this theorem. Off-axis nonvanishing at a positive y-floor
is not a tail-real-simplicity or a real-double-zero certificate.

No numerical separator, practical tail, exterior corner, selected l,
new record or claim of a published bound is made. This is one lemma
package: the mathematical proofs, a standalone verification script,
the independent arithmetic audit and clean-copy logs. Its externally
accepted second-line rung must be recorded before a record consumes it.
