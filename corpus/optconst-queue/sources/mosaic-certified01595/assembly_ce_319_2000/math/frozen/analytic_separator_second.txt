## 2. All-m parabolic leading term from the PDE

Let a real zero of F at (tau,b) have exact multiplicity m>=2 with leading
coefficient c != 0 in its z Taylor expansion. Differentiating F_t=-F_zz
repeatedly gives

    partial_t^j partial_z^k F=(-1)^j partial_z^(2j+k) F.

Set h=r^2 and z=b+r*w. All terms in the convergent joint Taylor series
with 2j+k<m vanish. The terms of degree m in r are therefore

    F(tau+r^2,b+r*w)=c*r^m*(P_m(w)+O(r)),
    F(tau-r^2,b+r*w)=c*r^m*(Q_m(w)+O(r)),
    P_m(w)=m!*sum_j (-1)^j*w^(m-2j)/(j!*(m-2j)!),
    Q_m(w)=m!*sum_j w^(m-2j)/(j!*(m-2j)!).

The O(r) is uniform on every fixed compact w set: after substitution the
convergent analytic series is divisible by r^m, with the displayed constant
coefficient; Cauchy convergence controls the remainder. This is an all-m
identity, not extrapolation from m<=12. Symbolic controls use the finite
heat exponential applied to an arbitrary polynomial germ with coefficients
in degrees m through m+3; each lower parabolic degree is checked zero and
the leading coefficient matched independently.

The exact relation is Q_m(w)=i^(-m)*P_m(i*w). The normalization matters:
changing the sign of the PDE swaps the forward/backward orientations.

## 3. Different universal proof: Rodrigues and Gaussian orthogonality

Let G(w)=exp(-w^2/4) and define

    R_m(w)=(-2)^m*G(w)^(-1)*d^m/dw^m G(w).

The generating-function identity is

    sum_(m>=0) R_m(w)*u^m/m! = G(w-2u)/G(w) = exp(w*u-u^2).

Comparison with the explicit coefficient formula in section 2 gives
R_m=P_m for **every m**. In particular P_m is real monic of degree m and
has parity (-1)^m. For each real polynomial q of degree less than m,

    integral_R P_m(w)*q(w)*G(w) dw
      =(-2)^m integral_R q(w)*G^(m)(w) dw = 0.

This is integration by parts m times. Every boundary term vanishes because
it is a polynomial times a derivative of the Gaussian; at the end q^(m)=0.
All integrals converge absolutely. Thus no unresolved numerical integral
or finite-degree induction is being used as an all-m premise.

Here is the root argument in full. Let a_1,...,a_k be the distinct real
roots of P_m of odd multiplicity, and put q(w)=product_(j=1)^k(w-a_j).
If k<m then deg q<m. Factor P_m over the reals: each nonreal conjugate pair
contributes a strictly positive quadratic on the real line, each remaining
real-root factor after multiplying q has even exponent, and the leading
coefficient is positive. Therefore P_m(w)*q(w)>=0 everywhere and is positive
on a nonempty open interval. Since G(w)>0, its integral is strictly positive,
contradicting orthogonality. Hence k=m. By degree there are exactly m distinct
real roots, each of multiplicity one, and there are no other roots.

For m>=2, at least two roots are nonzero: parity pairs the nonzero roots,
and simplicity permits at most one root at zero. Thus Q_m has a nonreal
conjugate pair of imaginary simple roots. This proves the two collision
orientations for all multiplicities using positivity, not the producer's
