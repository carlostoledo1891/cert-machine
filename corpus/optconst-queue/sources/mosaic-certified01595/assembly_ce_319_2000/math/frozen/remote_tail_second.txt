## B. Uniform analytic reduction, in independent order

Fix any allowed a and t and put `W=4*pi*exp(128/a)`. On `x>=W`, one has `L>=256` and `t*L>=128`. The lower bound `x>exp(L)>2^256` makes the input region and every denominator valid. Since `N<=sqrt(exp(L)+1/32)`,

`log N <= L/2+1`, and `log(q/n^2)>=-log(1+1/(32*q))>-1` for n<=N.

The latter lower bound uses the exact cutoff inequality, not the looser log-N estimate. This distinction protects the squared logarithm in eAB.

For `0<=y<=1`, the sigma correction is smaller than `1/8`. Thus the logarithmic exponent of `b_n*n^(-sigma)` is bounded by

`-log(n)*[(1+y)/2+t*L/8-t/4-1/8] <= -(65/4+y/2)*log(n)`.

It follows that the coefficient magnitude is at most `n^(-16)`, with no cutoff dependence. The gamma factor splits into `exp(y/50)<2` and `(n/sqrt(q))^y<2`. Moreover

`|kappa|*log(N) <= (L+2)/(4*x) <1/2`,

so its exponential is <2. Both chiralities together have the factor bound `1+2*2*2=9`. Therefore the discarded terms n>=2 cost at most

`9*S`, where `S=2^(-16)*(1+2/15)`.

This is the integral test for the decreasing function u^(-16), retaining n=2 and bounding the remaining tail by its integral from 2.

For eAB, the exponent is at most

`U(L)=2*(L^2/64+1)*exp(-L)`.

The derivative of `p(L)*exp(-L)` is `(p'(L)-p(L))*exp(-L)`. Our exact-polynomial checker translates L to `256+h` and verifies every coefficient of the derivative polynomial is negative. It does so independently for all four functions used here, so the reduction is a proof for every L>=256, not sampled endpoint evidence. In particular `U(L)<2050/2^256`. Since U<1/2, `expm1(U)<=2U`, and `9*(1+S)<10`, hence `eAB <=20*2050/2^256`.

For eC, `3^y+3^(-y)<=4` follows from monotonicity on [0,1] and the even hyperbolic-cosine expression. Since N>=16 and `|L+i*pi/2|<=L+2`, its positive exponent is at most

`(31/25)*4/(16-1/8)+(6*L+34)*exp(-L) <1`.

Drop only the negative `-t*L^2/16`; retaining `q^(-(1+y)/4)<=exp(-L/4)` gives `eC<3/2^64`. Again the L-dependent positive correction is decreasing by an exact shifted-polynomial certificate.

Define

`epsilon=9*(2^(-16)+2^(-15)/15)+20*2050/2^256+3/2^64`.

Our checker re-derives this rational and proves `epsilon<1/1024`. The whole analytic inequality is

`|H_t-G| <= epsilon*max(|A|,|B|)`

on x>=W and |y|<=1. It first holds for y>=0 and then for y<=0 because H and G are real-type, while A and B swap under conjugation. The value estimate is pointwise at every RS cutoff, including the endpoint. Both H and G are analytic independently of N. No index transition, differentiated discontinuity, or unverified analytic interpolation has entered the argument.

## C. Independent phase proof and branch audit

Use the logarithmic prefactor obtained by adding principal logs of s and s-1 in the fixed upper or lower half planes. Those domains contain every contour in question and a neighbourhood of it: the imaginary part of u or v stays bounded away from zero. Thus M_t is holomorphic, nonzero and conjugation-compatible, and a single logarithm of it is available on each relevant half plane.

Let `m_t=log(M_0)+t*alpha^2/4` on these branches and set

`K=(m_t(u)+m_t(v))/2`, `Theta=(m_t(u)-m_t(v))/(2i)`.

Then `G=2*exp(K)*cos(Theta)`. Direct differentiation gives

`Theta'=[beta(u)+beta(v)]/4`, `beta=alpha+(t/2)*alpha*alpha'`,

where `alpha'=-1/(2s^2)-1/(s-1)^2+1/(2s)`. The signs of the two chain factors are both positive after division by 2i. Our local automatic-differentiation checks form logarithmic prefactors as Arb complex power series and differentiate them, a representation independent of the producer's symbolic helper and pointwise beta formula. These are only normalization tests; the following inequalities give universality.

For `s=(h +/- i*x)/2`, `0<=h<=2`, write the leading logarithm as `L +/- i*pi/2`. Its difference from `Log(s/(2*pi))` is `Log(1+w)` with |w|<=2/x. The absolutely convergent logarithm series gives error <=4/x. The rational terms of alpha cost at most 1/x and 2/x. Hence

`|alpha-(L/2 +/- i*pi/4)|<=5/x`, `|alpha|<=L/2+2`, and `|alpha'|<=2/x`.

As t/2<=1/4, each beta differs from its respective leading value by at most `(L/4+6)/x`. Averaging the two cancels the opposite leading imaginary terms and yields

`|Theta'(x+iy)-L/4| <= (L/8+3)/x <= (L/8+3)*exp(-L) <1/8`.

The last function is decreasing on L>=256, certified in the exact-polynomial layer. Therefore `Re Theta'>=511/8` on the closed strip. On the real axis Theta is real, strictly increasing and unbounded. Every next phase node `Theta(x_k)=k*pi` exists uniquely. Its gap from a preceding point before that next level is at most `pi/(511/8)<1`. The checker additionally verifies the sharper bound below one twentieth, but this sharper bound is not needed for the theorem.

## D. Contour quantifiers, including the first partial cell

Let x_0 be the first phase node at or to the right of W, then enumerate all later consecutive nodes. The positive derivative implies `W<=x_0<W+1` and x_k tends to infinity. For each k use the rectangle `[x_k,x_(k+1)]+i[-1,1]`.

On a vertical side, integrating `dTheta/dy=i*Theta'` gives a change in Re Theta of magnitude at most 1/8 from k*pi. This uses the imaginary part of Theta', not its real part. If Theta=theta+i*eta and `r=exp(-2*|eta|)`, direct normalization gives

`|G|^2/max(|A|,|B|)^2=1+r^2+2*r*cos(2*theta)`.

Here cos(2*theta)>0 on those sides; the model dominates max(|A|,|B|). On the horizontal sides, integrating Re Theta' from the real axis gives `|eta|>=511/8`. The reverse triangle inequality then bounds the same model ratio below `1-exp(-511/4)>1/2`. All four edge comparisons exceed epsilon strictly, including the corners.

Rouche applies to H and G because both are analytic on a neighbourhood of the full closed rectangle. G has exactly one simple zero there: Im Theta has the strict sign of y off the real axis, and Theta runs strictly through exactly one zero value of cosine on the real segment. Its derivative does not vanish. H therefore also has exactly one zero counted with multiplicity. Real-type symmetry of H and conjugation invariance of the rectangle force this unique zero to be real; count one forces simplicity.

The boundaries are zero-free, so adjacent closed rectangles glue without a vertical-edge gap. Their union covers all real parts >=W+1. The earlier partial cell is NOT claimed; the explicit +1 pays for it. Zeros outside |Im z|<=1 are excluded by the classical de Bruijn strip bound for t>=0. Evenness gives the negative real half-plane. This proves the stated remote tail for every real a,t, with unbounded N and all imaginary parts. There is no grid extrapolation.

## E. Stall implication checked independently

At positive Lambda the real-zero property holds by the threshold characterization, or by Hurwitz/Rouche and local uniform convergence from larger t. The limiting function is not zero identically, since its Fourier-kernel representation has H_Lambda(0)>0.

Choose a=Lambda/2. De Bruijn's classical upper bound gives Lambda<=1/2. The remote theorem then supplies the SINGLE explicit radius

`R=4*pi*exp(256/Lambda)+1`

for every t in `[Lambda/2,1/2]`, including a whole left neighbourhood of Lambda. If all zeros at Lambda were simple, choose B>R avoiding their real abscissae. The rectangle `|Re z|<=B, |Im z|<=1` has only finitely many zeros, all real and simple, and none on its boundary. Disjoint small real-centred disks isolate those zeros. The complement of the disk interiors in the rectangle is compact and zero-free, so H_t remains zero-free there for all t sufficiently near Lambda. Rouche preserves count one in each disk; conjugation makes each continued zero real.

For a t strictly between Lambda/2 and Lambda and sufficiently close to Lambda, every remaining zero outside the compact rectangle is real by the uniform tail, and the de Bruijn strip excludes larger imaginary parts. Thus H_t would have only real zeros below Lambda, a contradiction. The upper endpoint Lambda=1/2 causes no difficulty: only the left neighbourhood is used. Initial simplicity from RH is not used anywhere.
