## 1. A univariate circular heat lemma by root dynamics

Write D=z*d/dz. Let q have degree n, no zero in |z|<=1, and let C>=n/4.
For h>=0 put q_h=exp(h*(D^2/4-C*D))q, coefficientwise; thus it has the
same degree and constant term for finite h. We prove all roots stay
outside |z|<=1. First suppose q has distinct roots; write those roots
locally as r_i(h), with their multiplicities initially all one. Since

  partial_h q_h=(z^2/4)q_h''+(1/4-C)z*q_h',

implicit differentiation at a simple root gives

  r_i'=(C-1/4)*r_i-(r_i^2/2)*sum_(j!=i)1/(r_i-r_j),
  d/dh log|r_i|=C-1/4-(1/2)*sum_(j!=i)Re(r_i/(r_i-r_j)).       (R)

If |r_i| is the MINIMUM root modulus, then for every other root
v=r_j/r_i, |v|>=1 and v!=1. The exact identity

   Re(1/(1-v))=1/2-(|v|^2-1)/(2*|1-v|^2)<=1/2

implies

                    d/dh log|r_i| >= C-n/4 >=0.             (Rmin)

On any compact time interval where all roots are distinct, finitely many
analytic root branches are available. Their log moduli are differentiable;
the minimum is locally Lipschitz, and its derivative almost everywhere is
a derivative of an active minimum branch. Thus(Rmin) makes the minimum
nondecreasing. No assertion is made for a branch that is not minimal.

There are only FINITELY many root-collision times on any compact h interval
for this distinct-start polynomial. Its discriminant is a finite linear
combination of exponentials in h, hence real analytic on a neighbourhood
of that compact interval and nonzero at h=0. Its zeros there are isolated
and finite. The constant coefficient stays nonzero, and the leading
coefficient stays nonzero, so no root is zero or infinite at finite h.
Roots as a multiset, and their minimum modulus, are continuous through
collision times. The nondecrease on each intervening interval joins
through those finitely many times. This proves the assertion for all h.

For repeated roots at the starting time, perturb the FINITELY many roots
slightly to distinct points. If the original minimum radius is rho>1,
choose every perturbed radius at least some fixed rho_* with1<rho_*<rho.
The coefficients of these q^(m) tend to q, and the same is true after
applying the fixed finite-dimensional multiplier. Their evolved roots
stay of modulus>=rho_*. Continuity of roots (degree and leading coefficient
are unchanged in the limit) gives the same bound for q_h. Since rho_*
can increase to rho, the original minimum root radius cannot decrease.
This also proves the lemma for lower-than-n actual degree by using that
actual degree; a constant has no roots and is fixed.
