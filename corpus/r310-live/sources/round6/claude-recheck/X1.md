## L1. G contains no K3,6 and no K4,5

Let G be triangle-free on 40 vertices with α(G) ≤ 9, and suppose G ⊇ K_{s,t} with parts A (|A|=s), B (|B|=t),
s,t ≥ 1. A and B are independent (two vertices of A share a neighbour in B, and vice versa). A vertex w outside
A ∪ B cannot have a neighbour in A and one in B (a ~ b would close a triangle). So V \ (A∪B) = X ⊔ Y ⊔ Z with X
touching A only, Y touching B only, Z touching neither. Y ∪ Z is anticomplete to A, so any independent set in it
extends by A: α(G[Y∪Z]) ≤ 9 − s, hence |Y∪Z| ≤ R(3,10−s) − 1. Likewise |X∪Z| ≤ R(3,10−t) − 1. Then
40 ≤ s + t + |X∪Z| + |Y∪Z|.
- K3,6: 40 ≤ 3 + 6 + (R(3,7)−1) + (R(3,4)−1) = 9 + 22 + 8 = 39. Contradiction.
- K4,5: 40 ≤ 4 + 5 + (R(3,6)−1) + (R(3,5)−1) = 9 + 17 + 13 = 39. Contradiction.
By symmetry of K_{s,t} this also excludes K6,3 and K5,4 (same graphs).

## §3.2–3.4: trace patterns A, B, C

**Setting.** S is independent with |S| = k and α(G) ≤ k (in the target k = 9; R(3,9) = 36 gives α(G) = 9, so a
root S9 exists, regular or not). For x ∉ S, T(x) = N(x) ∩ S (full trace; nonempty since S is maximum).
**Card lemma.** For J ⊆ S, the vertices X_J = {x : T(x) ⊆ J} are anticomplete to S \ J, so every independent set I
of G[J ∪ X_J] gives the independent set I ∪ (S\J): α(G[J ∪ X_J]) ≤ |J|. In particular, for |J| = 4, a set of
5 independent vertices among J ∪ X_J is impossible. **Forcing rule.** If P ⊆ J ∪ X_J has 5 vertices whose only
undetermined pair is uv (all other pairs are root–root, a root outside the other's trace, or externals with
meeting traces), then u ~ v.

**Pattern A / B** (J = {a,b,c,d}; externals A, B, AB, C, AC, AD, BD, Z with the subscript as trace; Z = {b,c,d}
for A, Z = {c,d} for B). All eight lie in X_J.
1. {c, d, A, B, AB}: c,d are in no trace here; A–AB and B–AB share a root; only A–B is open ⇒ A ~ B.
2. {b, d, A, C, AC}: b,d in no trace; A–AC, C–AC share a root ⇒ A ~ C.
3. {c, A, AB, AD, BD}: c in no trace; A, AB, AD pairwise share a; AB–BD share b; AD–BD share d ⇒ A ~ BD.
4. Z = {b,c,d}: {A, AB, AC, AD, Z}: A, AB, AC, AD pairwise share a; Z meets AB, AC, AD in b, c, d ⇒ A ~ Z.
   Z = {c,d}: {b, A, AC, AD, Z}: b in none of these traces; A, AC, AD share a; Z meets AC in c, AD in d ⇒ A ~ Z.
5. N(A) ⊇ {a, B, C, BD, Z}, five vertices of J ∪ X_J, independent because G is triangle-free. This contradicts the
   card lemma. Hence the eight traces never occur together (needs k ≥ 4 only).

**Pattern C** (roots a,b,c,d,e; externals x_c, x_abc¹, x_abc², x_bd, x_cd, x_e, x_ce, x_be, x_bde).
- Card J1 = {a,b,c,d} holds x_c, x_abc¹, x_abc², x_bd, x_cd. In {x_c, x_abc¹, x_abc², x_bd, x_cd} every pair shares
  a root except x_c–x_bd ⇒ x_c ~ x_bd.
- Card J2 = {b,c,d,e} holds x_c, x_bd, x_cd, x_e, x_ce, x_be, x_bde. {b, d, x_c, x_e, x_ce}: b,d in none of
  {c},{e},{c,e}; x_ce shares with both; only x_c–x_e open ⇒ x_c ~ x_e.
- {c, x_bd, x_e, x_be, x_bde}: c in none of these traces; every external pair shares b or e or d except
  x_bd–x_e ⇒ x_bd ~ x_e.
- x_c, x_bd, x_e is a triangle. Contradiction (needs k ≥ 5; the two copies of {a,b,c} must be distinct vertices).

## §4.2–4.4: the codegree-5 cliques (9-regular)

**Setting.** Roots r_1..r_m pairwise non-adjacent with |N_i ∩ N_j| = 5 (an H5-clique is independent in G: adjacent
vertices of a triangle-free graph have no common neighbour). N_U = |∩_{u∈U} N_u|; exact cells x_T by Möbius
inversion. Valid constraints: (C1) x_T ≥ 0; (C2) the exterior of U (vertices outside U with no neighbour in U)
has α ≤ 9 − |U|, so size ≤ R(3, 10−|U|) − 1; (C3) L1: N_U ≤ 5, 4, 3, 2 for |U| = 3, 4, 5, 6.

**Four roots.** |∪N_i| = 36 − 30 + Σt − q = 6 + Σt − q, exterior = 30 − Σt + q ≤ 17 (R(3,6)), so Σt − q ≥ 13.
Pair cells: x_ij = 5 − t_ijk − t_ijl + q ≥ 0. Summing over the six pairs: 3Σt ≤ 30 + 6q, so 13 + q ≤ Σt ≤ 10 + 2q
and q ≥ 3; q ≤ 4 by K4,5. q = 3: Σt = 16 and every pair sum ≤ 8, so t = (4,4,4,4) [type A]. q = 4: t_i ≥ q = 4,
Σt ≥ 17 and pair sums ≤ 9, so exactly one 5: (5,4,4,4) [type B, four labellings]. Both have all cells ≥ 0 and
exterior 17 (a (3,6;17) graph, α ≤ 5). Five labelled rows.

**Five roots.** Mark ij when t_{[5]∖{i,j}} = 5. The four-root subset omitting l contains the triples [5]∖{l,m},
and has at most one t = 5, so each l lies in at most one mark: a matching, degrees d_i. The quad omitting i has
Q_i = 3 + d_i (type A ↔ no mark at i). Möbius: x_{[5]∖{i,j}} = (4 + 1_ij) − Q_i − Q_j + p = p − 2 + 1_ij − d_i − d_j.
An unmatched i gives p ≤ Q_i = 3. Two marks ij, kl: the cell [5]∖{i,k} needs p ≥ 4. One mark ij: an unmarked pair
{i,m} needs p ≥ 3, so p = 3, and then x_{ij} = 5 − (4+4+4) + (3+3+3) − 3 = −1. No marks: triple cells need p ≥ 2,
pair cells x_ij = 5 − 12 + 9 − p = 2 − p need p ≤ 2. So p = 2: quad cells 1 each, singletons
9 − 20 + 24 − 12 + 2 = 3 each, all else 0; union 2 + 5 + 15 = 22, exterior 13 with α ≤ 4, so a (3,5;13) graph,
the unique one, C13(1,5) (L2).

**Six roots.** Each 5-subset has the unique type: N_U = 4, 3, 2 for |U| = 3, 4, 5; p = N_[6]. Cells: size 5:
2 − p; size 4: 3 − 2·2 + p = p − 1; size 3: 4 − 3·3 + 3·2 − p = 1 − p. So p = 1; then sizes 2, 3, 4 are 0,
size 5 is 1, singletons 9 − 25 + 40 − 30 + 10 − 1 = 3; union 1 + 6 + 18 = 25, exterior 9 with α ≤ 3,
impossible by R(3,4) = 9. H5 is K6-free.
