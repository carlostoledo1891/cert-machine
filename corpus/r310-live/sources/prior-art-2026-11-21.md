# R(3,10) prior-art scout (PRIVATE), 2026-11-21

## 1. Bounds and what remains
- **Lower bound 40:** Exoo, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 2(4) (1989) 488–490.
- **Upper bounds:**
  - ≤43: Radziszowski–Kreher, JCMCC 4 (1988).
  - ≤42: Goedgebeur–Radziszowski (GR), EJC 20(1) P30 (2013), arXiv 1210.5826. Any 42-vertex graph must be 9-regular; all (3,9;32,108)-graphs were then glued.
  - **≤41: Angeltveit, sole author.** arXiv 2401.00392 (Dec 2023); EJC 32(4) P4.30, published 3 Nov 2025, doi 10.37236/12936. McKay only ran sanity checks. Method: some vertex has G−N[v] in R(3,9,32,≤112) (1.55×10⁹ graphs), R(3,9,33,≤121) (14,395) or R(3,9,34,≤130) (5); these were glued, about 3 CPU-years.
- **What remains:** showing that no (3,10;40)-graph exists.
- **Known facts about such a graph:**
  - Δ = 9 and 161 ≤ e ≤ 180 (GR Table 5).
  - If it is 9-regular, every G−N[v] is a (3,9;30,99)-graph. Angeltveit calls that class many orders of magnitude larger; Unsolved Lab estimates about 10¹⁸ graphs.
  - At least 43,146,537 (3,10;39)-graphs are known.
- **Pandey–Ravi**, arXiv 2601.03572 (Jan 2026): the diameter is 2 or 3; there is a degree-4 structure lemma; if the diameter is 2, then δ ≥ 6. The proofs are terse.
- **No degree-4 vertex (δ ≥ 5)**, all unrefereed SAT proofs:
  - Daugherty–Ward–Ryan, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.22945442, 24 Sep 2026 (the earliest).
  - hraness/algal-lab, 28 Sep 2026 (the release URL now returns 404).
  - Unsolved Lab M09, 1 Oct 2026.
  - Chen, Oct 2026.
- **Chen**, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.23678020 (26 Oct 2026), which credits algal-lab:
  - One may assume the graph is maximal triangle-free (MTF) without loss of generality, and then δ ≥ 6.
  - 545 degree distributions remain possible.
  - Gluing (enumeration-only): a degree-8 vertex has e(G−N[v]) ≥ 97, and a degree-9 vertex has e(G−N[v]) ≥ 88.
- **McCrea**, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.23928054 (3 Nov 2026): no degree-7 vertex with e(G−N[v]) = 104.

## 2. (3,9)-graphs
- **The 35-vertex graph is unique:** GR Thm 3, the circulant {1,7,11,16}, 8-regular with 140 edges. McKay's page lists only r39_35.g6 (1 graph) and no smaller files.
- **e(3,9,n)** for n = 30…35: 86, 95, 104, 118, 129, 140.
- **(3,9;34)-graphs by edge count** (GR Table 14): 129: 1, 130: 4, 131: ≥15, 132: ≥40, 133: ≥54, 134: ≥43, 135: ≥20, 136: ≥7. That is **at least 184, and the total is unknown**. C35 minus a vertex has 132 edges, an incomplete slice.
- **Your reasoning** (d ≥ 4; d = 4 forces C35; d = 5 gives a 34-vertex core) is stated in Pandey–Ravi §3, Daugherty–Ward–Ryan, Chen and Unsolved Lab.

## 5. The C35 statement
- **Not found anywhere.** M20 is still open; Chen uses C35 only as a control.
- **Note:** C35 is maximal triangle-free (Δ ≤ 8 caps a 35-vertex (3,9)-graph at 140 edges). So "contains" means "induced" here, and the property survives adding edges to reach an MTF graph.
- **Extending (3,9;34)-graphs:**
  - At 40 vertices it is done only under diameter 2 or for MTF witnesses.
  - Angeltveit glued the five graphs with e ≤ 130, but at 41 vertices.
  - Daugherty–Ward–Ryan show that case 5 needs every slice with e in [129,136].

## Assessment
- **New, and partly known.** The degree-4 corollary is known four times over, all unrefereed.
- **What is new:**
  - Any triangle-free graph with α ≤ 9 that contains C35 has at most 39 vertices, whatever the degrees.
  - At 39 vertices the outside vertices induce C4, which is sharp: Unsolved Lab notes Exoo's graph is "C35 plus four vertices, in exactly one way".
  - It answers M20, in the negative and in a stronger form.
  - The certificate is LRAT plus a Lean bridge; the bridge rests on a native_decide axiom.
- **Small weight for R(3,10).** Witnesses can be taken MTF with δ ≥ 6, and the likely graph is near 9-regular, so C35-containing graphs are a corner.

## Next rung
- **Skip degree 5 over all (3,9;34)-graphs.** It is moot because δ ≥ 6 can be assumed, and the census is incomplete (at least 184 graphs).
- **C35−v is cheap but peripheral:** 6 outside vertices, the same scale as M20.
- **The main line is the degree-9 window.** Chen's Theorem A says some vertex has d = 9 with e ≤ 99, or d = 8 with e ≤ 96. Combined with his gluing bounds, this gives (my inference, not checked): **R(3,10) = 40 if no (3,10;40)-graph has a degree-9 vertex with G−N[v] in R(3,9;30, 88…99).**
  - The cheap rungs are e = 88, 89, … by gluing or per-slice certificates.
  - Counts are known only up to 87: R(3,9;30,86) = 1,800 and R(3,9;30,87) = 147,335.
  - e = 99 is about 10¹⁸ graphs.
