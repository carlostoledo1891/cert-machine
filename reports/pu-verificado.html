Tesouro Direto prices re-decided from the official rule table

PU Verificado

Tesouro Direto publishes a price for every federal bond it sells, every business day, and the Treasury publishes the formula that produces it — down to a table saying which intermediate value is truncated and which is rounded, and at how many decimals. So each published price has exactly one value under its own rule. This page re-decides every fixed-rate price in the public history, 111,101 of them, in exact arithmetic: 110,176 are the rule’s value to the centavo, 887 are not, 0 could not be decided. The conventions the publisher follows but does not print — which day a purchase settles, whether the centavos are cut or rounded — are measured, not assumed.

The pinned history precotaxatesourodireto.csv (Tesouro Transparente, ODbL; 55,588 rows of Tesouro Prefixado = LTN and Tesouro Prefixado com Juros Semestrais = NTN-F, 2004-12-31 to 2026-10-15, 61 titles), the ANBIMA national-holiday file to 2099, and the STN methodology of 29/04/2024 whose Tabela 3 is the rule (= ANBIMA 2021 §22), every byte sha256-pinned in corpus/pu/PINS.json. Pre-registered in corpus/pu/preregistration.json, with 2 dated amendments. Both sides of every row (compra, venda) under the pre-registered settlement hypotheses and printed readings. Phase A: fixed-rate titles only; the indexed titles (Selic, IPCA+), ANBIMA’s secondary-market prices and B3’s are phase B.

tl;dr
  • The finding. 99.17% of 111,101 prices are the rule’s value to the centavo. The census decides the conventions: a purchase settles on the next business day (D+1, 54,932 buy-side prices, the whole history); a sale settled on the next business day too until 2021-09-10 (42,728 prices) and on the quotation day since (D+0, 14,235 prices; every sell-side price from 2022 on); and the centavos are the 6-decimal price truncated, never rounded (55,005 prices reproduce only under truncation; 1 only under rounding). 17,911 prices reproduce only under the calendar in force on the day they were published: 20 November became a national holiday on 2023-12-21 (Lei 14.759), today’s holiday file carries it from 2024 on, and every earlier quotation of a title maturing after 2024 was priced without it — one business day more per weekday 20 November between settlement and maturity. The data place the publisher’s switch to the new calendar at the quotation of 2023-12-26 (the last quotation priced on the old calendar: 2023-12-22). 259 buy-side prices reproduce only when 24 and 31 December are skipped as settlement days — days the published calendar omits by its own note. 887 prices are OFF: not the rule’s value under any pre-registered settlement; 0 REFUSED (the enclosure straddled a truncation boundary). Each is listed with its exact value and the difference in centavos.
  • The mechanism. Every step of the Treasury’s formula is exact: business days on the pinned calendar, the rate truncated at 6 decimals, the exponent du/252 truncated at 14, each NTN-F flow rounded at 9, the price truncated at 6. The one irrational step, (1 + taxa)^(du/252), is an enclosure — BigInt fixed point with every series remainder bounded — and each truncation is a decision from the two ends or a refusal. A second decider in Python (decimal, 60 digits, directed rounding; no shared code) re-derives every OFF and REFUSED row and a seeded sample (2,387/2,387 agree). The methodology’s own worked examples (LTN 753.315323, NTN-F 903.075616 with its twelve flows) are reproduced digit for digit before any row is decided.
  • Check it. node tools/run-pu-ledger.js (about ten minutes on a laptop) · node instruments/pu/battery.js — 13 checks, 7 red controls · python3 -I instruments/pu/decide.py re-decides the worked examples with no engine present.

§1 · the atlas

Every title, every day, one verdict

The field is the result: an almost uniform texture of reproduced prices, with the exceptions where they are. The buy side is drawn because it carries the settlement convention; the sell side (D+0) reproduces at 99.01% and its exceptions are in the table of §3.

§2 · the census

The conventions the publisher follows, measured

Three things the methodology does not say about Tesouro Direto’s published prices were pre-registered as hypotheses and decided row by row: on which day a purchase and a sale settle, and how a 6-decimal price becomes the 2-decimal one printed. The census answers: buy D+1 across the whole history; sell D+1 until 2021-09-10, sell D+0 since — a change of settlement convention made during 2021 and not printed beside the price (752 sell-side prices of 2021 reproduce only under D+0, every one from 2022 on; 27 earlier prices do too, on 6 days where both settlements were quoted); centavos truncated throughout. A calendar is dated. The pinned holiday file is the one ANBIMA serves today, and it carries a holiday that did not exist when most of these prices were published: 20 November, national since Lei 14.759 of 21 December 2023. A quotation of 2019 for a title maturing in 2029 was priced with the calendar of 2019, in which 2024, 2025, 2026 and 2028 each had one more business day than today’s file says (2027’s 20 November is a Saturday). Re-decided with today’s calendar those prices are OFF by exactly that many days; re-decided with the calendar as of the quotation they are the rule’s value to the centavo. The pre-registration’s second amendment added that hypothesis after the full run showed the pattern (du + 1 for 2025 maturities, + 2 for 2026, + 4 for 2029 on a seeded sample of 30 rows); the rows that need it are the brighter lines in the atlas and the “calendar as of quote only” columns here. Around the law’s date the census reads, per quotation day, how many rows reproduce only under today’s calendar and how many only under the old one; the last day priced on the old calendar is 2023-12-22 and the first priced on the new one is 2023-12-26.

The settlement skip: Tesouro Direto quotes on 24 and 31 December but does not settle on them, and the ANBIMA holiday file says in its own notes that it lists only days that close bank reserves and omits the last business day of the year. The amendment of 2026-10-16 in the pre-registration added that hypothesis after the first 4,000 rows showed it; the rows that need it are counted apart, never folded into the primary count.

The first 80 of 887 rows; every row is in certs/pu-ledger.json (offRows). A difference of a few centavos with the sign of a later settlement is a settlement-day convention; a difference of about R$ 48 on an NTN-F quoted on the last business day of the year is a coupon gone ex because settlement crossed 1 January. Of the 887 rows, 664 are quotations whose next business day on the pinned calendar is a day the São Paulo exchange did not settle on — 25 January, 9 July and 20 November, which B3 observed as local holidays in those years, or 24 and 31 December — or sit in the last week of a year. The pattern says the settlement calendar is B3’s and not the bank calendar the methodology counts du on; B3’s historical trading calendar is not among the pinned sources, so these rows stay OFF here and the hypothesis is named for phase B. What is claimed: the printed number is not the methodology’s value for the printed rate on the pinned calendar under the pre-registered settlements. What is not claimed: that the publisher erred — a settlement rule or a calendar the publisher used and did not publish is a NEEDS DATA reading, and the pre-registration names it as such.

§4 · the certificate

One price, every step

The methodology’s own LTN example, re-decided here with every truncation shown. Maturity 01/07/2010, settlement 21/05/2008, rate 14,3600% a.a.

  • du = 532. Business days from settlement (inclusive) to maturity (exclusive) on the pinned ANBIMA calendar.
  • Exponent T-14. du/252 = 2.111111111111111… truncated at 14 decimals → 2.11111111111111 (the rule truncates; rounding would give the same digits here, and a different number for du = 5).
  • The irrational step as an enclosure. 1000 / (1.143600)^(2.11111111111111) ∈ [753.315323072994, 753.315323072994] — twelve decimals shown of forty-eight carried; the two ends agree on every digit the rule keeps.
