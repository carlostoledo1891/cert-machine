The Chvátal–Sankoff constant: two asterisked upper bounds replayed

Two asterisks on the Chvátal–Sankoff constant, replayed

On 2026-10-18 the optimization-constants registry merged PR #231: Lueker's 2009 upper-bound method for the binary Chvátal–Sankoff constant, run two history lengths past his paper, giving γ₂ ≤ 0.825101 at h = 9 and γ₂ ≤ 0.824120 at h = 10, below the 0.826280 that had stood since 2009. Both rows carry the registry's asterisk: every check so far was the claimant's own. Here the automaton is rebuilt from Lueker's paper and nothing else, in two implementations, calibrated on his own table, run with witness vectors of our own, and the printed bounds decided.

Published, not peer-reviewed. The claimant's code was neither read nor run: the automaton here is written from Lueker's Figure 7 and weights from his eq. (36); the claimant's commit supplies only the two printed bounds, the two values of z, and the state counts to compare against. Lueker's theorem and his proof that the automaton covers Σ* for every h are the paper's, peer-reviewed in 2009, and are used, not re-proved. The asterisk itself moves only by a pull request the maintainer merges.

§1 · the claim

What the registry prints, and what was checked before

The constant is the limit of E[LCS(x, y)]/n for two uniformly random binary strings of length n. Its value is unknown; simulations put it near 0.812. The best upper bound since 2009 was Lueker's 0.826280, from his automaton at history length h = 8. The claimant ran the same method at h = 9 and h = 10 and the registry merged both rows on 2026-10-18 with an asterisk, the mark it gives a bound replayed by nobody but its author. The claimant's own repository (cd99c689, pinned under corpus/optconst-queue/c31-2026-10-21) says what was checked: Lueker's unmodified Java agrees with the claimant's tables transition for transition at h ≤ 8 and on 200,000 random states at h = 9 and 10; the code reproduces Lueker's table; a second implementation of Figure 7, by the claimant in a later pass, re-derived every transition; the λ bound was also computed in a variant that does not depend on the rounding mode. A later commit corrects the README's "independent reviewer" to the claimant's own review pass, which is also what the e-mail asking for this replay said. What an outside replay adds is one thing only: another person's reading of Figure 7 and another arithmetic.

§2 · the method

Lueker's theorem, and the automaton read from his figure

The theorem. Lueker's Theorem 2 (journal Theorem 3.3), after Dančík–Paterson: M covers Σ*, z ∈ (0, 1/2), λ ∈ (0, 1), v > 0 with A_z v ≤ λ v component-wise ⇒ γ₂ ≤ 2 log(2z)/log λ; any positive v is a witness (eq. 41). The paper was read in the author's extended version (the author's extended version dated 2008-12-18 (41 pages), Wayback Machine capture of 2009-02-05; its Theorem 2, Figure 8 and Figure 9 are the journal's Theorem 3.3, Figure 7 and Table II; sha256 c662fc3b2473…), the journal article being closed and the author's page returning 403 since 2015.

The automaton. A state is (x, y, dx, dy): the last h characters of each string and the last h differences down the last column and along the last row of the dynamic-programming table. An input is a minimal match pair after Lueker's truncation μ: for a match character c, (c̄ᵃ c / c) with 0 ≤ a ≤ h−1 or (c / c̄ᵇ c) with 1 ≤ b ≤ h−1, 4h−2 inputs in all. The transition fills the new table entries with lower bounds (Figure 7, lines 7–13), backtracks canonically (lines 14–22) and rejects when the path misses the old corner. z^{a+2} for a < h−1, z^{h+1}/(1−z) at a = h−1 (eq. 36), rounded up from exact for the certificate. Figure 8 (journal Figure 7), transition by transition; states (x, y, dx, dy); one representative per complement orbit (x̄, ȳ, dx, dy), the symmetry Lueker names in §3.2 and checks here on every state at h = 4. The reference implementation (instruments/lueker/mh.py, standard library, exact where it can be) is the specification; the fast one (instruments/lueker/mh.c) is held to it: identical full tables at h ≤ 5, sampled states re-derived at h = 8, 9 and 10, the complement symmetry checked on every state at h = 4.

The certificate. λ_cert = max_s (A_z v)_s / v_s with FE_UPWARD in every thread (self-tested); λ_free the same in round-to-nearest inflated by (1+2^-52)^(4h−1); ρ_lower = min_s with FE_DOWNWARD, a lower bound on the spectral radius; the bound 2 ln(2z)/ln λ in 60-digit decimal, widened outward. The witness vector comes from power iteration, which only has to make it good; its quality shows in the spread between the largest and smallest ratio.

§6 · what is claimed, and what is not

The limits

  • Claimed. For each row, a certificate of our own — our automaton, our vector, directed rounding, the bound outward — that reaches the printed bound; the construction calibrated on Lueker's published table; the claimant's λ consistent with our certified bracket on the spectral radius.
  • Used, not re-proved. Lueker's Theorem 3.3 and his proof that M_h covers Σ* for every h (J. ACM 2009, peer-reviewed). A reader who doubts them doubts the 2009 bound too.
  • Not claimed. That the claimant's own vectors satisfy his inequality under our matrix — his vectors were offered and would make that a one-pass check; that the printed λ is the spectral radius (ours and his are both upper bounds on it, and the gap between them is the vectors' quality, not an error); anything about the lower bound rows of the same page.
  • Independence. From the claimant, whose code was never opened; not from the operator (one). The second implementation here is a reference the fast one is held to, not a second person.
