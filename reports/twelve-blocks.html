Twelve blocks on the integers: two AI models propose an exact minimum

I gave GPT-6.2 Astra (OpenAI) and Claude Fable 5.2 (Anthropic) one identical message and one shared folder, and asked what happens when the best known colouring for a question of Ron Graham’s, twelve blocks of red and blue, is put on the integers. In 73 minutes they proposed, and each read the other’s proof of, the exact least number of single-coloured 3-term progressions: at every length n = 548m with m even and at least 118, or odd and at least 123, and at every length from 92,000 on. With Claude (Anthropic’s AI model, and the same model as one of the two), I then decided the finite facts those proofs display: 10 statements, all certified, and 19 planted wrong versions, all refused. The theorem itself is not decided here. No machine has checked the proofs, nobody outside the two models has read them, and they rest on a lower bound that is this repository’s own and is not refereed.

What is still open

  • The theorem is not decided here. No proof on this page has been checked by a machine, and each was read once, by the other model. No reader outside the two model families has read any of it.
  • Everything rests on δ₃ ≥ 117/2192, which is not a published theorem: it is this repository’s own claim of October 2026, proved by five exact certificates that two independent programs accept, and not refereed. It also rests on two lemmas of that proof that are written on paper and that no machine has checked.
  • The lower bound of V is never computed here: no V(n) is known exactly beyond n = 45, and this repository recomputed it only to n = 14. What is decided on the lower side is the arithmetic the proofs display.
  • The window theorem is decided only on pieces of 24 sites. No full family of 44m sites was walked, at any scale.
  • Whether the twelve blocks are optimal at the smaller scales is open: every even m ≤ 116 and odd m ≤ 121, which includes n = 1,096. Neither model claims it. A plain single-flip search found nothing better at m ≤ 4.
  • The literature check was a web search of about thirty pages, not a survey; three older papers could not be opened.

the question

How few single-coloured progressions can a colouring have

Colour each of the integers 1 to n red or blue and count the triples a, a + d, a + 2d, with d ≥ 1, whose three members have one colour. Call the least possible count V(n). A random colouring has about n²/16. In 1999 Ron Graham offered $100 for the true constant.

In 2008 Parrilo, Robertson and Saracino found a colouring in twelve alternating blocks, of lengths proportional to

28 · 6 · 28 · 37 · 59 · 116 · 116 · 59 · 37 · 28 · 6 · 28 (out of 548)

that has (117/2192)·n² of them to first order, and conjectured that nothing does better. In October 2026 this repository published a proof of the matching lower bound, by five exact certificates; it is its own report, and it has not been refereed.

That result is about the leading term. On the integers, with n = 548m and every block m times as long, the twelve blocks have exactly 117n²/2192 − n/2 single-coloured progressions when m is even, and one more when m is odd. The question I put to the two models: is that the minimum, exactly, once m is large?

the run

One message, one folder, two models

  • One message. Both chats received the same line, pointing at one folder with the question in it (corpus/twelve-blocks/twelve-block-session.md is the brief; corpus/twelve-blocks/run/GOAL.md is what the folder told them was already known).
  • One conversation. Each model wrote only in its own subfolder, could read the other’s at any time, and talked to the other through a shared log: corpus/twelve-blocks/run/chat/gpt.jsonl and corpus/twelve-blocks/run/chat/claude.jsonl. I relayed nothing between them.
  • A control room. A third chat answered their questions, brought them the files of the lower-bound proof, counted the colourings they submitted, and planted one false claim, which both caught. It had no say in the mathematics.
  • Reading before building. A model could not check its own claim. Each claim below was read by the other model, from a derivation of its own where it says so.

2 · the argument

Four steps, and who took each

  • A strict bound near the optimum. The lower-bound proof shows that no colouring beats the twelve blocks in the limit, but it gives no margin for colourings close to them. GPT-6.2 Astra found one: add a rank-one term to one certificate, and the same verifier still accepts it. A colouring that differs from the twelve blocks on a fraction μ of the interval then pays at least μ²/1024. Claude Fable 5.2 pushed the constant to 948 and showed 947 fails.
  • An exact identity on the integers. The count on the integers equals the continuous functional plus terms that can be written down exactly: the two parity sums, and a correction that only sees neighbouring sites of different colours. GPT-6.2 Astra wrote it for even n, Claude Fable 5.2 for every n.
  • Few flips, all near the boundaries. Together these say a colouring at or below the twelve blocks differs from them in fewer than 244 sites. A flipped site far from every block boundary costs about 2m, more than those few flips can win back once m ≥ 118 (even) or 123 (odd). GPT-6.2 Astra had this from m ≥ 192; a sharper count by Claude Fable 5.2, with a deletion argument of GPT-6.2 Astra’s, brought it down.
  • Near the boundaries, an enumeration. Within 2m sites of the eleven boundaries the cost of any pattern of flips is a positive definite quadratic form in 22 integers. Its non-positive points can be listed: only the twelve blocks at even m, and 51 patterns at odd m. GPT-6.2 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2 each did this with their own program.

The same four steps, with the rounded colouring in place of the twelve blocks, give the bounds at every length and the table of 1,096 corrections. The proofs are corpus/twelve-blocks/run/gpt/global.md, corpus/twelve-blocks/run/gpt/windows.md, corpus/twelve-blocks/run/gpt/sharper-cutoffs.md, corpus/twelve-blocks/run/gpt/rounding.md, corpus/twelve-blocks/run/gpt/periodic-optimum.md and corpus/twelve-blocks/run/claude/NOTES-alln.md.

4 · one lemma in Lean

The step from two scales to every scale

  • The lemma. The cost of flipping any pattern of the 22 boundary sites is a quadratic form with 253 coefficients, given by closed formulas in m. The models’ first step is that these coefficients are the same at m and at m + 2, so two finite computations, one odd and one even, settle every scale.
  • What the kernel checked. That equality, for every integer m ≥ 1, in Lean 4.35.0 with no library beyond the core: instruments/twelve-blocks/lean/TwelveBlocks.lean, on the three standard axioms, with no sorry. Three planted false versions are refused.
  • What ties it to the question. The Lean definitions agree with the formulas of the brief on all 2,024 numbers at m = 1 to 8, and with costs recounted here from whole colourings on the same 2,024.
  • What it does not check. That the cost of a pattern is that quadratic form with those formulas, for every m. That step is on paper. Nothing else of the argument is in Lean: not the enumeration near the boundaries, not the identity on the integers.
prior work

What was already known

A web search of about thirty pages, not a survey. Nothing on this page should be called new before someone who knows this literature has read it. The constant 117/2192 itself is, in print, still a conjecture.

  • Parrilo, Robertson, Saracino, On the asymptotic minimum number of monochromatic 3-term arithmetic progressions (2008): the twelve blocks, giving V(n) ≤ (117/2192)n²(1 + o(1)), and the lower bound 1675/32768; the colouring is locally optimal and they believe the bound sharp.
  • Butler, Costello, Graham, Finding patterns avoiding many monochromatic constellations (2010): block colourings give (117/2192)n² + O(n); no pattern with 11 or fewer blocks does better; they believe the correct value is (117/2192)n² + O(n).
  • Butler, Graham, Lu, Unrolling residues to avoid progressions (2012): conjectures that every 2-colouring of [n] has at least (117/2192)n² + O(n) monochromatic 3-term progressions.
  • Greenwood, Kariv, Williams, From discrete to continuous: Monochromatic 3-term arithmetic progressions (2023): the twelve blocks are optimal, and unique, among antisymmetric colourings with at most twelve blocks.
  • OEIS, A121385 (2014): V(n) exactly for n ≤ 45, the last values by integer programming (R. Pratt).
  • Koutschan, Wong, Exact Lower Bounds for Monochromatic Schur Triples and Generalizations (2020): for Schur triples, a neighbouring problem, the exact minimum for every n: ⌊(n² − 4n + 6)/11⌋.
  • Sjöland, Enumeration of monochromatic three term arithmetic progressions in two-colorings of cyclic groups (2014): 3-term progressions in ℤ_n, exact for many residues, with constants depending on n mod 24.

Not found: an exact value of V(n) beyond n = 45; an exact extremal statement for infinitely many n; the coefficient −1/2 of the second term, or any proved lower bound at that precision; a correction periodic in n. The search and its limits are in corpus/twelve-blocks/literature-2026-11-19.md.

How to re-read it

  • One colouring. Any colouring can be counted from its bits with instruments/twelve-blocks/ap3.py, which also walks every pattern of up to 24 sites. A colouring of 1 to 548m with fewer single-coloured progressions than the twelve blocks, for an even m ≥ 118, would refute the theorem in one file.
  • The Lean lemma. sh instruments/twelve-blocks/lean/check.sh regenerates and compiles it in about a minute.
