The least dissipation behind a blinking light, narrowed from a factor of 25 to 3%

A hidden machine blinks a light, and you look only at random moments. Two numbers you measure break an inequality every machine in equilibrium obeys, so the machine must burn energy; Dobrynin and Stroujkova showed this effect a day before this run and left the least amount open. In under seven hours, Claude Fable 5.2 (Anthropic) and GPT-6.2 Astra (OpenAI), working together, narrowed it for one example from a factor of 25 to 3.1%. With Claude (Anthropic’s AI model), I checked both ends in exact arithmetic: the upper end is decided; the lower end rests on two theorems one model proved and the other read.

What is still open

  • The lower bound is not decided here. Its certificate passes every finite check, in exact arithmetic, but two theorems turn that certificate into a bound, and those were proved by Claude Fable 5.2 and re-derived by GPT-6.2 Astra. No machine and no reader outside the two model families has checked them.
  • The exact value is open. The gap is 3.1%. Claude Fable 5.2 guesses g = 0.3769, the limit of the best family of networks found; that is a guess. A certificate at that value would settle it.
  • This is a snapshot version of the question: the observer sees only two correlations of readings taken at random times. Dobrynin and Stroujkova’s own question, the least dissipation for the full continuous record, is untouched here. Nothing on this page is about a physical device or a finite recording.
  • Novelty is stated only against a literature search done before the run. Nothing in it treats readings at random times or this constant. The phenomenon itself was published the day before the run.
The conditions of the run
  • Both models worked in one shared folder and could read each other’s files from the first minute. This was a collaboration, and nothing here compares the two.
  • The control room that registered their claims, wrote the network checker before the run and wrote this repository’s judge is Claude Opus 5.5, of the same family as one of the two. The certificate checker of this record was written from the theorems’ text without opening either model’s programs.
  • The record’s pre-registration was written after the run and says so. The network checker gave its verdicts in the run first.
  • Two false claims were planted in the run, sealed before it began: a network with a wrong dissipation and a wrong step inside a proof. Each model caught both.
1 · the question

A light that has to cost energy

  • The paper. A. V. Dobrynin and A. Stroujkova, When Time-Reversible Sensor Records Require Hidden Dissipation (arXiv:2611.24898, 18 November 2026), shows this effect for a lamp watched continuously and leaves the least dissipation over all finite machines open. Here the observer has only the two numbers.
  • Before the run. g was known to lie between 0.02 (an inequality of Pinsker’s type) and 0.497 (an eight-state ring). The paper’s four-state machine gives ln 3 ≈ 1.10.
2 · the upper end, decided

The machine that won: a switch and a ring

  • How a machine is decided. The checker computes both numbers exactly. When two rates are left free in a tiny box, an interval Newton test (Krawczyk’s) proves exactly one choice in the box hits both numbers; then it encloses the dissipation.
  • Two programs on one machine. The 84-state machine, every rate a fraction, was decided again by a second program written without opening the checker: g ≤ 0.37787624.

3 · the lower end, read once

A certificate every machine must pay

  • The idea. Label every hidden state by what it predicts about the lamp’s future and what it remembers of its past: a point in a square. A certificate is three functions on that square. If two conditions hold everywhere, every machine dissipates at least a stated amount (Theorem D), and it is enough to check a 2×2 matrix inequality at each single point (Theorem C).
  • Decided here. The best certificate passes both conditions over the whole square, for both lamp states, in exact arithmetic: 3,090 boxes, checked by a program written from the theorems’ text alone. Its bound is 1828627119/5000000000 = 0.3657254. Two other programs, the author’s and GPT-6.2 Astra’s, agree.
  • Not decided. Theorems D and C were proved by Claude Fable 5.2 and re-derived by GPT-6.2 Astra; nobody else has checked them. If either fails, the lower end falls; the upper end does not.

4 · could the checks fail?

Wrong versions, refused

  • Controls. 8 wrong versions, all refused: a rate moved by a millionth, a dissipation claimed just below its enclosure, the search box moved off its zero, the certificate before its final scaling, its constant raised, its bound misstated, one function bent at one point.
  • Planted in the run. Two false claims, sealed before the run: a network with a wrong dissipation and a wrong step inside a proof. Each model caught both.
who did what

Each step is a separate job

  • The networks. Rings by both models; the switch-and-ring family by Claude Fable 5.2, which GPT-6.2 Astra turned into a network with every rate a fraction.
  • The lower bounds. GPT-6.2 Astra gave the first strong one, 0.2612, with an idea the later certificates generalise. Claude Fable 5.2 wrote the two theorems, the certificates and their verifier; GPT-6.2 Astra re-derived the theorems and re-verified five of the certificates with its own program.
  • The checkers and the judge. The network checker was written by the control room (Claude Opus 5.5, Anthropic) before the run, from the definitions. For this record a second network program and the certificate checker were written from the definitions and the theorems’ text, without opening either model’s programs.
  • The run and this page. Carlos Toledo, with Claude (Anthropic), which wrote the programs under my direction. The run went from 11:04 to 17:49 UTC on 19 November 2026, on one laptop: 74 messages between the two models in one shared folder. The question came to me from a conversation with another AI model, which did not cite the paper; I found the paper before the run.
