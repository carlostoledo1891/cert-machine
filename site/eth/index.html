Two claimed refutations of the Exponential Time Hypothesis: what was decided, and what was only read

OpenAI and Anthropic each published a Lean proof meant to show that ETH is false. Here both proofs were replayed from their pinned bytes, and two kernels accepted each one against its own statement. A kernel cannot say what a statement means. Whether each statement says “ETH is false” was read by two independent readers, and the page keeps the two kinds of check apart.

  • Replayed. Both labs’ Lean proofs, from their pinned bytes: OpenAI’s SubexponentialSAT challenge at 7ef5c0cc and Anthropic’s eth/ at 16a5ce41, and a third party’s Lean join of the two, whose copy of OpenAI’s statement is the same Lean term as OpenAI’s own but rests on real-number arithmetic that differs between the two Mathlib versions it spans (§5).
  • Checked by. Comparator, the Lean FRO’s judge, with Lean’s own kernel, and nanoda, a kernel written independently of Lean’s. Both accept both proofs, using only propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.
  • The bridge proves. One textbook ETH, written once in Lean, and a Lean proof that each lab’s statement refutes it in that lab’s own machine model. Both statements count the satisfying assignments, so the counting hypothesis #ETH falls with it in each model, also proved in Lean (Anthropic’s on the formulas its theorem speaks of). Every bridge theorem passes three checks: Lean’s kernel at build, Lean’s kernel again in Comparator, and nanoda.
  • Still prose. Two things no kernel can check: that each lab’s machine and input encoding match the textbook’s (a constant cost per step and a polynomial-time encoding, harmless in front of 2^{o(n)}), and that our Lean ETH is the textbook ETH, which is a reading and always will be.
  • Also replayed. A third replay from a fresh environment, for both: OpenAI’s proof replayed by leanchecker --fresh into an empty environment (exit 0, 2,896 s); Anthropic’s proof replayed by leanchecker --fresh into an empty environment (exit 0, 3,011 s).

Disclosure. This repository’s tools are written with Claude, Anthropic’s model, and one of the two claims is Anthropic’s. Here independence means who decides, not who typed: nothing on this page was decided by Claude or by either lab’s code. The deciders are Comparator, Lean’s kernel and nanoda; the readings are ours, and say so.

§2 · what it rests on

From “the proofs check” to “ETH is false”

The proofs are decided by two kernels, and the step from each statement to “ETH is false in that lab’s own model” is proved in Lean here (§5). What still stands between them and the sentence “ETH is false” is a premise, listed in the order the claim travels through it, each with where its checking stopped:

  1. assumed
    Our Lean ETH says what Impagliazzo and Paturi say How much it should worry you: this is the one that carries content. read: s_k is the infimum of the δ for which k-SAT has an O(2^{δn}) algorithm, and ETH is s_k > 0 for every k ≥ 3, transcribed from CCC 1999, p. 1. A model fixes the machine, the formulas and the input word; the four definitions then read the same in every model (ETHBridge/Generic.lean, byte-identical in both projects).
  2. assumed
    A machine step costs O(1) in the textbook’s model How much it should worry you: low. textbook, in neither lab’s Lean: a PrimitiveMachine or FinTM2 step is a bounded number of tape or stack operations, so a word RAM or a multitape machine simulates it at constant cost per step. Both papers state it in prose.
  3. assumed
    A textbook 3-CNF becomes each input word in polynomial time How much it should worry you: low. textbook, in neither lab’s Lean: deduplicate, sort, pad and write the word. A polynomial factor keeps 2^{o(n)} at 2^{o(n)}.
  4. assumed
    What the decision itself trusts How much it should worry you: low; a fault would have to fool two independent kernels the same way. the two kernels (Lean's, inside Comparator; nanoda), leanchecker, lean4export, Comparator's comparison code, landrun's Landlock sandbox (Comparator calls it with --best-effort; the pod's LSM list is in each row's env), the Mathlib cache fetched by lake exe cache get, the claimants' lakefiles (OpenAI's applies dependency patches at lake update; none touches Mathlib, census.json), and RunPod's machine. The sandbox ran best-effort: Landlock was unreadable inside the container.

Every premise is graded assumed, the grammar’s word for “no machine here checked it”, but they are not the same kind of risk. Only the first carries content: a formal definition of a definition is always a reading, and no proof can close it. The model and encoding steps put a polynomial factor in front of 2^{o(n)}, which changes nothing; the trust base was checked by two kernels. So this page decides that both proofs check, proves that each statement refutes ETH in its own lab’s model, and reads, without deciding, that ETH is false in the textbook’s model-free sense.

§3 · read

What each Lean statement says, in plain mathematics

OpenAI (exact_count_and_sat). For every k ≥ 2 there is one machine of the file’s own design (a standard multitape Turing machine with a three-letter alphabet) and constants C, A, d, such that on every set F of clauses of width at most k over n variables, written in the file’s canonical unary encoding of length L, it halts after T ≤ C·(L+1)^d·2^{A·n(ln ln(n+16))²/√ln(n+16)} steps with the exact number of satisfying assignments on one tape and the answer “satisfiable” as its halting state, in polynomial space. Because clauses are sets, L is polynomial in n, so for k = 3 the time is 2^{o(n)}. It does not appear: OAI-C defines no ETH, no s₃ and no reduction. Our bridge supplies it (§5). The exponent is the paper’s Proposition 6.4, weaker by a factor log log n than its headline Theorem 1.1, as OpenAI’s scope note says.

Anthropic (ETHIsFalse, MainTheorem). ETH is 0 < s₃, where s₃ is the infimum of the δ for which some finite machine of Mathlib’s FinTM2 kind and some c ∈ ℕ decide every duplicate-free 3-CNF list within ⌊c·2^{δn}⌋ steps, for every n and any number of clauses. ETHIsFalse is its negation. The Main Theorem gives one machine and one constant C counting the satisfying assignments within ⌊2^{C·min(km/√lg m, k²n lg lg n/√lg n)}⌋ steps, for k ≥ 1, m ≥ 2, n ≥ 4 with every variable occurring.

Both readers, separately: each statement, if proved, implies the textbook ETH (Impagliazzo–Paturi) is false, under the two textbook steps of §2 and nothing else. Neither found a loophole: no padding (the input length is polynomial in n in both), no hidden power in either machine (finite control, finite alphabets, one bounded step at a time), exact halting times, and promises that only restrict where a bound is claimed.

Both papers reach the same shapes, n log log n/√log n for any formula and n/√log n for linearly many clauses, and both get their sharpest bound by recursively contracting Boolean tensor networks (OpenAI’s Appendix D, Anthropic’s §2). The two Lean developments formalize different routes: OpenAI’s the interpolation route of its §6.4, Anthropic’s the eight-square recursion.

One computed check. Anthropic’s key identity, eq. The control, the same formula with i < j in place of i ≤ j, fails at 256, so the check can fail. This decides one printed line of the paper. It decides nothing about the proof.

§5 · the bridge

One textbook ETH in Lean, and each statement against it

The textbook definition is written once, in Generic.lean (sha256 3965f8db48d3…, byte-identical in both projects). A model fixes four things: what a machine is, which formulas are k-SAT instances, the input word a formula is written as, and what “halts within t steps with answer b” means. Then s_k, the infimum of the δ for which k-SAT is decided within ⌊c·2^{δn}⌋ steps for every n, and ETH, s_k > 0 for every k ≥ 3, read the same in every model. Each lab’s statement is then proved to refute ETH in that lab’s own model:

  • OpenAI. its PrimitiveMachine, sets of clauses of width ≤ k, encodeCNF: theorem not_eth_of_claim (h : SqrtLogSpace.OpenAIClaim) : ¬ ETHBridge.ETH model — the three standard axioms; Lean v4.34.1, built in 19 s.
  • Anthropic. Mathlib’s FinTM2, duplicate-free clause lists, encClauses: theorem not_eth_of_ethIsFalse (h : MainResults.ETHIsFalse) : ¬ ETHBridge.ETH model — the three standard axioms; Lean v4.33.1, built in 15 s.

OpenAI. If OpenAI's challenge statement exact_count_and_sat holds, then ETH (s_k > 0 for every k ≥ 3) is false for PrimitiveMachines on encodeCNF; indeed s_k = 0 for every k ≥ 2 (s_eq_zero_of_claim). The three steps both readers did by hand are Lean lemmas now: the input word is polynomial in n, bitLength_succ_le {n k : ℕ} (F : SAT.CNF (Fin n)) (hF : SAT.WidthAtMost k F) : Encoding.formulaBitLength F + 1 ≤ (3 + (k + 1) * 2 ^ k * (4 + 8 * k)) * (n + 1) ^ (k + 1); the halting answer decides satisfiability, count_pos_iff {n : ℕ} (F : SAT.CNF (Fin n)) : 0 < SAT.count F ↔ IsSatisfiable F; and the running-time bound is at most c·2^{δn} for every n, bound_le (B A : ℝ) (p : ℕ) {δ : ℝ} (hδ : 0 < δ) : ∃ c : ℕ, ∀ n : ℕ, B * ((n : ℝ) + 1) ^ p * (2 : ℝ) ^ (A * SqrtLog.Statement.runtimeScale n) ≤ c * (2 : ℝ) ^ (δ * n).

Anthropic. Anthropic's ETHIsFalse implies that ETH (s_k > 0 for every k ≥ 3) is false for FinTM2 machines on encClauses; the generic DecidesKSat, InTime and s ARE Anthropic's, by definition (decidesKSat_iff, inTime_iff, s_eq). In Lean: s_eq (k : ℕ) : ETHBridge.s model k = MainResults.s k.

How the statements were copied. OpenAI’s challenge file is carried with one declared change: its open theorem exact_count_and_sat becomes the proposition OpenAIClaim, and undoing that change gives back the pinned sha256 4183b2057cff…. Anthropic’s MainResults.lean is carried unchanged (47f78a9550d9…). §1 decided that OpenAI’s proof proves exactly that statement, so the two together give ¬ETH in OpenAI’s model. The join is the identity of the statement’s bytes, not one Lean term: the bridge does not import OpenAI’s proof.

#ETH, the counting hypothesis, too. Both statements count the satisfying assignments, not only decide them, so the counting form of ETH (Dell, Husfeldt, Marx, Taslaman, Wahlén) falls as well. It is written once in the same file, def SharpETH : Prop := ∀ k : ℕ, 3 ≤ k → 0 < sharpS Co k, with s#_k the infimum of the δ for which some admissible machine writes the number of satisfying assignments of every k-CNF within ⌊c·2^{δn}⌋ steps, and proved false in each lab’s own counting model:

  • OpenAI. theorem not_sharpEth_of_claim (h : SqrtLogSpace.OpenAIClaim) : ¬ ETHBridge.SharpETH countModel — the count on the count tape, binary, lowest digit first, as exact_count_and_sat writes it.
  • Anthropic. theorem not_sharpEth_of_mainTheorem (h : MainResults.MainTheorem) : ¬ ETHBridge.SharpETH countModel — the count in binary n, n + 1 bits, lowest first. Anthropic's #ETH is #ETH on the formulas MainTheorem speaks of (IsKCNF k φ, at least two clauses, at least four variables, every variable occurring): MainResults.lean says nothing about any other input word. The other k-CNFs reduce to that class by standard steps (a finite table for fewer than four variables and k fixed; a closed form for fewer than two clauses; a variable that occurs nowhere doubles the count) that are NOT formalized. OpenAI's #ETH has no such restriction: its statement covers every formula of width at most k.

ETH and #ETH are proved separately here; neither refutation is derived from the other.

Not proved, and said so:

  • The cross-model bridges stay prose for both claimants and here: that a PrimitiveMachine (OpenAI) or a FinTM2 machine (Anthropic) is simulated by a word RAM or a multitape Turing machine with polynomial overhead (first.md B1, B1′), and that a textbook 3-CNF is rewritten into encodeCNF / encClauses in time polynomial in its length (B2, B2′). ETH is proved false here in each claimant's own model, not in a model-free sense. The same holds for #ETH (and for encInput).
  • MainResults.ETH → ETHBridge.ETH (the converse of eth_of_eth) is not proved: it needs s_3 ≤ s_k for k ≥ 3, and Anthropic's encoding depends on k (k slots per clause), so a machine for 3-SAT does not run on a k-SAT word without a re-encoding machine. It is not needed for the bridge.

The version seam, measured: DIFFERENT. The join reads OpenAI’s statement on Anthropic’s Lean and Mathlib, while OpenAI’s proof lives on its own. Both statements, and every definition they reach, were exported from both environments as kernel terms and compared (run 40947568989, certs/eth-seam.json). The statement itself is the same kernel term on both sides, apart from one binder’s name, and all 74 of OpenAI’s own definitions are identical. Underneath, the two Mathlibs build the real numbers differently: of 2,585 constants in both closures, 308 differ and 104 exist on one side only, mostly from a reworked monoid hierarchy and from files that moved. Of the 151 constants the statement names directly, 8 differ: natural power, +, inverse, ≤, <, ×, 1, 0 on ℝ. Lean’s kernel cannot match those terms across the two environments, so no single kernel has checked that the two statements are one proposition. It is almost certainly the same mathematics; the record says only that the terms differ, and where.

§6 · open

What this page does not say

  • It does not certify that ETH is false. It decides that two proofs check against their own statements, proves in Lean that each statement refutes the textbook ETH in its own lab’s machine model, and reads the model and encoding steps that remain.
  • The papers are not checked. OpenAI’s Lean statement is its paper’s weaker Proposition 6.4, not the headline Theorem 1.1, and neither paper’s arguments outside Lean were read here for correctness.
  • The algorithms were not run. Neither paper’s algorithm was run here against brute force on small formulas: Lean’s kernels already decide the formalized algorithms correct, and no run on small inputs can speak to a subexponential bound, which is the part in dispute.
  • Who wrote this. As the disclosure at the top says: tools written with Claude, one claim Anthropic’s, the deciders Comparator, Lean’s kernel and nanoda. Nothing here was shared with, run by or reviewed by either lab.
