The audit
One real day over São Paulo — the world's busiest urban helicopter market — 61 aircraft, 148 flights, 10,391 km flown — ADS-B, hash-pinned. Each flight is re-flown on paper by an eVTOL and decided by interval arithmetic under Brazil's own reserve rule, ANAC RBAC 91.151(b). This is Embraer's home market: Eve's launch operator Revo holds a binding SP order (10 firm + 40 options, June 2025), and ANAC's vertiport sandbox runs at Campo de Marte — the busiest measured node on this map after Helicidade. Eve publishes the least of the four audited aircraft, so its column reads NEEDS DATA — and the flip threshold is computed, not rhetorical: at 253 kWh of published nameplate, half this day's flights become provable for the EVE-100 (proved on both sides of the flip, like every threshold here). The public UNDECIDABLE is the door. Click any aircraft — its certificate opens on the left.
Honest boundaries
The corpus is what the receiver network saw; coverage gaps and truncated tracks are flagged per flight. The audit is counterfactual arithmetic over stated boxes (quality-flagged in the repo; Eve publishes least, so Eve needs data most). ANAC = RBAC 91.151(b), the 20-min VFR helicopter reserve, quoted from ANAC's own pinned text (arithmetically identical to the FAA tier — the jurisdiction is the point); EASA = the 5-min final reserve, a NECESSARY condition only. Ambient layer = all other traffic, decor only. Data © adsb.lol contributors (ODbL). Rerun everything: apps/skyaudit. Methodology, sources and boundaries, citable: the companion note.
