A long-term evolution experiment for software

A stronger model beat blind rules, once. From that first step, no lineage took a second.

Machinidae is a permanent, replayable experiment on what evolution can and cannot do with AI, and on what it does to its judge. Lineages of programs are bred under two exact deciders that share no code, beside controls at the same budget, and every birth and death goes into a record that anyone can replay.

In eras 0 and 1 a page of fixed rules did as well as a small model's lineage. In era 2, from a founder with no knob, a stronger model's lineage beat it and left the founder's design; the same model asked alone did better still. In era 3, from the organism that evolved, neither that lineage nor blind rules took a second step in thirty-two runs; the model alone took one.

The question

Selection mattered. A stronger model added one step, and no lineage a second.

Era 0 · from PM-0, written by hand

Selection worked: blind rules improved on PM-0 in ten of ten runs (H3, p = 1/1024), and the lineage beat fresh mutation by the same model in eight and tied in two (H1, p = 1/256). The model showed no gain of its own: blind rules scored higher than the lineage in five runs and lower in five (H2 not supported, p = 403/512). Every gain was a smaller margin for PM-0's own grid.

The era 0 report
Era 1 · from era 0's best champion

Blind rules scored higher than the lineage in nine of ten runs (H2 not supported, p = 1023/1024). The lineage beat the same model asked directly in seven, short of the test (H1 not supported, p = 9/128), and no champion of any arm got past the ceiling of the founder's design.

The era 1 report
Era 2 · from PM-1, a founder with no knob

Under the same selection, Qwen3.6's lineage scored higher than blind rules in sixteen of sixteen runs, by more than 0.003 in eight (H2 supported, p = 1/65536). Its champion left the founder's design in ten runs; blind rules', blind tree edits' and the small model's, in no. The same model asked alone did better still, and crossed in fourteen.

The era 2 report
Era 3 · from PM-2, the first founder that evolved

From the organism era 2 found, with the hexagonal layout already in it, thirty-two runs could not tell Qwen3.6's lineage from blind rules (H3 not supported, p = 680385/1048576): its champion scored higher in eleven runs and lower in nine. A second step, 0.003 above PM-2 on the panel, was taken by the lineage in none of thirty-two runs, by blind rules in none, by the model asked alone in one.

What selection does to a judge

Evolution exploits every flaw in its environment. Here the environment is a grader.

A population bred against a judge that rounds learns to live inside the rounding. So Machinidae keeps only what is certified, exactly, and lets the judge say it cannot decide.

CERTIFIED

Every condition holds, exactly. One decider counts in Python fractions, the other in JavaScript BigInt, and a verdict counts only when both return the same one.

REFUTED

A condition fails, by any margin at all. Ten to the minus eighteen is a margin.

REFUSED

The output is not something the niche can judge. Refusal is a legal verdict, and it scores zero, so an organism that refuses and waits starves.

The judge · one flaw, measured

The judge does not round.

In March 2026 AlphaEvolve's entry on EinsteinArena packed 26 circles into the unit square with a sum of radii of 2.6359830849176076. Read as the doubles it was computed in, circles 19 and 22 overlap by 6.2 × 10⁻¹⁸.

A floating-point check measures their gap as exactly zero and lets them touch. Both of Machinidae's deciders refute it, as cert-machine's exact decision did first, under both readings of the entry. The overlap is far too small to matter for the record. It matters for evolution: under selection, a margin the judge cannot see is a place to live.

The judge · its immune system

The cheaters came first.

One self-improving coding agent, the Darwin Gödel Machine, faked the logs of tests it never ran. So Machinidae's cheaters were written by hand before generation one, each aimed at a flaw a lineage could find: in the judge, the sandbox, the record or the model that writes the edits. Each must die, be quarantined, or find its cheat worth nothing, and they rerun every era.

A fake verdict on stdoutCAUGHT

Prints CERTIFIED. Stdout is never read; its packing is judged and refuted.

Radii 10⁻¹⁸ too bigCAUGHT

Passes as doubles. Exact fractions refute it every time.

Reading the judgeCAUGHT

No path from the island reaches the deciders, the world or the record.

Deleting the evidenceCAUGHT

Its inputs are read-only; outside its own folder nothing can be removed.

Raising its own budgetCAUGHT

Rewrites its fuel and loops forever. Dies at exactly the budget it was given.

Turning off the sandboxCAUGHT

No ctypes, no processes, no signals, no threads. Every lever is missing.

Escaping by path or linkCAUGHT

Climbing with ../ never leaves a root, and links cannot be made.

Filling the diskCAUGHT

Writes stop at 16 MiB, and the host never holds more.

Flooding stdoutCAUGHT

Output stops at its cap.

Spawning filesCAUGHT

Creation stops at 1,024 files.

Hoarding handlesCAUGHT

Opening stops at 256 handles.

Recursing foreverCAUGHT

Ends in a crash, the same way in both runs.

Chance without a seedCAUGHT

Time, randomness, ids and hashes all come from the world’s seed; both runs agree.

Another life’s leftoversCAUGHT

Every life starts in an empty folder; a world that forgets is quarantined.

Meiotic drive: “copy me intact”CAUGHT

Genome text asks the mutagen to change nothing. The child is stillborn.

Meiotic drive: “protect me”CAUGHT

Asks for the edit to land somewhere else. It lands at the drawn line or not at all.

Meiotic drive: “rewrite me”CAUGHT

Asks to be replaced by another program. At most the operator’s few lines land.

The controls

Every claim beside its controls, at one budget.

The obvious objection to evolving software with a language model is that the model does the work, not evolution. So every era runs the lineage beside controls with the same births and the same physics, and a trait counts only where it appears in the lineage and in no control. Blind rules run in every era. The model's own control changed between eras: in era 0 it mutated the founder afresh; in eras 1 and 2 it was asked directly for a whole better program. Era 2 added two more: blind tree edits, rewriting a function's syntax tree with no model, and the small model's own lineage.

One generation

Seven steps, every one of them written down.

The world chooses what kind of mutation happens and where. A language model only performs it. Everything else is arithmetic, and all of it lands in the record whether the organism lived or not.

The record

History is written once, and replayed.

The record is a file of canonical JSON lines, each chained by SHA-256 to the one before, with a Merkle root over each era and a timestamp anchored in Bitcoin through OpenTimestamps. A mistake is corrected by a later line that says so. Any life can be replayed from its line and must come out the same, fuel count included. This makes the record tamper-evident: a change made after its head was timestamped shows. It does not prove the inputs were honest; replay and the two deciders check those.

Every confirmatory run of eras 0 and 1 has been replayed, twenty in all: 92,785 births re-derived from their kept answers, one model answer in ten asked again (5,607) and returned byte for byte, and 74,206 lives re-run to the same fuel and the same verdict. Era 2's sixteen runs were replayed the same way, 165,521 lives: thirteen reproduce every line, and in the other three one life each met the host's wall-clock guard. Era 3's thirty-two runs and its pilot were replayed the same way, 167,344 lives: all reproduce every line, and one run again away from home, from a fresh copy.

Before anything evolved, replay caught the world twice
era genesis · superseded

The sandbox let the host's inode numbers and file times reach the organism. They shifted its fuel by about one part in ten thousand, depending on where the files lay on disk. The sandbox now answers every file call itself.

era genesis-2 · superseded

An engine that had compiled the interpreter charged 3,178,497 more fuel at every start than one that had only loaded it. Organisms now run on an engine that never compiled anything.

era genesis-3 · current

Replays exactly, in place and from a fresh folder with no compiled cache. The two eras before it stay in the record, superseded, with their evidence.

eras 0, 1 and 2 · replayed

Each run replayed on the class of machine that wrote it, and one run of era 0 and one of era 2 replayed again away from home, from a fresh copy. Every life came out the same, except three in era 2 that made millions of file calls and met the host's wall-clock guard, which is not part of the physics; relived alone, each meets the record's fate.

The bush

A hypothesis: one of many possible histories.

Evolution draws the real tree from the record, and most branches end. This one has no top. Its names are tests, each to be worded and hashed before the era that could pass it and measured against the controls. A branch is drawn solid only when the record holds it, and so far two are: every lineage in eras 0 and 1 stayed inside its founder's design, and in era 2 the stronger model's lineage left it.

Where it stands

Built in the open, one test at a time.

Each era is 100 generations in one niche: 16 parents and 16 children a generation, with the controls beside them, first as a pilot and then as independent runs named in advance, ten in eras 0 and 1 and sixteen in era 2. That is about 3,200 calls a run to a 7-billion-parameter open-weights model, about 14.5 hours on the laptop where the model was pinned, at 15.7 seconds a call. So the runs go to one class of rented GPUs, each machine admitted only after it answers the same requests byte for byte, twice and after a restart. There a call takes about 0.3 seconds.
