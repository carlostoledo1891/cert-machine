Era 3 · results, built from the record

Thirty-two runs cannot tell the lineage from blind rules.

Era 2 found that a capable model's lineage leaves its founder's design where blind rules do not, and its knockouts said what was found: one gene in every champion, a hexagonal layout the model writes whole in one answer. Era 3 starts where era 2's lineages ended. Its founder, PM-2, is era-2-15's champion LQ-95-1 taken from the record byte for byte, the first founder that evolved. Three arms share one physics and one training set in every run. The lineage has Qwen3.6 rewrite functions under selection; blind rules make the same rewrites with no model; the AI alone is Qwen3.6 asked for whole programs about PM-2, as many as the lineage's written tokens buy. The line to cross is PM-2's own panel score, 0.22543, by at least 0.003: a second step.

Under the same selection and births, thirty-two runs cannot tell the lineage from blind rules: the mean difference is -0.0004, the 90% interval -0.0023 to +0.0015 (p = 680385/1048576); the lineage's champion scored higher in eleven runs, lower in nine, the same in twelve, and the two differed by more than 0.003 in seven. A second step, at least 0.003 above PM-2 on the panel and certified exactly, was taken by the lineage's champion in none of thirty-two runs, by blind rules' in none, by the AI alone's in one.

The tests, fixed in advance

One primary test, one gated behind it.

H3 is the exact sign-flip test on the sum of the sixteen differences, against all 65,536 ways of flipping their signs. Its verdict is read from the exact 90% interval for the mean shift, with an equivalence margin of 0.003. S1 is tested only if H3 is supported; as the only secondary it needs no correction. E is an existence claim: one champion at least 0.003 above PM-2 on the panel, certified exactly by both deciders, is proof of a second step.

After era 3

What the second step cost, and what era 4 could ask.

Era 3 started from an organism that evolved, PM-2, with the hexagonal layout already in it. Under the same selection that found that layout in era 2, no lineage took a second step of 0.003 on the panel in thirty-two runs, and blind rules took none either; the model asked alone did once, with a layout it had also written from PM-1 in era 2. Several champions that tied the founder on the training set scored lower than it on the panel: a change that is neutral on eight instances is not neutral on eight others. And the genome cap of 16,384 bytes, fixed since the M2 era, left PM-2 (13,098 bytes) little room: 314 of the AI alone's 514 answers and a share of every arm's duplications were stillborn for it. These are readings of the record, not tests; nothing below is fixed until its protocol is written, hashed and timestamped before generation one.

  • A cap that fits the founder. An era whose founder evolved needs a cap set from the founder, so a whole-program answer and a duplication have room; the cap is part of the physics, so it moves at the era boundary.
  • Selection that sees the panel's kind of variation. The training set is eight instances; champions overfit to them. More training instances, or a training set redrawn at checkpoints, would make a nudge that is real on the panel distinguishable from one that is not.
  • The AI alone's one step. It came from a whole program the model also wrote in era 2. Whether the model has further layouts in it, or only that one, is a question for the model alone, asked more times from PM-2, before any lineage is asked to find more.
  • Era 2's readings on era 3's record. The landscapes, the knockouts against PM-2 and the recall flag, as the protocol's §12 names them, are not run yet.

Asked since, outside the record (2026-10-27). A trial for era 4 asked the model alone 1,000 more times from PM-2 and spliced era 3's 529 answers again, under a cap of 65,536 bytes and the same judge. Under the lifted cap 1,304 of the 1,529 were born, against 399 under era 3's. Single steps stay rare: 12 answers step 0.003 above PM-2 on the panel and hold on sixteen fresh instances, 0.8% an answer. Pieces are common: 201 answers beat PM-2 on some panel instance while most lose overall. The trial is not an era and changes nothing on this page. Eras are now frozen, and the Laboratory takes those answers apart.
