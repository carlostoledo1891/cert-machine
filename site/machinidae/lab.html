The Laboratory · synthetic machinids, built outside any era

Built, not evolved: one answer's optimizer on another answer's layout, carried by PM-2, scores higher on the panel than any organism in the record.

A synthetic machinid is an organism made by hand or by a stated rule, never by an era. Its genes come from material already kept: here, 1,529 whole programs the AI wrote about PM-2, era 3's founder, 529 in era 3 and 1,000 in the era 4 trial, run before any era 4 exists. Run whole, most of those programs scored lower than PM-2. The Laboratory takes them apart: every layout function is lifted out as a gene, read as text and never run outside the sandbox, and PM-2 carries it beside its own genes, so a gene can only add. Each synthetic is then judged by the same two deciders, under era 3's physics, on the panel and on sixteen instances no choice ever read. None of this is evidence of what evolution did; it says what can be built from what the AI wrote.

The gene library: what each piece adds alone.

Every top-level function of (n, h) in the answers, lifted with the helpers it calls and carried by PM-2 as one more gene. Drawn: the 260 genes that add anything.

Plate III

The slim chimera: five genes, one that covers the rest.

For each panel instance, the gene that scored it highest was given to PM-2. Each gene was then taken away once. Taking away any of four costs nothing: one gene, trial:577's, covers them. Taking that one away costs about a third of the gain, and the other four hold the rest. They are redundant organs, not idle ones.

Plate IV

Compositions: one answer's optimizer on another answer's layout.

210 operators on layouts (optimizers, local searches, perturbations) were lifted from the answers and each applied to the library's best gene; 12 beat that gene alone on the panel and on the check set, and 2 ran out of fuel (not drawn). The rule joins two pieces the AI wrote in different answers; the panel alone chooses, and the check set reports. An optimizer costs fuel: across is what each life burned.

Plate V

Selection over the same pieces, blind: can it build the chimera by itself?

No model and no hand: the pool is fixed, the 2,158 genes and 210 operators above, as text. Arm S keeps sixteen organisms for a hundred generations, era 3's budget; each child is its parent with one change drawn at random (add a gene, wrap a carried piece in an operator, drop a piece), and the best sixteen live on. Arm A spends the same 1,600 births with no inheritance: PM-2 and one random gene each time. Each run chooses on eight training instances of its own; its champions then live once on the panel and on the check set, so neither score was chosen on. The rules were written before the first run lived (lab/README.md, the amendments). Eight runs, every one shown. This is not an era: it says what blind selection does with this pool, not what any era did.

Against the best single gene on the check set (+0.0072), selection's eight champions stand 4 above it, 1 level with it (within 0.0002), 3 below; the best is +0.0131, against the rule-made composition's +0.0136, and none passes it. The median champion: +0.0080 with inheritance, +0.0053 without; selection is higher in 6 of 8 runs. In 5 of 8 runs it carries era-3-22:DQ-12's optimize_layout, the operator the composition uses, found blind. The cost: its champions burn 19% to 100% of a life's fuel, against the composition's 7.3%, and 2 of 8 ran out of fuel on the panel and on part of the check set after scoring above PM-2 on their training set: nothing in era 3's rules charges for fuel until a life runs out, so selection filled the budget and fell over the edge on instances it had not seen.

Plate VI

One rule about fuel: a child that burns more than half dies.

The same eight runs again, by the same names, so the same training sets and the same random draws, with one rule added and written down before any of them lived: a child whose life on its training set burns more than half of a life's fuel dies there, and is never selected or champion. Everything else is Plate V's. A life does not depend on the rule, so lives the first runs had already lived are read from their rows; the rest lived on rented CPU, after each machine re-lived run 1's first ten generations identically.

Under the half-fuel rule 0 of 8 champions ran out of fuel, against 2 under the first rule. The median champion on the check set: +0.0075, against +0.0080. Against the best single gene (+0.0072) they stand 4 above it, 3 level with it (within 0.0002), 1 below; the best is +0.0120 (the rule-made composition: +0.0136). Run by run, the rule's champion is higher on the check set in 3 of 8, the same organism in 2 and lower in 3: the heavy optimizers it kills carried the best first-rule champions as well as the starved ones. Its champions burn 12% to 52% of a life's fuel on the panel; the rule killed 164 children in all, and 4 of 8 champions carry era-3-22:DQ-12's optimize_layout.

The rule card

How a synthetic is made, and what it may not say.

  • Not evidence of evolution. A synthetic shows what can be built from material the AI or evolution produced; no era's result is restated because of one.
  • Never in the record. The Laboratory writes its own dated files, once, beside the record and never into it.
  • Same judge, same world. Every life runs in the sandbox under era 3's physics, judged exactly by both deciders.
  • A recipe for every synthetic. Its founder by genome address and its genes by sha256, with the answer and the function each came from, so anyone can rebuild it byte for byte.
  • Chosen on the panel, shown on the check set. Any rule that picks genes reads the panel only; the check set says whether the choice holds. Amended before the first selection run: those runs (Plate V) choose on a training set of their own instead, so the panel and the check set both report.
  • This Mac, and rented CPU on the operator's word. No model is asked anywhere here. Selection runs 3 to 8 lived on rented CPU, each machine first re-living run 1's first ten generations and 26 genes exactly as this Mac did.
  • Organisms never run outside the sandbox. Genes are AI-written code: read as text, executed only inside a life.
