The roundtable: the grammar examined from eleven seats

On 29 October 2026 eleven seats judged WARRANT, the grammar this site is drawn in, against its rulebook and five built pages. They kept one law, that the display is a sound map of the record and the build decides it, and found ten places where the site broke its own rules. This page draws the law, each finding before and after its fix, the six axioms, and the fifteen rules the table proposed, with which of them a build now enforces.

who sat

Eleven seats, and what a seat was

Each seat was an AI agent (Claude, Anthropic’s model) briefed with the rulebook, the brainstorm behind it, the module that draws the marks and five built pages, and asked to judge the grammar by one discipline’s published principles. Each is named for the person whose work defines that discipline. None of those people took part, and nothing on this page is theirs: the seats argued from their published ideas, and every number the table reported was measured again on the built site before it was written here.

  • the Tufte seat a display of evidence: data ink, the lie factor, sources on the graphic
  • the Wilkinson seat a grammar in the strict sense: components, scales, guides, composition laws
  • the Bertin seat the retinal variables, and the level of organisation each can carry
  • the Victor seat an interface to a decision procedure: handles, the ladder of abstraction
  • the Sanderson seat the grammar in time: what a frame is, what may move
  • the Bostock seat a library and a data format: the API, the interchange object, the validator
  • the Norman seat the gulf of evaluation: signifiers, knowledge in the world, the first ten seconds
  • the Maeda seat computational design: generated, not drawn
  • the Sagmeister seat beauty as a function: scarcity, scale, the void as an event
  • the Chief Scientist seat order theory, provenance, Galois connections, theorems the house can check
  • the CTO seat a platform: the portable core, the sibling projects, the build plan and its cost
what the table agreed

Five points nobody argued against

  • The two axes are the invention. How a number was made (the stroke) is kept apart from what a question got (the word) and from how far a model was checked (the grade). Every seat built on that separation; none asked to merge the axes.
  • Ink is read, never chosen — enforced one level too low. The drawing module takes no style, but it takes a standing typed by the author. The first recommendation was to read the standing from a typed value that carries its record (R17).
  • The one red is the best decision in the house. One colour, kept for an exhibited counterexample. Nobody asked for a second hue.
  • A refusal drawn as a refusal is new. The empty set and a word, never a zero-height bar. The table asked for the void to be drawn larger, not smaller.
  • The display is a sound map of the record, and the build decides it. “A system that is the mathematics itself” was refused as a slogan and kept as this law, drawn at the top of this page.
the foundations

Six axioms

  • A1 · Soundness. Drawing is a map from a record to marks; reading is the map back to the records that could have produced them. A page is sound when every record it draws is among the records its marks can be read back to: no mark stronger than its record, in standing, in extent or in verdict word.
  • A2 · Completeness. Every row of the record a page draws from is a mark, or the empty set with a word.
  • A3 · Outward, in digits and in pixels. A lower end rounds down and an upper end rounds up, at the resolution the figure declares.
  • A4 · Standing is a scale with a guide. Four standings onto five strokes, with the legend derived from the scale; every channel the scale uses is monotone in it or constant.
  • A5 · Standing is read from a typed value. The value carries its datum, its standing, its premises and its record’s address; the arithmetic demotes it and the renderer only reads it.
  • A6 · A frame is a page. In motion every frame obeys A1; a standing never tweens and a value never travels: it appears, or its enclosure narrows onto it.
the findings

Ten findings, each as it was and as it is

Each finding is a fact about the built site on 29 October, measured again before the rulings. Where a number moved, it is counted here twice by the same rule: on the site as git holds it at e310e071^, the commit before the first fixes, and on the site as it is built today.

F2 A dash with no standing

Dashed marks in figures that carried no standing, so nothing could check what the dash meant. The chart kit took a plain “dashed” switch; now it takes a standing, and the census counts what is left, which may only shrink.

F3 The dotted line inks more than the dashed one

At the width the legend draws its strokes, 2.4 px, the chosen pattern inks 0.567 of its length and the computed one 0.556: the weaker standing carries more ink. It crosses above at 5/7 px in the small patterns and at 7/3 px in the large ones. Open. Since D9 the tone carries the order (F1), and whether the density should too is a question for the reader study below.

F4 Pixels rounded to the nearest, not outward

Drawn at the top of this page: the staircase’s top on [1, 3/2] sat 7/180 of a pixel inside its record. Every edge now rounds outward and the builder refuses one drawn inside.

F5 A verdict chip wore a dash, and its shape was not a function of its word

A dash is a standing, so a chip border may not be dashed; and a word like CERTIFIED wore several chip shapes across the site. Before: PARTIAL as open/dep/held; CERTIFIED as held/cert; REFUTED as cert/dep/open; REFUSED as open/dep; QUEUED as open/dep; REPAIRED as cert/open/held; NEEDS DATA as open/dep. Now each word has one shape, and the census refuses a second.

F6 Standings typed at the call site

A number’s standing was written by hand where it was printed. Since D10 a number can be printed from its record through an address, and a gate re-reads every addressed number from the built page. Partial: most values still carry no address.

F7 A reason that lives only in a tooltip

A value’s reason (“measured, not decided”) was a hover title: invisible on a phone, a door with no handle on a desktop. Open (R22).

F8 The ETH page said the opposite of its finding in ten seconds

Its first tile read “proofs decided · 2 of 2 · CERTIFIED, each against its own statement”. A reader left believing ETH had been certified false. The first tile now reads ““ETH is false” · not decided here · read against the textbook ETH (§2), bridged in each lab’s own machine model (§5); the step between models is prose (§6)”.

F9 The constants retyped in a sibling project

Machinidae carried its own copy of the five dash patterns, which is how twenty-five patterns once grew on this site. Open (R29): one registry file, lifted by digest.

F10 Motion in the standing channel

The landing’s flow animated a dash offset (2 animation then, 0 now): decoration in the channel that carries standing. It is a translated point now, and a video that slid a decided tick was rejected.

what is open

The research the table asked for

  • The soundness census. Over every figure the render gate already records: how many extents are drawn from a float midpoint or rounded inward, how many dashes carry no standing. A measurement before a rule.
  • The ink-density theorem. The ink each pattern carries at each width, the order checked at every width the site draws (F3).
  • The frame census. Every frame of a video checked like a page (R25).
  • The reader study. Pre-registered, a few hundred readers, the specimens on the grammar page, one question: which of these lines was proved? A grammar checked only by its own red controls has never left the house.

The rulebook itself is the grammar page; the rulings that followed this table are its dated sections.
