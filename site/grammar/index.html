Carlos Toledo · the visual grammar

What each mark rests on

WARRANT is the grammar every figure, table and number on this site is drawn in. A stroke says what a mark rests on: an exact decision, floating point, a choice, or nothing. A verdict is always a word.

the marks

Four standings, four strokes

Every mark has one standing, and each standing one stroke on a line and one underline in text. Drawn by the module as this page is built:

decided
Solid
An exact decision backs it: an interval enclosure, an exact rational comparison, a proof.
√(1/4) = 1/2
stroke solid · text plain
computed
Dashed
Floating point, a solver stopped at a tolerance, a fit, a forecast, a number someone printed. It may be right; nothing decided it.
Math.sqrt(2) = 1.4142135623730951
stroke 5 4, 1.5 2 under 1.5 px · text dashed underline
chosen
Dotted
One member of a set the data admit, picked by something that is not the data: a tie-break, a default, a seed, an interpolation.
the segment at 1/2: 2/3
stroke 1 5 round, 1 3 under 1.5 px · text dotted underline
refused
Nothing
The instrument declined or the input is missing. Never bridged, never averaged, never drawn as zero.
√(−1): refused
no stroke; a hatched void · text the word

The weakest on the path wins

The standings are ordered, and a mark takes the weakest standing on its path to the pixel: one float makes it computed, one choice makes it chosen, one refusal leaves the void. Standing is not a confidence: a value known to many decimals can be undecided.

1 · decided
2 · computed
3 · chosen
4 · refused
strongerweaker
A float anywhere on the path: the mark is computed.
A choice anywhere on the path: chosen. A straight line between two exact knots is one of many.
A refusal anywhere on the path: nothing is drawn, and the void says so.

the rules

Sixteen rules, each drawn

From the rulebook (v2, frozen 28 October 2026, with dated rulings since). Each was learned on a page of this site that broke it; each card names the page and the fix.

Marks

rule 1
Standing is not a verdict.
A verdict is a word in a chip; what a mark rests on is its stroke or underline. They never share a channel, so a refutation proved here is drawn solid.
Found on neural-geometry: a failed frame verdict struck its fitted number through. The verdict is a word now, and the number keeps its dashed underline. fixed 28 October
rule 2
A pick is a member of its set, and the build checks it.
A figure that draws one member of a set the data admit draws a real member, verified exactly when the page is built.
Found on census: its least-squares totals left the ±½ box a rounded integer allows. The repair is now a fit held inside the box, its optimality conditions checked exactly. fixed 28 OctoberFound on occultation: the curve joining its chords is not concave, so no convex body has it; it is drawn dotted, as a chosen picture. fixed 28 OctoberFound on navier-stokes: the drawn core was not incompressible (|div u| about 1.2 times its larger term); the build now refuses it above 10−6. fixed 28 October
rule 3
A tie is chosen.
When the optimum is not unique, the one drawn was picked by a tie-break: dotted, and said to be one of several.
Found on neural-geometry: nine of its fifteen minimum spanning trees tie; recounted here from its record by the same exact test. fixed 28 October
rule 4
Dash is for standing only.
Which series or model a line belongs to is carried by weight and opacity, never by a dash, in every figure.
Found on answer-shape: three models told apart by dash (6 4 and 1.6 3.4 among them); the weight-and-opacity ladder was already underneath. fixed 5 September
rule 5
The one accent.
The red × marks an exhibited counterexample: an object that broke a claim made about its own record, with words. Never a verdict word, a region, a scalar comparison or a deduction. When in doubt, ink.
Found on the return-level check: “not the certified fit” came off the × list: a box clearing a box has no witness point. fixed 28 October
rule 6
Mood.
A verdict that holds given a chosen input names it in italic, given h = 3/50; a hypothesis read but not machine-checked is named the same way.
Found on the return-level atlas: each claim’s box is a reading of the paper, drawn dotted with the readings one 4° cell in or out; of its ten claims one turns on the reading (south of South America (40–58° S, 80–56° W): does not hold as drawn, holds with an edge moved). fixed 27 October
rule 7
An empty set is ∅.
It gets ∅ and a word, never a zero-height bar or a zero.
Found on occultation: at face value no convex silhouette fits the chords; the row prints ∅ and the words instead of a bar. fixed 28 October
rule 8
Every printed number comes from its record, rounded outward.
The build refuses when a record stops supporting its sentence. A printed bound rounds outward, never truncated: drawn here on the hero’s own enclosure.
Found on blind-spot: the headline “Fourteen million certified verdicts could not see this mutant” had no record behind it; it now counts the 4,000-pair sample the page rests on. fixed 28 OctoberFound on contours: 18262/36525 was printed by cutting digits as 0.4999; rounding gives 0.5000. fixed 28 October
rule 9
A legend wherever a mark is.
Every page that uses a stroke or underline of the grammar prints its key; tools/check-wiring.js refuses one that does not.
Found on shape-hunt: its marks were committed without a legend, and the build refused until it had one. fixed 28 October
rule 10
A page copied in is never edited.
It changes only through its wrapper, by anchored replacements that each refuse unless their anchor occurs exactly once.
Found on affect: its script is a copy and stays untouched; every change is a named passage the wrapper replaces. fixed 28 October

Two rulings

ruling · 28 October
A layout placed by a float solver is a chosen picture.
The caption says so once; each mark keeps the standing of what it asserts, so an edge that exists is solid.
Found on neural-geometry: its eigenvector map’s ring is dotted as a chosen picture, and its sentence about the controls was corrected. fixed 28 October
ruling · 28 October
A model’s answer is a claim someone made.

Numbers that rest on a model

rule 11
A number that rests on a model names its premises.
In the order the number travelled, each with its grade and record, ending with the weakest.
Found on Swell: every wave limit is exact over a band that reaches the beach through an unvalidated island model; each beach card now says what its number rests on. fixed 28 October
rule 12
A comparison is calibrated first.
Before a comparison is trusted, the build re-derives it and it must reproduce the published one it stands in for.
Found on Swell: its island model against a Boussinesq model reproduced the published comparison first, with the same probes and tables. fixed 28 October
rule 13
The halo.
An extent carried by a model not validated where it is drawn keeps its crisp edges and gains a blur outside them.
Found on Janela: a band borrowed from the nearest measured site, and the wind band, validated nowhere, gained the halo. fixed 28 October
rule 14
A forecast is computed and dashed, on canvas too.
A canvas takes the same patterns: setLineDash is given G.claim(width).
Found on Swell: its forecast lines were solid until 28 October; every one is dashed now. fixed 28 October
rule 15
A field drawn from a model is a picture of it.
For crests or streamlines, the legend says so once.
Found on Swell: its legend says the crests are a picture of the island model, not a measurement. fixed 28 October
rule 16
Where the model reads low, “under the limit” is flagged.
A flag, not a refusal: a refusal would remove an exact statement.
Found on Swell: Pântano do Sul (0.39×), Ingleses (0.43×) and Galheta (0.99×) read below the Boussinesq model on swells of 11 s and longer; their cards say so. fixed 28 October
the colour law

Ink first, and one red

The law came from a measurement: a sibling project’s two main series colours, run through the validator, were one colour to a deuteranope. Seven rules:

  1. Ink first. Every figure reads in greyscale; a colour comes with a shape and a word.
  2. One colour, one meaning, in every project on the grammar, each registered with its date.
  3. Colour never carries standing. The stroke does; nor does it stand in for a verdict word.
  4. Four kinds only: the witness, identity, order, status.
  5. Every pair passes the validator, on the figure’s own ground, for every simulated deficiency.
  6. No yellow, no orange.
  7. A new colour needs the report and an approval, recorded in the rulebook.

Ink first: lightness falls with standing

the ink ramp
Five greys, OKLab lightness under each. The strokes take them in order of standing, so a copy in greyscale loses nothing.
the one red
Until 29 October the apps carried a second red for a refused state, ΔE about 4 from this one: one red to the eye with two meanings. It is folded: the apps’ refused state reads this token, with its meaning.

Four kinds of colour

the witness
One red, for a shown counterexample and nothing else.
identity
At most three hues for the series in one figure, each with its own shape.
order
One hue in lightness steps, for an ordered scale.
status
An app’s decided states, always with an icon and a word.

Every pair passes the validator

OKLab ΔE × 100, every pair that shares a figure, under the colour-vision model of Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009), computed as this page is built:

before
Distinct to most readers (ΔE 11.4), one colour to a deuteranope (ΔE 0.4). A third hue between them in the list hid it: the validator checks every pair.
after
Re-stepped: ΔE 28.3 for a deuteranope, 30.4 for most. The threshold is 8 for every simulated deficiency and 15 for normal vision.

the premise ladder

How far a premise was checked

Standing says how a number was computed. A number that rests on a physical model needs a second answer: the arithmetic can be exact while its equations never met the thing they describe. Each premise carries a grade, where its checking stopped, and a conclusion is as checked as its weakest premise.

the ladder, with a wave limit on Swell
The verification-and-validation ladder of Roache (1998), Oberkampf and Roy (2010) and ASME V&V 20 (2009), cut to four grades a reader can tell apart. Never a probability, never a confidence.
the same chain, as the page prints it
  1. validatedunverified
    The open sea and its band covered 96.5% of 21,259 satellite passes it had not seen; it claims 9 in 10.
  2. comparedunverified
    The island model beside a Boussinesq model at twelve beaches, 556 cases of a 1 m swell: r 0.44, the island 0.27 m higher on average.
  3. assumed
    Breaking at 0.55 times the depth (Thornton & Guza, 1982); no sandbars; the shallow profile from the nautical chart.

The arithmetic is exact; the weakest premise is assumed, and neither model was verified against an exact solution of its own equations.

  • validated — checked against observations of the thing itself, held out, with the number. On Swell: the open sea’s band, against satellite passes it had not seen.
  • compared — checked against a model that keeps more of the physics, with the number; not against the thing itself. On Swell: the island model, against a Boussinesq model at twelve beaches.
  • verified — the code reproduces an exact solution of its own equations; not measured against the thing itself. On Swell: none yet.
  • assumed — adopted from a source, chosen, or read; no machine here checked it. On Swell: breaking at 0.55 times the depth (Thornton & Guza, 1982), and no sandbars.

A read step — a proof step read and not run by a machine — gets no grade of its own: it is assumed. It never caps a verdict; it is listed in what the page rests on. δ₃ = 117/2192 is an example: a decided finite core, and three steps proved on paper and read.

the foundations

One law, six axioms

The display is a sound map of the record, and the build decides it.

A1
Soundness
Drawing maps a record to marks; reading maps marks back to the records that could have produced them. A page is sound when every record it draws is among them: no mark stronger than its record, in standing, extent or verdict word.
A2
Completeness
Every row of the record a page draws from is a mark, or the empty set with a word.
A3
Outward
A lower end rounds down and an upper end up, in digits and in pixels, at the resolution the figure declares.
A4
A scale with a guide
Four standings onto five strokes, the legend derived from the scale; every channel it uses is monotone in it, or constant.
A5
A typed value
A value carries its datum, its standing, its premises and its record’s address; the arithmetic demotes it and the renderer only reads it.
A6
A frame is a page
In motion every frame obeys A1; a standing never tweens and a value never travels.

the checks
  • check-grammar — only the five patterns may appear; three uses that are not lines are declared by name, each with its reason. The first census, on 5 September, counted twenty-five: the dash had been derived from the stroke width, one carrying 1.04 5.460000000000001 into published HTML.
  • check-marks — a value printed through W.val carries its record’s address; every build follows it back and prints the datum again by the same rule (design/record.js). Four planted marks are refused on every run.
  • check-wiring — a page with a mark of the grammar and no legend is refused; one hero per page, its × first and at weight.
  • check-render — no leaked template text, no paint that does not resolve, no figure thinner in ink than its record, no script error. Its paint check went strict when a legend swatch fell through to black on a near-black ground and passed every run.
  • check-measure — every page driven in Chrome at 1440, 768, 390 px; left edges, overflow, escapes and clipped elements may only go down.

This page is held to the same standard by its own builder, which reads every number on it from its record and refuses to build when one stops saying what the page says.

In figures of your own
