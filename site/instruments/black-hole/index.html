M87*: a black hole where every line says what it rests on

The shadow’s edge decided, the image computed, the disk assumed, on M87*’s own clock

M87*, the black hole the Event Horizon Telescope imaged, drawn so that each element carries its standing. The edge of its shadow is decided in interval arithmetic and drawn solid. The image is computed, ray by ray, through the Kerr metric and drawn as computed. The glowing disk is an assumed thin disk, and it turns at its Keplerian rate on M87*’s own clock, sped up and shown. Nothing here models the plasma, and the page says where the assumptions begin.

The chain the image travelled through, in order, each premise with the grade its checking reached:

  1. assumed
    The Kerr metric general relativity, an uncharged black hole in vacuum, no plasma lensing: adopted, not checked here
  2. assumed
    The inclination, 17° from the jet’s motions (Mertens et al. 2016, 17.2° ± 3.3°); the EHT’s 163° is the same curve mirrored
  3. assumed
    The spin, swept over four values the data constrain nothing; 0.94 is the chosen default
  4. verified
    The ray tracer (instruments/kerr/rays.js) its crossings against Gralla & Lupsasca’s closed form and its shadow against Phase 0’s decided curve: AGREES on every check, at every map (certs/bh-maps-check.json)
  5. assumed
    The thin disk and its emission equatorial, prograde Keplerian, cut at the ISCO, optically thin, J ∝ r⁻², I = Σ g³J; the brightness pattern that turns with it is chosen to be legible
  6. compared
    The ring-to-shadow factor (the mass-row comparison only) calibrated on GRMHD in the EHT’s Paper VI; a thin disk’s lensing ring sits about 5 % outside the curve (Gralla, Holz & Wald 2019)

The image is as checked as its weakest premise: assumed, the thin disk. The shadow’s edge rests only on the Kerr metric, the spin and the view.

The shadow against the ring, per mass row

The size of the shadow on the sky is the curve’s mean diameter d̄ in M times θg = GM/c²D, and θg for a dynamical mass does not depend on the distance assumed (the mass scales with it). The EHT measures a ring of 43.9 ± 0.6 µas, persistent 2017–2021. The ring is not the shadow’s edge: the factor between them is calibrated on simulations, so the last column is the factor each row would need, compared, never a verdict on the black hole, on general relativity, or on which mass is right.

The clock

The disk turns as a disk of matter on circular orbits would: at radius r its pattern goes round once every 2π(r3/2 + a) tg, where tg = GM/c³ is the black hole’s own unit of time. Here one second of the screen is four tg, and the clock on the image counts tg from the moment the page opened and prints them as days of M87*, an interval because the mass is one. The inner edge turns fastest, the outer disk slowest, so the pattern shears and never returns: the picture does not loop, and nothing resets it. Its features are born and fade every 40 tg (assumed), so the shear never winds the disk into stripes. The periods at the inner edge, the ISCO of Bardeen, Press & Teukolsky, decided in Phase 0:

What this does not show

  • The plasma. No magnetohydrodynamics, no electron temperatures, no polarization: the disk is a thin, optically thin sheet with J ∝ r−2, cut at the ISCO. What M87* looks like is set by its plasma, and the plasma is assumed. This is why the page claims a standing for each line and nothing more.
  • The second photon ring. The n = 2 image is far narrower than one pixel of the maps; the layer only samples it, so it is drawn blurred and labelled unresolved. No photon ring has been detected in M87*: every ring layer here is the model’s.
  • Light-travel time. The maps carry no arrival time, so every image of the disk is drawn at one moment; a real n = 1 image arrives later than the direct one.
  • The sky’s orientation. The image is in the black hole’s frame, the spin axis up; it is not rotated to M87*’s position angle.
  • The jet. No jet image is used. The 230 GHz data do not image the jet at this scale, and the jet maps at lower frequencies were not checked for a licence here; the funnel is a dotted sketch, chosen.
  • The ring the EHT measures. It is compared in the table above and not drawn over the image; that is the next step, with the interferometer’s data-allowed set (the interferometer).
