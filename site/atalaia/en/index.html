Atalaia · verifiable integrity for autonomous systems and critical infrastructure

When GNSS lies, Atalaia tells you what is still guaranteed.

A box and software that sit beside any autopilot. Without relying on GNSS, it computes the region where the vehicle is sure to be, accepts GNSS only inside it and, faced with a lie, raises the alarm and switches source, holds or lands. Before the mission, a verdict. Afterwards, a sealed record that another party recomputes.

The problem

Everything that moves on its own trusts a signal anyone can drown out.

Drones, field robots and vessels depend on GNSS, the common name for GPS, Galileo and similar systems. The signal arrives from space very weak: a cheap device drowns it out, and a spoofer imitates it. The worst case is not losing the signal. It is continuing to receive a signal that lies, while the vehicle carries on, convinced.

The rules already call for an answer: DECEA requires contingency planning and a flight termination plan for drones, ISO 18497 asks that an autonomous agricultural machine not leave its operating zone, and IMCA asks for GNSS to be checked against independent references in dynamic positioning. Atalaia proposes an answer that can be checked.

Pre-rendered replays of synthetic missions on Florianópolis Island and in the Itajaí Valley: worst-case energy, the terrain that does or does not give the position back, the airspace, and what the drone does when the GNSS lies. Some pass; others say exactly what is missing.

How it works

A judge beside the navigation system.

Atalaia does not replace the vehicle's navigation system: it judges it. The position from the autopilot, a vision system or the manufacturer comes in as a proposal, and Atalaia decides whether it can be used.

A region, not a point

From the inertial sensor, the terrain, the camera and the height above ground, the box computes the protection region: where the vehicle is sure to be if every sensor stays within its declared limit. GNSS plays no part in that calculation.

GNSS goes through a gate

The satellite position is accepted only if it falls inside the region, plus a small margin. Because the region was built without GNSS, no attacker can drag it.

Lie caught, response ready

Outside the region, GNSS is declared false: the box raises the alarm, identifies the source and switches the estimator's source, holds or lands, as agreed before the mission.

Three verdicts before departure

Route, energy, weather and GNSS, each in the worst case: ✓ PASS, × FAIL or ∅ NEEDS DATA. Needs data says which measurement would decide.

A record someone else recomputes

Every reading is signed at capture and chained. A verifier written by another party redoes every decision from the sealed record.

It speaks MAVLink. On ArduPilot, it delivers the checked position and switches the estimator's source. On a DJI, which today does not accept an external position, it warns and commands a hover or a landing.

Set-based estimation is an established field, and the earlier work is cited in the proof. What we did not find, together, is the combination: the inertial sensor's biases inside the region, the machine-checked proof, the record that another party redoes and a gate that accepts GNSS only inside a region built without it.

Silent by default · the active module

It listens to everything. It transmits only with your authorization, and leaves a receipt.

The active module (proposed)

References a spoofer cannot imitate, switched on only when the mission calls for them:

  • Cooperative ranging and authenticated beaconsPROPOSEDDistance measured round trip over the telemetry link itself, or by UWB, to a beacon at the post, the quay, the platform or the edge of the field plot.
  • Onboard radarsPROPOSEDA radar altimeter on the drone, at 24 GHz or at 60 to 64 GHz, and a Doppler ground-speed radar on land vehicles.
  • Integrity shared across the fleetPROPOSEDEach vehicle broadcasts its region; a local spoofer shows up as disagreement between them.
  • Integrity test benchPROPOSEDthe digital layer exists in simulation, with a sealed reportTesting and test reports for receivers, autopilots and machines, sealed and reproducible. It starts digital, with no radio transmission at all.

The envelope states which transmitters may switch on, where, at what power, for how long and who switches them off. Inside it, the box switches them on by itself when the gate declares GNSS false. Outside it, nothing switches on, not even on command. An empty envelope is silent mode.

Signals, in every mode

Atalaia receives signals and stays silent by default. An optional active module, proposed, switches on only inside an envelope approved before the mission, using type-approved low-power equipment, and every activation is logged. In no mode does it jam, spoof or interfere with third-party signals, or transmit in the GNSS bands.

In no mode: jamming, spoofing or interfering with third-party signals; transmitting in the GNSS bands; anti-drone by jamming; guiding weapons, designating targets or tracking people.

The test bench

Testing under attack, without transmitting a single signal.

Makers of drones, autopilots and autonomous machines need to know what their equipment does when GNSS lies. The Atalaia bench injects the attack, declared in advance, and delivers a report with five items: containment, alarm, false alarm, response and outcome, each with ✓ PASS, × FAIL or ∅ NEEDS DATA. The report is sealed, and another party redoes it from the package.

Questions

Quick questions

Has Atalaia flown yet?

No. No real-world flights yet. What we show comes from simulation with the real ArduPilot firmware, from recorded data, official test vectors, the bench and proof in Lean, and every number says where it came from. Calibration flights at an authorized site are gate G2.

Does it transmit any signal?

By default, no: it only receives. The active module, optional and proposed, switches on only inside an envelope approved before the mission, using type-approved low-power equipment, and every activation is logged. It never jams, spoofs or interferes with third-party signals, or transmits in the GNSS bands.

Does it work with my drone?

The box speaks MAVLink. On ArduPilot, it delivers the checked position and switches the estimator's source. On a DJI, it warns and commands a hover or a landing, because DJI does not accept an external position today.

What if GNSS disappears altogether?

The region then grows using only the other sensors. While it fits within the phase's limit, the mission continues; when it no longer fits, the box holds or lands where it was assessed in advance. With a consumer-grade inertial sensor, the guarantee is lost in about 3 minutes (simulation; /atalaia/doutrina/), which is why the verdict before the mission matters.

Why trust a bound you compute yourselves?

Because you do not have to trust it. The central bound is proven in Lean and checked by machine, and every decision sits in a sealed record that a verifier written by another party recomputes. If it disagrees, the disagreement shows.

Does the bench report replace type approval?

No. The bench sells testing and test reports, sealed and reproducible. Type approval of equipment is Anatel's role, through bodies it designates; authorizing operations is the role of ANAC and DECEA.

Who is behind it?

Carlos Toledo, author of the protocol and technical lead, with AI help (Claude) on research and writing. Research and pilot partners will be named once they confirm.
