cert-machine · verify

AI now publishes faster than anyone can check. Verify checks it.

Verify re-decides a published claim from its own bytes, with code that shares nothing with the claimant, and returns one of three words: CERTIFIED, REFUTED with the object that breaks it, or REFUSED with the reason. Every verdict comes with a certificate anyone can re-run.

Two ways to use it

  • Graders that cannot be gamed, for RL and evaluation teams. Send a task family, its answer format and what counts as correct. You get a grader whose ground truth is a certificate, in code you run, that must refuse planted wrong answers before it grades anything. On the mix measured here a tolerance grader admitted 89.55% of 4,000 provably wrong answers; the certificate grader admitted none and rejected nothing right.
  • Independent audits, for labs, AI-for-science companies and registries. Send a claim as published, with its bytes. You get the gaps listed first, every finite part decided in exact arithmetic or by a second, independent proof kernel, and every formal statement read against the claim and kept apart from what was decided. Before or after publication; the pilot’s terms say when the report is published.

below · how it works

From a claim to a certificate

The product in four steps. An audit is the whole path for one published claim; a grader states a task family once and runs steps 3 and 4 on every answer a model gives.

  • 1 · The claim. What a model, a paper or a leaderboard said, with the files behind it: a configuration, a table, a proof, an answer. Each file is pinned by its SHA-256 before anything is read, so the verdict names exactly what it decided.
  • 2 · Stated. claimbox turns the claim’s words into an object a machine can check, prints it back in plain words, and refuses what does not come down to finitely many exact facts, naming why. A statement is read against the claim before anything is decided, and the reading is kept apart from the decision.
  • 3 · Decided. Exact rational arithmetic, interval enclosures rounded outward, or a second proof kernel, in code written apart from the claimant’s. No floating-point number takes part in a decision, and the decider is attacked with planted forgeries before it is trusted.
  • 4 · The certificate. One of three words. CERTIFIED, with a falsifier that would break it. REFUTED, with the object that breaks the claim. REFUSED, with the reason and what would make it decidable. Each re-runs with one command, without this engine.
  • An audit. For labs, AI-for-science companies and registries: a published claim goes in at step 1, and a report comes out listing every gap first, then every finite part decided, every formal statement read against the claim.
  • A grader. For RL and evaluation teams: a task family is stated once at step 2, then every model answer is decided at step 3 and the certificate is the reward. It must refuse planted wrong answers before it grades anything, so a policy cannot learn to fool it.

below · the limits

What it refuses

  • Claims that do not come down to finitely many exact facts. An asymptotic statement, a “for all n”, or a proof written in prose is refused with the reason, and the refusal says what would make it decidable.
  • Your code. Nothing a claimant wrote is run. The decision comes from code written apart from it, so a bug cannot certify itself.
  • Meaning, passed off as a decision. A kernel cannot say what a statement means. Where a verdict rests on reading a statement, the report says so and keeps the reading apart from what was decided.
  • A verdict it cannot back. Absence of proof is never turned into CERTIFIED or REFUTED. Not peer-reviewed; every page ships its own falsifiers.

How a pilot starts

  • 1. Open a request. The pilot form asks what you want checked and where it is published. The form is public; if the claim is not, write to carlos@carlostoledo.co instead.
  • 2. Scope before work. Before anything is run, you get the list of what can be decided, what will be read, and what will be refused, and why.
  • 3. The certificate. Each verdict comes with its certificate and a one-line command that re-derives it without this engine.
