Era 2 · results, built from the record

The model's lineage beat blind rules. The same model, asked alone, did better.

Eras 0 and 1 asked whether a model's lineage does better than blind rules, and both times it did not. Their founders had one knob, and a near-miss was lethal, so every gain in every arm was the knob turned finer. Era 2 starts from PM-1, a founder written by hand with no knob, where a layout that fails costs nothing. Five arms share one physics and one training set in every run. The lineage has Qwen3.6 rewrite functions under selection. Blind rules and blind tree edits make the same rewrites with no model. The 7B's lineage uses the small model of eras 0 and 1. The AI alone is Qwen3.6 asked for whole programs, as many as the lineage's written tokens buy. The line to cross is 0.20516 on the panel, the ceiling of the founder's grid.

Under the same selection and births, the model's lineage beat blind rules by 0.0103, higher in sixteen of sixteen runs (p = 1/65536): by more than the 0.003 margin in eight, and in six by less than 10⁻⁹, both champions still on the founder's grid. In ten of sixteen runs its champion left the founder's design, certified exactly; the rules' did in none. The same model asked alone for whole programs, at the lineage's written tokens, did better still, by 0.0136, and crossed in fourteen.

The tests, fixed in advance

One primary test, three gated behind it.

H2 is the exact sign-flip test on the sum of the sixteen differences, against all 65,536 ways of flipping their signs. Its verdict is read from the exact 90% interval for the mean shift, with an equivalence margin of 0.003. S1 to S3 were tested only because H2 was supported, under Holm's correction. E is an existence claim: one champion above the ceiling, certified exactly by both deciders, is proof that a lineage left its founder's design.

After era 2

What era 3 should ask.

Era 2 found structure that blind rules and blind tree search did not, and found it only where the stronger model wrote the rewrites. It also found that the same model, asked alone for whole programs, did better than its own lineage. These are proposals; nothing below is fixed until its protocol is written, hashed and timestamped before generation one.

  • Selection over whole programs. The AI alone wins with a dozen answers a run. A lineage whose rewrite is the whole program, under the same selection, would ask whether heredity adds anything to what the model already writes in one answer.
  • Steps after the first. Six of the ten lineage champions that crossed score the same on the panel to nine digits, 0.216272380, and only one went further. Landscapes of tree-edit distance against exact fitness would show whether the next step is far, or simply never drawn.
  • Units and habilis. Modules as content-addressed units, so a layout gene found once can be reused by name.
  • The island file system. Lives that make millions of file calls sit near the host's wall-clock guard, because each call is answered in Python. A faster island, at an era boundary, takes them away from it.
The runs

Every run is reported. None is replaced or dropped.

A run's name is its seed. Each run's head is timestamped on Bitcoin through OpenTimestamps when it comes in. The sign-blind rule, which sees the sizes of the differences and never their signs, kept the era at sixteen runs.

Every run was replayed on the pod that wrote it, one model answer in ten asked again: thirteen of sixteen reproduce every line. In the other three, one life each differs, every one a life of millions of file calls near the host's 300-second wall-clock guard, which is not part of the physics: two met it in the replay and reproduce when relived alone, and one, the era's only quarantine, met it in the run itself and meets it again alone. No line that selection, a champion or the summary depends on differs. Away from home, era-2-1 replays every line from a fresh copy on a machine that did not write it. strata/era-2.replay-2026-10-26.json
