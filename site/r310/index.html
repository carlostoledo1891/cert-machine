R(3,10), in the open

Is there a graph on 40 vertices with no triangle and no 10 vertices pairwise unjoined? If none exists, R(3,10) = 40; if one does, 41. Nobody knows, and nothing on this page decides it. This is the work, shown while it happens: every case of the question and who closed it, what runs now in a sealed box, and every error caught on the way.

A live page, rebuilt from its records; this build reads them up to 05:38 UTC on 23 November 2026. Nothing on it is refereed. How this page updates: it is rebuilt from its records at every phase finish, and its change log at the foot says what changed.

what is not established, first
  • R(3,10) is open. 40 ≤ R(3,10) ≤ 41 still stands. Neither opened result decides it, and nothing here moves its value.
  • Case R(7) was closed first by someone else. Zhiyang Chen’s own AI run excluded it on 21 November. Ours is a second computation of it, and it holds only under six premises the run does not check.
  • Nobody outside has read any of it. No referee, no outside replay, no reply. Most sources on the map are unrefereed too, and each is marked.
  • One person, models of two families, no second party. The tracks are AI sessions working for one person. “Own” and “independent” mean code written without reading the other track’s code, nothing more.
  • The sealed box claims nothing. It shows what it is, its anchors and its progress. Its contents are not shown and are not stated.
  • The map is a reading. It is the known reduction as read from the sources on a date, not a proof that its cases cover every graph.

box 1 · opened

Theorem C35

Any finite triangle-free simple graph with no independent set of size 10 that contains an induced copy of the circulant C35(1,7,11,16) (vertices 0..34, u ~ v iff v − u ≡ ±1, ±7, ±11 or ±16 mod 35) has at most 39 vertices, and any four distinct vertices outside the copy induce a 4-cycle.

The bound 39 is attained. So no triangle-free graph on 40 vertices with no independent 10-set contains an induced C35(1,7,11,16).

Proved in Lean 4.35.0 with Mathlib, on the three standard axioms only. Its key lemma is checked by Lean’s kernel on a certificate of 32,529 nodes, with no native code. It was also decided twice outside Lean, by a SAT proof two checkers accept and by our own search over 403,541 nodes.

  • CERTIFIED S the Lean statements say the claim (read once, by cert-machine)
  • CERTIFIED D1 a SAT decider: our formula equals the other family’s; lrat-check and the verified cake_lpr accept both proofs
  • CERTIFIED D2 a second decider, no SAT: our own branching search closes every branch, 403,541 nodes
  • CERTIFIED L Lemma P checked by Lean’s kernel on a 32,529-node certificate, no native code; the three standard axioms only
  • CERTIFIED B the bridge from Lemma P to the theorem, the other family’s files unchanged
  • CERTIFIED K second checkers: leanchecker replay, nanoda, con-leche (leanchecker --fresh not run)
  • CERTIFIED corollary no vertex of degree 4, given Goedgebeur–Radziszowski’s uniqueness of the (3,9;35)-graph; known before

A corollary, prose proof, not in Lean, conditional on Goedgebeur–Radziszowski’s uniqueness of the (3,9;35)-graph: In a triangle-free graph on 40 vertices with no independent set of size 10, deleting any 5 vertices leaves an independent set of size 9; so at least 6 deletions are needed to leave none. It contains the degree-4 corollary, which was known before.

The question was posed in public before: by Unsolved Lab (M20, 1 Oct, not run), in the repository andrey-kokoev/marici (a note dated 9 Nov: “this extension family has not been excluded”) and by an idea scout of Chen’s run (21 Nov, timed out). Nobody had decided it.

Closed first by Zhiyang Chen’s own AI run, r310_ralph: claim C-004, added in its round 1 (21 Nov, 16:50:52 UTC) and confirmed in round 2 (17:49:01 UTC) as a certificate with a custom trace, over all 977,008 admissible package pairs; a hand reduction, not Lean. Ours is a second computation of the case, with our own reduction and code, registered at 22 Nov, 02:13 UTC, before the rented machine existed; the reduction uses Chen’s Lemma R7. Our 977,008 configurations are Chen’s package pairs, set for set, by our reading of his pair_packages.py: his filters re-implemented, not run. It holds only under six premises, listed first:

  1. P1 e(3,8,27) = 85 (Goedgebeur–Radziszowski) — from the literature; not checked here.
  2. P2 McKay’s list of (3,8;27)-graphs is complete (Brinkmann and Goedgebeur, 2012, by McKay’s page) — from the literature; our filter of it finds 4 graphs with 85 edges and none below, which does not test completeness.
  3. P3 the list of 977,008 configurations is complete — a checked statement on the four cores: the cover formula is unsatisfiable on each, both checkers accept, two generators give the same clauses; in Lean it enters as a hypothesis.
  4. P4 the CNF written for each configuration is the formula the reduction describes — the encoding’s soundness (T0, T1) is proved in Lean, built and replayed by the control room and accepted by nanoda, con-leche and leanchecker --fresh; that the program’s bytes equal the formula was computed on all 977,008 rows (T2), not proved; Lean holds no instance of the 977,008 refutations.
  5. P5 the reduction from a graph to a configuration — a Lean chain from the other model family, a conditional whose hypotheses are the literature, the cover and every record’s refutation; read once by one agent; Lean does not prove that its embedded data are the pinned file.
  6. P6 the two proof checkers, lrat-check and cake_lpr — trusted as tools; cake_lpr is formally verified.

Then: 977,008 of 977,008 configurations refuted, each by an LRAT proof that lrat-check and the formally verified cake_lpr both accept: 879,379 at level 3, 97,627 at level 4, 2 on the full CNF.

If P1–P6 hold: no 9-regular triangle-free graph on 40 vertices with independence number at most 9 has two non-adjacent vertices with exactly 7 common neighbours.

What ours adds to C-004, as facts: LRAT proofs; two checkers, one formally verified (cake_lpr); a Lean proof that the encoding is sound (T0, T1); the CNF bytes recomputed from the Lean definition on every row (T2, computed); the premises written out; the rules timestamped before the pod existed; four controls before production.

How a claim travels

  1. 1ProposedOne model family proposes: Claude, or GPT-6.2-astra. Until something checks it, it is a claim.
  2. 2CheckedCode written from the statement alone, without reading the proposer’s code, or the other family: an exact checker, a kernel, a second enumeration.
  3. 3AuditedBy us, not a second party: a control-room note, gaps first: what holds, what the report says that the files do not, what was not run.
  4. 4VerdictCERTIFIED, REFUTED or REFUSED, each with its reason. A refusal is a verdict.
  5. 5SealedThe record’s digest is stamped in Bitcoin, before anyone outside sees it.
  6. 6OpenedAfter its audit, on my word: the files, the salt and the digest, so anyone can re-derive the anchor.

On the two opened boxes, 28 checks have a verdict: 19 CERTIFIED, 3 EQUAL, 2 AGREED, 3 PASS, 1 REFUSED. Each is counted once; our own corrections are never counted as findings.
