The claim that matrix multiplication has exponent 9/4, checked in the open

OpenAI’s November 24 release says the exponent of matrix multiplication is 9/4 over every field; the new part is the lower bound. The paper credits the upper bound, ω ≤ 9/4, to an OpenAI preprint (over the complex numbers) and to Amanov, van den Berg and Zuiddam (over every field); its Lean formalization states both directions. This page checks, while it happens, what a machine can check: whether the Lean proof checks, and whether its statement says what the paper claims.

Read the release, at the commit pinned for this audit, and the pre-registration, sealed before any run. A live page, rebuilt from its records as the rows move; this build reads them up to 04:45 UTC on 25 November 2026. Nothing on it is refereed, and its change log at the foot says what changed.

Independence here is by procedure (separate prompts, sealed outputs, no shared files), not by vendor. Who does what is below.
  • No person checks the mathematics here. The rows check what a machine can check: whether the Lean proof checks, and whether its Lean statement says what the paper claims. Even a CERTIFIED verdict would not say that any human has checked the mathematics.
  • What a pass would still not settle. By the sealed rule, CERTIFIED says nothing about divisions (G2), the per-n bound or Corollary 1.2 (G3) unless the census finds them proved, and nothing beyond the trusted base: the kernels, the three axioms and the Mathlib definitions the statements use.
the gaps

Seven gaps, read at the pin

Read from the release at the commit pinned before anything ran, and worded here as the pre-registration words them (sealed 02:52 UTC, 25 November 2026). Each is a fact about what the release states or checks; none is a verdict on the proof.

  • G1. ONE KERNEL in their bar: MatrixExponent.json has enable_nanoda: false.
  • G2. The Lean model is DIVISION-FREE (gates: constant, input, add, sub, mul); the paper's Theorem 1.1 and its per-n bound allow division gates. A lower bound for division-free programs is weaker than one with divisions.
  • G3. The per-n headline (⌈n^{9/4}/2⌉ gates at EVERY n ≥ 2) and Corollary 1.2 (geometric border rank of T_n ≥ n^{9/4}; asymptotic rank = n^{9/4}) are NOT in the challenge, which states exponents only.
  • G4. The paper claims 1/2 ≤ α_F ≤ 2/3; the Lean states α = 1/2 and an exact rectangular formula, i.e. more than this paper claims; docs/107.md attributes the extra to three other manuscripts (two of 2026-09-24, one companion of 2026-11-24).
  • G5. The literature as read disagrees on the upper bound: DeepMind's notebook of 2026-11-04 says "The current best bound is ω < 2.371339" and claims ω < 2.371177; the paper calls ω ≤ 9/4 known. The Lean, if it checks, proves both directions. (Read with correction C-3.)
  • G6. The build applies 28 compatibility patches to dependencies (lean/patches/); whether Mathlib or any patched dependency is in the closure of the three theorems is unmeasured. (Read with correction C-1.)
  • G7. formalOmega is an sInf of real numbers; Lean's sInf of an empty or unbounded-below set is 0. The control room's reading: the set is non-empty (schoolbook) and bounded below (N² degree-2 outputs) — to be PROVED (F3).

What each verdict would mean

The rule was sealed with the rows, before any run. Quoted as sealed:

  • “CERTIFIED (the formal claim, as the textbooks define ω for division-free straight-line programs): K0, K1, K1N, K2, K3, KR and CR pass; C1, C2 and C3 are refused; F2 proves BOTH blind statements' propositions with Comparator accepting our challenge; F3 holds; every MATERIAL item of F4 is decided.”
  • “Otherwise the verdict names the first failing row:”
    • “REFUTED (a proved defect in meaning: the Lean is weaker than, vacuous for, or different from the English claim)”
    • “REFUSED (a row could not be run within the pod ceiling or the time it needs)”
    • “OPEN (undecided, with the reason)”
  • “What CERTIFIED would NOT say: that any human has checked the mathematics; anything about divisions, the per-n bound, border rank or asymptotic rank unless CEN finds them proved; anything beyond the trusted base (the kernels, the three axioms, the Mathlib definitions the statements use).”

Amended before any bridge attempt (A8, 2026-11-25 03:19 UTC): “F2’s scope (a flaw in PREREG.md, fixed before any bridge attempt): the writers were asked for a with-division proposition, which the challenge does not claim. CERTIFIED's F2 condition covers the propositions in the challenge's scope (each writer's SquareExponent, DualExponent, RectangularExponent). Each writer's SquareExponentWithDivision is attempted from any theorem of the release (N2 included) and reported proved or not, with the reason, under G2; it does not decide the verdict.”

In plain words: CERTIFIED would say that the release’s Lean theorems check under three kernels and on a second machine, that planted errors are refused, and that two statements of the claim written without seeing the release’s follow from its theorems. The rule’s own last sentence says what it would not say.
