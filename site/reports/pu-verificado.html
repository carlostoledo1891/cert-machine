Tesouro Direto prices re-decided from the official rule table

PU Verificado

Tesouro Direto publishes a price for every federal bond it sells, every business day, and the Treasury publishes the formula that produces it — down to a table saying which intermediate value is truncated and which is rounded, and at how many decimals. So each published price has exactly one value under its own rule. This page re-decides every price of the titles that need no price index — Tesouro Prefixado (LTN), Prefixado com Juros Semestrais (NTN-F) and Tesouro Selic (LFT) — in the public history, 145,679 of them, in exact arithmetic: 132,538 are the rule’s value to the centavo, 12,791 lie inside the interval the published rate’s two decimals allow, 289 are not the rule’s value, 0 could not be decided, and 61 are NEEDS DATA (settlement on or after maturity). The conventions the publisher follows but does not print — which day a purchase settles, whether the centavos are cut or rounded — are measured, not assumed.

The pinned history precotaxatesourodireto.csv (Tesouro Transparente, ODbL; 77,173 rows of Tesouro Prefixado = LTN, Tesouro Prefixado com Juros Semestrais = NTN-F and Tesouro Selic = LFT, 2004-12-31 to 2026-10-27, 83 titles), the ANBIMA national-holiday file to 2099, and the STN methodology of 29/04/2024 whose Tabela 3 is the rule (= ANBIMA 2021 §22), every byte sha256-pinned in corpus/pu/PINS.json. Pre-registered in corpus/pu/preregistration.json, with 6 dated amendments. For the LFT, the Selic daily rate (BCB SGS 11) builds the VNA and the Selic target (SGS 432) the projection, both pinned (6,603 Selic days, 2000-07-03 → 2026-10-15); the settlement days B3 did not trade are derived from B3’s circulars and archived calendar pages (corpus/pu/b3-settlement.json). Both sides of every row (compra, venda) under the pre-registered settlement hypotheses and printed readings. Still out: the IPCA-indexed titles (their daily projection is not public), ANBIMA’s secondary-market prices and B3’s.

tl;dr
  • The finding. 90.98% of 145,679 prices are the rule’s value to the centavo. The census decides the conventions: a purchase settles on the next business day (D+1, 62,692 buy-side prices, the whole history); a sale settled on the next business day too until 2021-09-10 (56,065 prices) and on the quotation day since (D+0, 14,674 prices; every sell-side price from 2022 on); and the centavos are the 6-decimal price truncated, never rounded (66,241 prices reproduce only under truncation; 7 only under rounding). 18,722 prices reproduce only under the calendar in force on the day they were published: 20 November became a national holiday on 2023-12-21 (Lei 14.759), today’s holiday file carries it from 2024 on, and every earlier quotation of a title maturing after 2024 was priced without it — one business day more per weekday 20 November between settlement and maturity. The data place the publisher’s switch to the new calendar at the quotation of 2023-12-26 (the last quotation priced on the old calendar: 2023-12-22). 804 prices reproduce only with settlement on the next day B3 traded — a São Paulo holiday the exchange observed (25 January, 9 July, 20 November through 2021), 24 or 31 December, or the 2014 World Cup days — the settlement calendar is B3’s, derived here from B3’s own circulars and the exchange’s archived calendar pages (80 such days 2004–2026, 56 named in a pinned document, 24 following the rule alone; 181 prices rest on a rule-only day). 12,791 prices are CONSISTENT, not reproduced: Tesouro Direto prints the LFT’s ágio/deságio at two decimals and prices with more — the printed centavos lie inside the interval the printed rate’s half-unit allows, and no closer decision is possible from what is published (LFT 12,751, NTN-F 39, LTN 1). The box holds under the side’s primary settlement for 12,058 of them and only under another pre-registered settlement for 733 (D1 573, D1x 28, D1b3 25, D0 33, D1/pre 27, D0/pre 47) — the sell side settled D+1 until 2021-09-10, and the primary D+0 alone never saw those rows (amendment 6). 289 prices are OFF: not the rule’s value under any pre-registered settlement; 0 REFUSED (the enclosure straddled a truncation boundary); 61 NEEDS DATA (settlement on or after maturity). Each is listed with its exact value and the difference in centavos, or its reason.
  • The mechanism. Every step of the Treasury’s formula is exact: business days on the pinned calendar, the rate truncated at 6 decimals, the exponent du/252 truncated at 14, each NTN-F flow rounded at 9, the price truncated at 6. The one irrational step, (1 + taxa)^(du/252), is an enclosure — BigInt fixed point with every series remainder bounded — and each truncation is a decision from the two ends or a refusal. A second decider in Python (decimal, 60 digits, directed rounding; no shared code) re-derives every OFF and REFUSED row and a seeded sample (1,789/1,789 agree). The methodology’s own worked examples (LTN 753.315323, NTN-F 903.075616 with its 12 flows, LFT 3455.211852 with its VNA and projection) are reproduced digit for digit before any row is decided. What each verdict rests on, and how far each premise was checked, is in §4.

§1 · the atlas

Every title, every day, one verdict

The field is the result: an almost uniform texture of reproduced prices, with the exceptions where they are. The buy side is drawn because it carries the settlement convention; on the sell side (D+1 until 2021-09-10, D+0 since) 91.16% of 75,982 prices are REPRODUCED, 6,551 are CONSISTENT and 164 OFF, and its exceptions are in the table of §3.

§2 · the census

The conventions the publisher follows, measured

Three things the methodology does not say about Tesouro Direto’s published prices were pre-registered as hypotheses and decided row by row: on which day a purchase and a sale settle, and how a 6-decimal price becomes the 2-decimal one printed. The census answers: buy D+1 across the whole history; sell D+1 until 2021-09-10, sell D+0 since — a change of settlement convention made during 2021 and not printed beside the price (770 sell-side prices of 2021 reproduce only under D+0, every one from 2022 on; 28 earlier prices do too, on 9 days where both settlements were quoted); centavos truncated throughout. A calendar is dated. The pinned holiday file is the one ANBIMA serves today, and it carries a holiday that did not exist when most of these prices were published: 20 November, national since Lei 14.759 of 21 December 2023. A quotation of 2019 for a title maturing in 2029 was priced with the calendar of 2019, in which 2024, 2025, 2026 and 2028 each had one more business day than today’s file says (2027’s 20 November is a Saturday). Re-decided with today’s calendar those prices are OFF by exactly that many days; re-decided with the calendar as of the quotation they are the rule’s value to the centavo. The pre-registration’s second amendment added that hypothesis after the full run showed the pattern (du + 1 for 2025 maturities, + 2 for 2026, + 4 for 2029 on a seeded sample of 30 rows); the rows that need it are the heavier bright lines in the atlas and the “of which old calendar only” columns here. Around the law’s date the census reads, per quotation day, how many rows reproduce only under today’s calendar and how many only under the old one; the last day priced on the old calendar is 2023-12-22 and the first priced on the new one is 2023-12-26.

The settlement calendar is the exchange’s. The methodology counts du on the national bank calendar, but Tesouro Direto settles through B3, which did not operate on São Paulo’s own holidays (25 January, 9 July, and 20 November from 2006) through 2021, on 24 and 31 December every year, and on the 2014 World Cup days the exchange listed. A purchase quoted the day before settles on the next day B3 traded, and the published price says so. corpus/pu/b3-settlement.json derives those days from B3’s circulars (2017–2026) and from the exchange’s calendar pages as the Wayback Machine captured them (2004–2016), citing for each date the documents that name the holiday; dates no captured document names (2007 and 2008 among them) follow the rule alone and the prices resting on them are counted apart. The July 2014 match days are not in the table because no page listing them was captured; those prices stay OFF.

The settlement skip: Tesouro Direto quotes on 24 and 31 December but does not settle on them, and the ANBIMA holiday file says in its own notes that it lists only days that close bank reserves and omits the last business day of the year. The amendment of 2026-10-16 in the pre-registration added that hypothesis after the first 4,000 rows showed it; the rows that need it are counted apart, never folded into the primary count.

§5 · what this is, and is not

Scope, limits, and what comes next

  • The titles without a price index. LTN and NTN-F need none; the LFT’s VNA is recomputed from the pinned Selic series under the rule (the accumulated factor rounded at 16 decimals each day) and projected to the settlement with the Selic target, as the methodology’s own example does. IPCA+ and IGPM+ need, before the month’s index is published, a projection the publisher used and does not print beside the price; no public archive of it was found, so those titles would be NEEDS DATA by construction — the count of them is the honest headline of that slice, and it is next.
  • One publisher. Tesouro Direto’s own prices against the Treasury’s own rule. ANBIMA publishes indicative prices for the same titles at 6 decimals and B3 states the same formulas with no truncation rule at all; measuring the three against each other is phase B, pending ANBIMA’s republication terms.
  • The rate is the publisher’s. The published rate (2 decimals) is taken as the input the rule names; whether that rate is itself a rounding of an internal one is not decidable from what is published.
  • What a slip is. A printed number differing from its own rule’s value for its own inputs. It is not an error attributed to anyone, and a convention the publisher follows without publishing is named as NEEDS DATA where the record cannot say which it was.
  • Daily. The history re-decides at every build; the day’s new prices are the next step: one run after the publisher posts, appended to the ledger, with the publication time and the decision time kept apart.
