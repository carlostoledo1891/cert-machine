§1 · the gaps

Where the claims stop short of their headlines

Each claim is filed under every kind of gap it shows. A claim can show several.

kind of gapclaimswhat it means
The statement is not the headline20The checked statement is a different object, a weaker or partial statement, has a moved clause, an old or degenerate target, or is missing, so the proof can pass while the headline stays unchecked.
One kernel, one party17The second kernel is mostly off, checks are run by the claimant or graded by AI, and the few rebuilds often vendor the claimant's construction modules.
Custom definitions are trusted14Machine models, encodings and definitions written in the challenge file, and Comparator definition holes compared by type only, are part of what a reader must trust.
Missing files and checkers that stop short14Matrices, certificate files, witnesses, execution archives and ancillary files are not published, or a shipped checker skips what it names.
Floats and tolerances in the checker8Numerical certificates were checked with floating-point verifiers, tolerances, sampling grids or one-sided validators.
Large computations nobody has replayed8The cost of replaying a search or a build (pod-hours, memory, unreleased tooling) is itself the open gap.
Existence without a witness5Objects are proved to exist and never shown, or a disproof is non-effective.
Gaps the claimant declares5Some claimants list their own gaps by design, and those declared gaps become the audit's reading list.
Credit and novelty, not correctness5Literature rediscovery, priority disputes and withdrawals bear on credit rather than on whether a checked statement holds.
§2 · the lead case

ETH: from the labs' machines to the textbook

In October 2026 OpenAI and Anthropic each published a Lean proof that the Exponential Time Hypothesis is false. Both proofs check. Each is stated for a machine and an input format of its own lab's choosing, so the question left is whether those statements are the textbook ETH. This is where that question stands, step by step, as of 2026-11-04.

  • CERTIFIED. Both labs' Lean proofs check against their own statements, on two kernels each. Record: certs/eth-kernel.json.
  • CERTIFIED. OpenAI's theorem implies Anthropic's ETHIsFalse, kernel-checked inside OpenAI's Lean 4.34.1 (Hawkley's chain, nanoda on); the seam to Anthropic's Lean 4.33.1 is read, not decided. Record: certs/eth-hawkley-heavy.json.
  • CERTIFIED. complexitylib's native ¬ETH on cslib's deterministic multitape Turing machine with the binary encoding encCNF (PR #1338) decided here: built from source, the three standard axioms from our own file, nanoda accepting all 49,025 declarations beneath not_eth, leanchecker --fresh exit 0. It formalizes OpenAI's Proposition 6.4 route as a native re-proof, not a bridge from either lab's own statement. Record: certs/eth-cslib-d1.json.
  • IN PROGRESS. ¬ETH on the word RAM at logarithmic word size: complexitylib's TM-to-RAM inclusions need polynomial time, so the space-based address bound is new (claimbox lean/eth-bridge, a proposal until decided here).
  • NOT STARTED. Mathlib's FinTM2 on encClauses simulated by cslib's multitape TM on encCNF, so both labs' own statements reach the standard machine.
  • A READING. That any formal ETH (ours, cslib's, a lab's) is the textbook ETH is a reading of a definition. It stays one; the best outcome makes it a reading of a standard multitape machine and a word RAM with a reasonable binary encoding.

The page on the two proofs is /eth/; the plan for the open steps is pre-registered in notes/eth-model-gap-preregistration-2026-11-04.md.

What this census is, and is not

  • A reading, dated. The outside facts were read on 2026-11-04 from the sources each row cites. A claim, a fix or an outside check after that date is not here. Confidence C means confirmed at the primary source, P partly, U not confirmed (7 rows).
  • Our verdicts are records. Every verdict in the "decided here" column is re-read from the record and pointer printed beside it when this page is built; the page refuses to build if one has moved. A verdict covers exactly the scope written beside it.
  • Not a ranking. Labs publish in different forms and amounts; counting their rows says nothing about the quality of their mathematics.
  • Not exhaustive. The census found 56 claims; there are more. Headline research claims from several labs were looked for and not found in the window.
  • Same operator. Where a new proof is written for the ETH steps, claimbox writes it and cert-machine decides it; both are this author's, so a decision here is a separation of roles, not a second party.
