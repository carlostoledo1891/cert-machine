Two AI models on a mean-field game: claims decided, proofs read

I gave GPT-6.2 Astra (OpenAI) and Claude Fable 5.2 (Anthropic) the same single message: find something true and new about a stationary mean-field game whose published map left 59% of its area undecided. Each answered once, and then each read the other’s mathematics. With Claude (Anthropic’s AI model, and the same model as one of the two), I decided every list of solutions they submitted with this repository’s certifier. Nothing either model claimed was refuted. Five equilibria are certified where three was the most known, seven by a construction that follows from both answers, and a proof that the equilibrium is unique reaches 40% of the map. The proofs were read line by line. They were not formally checked, and one model’s proofs have not yet had a reader outside one model family.

the game

A crowd that is drawn to itself

A mean-field game describes many small players, each choosing a path while reacting to the density of all the others. The stationary quadratic game on a circle is the pair

−σu″ + ½(u′)² + ρ = c·m + A·cos 2πx, −σm″ − (m·u′)′ = 0, ∫m = 1, ∫u = 0, m > 0, σ = ½

with m the density, u the value function and ρ a constant. When the coupling c is positive the players avoid each other and the equilibrium is unique, a theorem of Lasry and Lions. When c is negative they are drawn to density, and there can be several equilibria. A is the depth of a potential well.

This repository had published a map of the rectangle −18 ≤ c ≤ 2, 0 ≤ A ≤ 1.2 in 19,800 cells, each decided over the whole cell: at least two equilibria on 31% of the area, exactly one on 10% (the part with c ≥ 0), and no decision on 59%. The most equilibria certified at one parameter was three.

the experiment

One message each, then each reads the other

  • One message. Both models received the same text, with the whole task and nothing attached: the game, what was already known, what would count as new, and how to hand over the numbers of every solution claimed. It is corpus/mfg-two-models/message.md.
  • One answer. Each model worked in one reply and ended it with its claims, each labelled proved, computationally supported or conjecture, with a probability.
  • The check. Every list of candidate solutions was given to the certifier exactly as submitted. Nothing was polished: a polished candidate would be the solver’s discovery, not the model’s.
  • The cross-read. Each model then received the other’s mathematics with one identical instruction, to find the strongest reason not to accept it (corpus/mfg-two-models/cross-read-instruction.md).
1 · how many equilibria

Five from each model, then seven

  • The count without a potential. Both models found, independently in their first answers, the same exact law at A = 0: the number of even equilibria is 1 + 2·#{j ≥ 1 : π²j² < −c}. Past each threshold c = −π²j² a new pair appears, with j peaks. Each read the other’s proof and found it to hold.
  • Five. Just past the third threshold, near c = −90, the two-peak and three-peak pairs coexist with the continuation of the constant state. GPT-6.2 Astra submitted five there over a small box at A = 0.5; Claude Fable 5.2 submitted five at 6 points and over 2 boxes. All are certified.
  • Seven. Both models stopped at five and said why: the message allowed 64 modes, and the one-peak states are too close to empty to resolve. Their law says how to go further. At A = 0 a j-peak state is a one-peak state rescaled, so at c = −360 the 4-, 5- and 6-peak pairs are one-peak states at c = −22.5, −14.4 and −10, all well resolved. In this session I built those six and the constant with 160 modes, and re-solved them at A = 0.1. Seven pairwise distinct equilibria are certified at both, by both deciders. The candidates are mine; the law is the models’.
  • Positivity. The certifier proves the density positive by a bound on a grid, and refuses a density that comes close to zero. Both models prove a short lemma that makes this unnecessary: the flux of any real periodic solution vanishes, so its density is an exponential and cannot change sign. Claude Fable 5.2 drew the consequence in the cross-read. On this page a list is CERTIFIED when every candidate is enclosed and the enclosures are pairwise disjoint; the column beside it says whether the grid bound agrees. It disagrees on 3 lists, each a five whose one-peak pair has a density near 10⁻⁷.
  • What was not certified. GPT-6.2 Astra’s two remaining boxes are too wide for the certifier: its analytic five over a whole unit of c, and the uniqueness rectangle, which is a statement and not a list of solutions. A refusal is not a refutation.

2 · where the equilibrium is unique

A proof, and how far it reaches

  • The proof. GPT-6.2 Astra proved that for −7 ≤ c ≤ 0 and 0 ≤ A ≤ 1.2 the game has exactly one equilibrium, even or not. The proof is in corpus/mfg-two-models/astra-report.tex. It bounds every possible solution, then shows that two different solutions would force a quantity above 14.18 to be at most 14.
  • Its readers. I read it with Claude line by line and found no error. Claude Fable 5.2 read it in the cross-read, filled eight steps the text leaves implicit, recomputed every constant and found none either. Both readers are the same model, and the second knew the result of the first. No one outside that family has read it.
  • The proof as an instrument. The proof uses only the largest |c| and the largest A of its rectangle, so its last inequality decides any rectangle. labs/mfg/uniqueness.py evaluates it in exact rational arithmetic and finds, for each depth, the most negative coupling at which it holds. Claude Fable 5.2 computed the same curve independently in the cross-read.
  • On the map. 1,786 of the 8,086 undecided cells lie inside the region where the inequality holds, 40.1% of the map’s area; the rectangle as its author stated it covers 1,344 cells, 35%. No cell the map proves to hold two equilibria lies inside. The published map is not changed by this page.
  • Against the truth. By Claude Fable 5.2’s fold table, the part of the map with c < 0 where the equilibrium is unique is 56.1% of the area. The proof reaches 71.5% of it. Claude Fable 5.2 also showed why this kind of argument cannot pass c = −π²: it is uniform down to A = 0, where uniqueness fails beyond that value.
  • A second, weaker proof, and what a reader found in it. Claude Fable 5.2 proved uniqueness when |c|·exp(3|c| + 3A/π) < π², a thin strip. I had read that proof and reported no error. GPT-6.2 Astra, reading it afterwards, found that it never proves an equilibrium exists (and supplied the argument), that one entry of its table was rounded the wrong way, and that one step tightens to give exp(2|c| + 2A/π). The uniqueness itself holds.

3 · the first fold

Two derivations, one curve

  • The law. Where the count goes from one to three there is a fold. GPT-6.2 Astra derived its depth as A = (4/3)·√(2/(3π²))·δ^(3/2)·(1 + δ/(16π²) + …) with δ = −c − π². Claude Fable 5.2 derived the leading term another way and computed the fold as a table. The two leading terms are algebraically the same.
  • The comparison. At the couplings where Claude Fable 5.2’s table puts the fold for A = 0.1, 0.5, 1, 1.2, GPT-6.2 Astra’s two-term formula returns A = 0.09997, 0.49873, 0.99376, 1.19054. It is an expansion near the cusp, and it drifts as the depth grows.
  • The higher folds. In the cross-read GPT-6.2 Astra gave the leading behaviour of the second and third folds: they leave −4π² and −9π² like 0.1334·A^(4/3) and 0.005402·A². Claude Fable 5.2’s independent table gives 0.005402 for the third, and 0.1346, 0.1354, 0.1365 at A = 0.05, 0.1, 0.2 for the second, falling toward the stated value. That proof has had no reader but its author.
  • What this is. Floating point on both sides. Nothing on this page certifies a fold.

prior work

What was already known

A short search, not a survey. Nothing on this page should be called new before someone who knows this literature has read it.

  • M. Cirant, Stationary focusing Mean Field Games (2016): the Schrödinger form of the quadratic game (Lemma 4.1); with a potential, uniqueness of the normalised solution is ’in general an open problem’.
  • L. D. Carr, C. W. Clark, W. P. Reinhardt, Stationary solutions of the one-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation. II. Case of attractive nonlinearity (2000): all stationary solutions under periodic conditions, the dn profiles among them.
  • R. Kanamoto, H. Saito, M. Ueda, Quantum phase transition in one-dimensional Bose-Einstein condensates with attractive interactions (2003): the uniform state stops being the ground state at the coupling that is c = -pi^2 here.

Not found: a uniqueness result for this game with a potential, with an explicit region; a certified coexistence of five or seven equilibria.

who did what

Each step is a separate job

  • The mathematics. GPT-6.2 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2: the reduction, the count, the proofs of uniqueness, the fold laws, and the candidate solutions each submitted. Each finding is credited in the table above.
  • The readings. Each model on the other’s proofs; and I, with Claude, on both.
  • The certifier. This repository’s validated-numerics certifier for the game, and its point version as a second decider. It decides what it is given; it found nothing.
  • The seven, the instrument and this page. Carlos Toledo, with Claude (Anthropic), which wrote the programs under my direction. The runs were made on 18 November 2026 on one laptop.
