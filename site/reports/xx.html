The real roots of xˣ = y: every fraction bracket an AI model proposed, decided exactly

xˣ = y, by fractions

It began with one picture, xˣ = 64. The equation has one real root, and no fraction equals it. What a fraction can do is trap it: one fraction on either side, each side one comparison of two whole numbers, decided exactly. An AI model computed the root for every y to a thousand and proposed the traps; claimbox stated them as claims; this page decides every side again with code of its own and reads what the fractions show.

Decided here: every side of every trap the study proposed, the four whole-number roots from both sides, and the eight claims about xˣ = 64, each by one exact comparison of integers (certs/xx-ledger.json, tools/run-xx-ledger.py). claimbox’s words are shown beside ours. Computed and not decided: the roots’ digits, their continued fractions, the closest fractions and every statistic. Proved in Lean and checked by two kernels: that the root is unique and that a fraction bounds it exactly when one integer exceeds another (certs/xx-reduction-lean.json). Read and not decided: the negative-base convention. Refused: one claim of our own, that the root of 64 is transcendental. Cited and not decided: the Gelfond–Schneider theorem.

tl;dr
  • The finding. 210 of 218 claims CERTIFIED and the other 8, the planted wrong brackets, REFUTED: 105 roots of xˣ = y each trapped between two fractions, and at y = 4, 27, 256 and 3,125 the root pinned to a whole number from both sides. The root of 64 is decided to nine decimals. claimbox’s record gives the same word on every claim. One claim of cert-machine’s own, that the root of 64 is transcendental, is REFUSED: it is true, by a cited theorem, and no comparison of two integers decides it.
  • The mechanism. For a fraction p/q above 1, x > p/q exactly when pp < Nq·qp, where N = y is read from the pinned file; both sides are computed as whole numbers and compared. No logarithm is evaluated. 216 mutants, each fraction moved one unit the wrong way or each whole-number bound made strict, must flip to REFUTED, and all do. That the comparison decides the bracket is proved in Lean and checked by two kernels (§4).
  • Check it. python3 -I tools/run-xx-ledger.py --check (seconds; the Python standard library) re-hashes the copies and re-decides every claim.

The first two rows are the AI’s four-decimal proposal; the planted rows move it one unit in the last decimal, the wrong way, and are refuted. A refutation here is a comparison of two numbers, so it has no witness point to draw.

The last row is cert-machine’s own claim, and it is refused. This decider answers one question: does the root lie above or below a given fraction, by one comparison of two whole numbers. "Transcendental" says the root is a root of no polynomial with integer coefficients: infinitely many polynomials, not one comparison, and no number of decided brackets reaches it. It is true: the root is not a fraction (p/q in lowest terms with q > 1 would give pp = 64q·qp, which q cannot divide, and no whole k has kk = 64), and an irrational algebraic x would make xˣ transcendental by the Gelfond–Schneider theorem, while 64 is not. That theorem is cited, not decided here (§3, one). What would make it decidable. A kernel-checked proof: the Gelfond–Schneider theorem formalized (Mathlib at the pin of lean/xx-reduction, d13f23b7, holds no Gelfond–Schneider theorem), then the two-line argument above in Lean on top of XXReduction.existsUnique_root. With that, it is a theorem to check, not a comparison to compute. claimbox stated no such claim; it is the one row of this page that is not claimbox’s.

§3 · what the fractions show

Four things the continued fractions say

Read from the computed roots. The brackets are decided; the patterns below are observations on computed numbers, and each is labelled as one. The first is a theorem, cited.

One: only kᵏ has a fraction for a root (a theorem, cited)

The root is a whole number exactly at y = 4, 27, 256 and 3,125, and each is pinned from both sides: x ≥ k and x ≤ k, both certified here. For every other whole y the root is not even algebraic. If it were a fraction p/q in lowest terms with q above 1, then pp = yq·qp, which q cannot divide; if it were an irrational algebraic number, xˣ would be transcendental by the Gelfond–Schneider theorem. That theorem is cited, not decided here, which is why the claim of §1’s last row is refused. So 4 roots are fractions, and every other one is a number no fraction will ever equal.

Two: near kᵏ, one enormous step (an observation)

Just off kᵏ the root sits just off k, by about 1/(kᵏ(1 + ln k)), the reciprocal of the slope of xˣ at k. Its continued fraction says so with one huge quotient: at y = 255 it is 608, where the slope predicts 610.9; at y = 3,126 it is 8,155, against 8154.5. The closest fraction there is the whole number k.

Three: elsewhere, the roots look like typical numbers (an observation)

Pool the partial quotients of all the irrational roots and they follow the law that almost every real number obeys: a quotient of 1 occurs 0.41162 of the time, against the law’s 0.41504. Their geometric mean is 2.6729, against Khinchin’s constant 2.6855, and (ln qn)/n averages 1.1777, against Lévy’s 1.1866. These are observations on 1,002 computed roots, not a proof.

Four: a few roots have fractions that are almost too good (an observation)

A large partial quotient makes the convergent just before it unusually close. The largest found is 4,651,670, in the root for y = 709. Its convergent sits closer to the root than a fraction of its size usually can. For y = 100, the fraction 795/221 is already off by only 4.433289e-08.

§4 · how it was done

Proposed, stated, decided

  • An AI model proposes. claude-opus-5-5 computed each root by Newton’s method, its continued fraction, and the two fractions that bracket it (claimbox’s tools/xx_study.py, copied here as evidence and never run). Computed, not trusted.
  • claimbox states. Each bracket became a claim in its language, xroot(n) > p/q, with y read from a pinned file, and cert-machine’s receiver decided it there (claimbox’s check records, the second decision on this page). Eight planted wrong brackets went in with them.
  • cert-machine decides, again. tools/run-xx-ledger.py reads the copies of those records that LIFT-claimbox.json made (every file re-hashed against PROVENANCE-claimbox.json, which names the claimbox commit they came from), decides every side with code that shares nothing with claimbox or the receiver, refuses if any word differs from claimbox’s, and requires every planted bracket and every mutant to come out REFUTED: 228 of 228 red controls fired.
  • Lean proves the step. The reduction from the root to the integer comparison is a Lean theorem, checked by two kernels on GitHub’s runners; the ledger re-hashes the Lean file against the run’s record before it names the theorems.
  • Our own claim is refused. One claim the pipeline cannot decide went in beside claimbox’s, and the decider refused it, with the reason and what would decide it written down (§1).

What the decisions rest on. Each verdict above is an exact integer comparison, and the step from the root to that comparison is decided: proved in Lean 4 with Mathlib (lean/xx-reduction). For a whole N ≥ 2, xˣ = N has exactly one positive real root (XXReduction.existsUnique_root), and for whole numbers 1 ≤ q ≤ p it lies above p/q exactly when pp < Nq·qp (XXReduction.lt_root_iff; XXReduction.le_root_iff, XXReduction.root_lt_iff and XXReduction.root_le_iff for the other three comparisons, XXReduction.natCast_le_root_iff and XXReduction.root_le_natCast_iff for a whole number). Every one of the 15 theorems passes Lean’s kernel inside lake build, Lean’s kernel again inside Comparator on a fresh runner, and nanoda, a kernel written independently of Lean’s (run 41221355209), and depends on no axiom beyond propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. A fraction the study proposed is above 1 in lowest terms, so 1 ≤ q < p; a whole-number bound is q = 1: every comparison on this page is inside the theorems. What is left is read or cited:

  1. assumed
    Only a positive x counts as a root the theorems speak of positive reals (Mathlib’s Real.rpow); what xˣ means for a negative x, and that no negative x is a root worth counting, is the convention claimbox’s library states (claimbox/library/selfpower.py, copied here); read, not decided
  2. assumed
    The recorder computes pp and Nq·qp correctly Python’s whole numbers in tools/run-xx-ledger.py; the theorems say what the comparison means, not that the code computes it. Tested by 228 red controls, not proved
  3. assumed
    Only kᵏ has a fraction for a root (§3, one) the Gelfond–Schneider theorem, cited, with the two-line divisibility argument for a fraction; not decided here, and the claim of §1’s last row is refused for that reason

What is not claimed. That the computed digits are right beyond the decided ones; that the observations of §3 are theorems; that the root of 64 is transcendental (true, cited, refused here); anything about a y the study did not decide. The study’s numbers are a proposer’s, and this page draws them dashed.
