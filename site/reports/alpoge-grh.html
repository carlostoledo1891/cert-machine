Alpöge’s Lean proof of RH: the right statement. Now, the proof.

On 20 November 2026 Levent Alpöge announced a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, made with Claude and released only as a computer proof: 175,187 files of Lean, a language in which a small program, the kernel, checks every step. Done: the files are the announced ones, and Lean proofs show that the statement is exactly the textbook RH, even with ζ defined by its series rather than by Mathlib. Running now: the replay of every file, the statement judge, three kernels (two of them written independently of Lean) and five planted errors the checkers must catch. What a computer check cannot tell you is said just as plainly.

This page checks a computer proof: whether the files compile, what the theorem says, and whether independent checkers accept it. It does not judge the mathematics as mathematics, and it is not a refereed review.

1 · what is claimed

One sentence about where ζ vanishes

  • The Riemann Hypothesis. The zeta function ζ(s) is zero at s = −2, −4, −6, … (the “trivial” zeros) and at infinitely many other points, all inside the vertical band 0 < Re s < 1. RH says all those other zeros lie exactly on the middle line, Re s = 1/2. It controls how evenly the primes are spread.
  • What the proof states. The headline theorem is riemannHypothesis : RiemannHypothesis, where RiemannHypothesis is Mathlib’s own definition, written years before this proof: every zero of ζ, except the trivial ones and the point s = 1, has real part 1/2.
  • Is that really RH? Two readers from two model families say yes. More: we wrote RH a second way, the textbook way (“every zero in the critical strip has real part 1/2”), and proved in Lean that the two statements are equivalent. That turns a reading into a proof.
  • Even without Mathlib’s ζ. One step further: we defined ζ and the L-functions the way a textbook does — the functions given by the series 1 + 2^−s + 3^−s + … (and χ(n)·n^−s) that continue smoothly to the rest of the plane — and proved in Lean that the release’s statements say exactly the same thing about them. The statement no longer depends on how Mathlib happens to define ζ.

Select a statement to see what it talks about.

  • And GRH? Four more theorems say the same for Dirichlet L-functions, the relatives of ζ that count primes in arithmetic progressions: every modulus, every character. That is the usual meaning of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. It is not the “Extended” RH for other number fields, which the announcement expects “with more time”.
2 · how big is it

17 million lines, one theorem

  • Why a computer check matters. A human-readable version of this argument would run past 250,000 pages, the author says. A proof this size can only be trusted the way a large program is trusted: by machines that check every step, written by different people, agreeing.
  • How much of it carries the proof? Not every file has to matter. Once everything is compiled we walk the finished proof of the five theorems, declaration by declaration, and count what it really reaches — in the release and in Mathlib.

5 · inside the proof

What carries the conclusion

  • The route. Read from the top down: RH follows from “no Dirichlet L-function vanishes right of 1/2”, plus ζ’s functional equation for the left half. That in turn rests on a window criterion and on a link whose two inequalities come from matrix estimates.
  • Names are not evidence. Several parts are named for deep mathematics but are empty in the code: a geometry condition that is literally True, a geometric model whose key map is zero, a theorem named for a simple pole that proves True, two “literature” inputs that are one statement. The proof can still be valid — the kernel checks what is there, not what things are called — but those names do not explain it.
  • J. L. Nielsen’s critique. A paper posted the same day, The Invalidity of Alpöge’s Proposed Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, reads the source and finds the vacuous geometric and lattice witnesses above, and a height set equal to its own formula. We confirmed each quote in the pinned files. Our verdict on that point: the geometric interpretation of this step remains unverified. The paper’s title goes further than its analysis: the formal theorem is decided by the kernels, not by the names, and the paper’s own appendix grants that the proof term may well check.
  • The question that remains. The height enters with two inequalities supplied separately, derived from about forty matrix-norm, covolume and valuation estimates. Whether those estimates are where the argument really lives — and what they mean — is a question for number theorists, not for a kernel. We trace, in the compiled proof, which sub-proofs feed them (running).
6 · connections

Two other claims, connected in Lean

  • The August release. Anthropic’s August proof that at least two thirds of the zeros are simple and on the critical line checks unchanged — all 326 files — on the Mathlib of today’s release, and both kernels accept it. Put together with today’s RH, its bound that 5/6 of the zeros are distinct becomes a bound for distinct zeros on the line. The simplicity bounds this implies are the August ones again; nothing new beyond the composition.
  • Masullo’s draft. Alessandro Masullo’s all-human draft (posted by Ravi Vakil, 940 pages, reviewed for months by Brian Conrad) claims GRH for every number field and more: off the centre, every zero simple. Its main zero theorem is not yet written in the draft (page 522: “Insert statement.”). Read for ℚ, the claim implies this release’s statements; one double zero would refute it while leaving RH intact.
