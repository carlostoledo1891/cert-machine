The ten minimum universal sets of MacMahon cubes: a classification decided twice

On 1 November 2026 Jason Davies published a proof, developed with OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra and written in Lean, that exactly ten collections of twelve MacMahon cubes can build every one of the thirty cubes as a 2×2×2 model. It settles two conjectures of Johnson and Roldán from 2024. The result and the proof are his. With Claude (Anthropic’s AI model), I checked his release two ways that share no code with each other: my own program rebuilt all 300 of his released constructions, and two independent proof checkers accepted his ten Lean theorems.

  • The second route repeats a check that had already been run. The author’s own automated checks and the Palomar registry (entry PALOMAR-2026-11-01-000019) had checked the same commit.   • The first route uses his table of names. My program takes his table of the 30 colourings as the naming of the cubes, after checking that it lists every cube exactly once, and it takes his lists of which eight cubes to use. How to turn and place them it works out itself.

The paper’s written argument was not checked, and the author’s disclosure says the Lean proof takes a different route from it. The paper’s other computations (counts of how many cubes sets of eight to thirteen can build) are not Lean theorems, and I did not re-run them: his search and his verifier were read, never run.
  • Two commits. The kernels ran on commit d8856c97, the one the registry names. the claim

Thirty cubes, twelve at a time

Paint the six sides of a cube with six colours, one each. Up to turning the cube in the hand there are 30 ways, mirror images counted apart: the MacMahon cubes. A collection of cubes is universal if, for every one of the 30, eight cubes of the collection can be stacked into a 2×2×2 block whose outside is that cube, twice as large; the hidden sides need not match.

Kazuya Haraguchi showed that a universal collection needs at least 12 cubes and gave one of that size. In 2024 Inga Johnson and Erika Roldán conjectured that exactly 10 sets of 12 different cubes are universal (their Conjectures 4 and 8), and described them: the two-triple splits drawn above. Davies’s theorem is that the conjecture is right, and stays right when a collection may hold the same cube more than once: no collection with a repeat reaches the minimum.

1 · my own program

All 300 released constructions, rebuilt

  • What he released. For each of the 10 sets and each of the 30 cubes to build, the eight cubes that build it: 300 constructions.
  • What the program does. instruments/macmahon/check.py was written from the definitions by Claude (Anthropic’s AI model) under my direction, in Python’s standard library. It makes its own 24 rotations of a cube and its own eight corner positions. For each construction it searches for a way to turn the eight cubes and put one in each corner so that every outside side shows the right colour. It ignores the rotations and positions his file suggests, and runs none of his code.
  • First, the names. His table gives 30 colourings, six different colours on each, and no rotation takes one to another. They are the same 30 classes that all 720 colourings of a cube fall into, so the table lists every cube once. CERTIFIED
  • Then the sets. The released sets are 10 different sets of 12 cubes, and they are exactly the 10 splits of six letters into two triples. CERTIFIED
  • Then the constructions. 300 of 300 build their cube. CERTIFIED
  • What that decides. That each of the 10 sets can build all 30 cubes. It says nothing about whether other sets can.

It matches: a collection counts copies, so repeats are in the statement; a construction needs eight distinct physical cubes; only the outside sides are constrained; all 30 cubes must be built. This is a reading, kept apart from what the kernels decided.

The reading, in 6 points

Challenge.lean (imports only Mathlib) read against the paper's Theorem 1 ("The minimum size of a universal collection is twelve. Exactly ten labelled collections attain that size, even when repeated varieties are permitted. Each unordered partition of the six labels into triples gives one; these are all; none has repeats.") — a reading, not a decision.

  • 1. An Inventory is Pair → ℕ: multiplicities allowed, so "repeated varieties permitted" is in the statement; DiagonalZero removes the thirty diagonal non-varieties; total is the collection size.
  • 2. PhysicalBuilds a t asks for an INJECTION of the eight slots into the stock (eight distinct physical copies) with a rotation each, and FaceFits constrains only the three exposed faces of each slot: "exteriors only, internal faces free" as the paper says.
  • 3. PhysicalUniversal quantifies over every ordered pair i ≠ j: all thirty varieties, each labelled by Conway's tableau; "labelled" (colours fixed, no quotient by colour permutations) is the statement's own frame, as the paper's Theorem 1 says.
  • 4. minimum_size (IsLeast … 12) and physical_minimum are the first sentence; physical_exactly_ten (Nat.card = 10) the second; physical_iff_two_triangles the third (twoTriangles s for a 3-subset s); physical_minimum_has_no_repeats the last.
  • 5. The five auxiliary theorems (proper_rotation_iff, exposed_face_rule, face_colours_valid, face_assignment_coverage, face_assignment_unique) certify the tables the statement rests on: the 24 rotations are exactly the proper ones, the slots expose the outward faces, and the faceColours table lists every six-distinct-colour colouring exactly once up to rotation — the same facts certs/macmahon-witnesses.json decided independently (T1).
  • 6. Not in the Lean statement: the physical cubes themselves (the model is the discrete one the paper declares: six face directions, signed-axis permutations of determinant +1). The paper says so; this is the one step a reader takes on the paper's word.
3 · planted errors

What would have caught a wrong construction

  • A cube from outside the set. In one released construction, one of the eight cubes is swapped for a cube that is not in the set. The program refuses it.
  • A wrong cube to build. The same eight cubes are asked to build a cube with two of its sides exchanged. The program refuses it.
  • Mirror images allowed. If turning a cube could include mirror reflections (48 maps in place of 24), the 30 cubes would merge into 15 and the check of the names would fail. Under that table it does.
  • None on the second route. I planted no false theorem in the kernel run. What guards the statement there is Comparator’s own comparison with the separate statement file.
