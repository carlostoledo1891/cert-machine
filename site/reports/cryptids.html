The cryptids audit: a repository's finite layer, decided twice

What a producer's checker checks, and what it does not

google-deepmind/cryptids publishes small Turing machines whose halting encodes Goldbach, ZF consistency and the Riemann Hypothesis, with Lean proofs of the state reductions and a standard-library checker of its own, verify.py. The theorems are the kernel's. Underneath them is a finite layer — pinned tables, digests, state and cell counts, the step at which a bootstrap ends and the configuration it ends in, ratios printed to their digits — and that layer can be decided exactly from the bytes. It was decided here twice, at commit 664cd03904a8…: once by hand, one lane written for this source, and once through claimbox, the claim-machine, with the receiver's decider for machines. The gaps come first.

Published, not peer-reviewed. Nothing here verifies a halting equivalence, a non-halting proof or the semantics; those are the Lean kernel's, and the kernel lane below only re-checks the build, the stated theorems and the axioms under a second kernel. The two arms were run by the same agent, the hand lane first, so what it learned about the source was available to the other; the pre-registration says so. The repository's authors were not contacted before this page; the asks are in the gaps.

tl;dr
  • The finding. 8 gaps in the repository's finite layer, found by reading verify.py and confirmed by running. The checker never builds Lean and tests theorem statements by substring; its riemann_104 check says it runs riemann_120 and never does; it records the proof files' digests and compares them to nothing; one write-up counts "non-halting transitions" by one convention and another by the other; the riemann_104 checkpoint table prints steps at "checkpoint k" without saying which event k counts (the printed steps are root entries, the 179th, 210th, 879th and 1,721st, not the 100th, 116th, 500th and 1,000th). Everything else the finite layer prints holds: 143 of 152 claims CERTIFIED by hand, 95 of 107 through claimbox, 124 of 128 paired claims with the same word.
  • The mechanism. Every table parsed, every upstream source compiled under the stated convention, every machine simulated from the blank tape in exact integers — by two implementations that share no code in each arm. A claim is one of three words: CERTIFIED with the certificate, REFUTED with the witness, REFUSED with the rule and, where there is one, the threshold that would change the word.
  • Check it. node tools/build-cryptids-benchmark.js --check recomputes the comparison from the two arms' records; node tools/run-cryptids.js re-derives arm H's rows in JavaScript; python3 instruments/claimbox/tm.py --decide and tm.js re-decide claimbox's objects; make drift re-hashes the repository's copy against its pin.

§1 · the gaps

What verify.py does not check, and what the write-ups print loosely

The producer's checker is a careful piece of work: it pins five upstream sources and their derived tables by sha256, re-derives each table from its source in three formats, checks that the Lean definitions load the right files, and replays bootstraps and lockstep checkpoints. What follows is what it does not do, read before any run and confirmed by running; each row names the claims here that decide the matter.

The asks. Publish the selection rule that makes a root entry a "checkpoint", and the four candidate rows and five reference rows of the riemann_104 table become decidable from the bytes. Pin the proof files' digests, or drop the lines that record them. Use one convention for "non-halting transitions". Run riemann_120 in the check that says it does.

§2 · by hand

The one refutation. the ZF reduction takes 785 non-halting transitions to 759, computed [784,758] against [785,759]. each ZF table has two halting cells: the explicit halt line of the source and one (state, symbol) pair the source never defines; the printed counts are the non-halting cells plus one, i.e. the transitions the source defines including the one that halts — the riemann_104 write-up counts the other way (238 and 207 are non-halting cells exactly), so the two write-ups use two conventions for one phrase

The refusals, each with its rule.

  • core-transitions-goldbach. no definition of "core" is printed; the table has 49 and 41 non-halting cells; the claim is not decidable from the bytes until "core" is defined
  • riemann-104-checkpoint-k. the event "checkpoint k" is not defined in finite terms: the printed steps are root entries, but the 179th, 210th, 879th and 1,721st, not the 100th, 116th, 500th and 1,000th; which root entries count as instruction checkpoints is the register machine's abstraction and is not printed. Publish the selection rule and the four rows become decidable from the bytes
  • riemann-120-checkpoint-table. the root entry is named by a register-machine state (main.0), not by a state of riemann_120, and "checkpoint k" is not defined in finite terms (the candidate column above); publish the state of riemann_120 that is the root and the selection rule, and the column becomes decidable — the stage verify.py says it checks and never runs
  • kernel-all-proofs. a theorem, not a finite claim: the Lean kernel is the authority; the kernel lane re-checks the build and the axioms as stated (a concordance), this lane does not
  • kernel-equivalences. a theorem, not a finite claim: the Lean kernel is the authority; the kernel lane re-checks the build and the axioms as stated (a concordance), this lane does not
  • kernel-holdouts. a theorem, not a finite claim: the Lean kernel is the authority; the kernel lane re-checks the build and the axioms as stated (a concordance), this lane does not
  • riemann-104-register-values. the encoding of the five registers on the tape is not stated in finite terms in the write-up; the step counts are decided above, the register values are not
  • verifypy-proof-hashes. recorded and compared to nothing: no pin exists for the proof files in verify.py or PROVENANCE.md, so there is no claim to decide; a plant in a proof file flips no word (recall plant 10)

What the long runs decided. zf_380 after 10,015,814 steps has the tape and head zf_393 has after 10,000,000 steps (the write-up's step overhead, read as a configuration correspondence): CERTIFIED. riemann_120 does not halt within 89,775,610 steps, the length the write-up prints for its bootstrap: CERTIFIED. each candidate step the write-up's checkpoint table prints (26,093; 28,934; 165,200; 498,382) is a step at which riemann_104 enters its root state 19: CERTIFIED; they are the 26,093 → 179th, 28,934 → 210th, 165,200 → 879th, 498,382 → 1,721th entries, counting the boot entry as the 0th.

§3 · through claimbox

Arm C: a reader, the grammar, the receiver

claimbox's reader (claimbox/readers/cryptids.py) turns the sentences the sources print — the provenance table, verify.py's pinned constants and assertions, the write-ups — into jobs, each with the quote byte for byte and the file it is about; the grammar's new machine family states the claim or refuses it with a rule; the translator writes the object; cert-machine's decider for machines (instruments/claimbox/tm.py, with tm.js held to it) decides the named functions over the pinned bytes; the ratios and percentages are exact expressions the checker decides from the object alone; and the checker (instruments/claimbox/check.py) admits or refuses every object: 106 admitted, 1 refused (ratio-riemann-104-boot: a digit-grouped decimal, 9,934.23, which the receiver's printed-bar rule does not read).

Reading it. The hand lane reaches further: it states 46 claims the reader has no object for (its own measurements, the Lean-definition and statement-string checks, the counts the receiver has no decider for) and it supplied readings where the write-ups are loose. The claimbox lane is the one an outsider can check without reading the auditor: every object carries the quote, the pin and the record, and a checker that shares no code with the reader accepts or refuses it. On the claims both state, the words agree except where the receiver's format or decider surface falls short, which is a measurement of the receiver, not a disagreement about the repository. The same two findings, the counting convention and the checkpoint event with no finite definition, came out of both arms.

§6 · what is claimed, and what is not

The limits

  • Claimed. For each finite claim the sources print, a decision from the bytes at the pin by two implementations that share no code, with the reading stated where the auditor supplied one; the gaps of §1, each decided or refused with its rule; the comparison of the two arms on the metrics fixed before the run.
  • Used, not re-proved. The Lean kernel's acceptance of the proofs, and comparator's check that the stated theorems are the proved ones; the compilation convention the provenance file states; the checkpoint pattern verify.py states for the Goldbach stages.
  • Not claimed. Any halting equivalence, non-halting proof or semantics; that the "core" transitions, the "checkpoint k" events or the register values mean anything in particular; that the holdout machines are cryptids (the community's word).
  • Independence. From the repository, whose checker was read but never run as evidence; not from the operator (one), and not between the arms (the same agent, the hand lane first).
