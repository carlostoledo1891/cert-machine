Pálvölgyi’s heptagon and his unchecked Theorem A.3, decided in Lean

Graham conjectured that every finite set of points on a sphere is Ramsey. In September 2026 Dömötör Pálvölgyi, with ChatGPT, refuted it with seven points on a circle, and OpenAI classified exactly which finite sets are Ramsey, in Lean. Pálvölgyi’s appendix, labelled by its author as ChatGPT’s and unchecked, goes further: almost any seven points on a circle are not Ramsey. I decided both in Lean through OpenAI’s own theorem, with the rules sealed before any code ran, and the pull request that marks Graham’s conjecture solved in Google DeepMind’s list now links the proof.

tl;dr
  • The finding. The heptagon is not Ramsey, so formal-conjectures’ statement of Graham’s conjecture is false; and Theorem A.3 of the appendix holds as printed. Lean’s kernel accepts all six theorems from scratch, four independent kernels accept them, and nothing but propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound is used.
  • The mechanism. Pálvölgyi’s weights make a derivation’s “energy” come out as −24/((2r−1)(2r−3)(2r−4)), never zero, which fails OpenAI’s exact criterion; the necessity half of their classification then says the set is not Ramsey. One remark on the paper came out of it: the main text’s Equation (2) does not hold for A.3’s points, and the appendix’s proof, correctly, never needs it.
  • Check it. node tools/record-heptagon.js --check re-derives every verdict from the runs’ facts and re-runs one of the two exact deciders; the Lean is heptagon-ramsey-lean, one command to rebuild.

first, the limits

What this does not decide

  • The “almost every” clause of A.3. The appendix concludes that almost every seven points of a circle are not Ramsey. That needs a measure-zero argument, not formalized here: the Lean theorem assumes the independence, as OpenAI’s nine-point theorem does.
  • The rest of the appendix. Theorems A.7 and A.12 and every other appendix result except A.3.
  • That seven is the least. Every six points on a circle are Ramsey: proved in Lean by JD Jones and by wstrinz (Will Strinz), not here.
  • The definitions. The theorems are about OpenAI’s Ramsey and FieldCriterion and formal-conjectures’ IsRamsey. That these capture the informal notions is their authors’ formalization, taken as given; their proofs are inside what the kernels replay.
  • Formal-conjectures’ own environment. The proof builds inside openai/math (Lean v4.34.1), where formal-conjectures’ IsRamsey and statement are copied byte for byte and checked against the pinned file. It does not build in formal-conjectures’ repository; a registry entry that does is in preparation.
  • What the second kernels see. nanoda, con-ron, lean4lean and con-leche read an export of the environment: they check what the exporter wrote.

the question

Is every set of points on a sphere Ramsey?

Take a finite set of points, like the corners of a triangle. It is Ramsey if, for any number of colours, once the dimension is high enough every colouring of space contains a copy of it, the same shape and size, all in one colour. In 1973 Erdős, Graham, Montgomery, Rothschild, Spencer and Straus proved that a Ramsey set must lie on a sphere. Graham conjectured the converse: every finite set on a sphere is Ramsey.

In DeepMind’s list the conjecture is Erdos174.erdos_174.variants.graham in FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/174.lean, still marked open at commit 2055e5a.

the claims

A counterexample, a classification, and an appendix marked unchecked

On 20 September 2026 Pálvölgyi posted A cyclic non-Ramsey heptagon. Its Theorem 1 is the seven points above; its proofs are attributed to ChatGPT (“I contributed nothing to the proofs or ideas”), and its appendix, headed “Appendix by ChatGPT — unchecked”, states Theorem A.3: seven points on a circle whose parameters are generic are not Ramsey. Three days later OpenAI published a classification of finite Euclidean Ramsey configurations with its Lean in openai/math: a set is Ramsey exactly when it passes an algebraic test, the field criterion. OpenAI cites the heptagon and says A.3 “already implies” its own nine-point counterexample.

No one had the heptagon in Lean. DeepMind’s entry for Graham’s conjecture stayed open; on 31 October Anas Khan opened pull request #7706 to re-tag it as solved, with the statement ¬ (∀ …) and no proof.

H1 · the identities

Two deciders, written apart, agree on every identity

Before any Lean, two programs decided the paper’s algebra exactly, each written from the paper’s text alone, neither reading the other: one in SymPy (97 identities, all 97 CERTIFIED, its 11 planted false identities all refused), one in the Python standard library with its own polynomial arithmetic (152 identities, all CERTIFIED; 3 planted wrong versions of the heptagon and of A.3, each caught). For the heptagon: the moments vanish and the energy is Σλ‖D(p)‖² = −24/((2r−1)(2r−3)(2r−4)). For A.3, every finite identity of the proof holds for any seven distinct parameters; algebraic independence enters only where a derivation is chosen.

One remark on the paper. The main text’s Equation (2) asks the matrix M = Σλ D(p) pᵀ to vanish, and for the heptagon it does. For A.3’s points it does not: at t = (0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 11) it is [[92/19825, -86/19825], [-86/19825, -92/19825]], symmetric and not zero. The appendix’s proof goes through Lemma A.1, which asks only that M be symmetric, so A.3 is right as printed; it could not have been proved through Equation (2). The Lean proof carries the symmetric form too.

H2 · the heptagon in Lean

Not Ramsey, through the other lab’s theorem

On OpenAI’s chain, unchanged: a derivation D of the reals with D r = 1 (their exists_real_derivation), the pairing x ⊗ y ↦ D x · D y on their tensor ring, and Pálvölgyi’s weights give a nonzero trace, so the heptagon fails the criterion:

theorem not_fieldCriterion : ∀ (r : ℝ), 2 < r → Transcendental ℚ r → ¬OAI.EuclideanRamsey.FieldCriterion (HeptagonRamsey.Heptagon.heptagon r)

The necessity half of their classification turns that into the theorem, and a transport carries it to DeepMind’s definition, with the zero-dimensional case handled as in issue #7517:

theorem not_ramsey : ∀ (r : ℝ), 2 < r → Transcendental ℚ r → ¬OAI.EuclideanRamsey.Ramsey (HeptagonRamsey.Heptagon.heptagon r)

theorem graham_false' : ¬∀ {n : ℕ} (A : Set (EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin n))), A.Finite → EuclideanGeometry.Cospherical A → Erdos174.IsRamsey A

The second statement is pull request #7706’s, token for token. DeepMind’s IsRamsey and the conjecture’s statement are copied from the pinned file, 273 and 113 bytes, and a script refuses the build if one byte differs (it refuses a one-token change, as it must). The build: 8,949 jobs, no warning from our files.

H4 · D · Theorem A.3

The appendix theorem, as printed, and its corollary

A.3 says: if three of the parameters are distinct and the other four are algebraically independent over the field the three generate, the seven points ((1 − t²)/(1 + t²), 2t/(1 + t²)) are not Ramsey. The Lean proof follows the appendix: Lemma A.1 in its symmetric form, a derivation vanishing on that field with prescribed values on the four (OpenAI’s construction generalised from ℚ to any base field), and the weighted moments by Lagrange interpolation.

theorem generic_seven_not_ramsey : ∀ (t : Fin 7 → ℝ),
  (Function.Injective fun i => t ⟨↑i, ⋯⟩) →
    (AlgebraicIndependent ↥(HeptagonRamsey.Generic.baseField t) fun i => t ⟨↑i + 3, ⋯⟩) →
      ¬OAI.EuclideanRamsey.Ramsey (HeptagonRamsey.Generic.circ t)

Its corollary in exactly the shape of OpenAI’s nine-point theorem, seven points whose parameters are algebraically independent over ℚ, in their literal parametrisation (a script checks it against their statement token for token, and refuses two planted changes):

theorem seven_circle_configuration : ∀ (t : Fin 7 → ℝ),
  AlgebraicIndependent ℚ t →
    Function.Injective (HeptagonRamsey.Generic.literal t) ∧
      (∀ (i : Fin 7), dist (HeptagonRamsey.Generic.literal t i) 0 = 1) ∧
        ¬OAI.EuclideanRamsey.Ramsey (HeptagonRamsey.Generic.literal t)

wstrinz (Will Strinz) had certified one instance of A.3 exactly in October, three rational points and four independent ones. This is the first proof of the theorem as stated, and the first in Lean.
