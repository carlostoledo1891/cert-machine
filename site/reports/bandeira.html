Bandeira's Open Problem 0.2(a): the disproof, decided

A university group's autonomous research system published a disproof of Bandeira's matrix AM–GM problem at n = 5: a reduction to Lai–Lim's refutation of the Recht–Ré conjecture, an exact rational Farkas certificate for the semidefinite program behind it, and a Lean 4 formalization. Two gaps, both at the boundary the paper itself declares external: the claimant's certificate decides a different semidefinite program — the one its code wrote, without the polynomial transposes that make the Gram form a sum of squares — and the Lean development checks the certificate's arithmetic, not the program's infeasibility: no SDP is defined in Lean, and its impossibility theorems assume as fields what the certificate must deliver. The finite core is re-decided here with a certificate of our own, found in seconds and decided in exact rationals, so the conclusion stands.

The claim: github.com/Oxelra-AI/Bandeira-0.2a-open-source at commit c4137344 (MIT; 2026-06), its paper, its certificate (an npz of pickled object arrays, read inertly by opcode and never unpickled) and its Lean sources, all pinned by sha256 in corpus/bandeira/PINS.json. What is decided is the semidefinite program's infeasibility, from the SDP's definition in Lai–Lim (ICML 2020) as read here, with no code of the claimant's run; the Positivstellensatz step is a published theorem, cited. Pre-registered before any verdict (corpus/bandeira/preregistration.json). Nothing has been sent to the claimant.

tl;dr
  • The finding. Gap 1 — the claimant's certificate certifies a different SDP. Its code reproduces Lai–Lim's "β X_i βᵀ" literally, with no transpose of the basis monomials; under that convention its certificate holds, pivot for pivot as published, and under the sum-of-squares form fᵀ X_i f it does not. In floating point the two programs have the same optimum on every case of Lai–Lim's Table 1 and at (5,5) — 144.6488 against 144.6488 — and whether they are equivalent in general is not decided here. Gap 2 — the Lean checks arithmetic, not infeasibility. The development proves (−M) ⪰ 0 for the claimant's matrices and an abstract contradiction over a structure whose fields assume the standard-form pairing and the cone inequality; no SDP is defined in Lean and the proved (−M) ⪰ 0 is never supplied to the contradiction — its own docstrings say the adapter remains to be filled "rather than by assumptions". The paper's boundary section says as much of C_i and b; its abstract says "the entire certificate". The disproof holds. Lai–Lim's sum-of-squares SDP at m = n = 5 is infeasible at λ = 120 by a Farkas certificate of our own (y₀ = −1, denominators ≤ 9,970, margin m₀₀ = -19/8568, scalar 1), so λ₂ > 120 = 5! and Bandeira's inequality (a) fails for n = 5 — with the noncommutative Positivstellensatz cited, as the claimant's paper also cites it. Nothing here rests on the claimant's certificate or its Lean.
  • The mechanism. The 3,906 monomial constraints of the SDP built from the definition in exact rationals (the one convention taken from the claimant, the order of the monomials, is tested by the scalar value); the dual slack's six 31 × 31 blocks decided positive definite by an exact LDLᵀ; the scalar inequality decided; a second implementation in JavaScript BigInt agreeing on every pivot. Our certificate was proposed by a float search (cvxpy, CLARABEL) that maximises the blocks' margin, then rounded and decided; the solver is never in the trust path.

§2 · the transpose

Which semidefinite program a certificate belongs to

Lai–Lim write the sum-of-squares side as tr(β X_i βᵀ Y_i) with β the column of basis monomials. A noncommutative square is fᵀ X_i f with f = Σ u_a β_a, and fᵀ X_i f = Σ u_a u_b β_aᵀ X_i β_b: the Gram form needs the first basis monomial reversed (X₁X₂ ↦ X₂X₁), which the notation βᵀ carries silently. The claimant's builder (corpus/bandeira/sources/lailim_sdp.py.txt, read as text) forms β_a X_i β_b with no reversal. That is a different linear map from Gram matrices to polynomials, so its Farkas certificate is a certificate of a different program.

The optimum of the two programs coincides in floating point on every case tried (§3), so the finding is about what the published certificate decides, not about the theorem.

§4 · the Lean

What the formalization proves, read at the pins

What the files state, and the paper lists: the equivalence 0.2(a) ⇔ (RR)ₙ,ₙ over an abstract ordered type (Core/BandeiraAMGM.lean), the spectral-norm power identity (Core/SpectralNormPSD.lean), the abstract scalar Farkas contradiction (Farkas/AbstractFarkas.lean), 5,766 standard-form entry equalities Q = −M by norm_num (Certificate/FarkasStandard/FarkasStandardFull.lean), six 31 × 31 blocks Q_b = L D Lᵀ row by row with positive pivots, hence PosSemidef (Certificate/Blocks/B*/), and the 187 × 187 block-diagonal −M PosSemidef (Farkas/AllBlocksFarkasChain.lean: negM_full_posSemidef). All of it for the claimant's matrices — the SDP of §2 without the transposes.

What they do not state: a definition of Lai–Lim's SDP, or a theorem that any SDP is infeasible. The two impossibility theorems — no_lailim_feasible_from_certificate_semantics (Farkas/SDPFarkasSemantics.lean) and no_native_farkas_feasible (Bridges/NativeFarkasAdapter.lean) — are IsEmpty of structures whose fields assume the standard-form pairing pairM = y₀c₀ + lin, the cone inequality or (−M) ⪰ 0, the bound c₀ ≤ 120 and the scalar inequality; negM_full_posSemidef is never supplied to either, nothing imports AllBlocksFarkasChain, and GeneratedFarkasTheoremShape takes three arbitrary Props as hypotheses. The docstrings say it: "the remaining work is to fill GeneratedCertificateAdapter from the actual generated theorem families rather than by assumptions." So Lean checks the certificate's arithmetic — which §2 also checks here, exactly, in two implementations — and the identification with Lai–Lim's program, the step §2 finds wanting, is outside Lean, as the paper's boundary section says of C_i and b. Its abstract says "the entire certificate" and its overview names "an SDP infeasibility semantic bridge".

Smaller, at the pin: Tests/UnformalizedDependencies.lean still lists the spectral-norm power identity as unformalized while Core/SpectralNormPSD.lean proves it, and calls the integer bound 144 > 120 "floating-point and NOT a rigorous bound" — that bound is proved by native_decide and is not load-bearing. native_decide (trust in the compiled evaluator, axiom Lean.ofReduceBool) appears on the default target in the NativePSD probes, a test file and that bound; on no theorem of the −M chain.

§5 · limits

What is not decided here

  • The Positivstellensatz. That infeasibility of the sum-of-squares SDP yields a PSD tuple violating the inequality is Helton–Klep–McCullough's theorem for bounded linear matrix-inequality domains, as Lai–Lim state it; cited, not re-derived, here as in the claimant's paper.
  • The equivalence of the two programs. The program the claimant certified and the sum-of-squares program have the same optimum on every case solved; whether their feasible sets coincide in general is not decided. It does not affect the conclusion, which rests on our certificate for the sum-of-squares program.
  • Same-author second implementation. verify.py and verify.js were written by the same author from the same definition; they catch a slip in one arithmetic, not a shared misreading of Lai–Lim. The claimant's published pivots, reproduced exactly under its convention, are the outside check on the construction.
  • The Lean, when built. Compilation and the kernel's axiom list say whether the statements read in §4 are proved as written; they cannot add a theorem the sources do not state.
  • Explicit witnesses. No five PSD matrices violating the inequality are exhibited, by the claimant or here; Lai–Lim note that none is known.
